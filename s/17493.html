LLMs for Innovation: Comparing Generative and Traditional LLM Approaches for Patent Analysis

Authors: Vito Giordano University of Pisa; Marco Consoloni University of Pisa; Gualtiero Fantoni University of Pisa

Session overview

Innovation management is inherently knowledge-intensive, generating and relying on large volumes of codified knowledge embedded in documents. Among these, patents are one of the most frequently used documents in innovation contexts due to their rich and structured information content, and the extraction and analysis of information from patents—commonly referred to as patent analysis—plays a central role in supporting innovation activities. As the scale of textual data grows, organizations increasingly depend on Natural Language Processing to extract actionable insights that support inventive activities, technological foresight, and intellectual property management. Recent advances in transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded the toolkit available for patent analysis, raising questions about the relative performance of traditional and generative approaches. This study compares traditional LLMs trained on labelled data with generative LLMs using prompting across three supervised patent analysis tasks: sentence classification, entity extraction, and relation extraction. We build a manually annotated dataset of 6,000 patent sentences and evaluate model effectiveness using precision, recall, and F1 scores, alongside efficiency in terms of time and computational cost. By varying training set sizes, we also examine how much labelled data traditional models require to match generative performance. The results provide guidance for selecting appropriate NLP approaches in Innovation contexts based on task requirements, data availability, and resource constraints.
