Session overview

Understanding identity management in organizations is essential for explaining why individuals sometimes thrive and other times struggle at work. Identity management involves the dynamic processes through which individuals construct, express, protect, and adjust their self- definitions in response to organizational cues, role demands, and social evaluation. This symposium brings together four complementary papers that collectively advance the nomological network of identity management by clarifying its antecedents, strategies, and outcomes across diverse organizational contexts. Specifically, the papers show how identity-relevant organizational cues activate identity threats or affordances, how individuals respond through distinct identity management strategies—including effort regulation, code-switching, and role calibration—and how these responses cascade into consequential psychological, behavioral, ethical, and relational outcomes. Drawing on experimental, field, and qualitative methods, the symposium positions identity management as a relational and institutional phenomenon embedded in leadership behaviors, diversity climates, occupational norms, and client interactions, rather than merely an individual difference construct. In line with recent theory, the session offers a unified, multi-method perspective demonstrating how identity management shapes adaptation, stigma, and authenticity at work, while highlighting organizational practices that can cultivate identity-safe climates and enable authenticity with psychological safety.
