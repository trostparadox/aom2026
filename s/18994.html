The Silent Disruptor: Risk Factors and Consequences of Workplace Phubbing-A Cross National Study

Session overview

Phubbing (phone + snubbing), the use of smartphones during face-to-face interactions, has become increasingly prevalent with digitalization and is associated with poorer relationship quality, loneliness, and reduced trust, communication, and productivity. Prior research suggests that individual, couple, and family dynamics, specifically low differentiation-of-self, may predispose individuals to phubbing as a strategy for regulating emotions. However, workplace phubbing remains largely understudied. This study examined whether workplace phubbing mediates the associations between differentiation-of-self and organizational trust with work engagement and productivity, across three countries differing in levels of digitalization and workplace relational norms: Israel, Spain, and Portugal. Data were collected from 797 employed adults in Israel (n = 377), Portugal (n = 192), and Spain (n = 228), and analyzed using structural equation modeling. Results showed that lower differentiation-of-self was associated with higher workplace phubbing across all three countries. However, outcomes were culture-dependent. In Israel, phubbing mediated the association between differentiation-of-self and productivity, whereas in Spain and Portugal phubbing was weakly or non-significantly related to work outcomes, which were more strongly associated with coworker support and organizational trust. Overall, the findings advance a cross-contextual perspective, demonstrating that family-dynamics extend into workplace interactions, highlighting the need for culturally-sensitive approaches to digital behaviour in organizational settings.
