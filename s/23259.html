Disentangling the Role of Internal Carbon Pricing in Corporate Tax Avoidance

ONE
Manon Desjardins (UCLA Anderson School of Management)
This paper examines how the adoption and design of internal carbon pricing (ICP) relates to corporate tax avoidance. Drawing on a panel of 539 large firms across 35 countries between 2017 and 2021, it provides the first empirical analysis of the fiscal implications of ICP. Two main ICP designs described by the ISSB are analyzed: shadow pricing and internal carbon fees. Shadow pricing, which assigns a hypothetical cost to emissions without involving financial transfers, is associated with higher effective tax rates. This relationship appears strongest among manufacturing and Japanese firms, in accordance with a risk management and compliance orientation. In contrast, internal carbon fees involve actual intra-firm charges based on emissions which might facilitate income shifting. Although the adoption of internal carbon fees correlates with lower effective tax rates, this link is not statistically significant. The results underscore the strategic heterogeneity of ICP adoption and its relationship with firms' fiscal strategies. They also highlight the need for more granular disclosure and regulatory oversight where environmental instruments intersect with tax-sensitive financial flows.

Supply Chain Decarbonization Strategies: Literature Review and Research Agenda

ONE
Giorgia Condomitti (Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna); Maria Rosa De Giacomo; Marco Frey (Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna)
Climate change mitigation has become a central challenge for supply chain management, prompting growing scholarly attention to supply chain decarbonization and net-zero transitions. Despite the expansion of the sustainable supply chain management (SSCM) literature, prior research has highlighted an uneven and fragmented use of organizational and management theories, limiting cumulative theory development. Addressing this gap, this paper conducts a theory-driven systematic literature review on climate change mitigation in supply chains. The study analyses a final sample of 47 theoretically grounded articles and adopts an interpretative approach to examine theory use, combinations, and their evolution over time. The findings reveal that research is dominated by single-theory studies relying on a limited set of established perspectives, while multi-theoretical approaches remain underdeveloped. Overall, 30 organizational and management theories are identified, showing substantial heterogeneity and a temporal shift from external governance-oriented perspectives to internal capability-based and, more recently, systemic and complexity-oriented approaches. Based on these insights, the paper develops an interpretative framework integrating external-, internal-, and systemic-oriented perspectives and outlines a future research agenda to support more integrative theorizing on supply chain decarbonization.

Carbon Credits and Finance: A Systematic Literature Review and Bibliometric Analysis

ONE
André Augusto Santos (FGV EAESP)
This study provides the first systematic review specifically focused on carbon credits within finance, analyzing 342 Scopus-indexed articles (2005-2025) using PRISMA 2020 guidelines and bibliometric methods. The field has grown exponentially-68.7% of publications appeared after 2020-yet we identify a critical methodological paradox: despite carbon markets providing ideal quasi-experimental settings through staggered policy rollouts, eligibility thresholds, and allocation mechanism transitions, only 5.0% of studies employ causal inference designs. Time series methods dominate instead, with spillover analysis (15.2%), connectedness frameworks (9.4%), and VAR models (6.4%) imported from mainstream financial econometrics. Keyword co-occurrence analysis reveals four thematic clusters, with research evolving from Policy Foundations toward Financial Market Integration, where sophisticated quantitative methods now concentrate. Geographic concentration compounds the methodological gap: China (38.6%) and Europe (42%) account for over 80% of publications, limiting external validity for voluntary markets and emerging economies. These results show the value of rigorous causal identification. We outline a research agenda that prioritizes regression discontinuity designs using coverage thresholds, difference-in-differences methods suited to staggered rollouts, and cross-regime comparisons of carbon market rules.

Operationally Rich, Theoretically Thin? A Review of SME Decarbonisation Research

ONE
Guillaume LAURIE (De Vinci Higher Education, De Vinci Research Center)
Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) play a critical role in the transition toward a low-carbon economy, yet their contribution remains conceptually fragmented in the academic literature. This article presents a critical bibliometric review of 73 peer-reviewed articles published in ABS-ranked journals between 2006 and 2024. Using VOSviewer and CiteSpace, we map the intellectual structure of SME decarbonisation research and examine how theory is mobilised. While empirical studies are growing in volume, explanatory depth remains limited. Theoretical perspectives are often referenced but rarely used as integrative explanatory devices, and explanations seldom connect firm-level dynamics with organisational or institutional contexts. We argue that this theoretical thinness reflects the epistemic dominance of measurement: because emissions and energy use are physically quantifiable, empirical measurement is frequently treated as sufficient explanation. As a result, decarbonisation is framed as a technical issue, partially decoupled from CSR, governance, and strategy.
