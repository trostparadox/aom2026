Publish or Perish, at What Cost? Work–Family Balance Dynamics Among Brazilian Faculty

Authors: Ivan Hadlich FURB - Fundação Universidade Regional de Blumenau; Christian Daniel Falaster FURB - Fundação Universidade Regional de Blumenau; Giancarlo Gomes FURB - Fundação Universidade Regional de Blumenau; Igor Tairan Mazzini FURB - Fundação Universidade Regional de Blumenau

Session overview

This study examines how publication pressure shapes both conflict and enrichment at the work–family interface among Brazilian graduate-level faculty, drawing on Work–Family Border Theory. We surveyed with faculty members from stricto sensu graduate programs in Business Administration in Brazil and analysed the data using validated multidimensional scales and PLS-SEM. The model specifies bidirectional paths for conflict (Work-to-Family; Family-to-Work) and for positive interfaces between work and family. Publication pressure was associated with higher levels of work–family conflict in both directions. In turn, this conflict reduced positive interfaces between domains, with a more pronounced suppression of enrichment from family to work than from work to family. Publication pressure also showed a direct negative association with the positive family-to-work interface. In contrast, its direct effect on the positive work-to-family interface was limited and operated primarily through conflict. The findings suggest that institutions should calibrate performance metrics, normalise sustainable publication pacing, and adopt family-supportive policies (e.g., more transparent, more realistic goal-setting, family-inclusive tenure clocks, and mentoring to reduce self-imposed stress). Expanding structural resources, such as on-campus childcare and protected within-hours writing time, may help mitigate publication-induced strain and preserve positive cross-domain spillovers. Based on evidence from a Brazilian academic context, the study jointly considers conflict and enrichment within Work–Family Border Theory and identifies publication pressure as a border-hardening mechanism that heightens strain and curtails cross-domain resource transfer. This integrated view advances understanding of how institutional evaluation regimes shape the everyday work–family experiences of graduate-level faculty.
