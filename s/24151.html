The Traceability Paradox: GenAI, Synthetic Documentation, and Accountability Drift

CTO
Hantong Zhao (Harbin University of Commerce)
How does accountability change when generative AI writes the documents that regulated organizations treat as evidence? We study GenAI-assisted documentation—meeting summaries, decision rationales, and compliance narratives—in three accountability-intensive organizations (financial services, healthcare, and public administration). Drawing on 62 interviews, 103 hours of observation, and an archive of 180+ decision artifacts (including paired transcripts and summaries), we develop a process theory of synthetic trace regimes: sociomaterial configurations in which machine-generated records become primary traces of organizational action. We uncover a traceability paradox. GenAI expands formal traceability by increasing the volume, standardization, and audit readiness of records, yet it erodes interpretive traceability—the ability to reconstruct how and why decisions were made—by loosening the coupling between deliberation and its documentary inscription. This paradox unfolds through six mechanisms: delegated articulation, plausibility inflation, evidentiary drift, responsibility inversion, institutional anchoring, and epistemic debt. Together they produce accountability drift: organizations become increasingly able to “produce the file,” while becoming less able to explain decisions under contestation. We contribute to CTO research by theorizing synthetic traces, extending algorithmic organizing from allocation to narrative infrastructure, and specifying sociomaterial conditions under which GenAI strengthens auditability while undermining substantive accountability.
