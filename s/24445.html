Inventing for Nature: Global Innovation Drivers and Innovation Cycles in Biodiversity Conservation

ONE
Magnus Buggenhagen; Carsten Christian Guderian (LexisNexis Intellectual Property Solutions / PatentSight GmbH)
The accelerating loss of biodiversity poses significant risks to global economic stability and social well-being. Addressing this multifaceted challenge requires the development and diffusion of innovative technologies by diverse stakeholders, including companies, research institutions, government authorities, and inventors. However, a comprehensive overview of technological trajectories in the field of biodiversity conservation remains absent. In this study, we explore the nexus between biodiversity and patent data as measures of successful research and development outcomes to identify emerging technology trends and innovation patterns. We uncover who develops biodiversity-focused technologies, where these originate, and in which regions they are protected. Our findings reveal that, compared to other sustainability-related technologies, patenting in biodiversity conservation remains relatively limited. Early innovation cycles are predominantly driven by corporate actors rather than academic or public institutions, with initial technological developments emerging in Japan and subsequently diffusing across North America, Europe, and the broader Asia-Pacific region, including China and South Korea. These results provide valuable insights for managers, policymakers, and scholars seeking to stimulate and sustain innovation for biodiversity protection. They underscore the need for targeted incentives and policy frameworks that foster collaboration across regions and institutional boundaries, ultimately strengthening the global capacity to address biodiversity loss through technological innovation.
