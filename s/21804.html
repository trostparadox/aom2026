How Employees Reconnect With Work: Reattachment Trajectories and Early-Week Affect and Engagement

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM

Authors: Heewon Jang George Mason University; Seth A. Kaplan George Mason University; Lauren Kuykendall George Mason University; Philseok Lee George Mason University; Xueyi Sherry Liao George Mason University

Session overview

This study examines how employees psychologically reconnect with work across the weekend-to-workweek transition by focusing on reattachment trajectories and their associations with early-week work engagement and affect. Using an experience sampling design, full-time employees (N = 187) completed repeated surveys from Friday evening through Monday afternoon over four weeks. Latent growth modeling (LGM) examined overall changes in reattachment across time, and growth mixture modeling (GMM) identified 3-class of reattachment trajectories. BCH-equivalent tests were conducted to compare distal outcomes across trajectory classes. LGM results showed a systematic increase in reattachment from Saturday to Monday morning, along with significant individual differences in initial levels and rates of change. GMM supported a three-class solution, identifying low–gradual, moderate–rapid, and high–stable reattachment trajectories. BCH-equivalent tests found significant class differences in work engagement and positive affect on Monday afternoon, with higher and more rapidly increasing trajectories reporting higher work engagement and positive affect. Negative affect did not differ across classes. By modeling reattachment as a dynamic and heterogeneous process, this study extends prior research that has relied on static, morning-based assessments and highlights the importance of reattachment trajectories during nonwork periods. Keywords: Psychological Reattachment, Weekend-to-workweek transition, Reattachment Trajectories, Affect, Work Engagement
