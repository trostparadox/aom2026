Relational Boundary Work in Entrepreneur-Employee Dyads: Navigating the Closeness-Distance-Paradox

ENT
Nadine Albrecht (Technical University of Munich); Sonja Kristin Franzke (University of Bayreuth); Matthias Baum (University of Bayreuth); Viktoria Kilian
Entrepreneurs and start-up employees face a paradox: how to cultivate close, family-like relationships while maintaining the professional distance necessary in workplace settings? Although boundaries are crucial to separate personal and professional domains, we know little about how entrepreneurs and employees navigate relational boundaries in the context of a new venture. Taking on a relational boundary perspective, our qualitative study analyzes dyadic exchanges between entrepreneurs and employees through 30 interviews (15 matched pairs). Our findings contribute to theory in three ways. First, we reveal how closeness and distance are simultaneously enacted through diverging and converging forms of boundary work, as entrepreneurs and employees flexibly and contextually adjust boundaries in response to evolving relational needs. Second, we advance the discourse on the antecedents of boundary work by theorizing entrepreneurs’ visible (frontstage) and invisibly negotiated considerations (backstage). Third, we offer initial insights into how venture-level boundaries are co-constructed through ongoing dyadic interactions, rather than being unilaterally imposed by entrepreneurs.
