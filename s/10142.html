Session overview

Machine learning (ML) is expanding the empirical and theoretical possibilities available to strategy and organization scholars, yet its adoption raises important questions about how researchers can responsibly generate data, interpret meaning, identify causal relationships, and build theory. This symposium brings together four complementary papers that illustrate a multi-method perspective on ML’s role in advancing management research. The first paper addresses a core barrier to ML adoption, namely data scarcity, by introducing a synthetic data framework that enables model training without depleting valuable focal datasets. The second shows how Large Language Models (LLMs) can support qualitative interpretation while preserving researcher voice. The third demonstrates how combining structural econometrics with ML tools can reveal individual, firm, and match-specific effects that conventional estimators struggle to isolate. The fourth uses LLM agents to replicate and extend behavioral strategy experiments, offering a novel computational pathway for theorizing decision processes. Collectively, the symposium highlights how diverse ML approaches can enrich the research cycle and expand the range of phenomena scholars are equipped to study.
