Trainability as Sovereignty: How Decentralised AI Reallocates Control in Innovation Ecosystems

Author: Fabio Gualandri Silesian University of Technology

Session overview

Decentralised, open-weight AI is transforming how algorithmic power is distributed, shifting control from hyperscale platforms toward edge devices, open-source toolchains, and community-driven ecosystems. As language models become accessible for offline use and easier to customise through local fine-tuning, traditional pillars of digital sovereignty, territorial jurisdiction, infrastructural control, and centralised data governance, are increasingly challenged. This poster examines how sovereignty shifts when key control points for deploying and modifying models are dispersed across innovation ecosystems. It identifies three linked dynamics: (1) jurisdictional collapse, where offline deployment and repeated model forking reduce visibility, traceability, and enforceability; (2) fragmentation of accountability and safety, as harms scale more easily and responsibility diffuses among base-model developers, fine-tuners, deployers, and community maintainers; and (3) the emergence of alternative algorithmic sovereignties, enabling communities to develop culturally specific models outside state- or platform-controlled frameworks and licensing regimes. We contribute to technology and innovation management by defining trainability: the capability to develop, adapt, evaluate, and oversee models locally, as a sovereign capability, clarifying strategic roles in decentralised ecosystems and pointing to governance approaches that improve safety and accountability without reverting to centralised control.
