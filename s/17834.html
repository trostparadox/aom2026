Session overview

This study examines how social enterprises emerge when social entrepreneurs are closely connected to the social problems they seek to address. Drawing on in-depth qualitative interviews with nineteen social entrepreneurs in India, the study traces how exposure to social problems, motivational orientations, and prosocial behaviour interact over time. The findings suggest that social enterprise creation does not begin with an entrepreneurial intention but develops as individuals seek to continue addressing problems they encounter in their communities. By offering a process of social enterprise emergence, the study contributes to a more nuanced understanding of how motivation and action are organized into social entrepreneurship.
