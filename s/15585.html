Session overview

Management Consulting is a practice-oriented division that brings scholarship and practical knowledge together to enhance organizational performance. Textbooks and formal sources of knowledge used in teaching describe the forms of consulting available and how they are used but often do not reveal the actual processes of “doing consulting”. This “doing” involves the “who says or does what to who, when, where and why” aspects of consulting practice that formal education often fails to describe. This symposium is intended to explore the under-addressed aspect of management knowledge that comes from experience. The general objectives are to describe and elicit the experiences of veteran management consultants by using storytelling, provide useful advice about management consulting, explore how scholarship and practice work together, inspire interest in management consulting, and support AOM’s practice mission. These objectives will inspire reflection on the processes and practices of consulting, offer interesting insights based on consulting experience, and highlight the role of scholarship in consulting. To do so, the PDW will assemble several (5) scholar/consultants and invite each one to tell a story or two (10 minutes per presenter) about significant and interesting experiences and knowledge they have gleaned through their scholarly and consulting work. The discussion time will allow participants to share their questions and stories with the panelists.
