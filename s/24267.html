How Handoff Givers Facilitate Handoff Takers’ Work Role Entry and Task Performance

OB
Depeng Liu (Shandong University); Bingjie Yu (Shandong University); Jiangjin Li (Nanjing Normal University)
Employees make transitions between home and work in everyday life, and these role transitions are impacted by others, yet surprisingly little research has examined how others influence employees’ role transitions. Drawing on boundary transition theory and organizational routine research, we explore how work domain partners actively facilitate employees’ home-to-work transitions, subsequent work engagement, and ultimately task performance. We study work domain partners’ agentic involvement during the handoff routine, a setting in which their role is especially consequential. We hypothesize that, in a work shift episode, the handoff givers’ discretionary information transfer behaviors enhance handoff takers’ perceived information adequacy, which in turn increases the level of engagement and ultimately improves task performance. We also hypothesize that the handoff givers’ other-directed positive affect display elicits handoff takers’ felt gratitude, which accelerates the speed of engagement and ultimately leads to high task performance. Finally, we hypothesize that the above effects were stronger for handoff takers high in relational identity, who are more attuned to interpersonal support during transitions. We test our hypotheses using an event-contingent diary study of 574 shift-level data points from 134 nurses. The results largely supported our hypotheses, except for the moderating effect of relational identity on the impact of the handoff givers’ discretionary information transfer behaviors. We discuss the implications of our findings.
