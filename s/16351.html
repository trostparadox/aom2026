Reimagining Peer Review in Management Studies: A Deweyan Perspective on Aesthetic Engagement

Author: Stephanie Heitmann Friedrich Schiller University Jena

Session overview

The peer review process is a cornerstone of academic publishing in Management and Organization Studies (MOS), regarded as essential for ensuring scholarly quality and advancing knowledge. At the same time, peer review has been subject to sustained critique. While existing debates have largely focused on procedural issues such as efficiency, fairness, and evaluation criteria, the aesthetic, sensory, and experiential dimensions of peer review have received little attention. This poster addresses this gap by conceptualizing peer review as a form of art criticism, drawing on John Dewey’s pragmatist aesthetics and his notion of art as experience. Building on Dewey’s argument that art is a dynamic, relational process between artwork and perceiver, the poster reframes peer review as an experiential, embodied, and collaborative practice. Dewey’s understanding of criticism emphasizes perceptual engagement and generative interpretation rather than detached judgment, offering a productive analogy for reimagining the role of reviewers in MOS. From this perspective, peer review becomes a process in which reviewers engage intellectually, emotionally, and sensorially with manuscripts, contributing to their development rather than merely evaluating their worth. The poster develops a framework for a Deweyan approach to peer review centered on two core dimensions. First, aesthetic engagement foregrounds sensory and affective experiences in reviewing and encourages attentiveness to how manuscripts resonate with reviewers. Second, collaborative criticism positions reviewers as co-creators who provide constructive, generative feedback that supports the evolution of scholarly work. Overall, it invites scholars to reconsider peer review as sensory, embodied practice.
