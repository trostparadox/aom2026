Configuring Governance and Affiliation Levers to Mitigate the Control Dilemma at IPO

ENT
Sayan Sarkar (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Jingxuan Lu (The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology)
To prevent loss of control after an investment round, founder CEOs can implement control-enhancing mechanisms to delink control rights and stock ownership. Prior research argues that investors assess CEMs negatively owing to concerns of opportunism, placing founder-CEOs in a rich vs. king dilemma. They can either retain control or attract investment; the foregone investment only recoverable in the long term. We depart from past work by adopting a configurational perspective on control dilemma, to contend that investors need not universally assess CEMs negatively as they foster stewardship. This enables us to articulate a typology of mitigation strategy centered on governance and affiliations that can attenuate or reverse the CEM-penalty. fsQCA analysis of 979 recent US IPOs suggests that certain strategies in conjunction with CEMs are consistently linked with avoiding wealth loss at IPO itself. We add to the understanding of resource-assembly and governance implications of growing organizations

Governance, Signaling and Survival in the B2B Digital Platform Startups

ENT
Tali Hadasa Blank (Tel Hai Academic College); Gabriel David Pinto
B2B digital platforms operate in technologically complex, investment-intensive ecosystems where survival depends on both external finance and internal governance. While business angels are often portrayed as uniformly beneficial, we know less about how angel investment interacts with governance and leadership in B2B platform startups. Drawing on Upper Echelons and signaling perspectives, we examine how early angel investment and startups’ governance jointly shape the survival of B2B digital platforms. Using a hand-collected dataset of 164 Israeli B2B platform ventures from the IVC database, we model firm age (years) as a proxy for platform survival and estimate generalized linear models with board size, Founder-CEO status, and angel investment as focal predictors. The results show that both larger boards and Founder-CEO leadership are positively associated with platform longevity, whereas angel investment on its own is not significantly related to survival. We show that larger boards enhance survival mainly in platforms without angel investors, suggesting that boards and angels serve partly substitutable governance roles. A negative interaction between Founder-CEO status and angel backing suggests potential tension between founder control and external ownership. Overall, the study advances research on entrepreneurial finance and digital platforms by showing that external capital and internal governance jointly shape venture survival in complex ecosystems.

One, No One, and One Hundred Thousand: Founder and Venture Identity Work on Social Media Platforms

ENT
Elisa Montori (Venice School of Management - Ca' Foscari University)
Despite visibility being crucial for social media-based entrepreneurs, they have remained nearly invisible to entrepreneurship research. Particularly, insights are scarce on what digital platforms entail for the identity work of individuals and ventures operating on them, offering no guidance for one of the crucial processes at the very root of entrepreneurship. Drawing on an online ethography of an Italian content creator operating on an algorithmically shaped social media platform, this study examines how founder and venture identities are continuously and simultaneously worked on thanks to and constrained by the affordances of the platform. To work on their multiple identities, while moving within an algorithmic space, social media-based entrepreneurs unblur their work for and with the platform, and interact with their community. Findings underscore how, at an individual level, the entrepreneur must balance being a professional and a user of the platform. At the venture level, the platform-dependent entrepreneur must create and share original content while undertaking value-generating marketing activities. With no choice but to remain entangled with their venture, the two identity processes are inextricably intertwined, and the identities are refracted, rather than reflected, to and from the community by the algorithms shaping the platform.

Understanding Platform-Dependent Entrepreneurship: Towards a New Paradigm of Platform Governance

ENT
Apan ZHOU (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Kee-hung Lai (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University)
The digital economy has enabled unprecedented entrepreneurial activities on online platforms, yet understanding of intrinsic entrepreneurial dynamism within these platforms and platform governance remains limited. This paper advocates for a paradigm shift in platform governance by conceptualizing platforms as entrepreneurial ecosystems for platform-dependent entrepreneurship (PDE). We contribute through three key elements: (1) extending the PDE concept by articulating its distinctive attributes of accessibility, dependency, informality, and thick-tailed performance distribution, (2) developing a novel typology that categorizes PDE activities based on their distinct focus on entrepreneurial opportunities and fundamental value propositions into sales-based, influence-based, support-based, and service-based PDE, revealing the nuanced manifestation of PDE attributes across these entrepreneurial forms, and (3) examining platform governance through the lens of PDE ecosystem by introducing the value-attribute governance framework and the platform governance frontier concept, which serve both as analytical tools for evaluating governance mechanisms and as strategic guides for balancing value creation and appropriation across different PDE types and attributes. This new paradigm emphasizes nurturing entrepreneurial success within platforms, fostering mutual benefits for platform owners and entrepreneurs through effective ecosystem governance.

Variance Decomposition Analysis of Performance in Digital Platform Entrepreneurship

ENT
Kaushik Gala (University of Kansas); Andreas Schwab (Iowa State University)
Digital platforms are often hierarchically structured, with products organized and listed within submarkets. Platform owner-operators, sometimes supported by platform-dependent entrepreneurs, create these levels to respond to customer needs and facilitate search and discovery. The resulting multi-level structures on digital platforms are analogous to firms operating in sectors, industries, and submarkets. While management research has often examined the extent to which the variance in firm performance can be attributed to characteristics at such levels, there is a need for similar studies of level-wise variance in entrepreneurial performance on digital platforms. Understanding the relative impact of levels on performance can inform entrepreneurial strategy and, consequently, improve performance on digital platforms. In response, this study conducts a variance decomposition analysis to evaluate the relative importance of each hierarchical level for a global digital e-learning platform. It employs (a) standard multi-level modeling, (b) Bayesian hierarchical modeling, and (c) unconditional quantile regression to estimate the relative impact of each level on product performance. In doing so, this investigation guides future empirical studies that examine how multiple factors or levels may impact entrepreneurial performance on digital platforms.
