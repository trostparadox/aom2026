From Greenwashing to Deep Green: Advancing Responsible Management Education

Moderator: Fernando Eduardo Kerschbaumer

Session overview

The sustainability imperative has arrived at business schools' doorstep, but how deeply have they responded? This session examines how institutions configure responsible management learning through PRME adoption, why ESG inertia persists in Global South universities despite the global urgency, how game-based learning and circular-economy simulations can move sustainability education from rhetoric to practice, and what it would take to train truly future-oriented, responsibility-driven leaders.

The Making of Sustainability Leaders Through Accelerated Heuristics Development

MED
Emma Stendahl (Stockholm University); Dorota Piaskowska (University College Dublin); Esther Tippmann (University of Galway)
As sustainability challenges escalate, higher education institutions in general and business schools in particular must equip graduates to become sustainability leaders. Departing from previous findings that detail heuristics development for effective leadership as occurring post-graduation during employment, we explore whether and how heuristics development can enable the making of sustainability leaders during business education through program design. Drawing on theory on heuristics development and data collected from the participants of one graduate program in business sustainability over a period of two years, including interview, diary and survey data, we found that a repeated interplay of formal and informal learning experiences enabled the progression from rough to smart heuristics due to confidence building and improved situational judgement. The theoretical model derived from this study details the accelerated making of sustainability leaders during business education. The findings contribute to the management learning and education literature by advancing insights on heuristics learning and interplay of formal and informal learning for leadership development. They also offer new insights into how business schools can deliver impact initiatives for addressing grand challenges through MLE.

Unpacking ESG Inertia in Management Education: An Institutional Analysis of Brazilian HEIs

MED
Fernando Eduardo Kerschbaumer; Gabriela Fracasso Moraes (Positivo University); Danielle Denes Dos Santos (Pontifical Catholic University of Parana); Brauer Brayan Koren (Universidade Positivo)
Despite the global urgency for Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) integration, Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Global South are trapped in a state of institutional inertia. This paper investigates the root causes of this stagnation through an institutional analysis of Brazilian HEIs. Drawing on a robust qualitative design, the study features in-depth interviews with 42 senior leaders - including rectors and academic directors with over 20 years of management experience -triangulated with United Nations documents and multi-country evidence from the Global South. Applying the dual lenses of Institutional Isomorphism and Transformative Innovation Policy (TIP), the findings reveal that while institutions often adopt "symbolic compliance" to maintain global legitimacy, a profound "preparedness gap" among faculty members prevents deep pedagogical and administrative transformation. The study identifies financial, infrastructural, and cultural barriers as mediators that can be navigated through strategic leadership and reflexive mentorship. We argue that breaking ESG inertia requires a radical reconfiguration of academic routines, transitioning universities from passive degree providers into dynamic hubs for generating specialist corporate sustainability knowledge. This research offers a scalable framework for HEI managers and policymakers to align institutional missions with global sustainability standards, providing a roadmap for societal transformation in underserved regions.

Advancing Quality Education for Sustainable Development: Mapping Global Scholarship on SDG-4

MED
Anshul Verma (S. P. Jain Institute of Management and Research, INDIA); Amol Dhaigude; Soma Amol Dhaigude (National Institute of Technology Karnataka)
Advancing quality education is central to achieving sustainable development, yet the rapidly expanding body of research on Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG-4) remains fragmented across disciplines, regions, and policy domains. This study systematically maps the global scholarly landscape on SDG-4 to clarify its intellectual structure, thematic evolution, and emerging research frontiers. Drawing on a large corpus of peer-reviewed studies indexed in Scopus and Web of Science, we examine publication trends, influential contributors, institutional and country-level collaborations, and dominant knowledge clusters shaping the field. Our findings highlight the interdisciplinary nature of SDG-4 scholarship and its strong linkages with policy, social equity, and capacity building, while also revealing persistent gaps in research from emerging and underrepresented regions. By synthesizing global patterns in SDG-4 research, this study contributes to ongoing debates on education for sustainable development and offers direction for future research, policy design, and managerial practice aimed at strengthening inclusive and lifelong learning systems worldwide.

Educating Responsible Leaders: Integrating Sustainability into Managerial Training for Future-Orient

MED
Alverth Miguel Caicedo Riera (Business Education)
Despite the growing emphasis on sustainability and responsible leadership, managerial training programs remain largely anchored in short-term performance logics. This misalignment creates a gap between contemporary organizational challenges and the leadership capabilities developed through traditional management education. The growing complexity of global sustainability challenges has intensified the need for leadership that is not only effective in managerial terms but also ethically grounded, socially responsible, and future oriented. Traditional managerial training models, which have historically emphasized efficiency, control, and short-term performance, are increasingly insufficient for preparing leaders to navigate sustainability-driven organizational environments. In parallel, education for sustainable development has emerged as a critical educational paradigm aimed at fostering long-term thinking, values-based decision-making, and systemic awareness. However, the integration of sustainability-oriented education into managerial training and leadership development remains fragmented and under-theorized. This study develops a conceptual framework that integrates education for sustainable development with managerial training to support the formation of responsible and future-oriented leadership. Drawing on an integrative review of interdisciplinary literature spanning management education, leadership theory, and sustainability studies, the article synthesizes existing knowledge to clarify how sustainability-focused educational practices can shape managerial competencies, leadership values, and strategic orientations. Rather than presenting empirical findings, the study offers a theoretically grounded framework that explains the mechanisms through which sustainability education informs managerial training processes and leadership outcomes. The paper contributes to the literature by bridging the gap between education for sustainable development and leadership development research, offering a structured conceptual model and theory-driven propositions. The framework provides a foundation for future empirical research and offers insights for educators, training institutions, and organizations seeking to cultivate leadership capable of balancing economic performance with social and environmental responsibility.
