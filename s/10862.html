Session overview

The commercialization of space exploration represents a significant technological and entrepreneurial frontier of the 21st century. As private enterprises increasingly complement and compete with governmental space agencies, questions arise about the role of public policy in shaping this industry. This symposium brings together scholars from entrepreneurship, strategy, public policy, and technology management to examine how policy frameworks influence entrepreneurial opportunities, competitive dynamics, and innovation in the space sector. The session addresses tensions between established international space treaties and emerging commercial realities, explores how different national regulatory approaches create varying entrepreneurial ecosystems, and investigates the implications of public-private partnerships in space ventures. Panelists will analyze policy initiatives from the United States, Europe, and China, examining how these distinct regulatory approaches shape entrepreneurial opportunities and competitive dynamics across regions. They will also assess how policy uncertainty affects venture capital investment, technology development pathways, and firm entry strategies in each major space economy. By synthesizing perspectives across multiple management disciplines, this symposium aims to enhance understanding of policy entrepreneurship in contexts of uncertainty while providing actionable insights for practitioners navigating this regulatory landscape.
