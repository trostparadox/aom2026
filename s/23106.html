Signals in the Feed: Social Media Misconduct as a Predictor of Service Employee Job Outcomes

HR
Stacy L Boyer (West Chester University of Pennsylvania); Yoonhee Kim (West Chester University of Pennsylvania)
Employees are increasingly using social media platforms to express frustrations, intolerance, and other characteristics that can erode public trust in service organizations and occupations alike. In response, many organizations now use social media to screen applicants and monitor employees, but empirical support for these practices is lacking. In this paper, we draw on signaling theory to conceptualize social media misconduct as an indicator of misfit with a user’s occupation or organization and examine its relationship with job outcomes. Study 1 analyzed archival data from a metropolitan police department to investigate incivility among officers who engaged in social media misconduct. Study 2 analyzed two-wave survey data from participants working in various service occupations to investigate the effects of social media misconduct on employee incivility, burnout, and turnover intentions, and to test the moderating effects of perceived organizational support as an attenuating resource. Results indicate that social media misconduct positively predicts burnout and turnover intentions, with mixed results for employee incivility and perceived organizational support. We conclude with practical implications for addressing this phenomenon and identify directions for future research.

The Differential Coping Strategies for Service Employees with Vocational Interest Incongruence

HR
lixin Chen (University of Science and Technology of China); Lu Lucy Xing (The University of Auckland); Li-Xin Jiang (The University of Auckland)
Guided by the transactional theory of stress and coping theory, we developed a process model examining the sequential mediating roles of psychological distress (i.e., negative moods) and two types of coping strategies (i.e., job crafting towards interests: JC-interests, and surface acting) in the relationship between interest incongruence and employees’ well-being (i.e., energy depletion) and performance evaluation (i.e., supervisor-rated service performance). The results of a multi-wave, multi-source survey study conducted in service sectors suggested that interest incongruence positively related to negative moods, which, in turn, led to JC-interests and surface acting. Furthermore, while JC-interests positively related to performance evaluation and negatively related to energy depletion, surface acting positively related to both performance evaluation and energy depletion. We also demonstrate the relationships between interest incongruence and performance evaluation and energy depletion were each sequentially mediated first by negative moods and then by JC-interests or surface acting. Finally, we found that work centrality exacerbated the positive relationship between interest incongruence and negative moods. Our findings provide new insight into how employees experience and react to interest incongruence.

Unpacking HRM Implementation Gaps, Service Climate, and Customer Complaint Behavior

HR
Zixiang Ma (Shandong University); Qiquan Zhu; Yucheng (Eason) Zhang (University of Southampton); Junwei Zhang; Muhammad Naseer Akhtar (Buckinghamshire New University); Lirong Long (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Although prior research acknowledges that managers and employees often hold divergent views of High-Performance Work Systems (HPWS), limited attention has been paid to the consequences of this misalignment. We argue that performance-relevant HRM outcomes depend not only on the level of HPWS alone but also on the congruence (incongruence) between managerial implementation and employees’ experiences of HPWS. This study extends prior research by examining the effects of the HPWS gap between managers and employees. Drawing on social exchange theory, we develop a unit level moderated mediation model proposing that the HPWS gap undermines the service climate, which in turn increases customer complaint behavior. We further posit that this indirect relationship is stronger when reciprocity climate is high rather than low. We tested our hypotheses using time-lagged data collected from 4186 employees and 439-line managers across 83 units in a large electricity service enterprise in South China. The results supported the proposed model and shed light on the mechanisms through which the HPWS gap affects organizational effectiveness in the services sector. We conclude by discussing theoretical and practical implications, acknowledging limitations, and outlining directions for future research.

Reality on the Ground: Frontline Managers’ Exigency-Induced HR Enactment, and Self-Romanticization

HR
Hossein Heidarian Ghaleh (University of East London)
Transferring HR responsibilities to frontline managers (FLMs) received widespread attention, yet the focus of much debate. This debate continues partly because prior research, perhaps inadvertently, failed to consider the simultaneous and bilateral formal and normative pressures on FLMs, which enabled past research to secure their desired outcomes. In this study, we first set the stage by examining the two-way interactive effect of superiors’ formal pressures for target achievement and subordinates’ normative pressure for HR involvement on FLMs which predict FLMs’ ambivalent HR involvement. We further propose that FLMs’ ambivalent HR involvement triggers self-romanticization via the mediating role of exigency-induced HR enactment. Further, we also test for the moderating effect of peer FLMs’ calling, to unveil how FLMs’ HR involvement and self-romanticization behavior might be contingent on peer FLMs. We provide empirical support for our theoretical model across a large-scale, multisource and time-lagged study. Findings yielded by this endeavor, complicates our understanding of how FLMs manage to safeguard their credibility via self-romanticization, despite being minimally involved in HR responsibilities. Our study is a departure from prior research which has weighed heavily toward favorable aspects of FLMs’ HR involvement. We open new frontiers in this research territory along with implications for practitioners.
