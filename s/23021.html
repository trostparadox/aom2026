How MNEs’ Environmental Misconduct in Pollution Havens Affects Sales Performance

IM
Mingjun CAI (Indiana University); Juan Bu (Indiana University Bloomington); Jing Gu (Xiamen University)
This study explores how MNEs’ environmental misconduct in pollution havens affects their sales performance. Drawing on the attribution theory and corporate social irresponsibility (CSI) literature, we argue that pollution haven serves as an attributional cue that elevates perceived corporate culpability of environmental misconduct because it enhances inferences of internal causality and moral responsibility, leading to stronger penalties on sales performance for environmental misconduct in pollution havens than in non-pollution havens. We further propose two boundary conditions that influence these mechanisms. Specifically, industry-level environmental misconduct in pollution havens weakens internal-causality inferences and attenuates the sales penalty, whereas MNEs’ environmental capabilities heighten moral-responsibility attributions and amplify this penalty. Analyzing a panel of 1,290 U.S. publicly listed MNEs from 2007 to 2021, we find evidence for these arguments. Our findings contribute to pollution-haven research by extending from reduced compliance costs to heightened violation costs in pollution havens, and advance attribution-based accounts of CSI by identifying institutional context as a novel driver of customer attributions.

Unpacking the Curvilinear Relationship Between ESG Strategy and EMNE‘s OFDI

IM
SHAN ZHU (China University of Petroleum); Jiuyu Dong (Dalian University of Technology)
This study investigates how environmental, social, and governance (ESG) strategies influence emerging-market multinational enterprises’ (EMNEs) outward foreign direct investment (OFDI). Drawing on a resource-based perspective, we argue that ESG engagement exerts dual effects on EMNEs’ OFDI through two mechanisms: cross-border spillover of reputation, which enhances EMNEs’ positive image in foreign markets and facilitates international investment; and resource crowding-out, which imposes financial and managerial constraints on their OFDI. Based on this tension, we propose an inverted U-shaped relationship between ESG strategy and EMNEs’ OFDI. We further examine the moderating role of regional pro-market reforms, which shapes the visibility and perceived credibility of EMNEs’ ESG efforts, as well as the resource reallocation costs for international investment. Using data from Chinese listed MNEs, our hypotheses are generally supported. This study contributes to the ESG and OFDI literature by revealing the strategic trade-offs of ESG strategies and emphasizing the role of subnational environments in shaping EMNEs’ OFDI behavior.

Green Governance and Internationalization Under Uncertainty

IM
Sultan Sikandar Mirza (The University of Waikato); Adnan Khan; ChengMing Huang (Monash University)
This study explores how directors’ environmental backgrounds influence the international expansion of SOEs, focusing on green innovation (GI) and perceived economic policy uncertainty (FEPU). Using panel data from China’s A-share firms (2010-2023) and guided by signalling, stakeholder, and upper-echelons theories, it finds that environmental backgrounds significantly promote SOEs’ internationalization in a nonlinear inverted U pattern. Green innovation enhances this effect, while FEPU weakens it. Sensitivity tests during major global events, the 2015 Paris Agreement and COVID-19, show environmental expertise’s strategic value depends on context. After the Paris Agreement, environmental expertise boosts SOE internationalization but harms non-SOEs; post-COVID-19, the relationship reverses, turning negative for SOEs and positive for non-SOEs, with shifts in the roles of green innovation and policy uncertainty. Heterogeneity analyses reveal stronger effects in eastern regions, high-pollution/high-tech industries, strict governance, and gender-diverse boards. These findings offer policy, managerial, and investor insights on board composition, global expansion, and green competitiveness.

Responsible Investment: How ESG Shapes Outward FDI of Tourism Firms

IM
Tingyu Zhu (Fudan University); Seoki Lee (The Pennsylvania State University); Xuerong Peng (Zhejiang Gongshang University)
Abstract: This study examines how environmental, social, and governance (ESG) performance influences outward foreign direct investment (OFDI), and how multi-level openness and institutional constraints moderate this relationship in an emerging market. Drawing on institutional theory and upper echelons theory, we propose that ESG acts as a legitimacy signal facilitating OFDI, but its effectiveness is contingent on individual characteristics, political embeddedness, and ownership structures. Using an unbalanced panel dataset of 55 publicly listed tourism firms in China from 2009 to 2019, we find that ESG performance significantly promotes OFDI, supporting the legitimacy signaling mechanism. However, this relationship is contingent upon multiple contextual factors. Board chair political connections and foreign ownership significantly weaken the ESG-OFDI relationship, while, contrary to expectations, CEO foreign experience does not significantly moderate this relationship, possibly reflecting industry-specific governance structures and human capital characteristics in China's tourism sector. For tourism firms in emerging markets, the results underscore that leveraging ESG for global expansion requires navigating complex institutional dynamics across multiple levels. Keywords: ESG; outward foreign direct investment; OFDI; tourism; institutional logic; emerging markets.
