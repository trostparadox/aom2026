How and When Negative Career Shocks Affect Employees’ Family Life?

CAR
Shanghao Song (Capital University of Economics and Business); Lei Gao (Beijing Normal University); Jingwei Zhao (Beijing Normal University)
With the frequent occurrence of uncertain events in the global economic and social environment, negative career shocks have become an important research topic for organizational management scholars and practitioners. A review of existing research shows that most scholars have focused on the effects of negative career shocks on employees’ work and career-related outcomes, lacking an understanding of the results within the domain of family life. Based on social information processing theory and the spillover-crossover model, this paper analyzes three-wave data from 369 employee-spouse dyads and constructs a moderated mediation model to explore how negative career shocks affect spouses’ family satisfaction. The results indicate that negative career shocks negatively affect spouses’ family satisfaction through the serial mediating effects of career insecurity and family undermining. Additionally, employees’ protean career orientation can weaken the serial mediated relationship. Theoretical and practical implications are also discussed in this article.
