How Circular Are Latin American Large Firms? Strategy Profiles and Boundary Constraints

Session overview

The circular economy (CE) is increasingly framed as a strategic response to resource constraints and environmental degradation, yet comparative evidence on how firms implement CE strategies remains geographically uneven, particularly outside the Global North. This limits CE theory and practice because implementation likely depends on regulatory enforcement, infrastructure (including reverse logistics), value-chain position, and sectoral material intensity— conditions that vary markedly across Latin America. We provide the first large-scale, multi-country benchmark of CE strategy implementation among large firms across the five largest Latin American economies (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile) and three material-intensive sectors (Agriculture/forestry/fishing; Manufacturing; Construction) (N = 834). Using a survey of managers knowledgeable about their firm’s CE and sustainability strategy, we operationalize CE implementation as a portfolio of four strategies—Narrowing, Slowing, Closing, and Regenerating—and examine implementation across three organizational levels (Product/Service Design, Internal Operations, Value Chain Collaboration). We report descriptive country and sector profiles and cross-country difference tests. Results show high uptake but patterned heterogeneity. Slowing is most implemented and does not differ significantly across countries, consistent with convergence on operationally legible life-extension practices. In contrast, Narrowin), Closing, and Regenerating vary: Brazil/Mexico/Chile form a high-adoption cluster, Argentina is the lagging case, and Colombia is intermediate. Implementation is higher in Internal Operations than in Value Chain Collaboration, indicating an internalization bias and a collaboration gap. Overall, the study supports a contextualized institutionalisation view of CE strategies and highlights organizational boundary constraints on networked circularity, motivating follow-up explanatory analyses of institutional and capability contingencies.
