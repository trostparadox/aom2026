The Effects of European Circular Economy Regional Specialization Strategies

TIM
Raffaele Mancuso (University of Bologna); Riccardo Fini (University of Bologna); Laura Toschi (University of Bologna)
Regions of the European Union are implementing Circular Economy (CE) policies, not only to foster environmental sustainability but also to spur economic growth and create new jobs. We utilize metadata on the Smart Specialization Strategies (S3) for 245 regions for the 2014-2020 round and for 190 regions for the 2021-2027 periods, and classify which policies specialize in the Circular Economy (CE). Merging EU-NED data on the vote share of political parties in the European Parliament elections with CHES data on the political ideology of those parties, we find that political parties with stronger EU Cohesion ideology, stronger environmentalism ideology, and leftist parties are more likely to implement an S3 strategy focused on CE. We define a region as treated if its 2021 policy focuses on CE, but its 2014 policy does not, and as control if the region never specializes in CE. We match treated regions with twins in the same country governed by parties with similar political ideology and with similar macroeconomic variables. We then employ a diff-in-diff design. Exploiting data on the 40 million firms founded in Europe between 2014 and 2024, we find that the implementation of a CE specialization strategy actually decreases the number of firms founded, including those operating in CE industrial sectors. Exploiting DealRoom data on 100 thousand innovative startups matched on Orbis, we find that CE specialization does not foster the creation of innovative startups and may actually decrease the number of innovative startups employing a CE business model. We also find no statistically significant effect of CE specialization on CE patent creation, as reported in PATSTAT. Exploiting CIRCTER data on regional circularity indicators, we find that implementing a CE specialization strategy yields better circularity indicators, including a lower material footprint and reduced waste generation. We discuss our results and conclude with policy implications.
