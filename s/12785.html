BIPOC Women’s Division of Domestic Labor: The Emotional Burden of Work-Nonwork Balance

Authors: Ayesha Tabassum York University; Tina Sharifi MacEwan University

Session overview

Research on work-nonwork balance (WNWB) suggests that women experience greater challenges due to inequities in the division of domestic labor, inequities that are particularly pronounced for BIPOC (Black, Indigenous, People of Color) women whose experiences are shaped by distinct cultural value orientations. While equity in domestic labor varies across cultural contexts, less is known about how cultural values shape WNWB through domestic labor allocation. This conceptual paper develops a theoretical framework examining how BIPOC women’s cultural value orientations shape perceptions of equity in domestic labor, and subsequent WNWB. Drawing on Schwartz’s theory of cultural value orientation and social interactional theory of emotion, the framework proposes that cultural value dimensions influence perceived domestic labor equity, which subsequently affects work-nonwork balance through emotional responses. This framework advances culturally grounded explanations of WNWB, challenging dominant assumptions of universality and centering structural and affective realities shaping BIPOC women’s experiences.
