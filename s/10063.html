Session overview

Entrepreneurship is increasingly a component of individuals’ career paths, yet we know surprisingly little about how entrepreneurial career transitions— defined as disruptions and turning points in a career path involving movement into, out of, or through periods of entrepreneurial activity—shape individuals’ career experiences and outcomes. Career scholars emphasize that transitions are pivotal moments that influence how work lives unfold, but studies on career transitions within entrepreneurship remain limited and fragmented. Prior work has examined entry into entrepreneurship, exits back into wage employment, and role changes within entrepreneurial ventures. However, these studies exist in disparate literature streams, each employing different temporal lenses. Without integration across the careers and entrepreneurship literatures, we risk overlooking how transitions accumulate and interact over time to shape entrepreneurial careers and their outcomes. This panel brings together leading scholars who study entrepreneurial career transitions from various perspectives to discuss how they conceptualize these unique career transitions and envision paths forward for the field. Attendees will gain an understanding of entrepreneurial careers from various perspectives, learn how different types of transitions unfold and interact, and identify new questions, methods, and outcomes that emerge when these perspectives are combined.
