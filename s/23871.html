Voice as the Endpoint of Metacognitive Closure: A Theory of Self-validated Voice

OB
Mohammed Yekini (Newcastle University); Rekha Rao-Nicholson (Greenshoots Pvt Ltd)
Why do some ideas end up as voice while others remain unspoken? Existing voice perspectives have assumed a pre-validated lens, emphasizing contextual permission and individual-level dispositions as drivers of voice, thereby overlooking the metacognitive journey through which employees determine whether an idea is sufficiently valid to warrant expression. We advance a metacognitive theory of voice that positions iterative refinement—the repeated testing and reconstruction of causal reasoning aimed at closing a concept-outcome causal gap, as the missing cognitive bridge between idea generation and expression. Drawing on Nelson’s monitoring–control framework, we posit two distinct forms of iterative collaboration through which employees externalize metacognitive regulation: human–human iterative collaboration, which removes interpretive ambiguities, thereby cultivating interpretive coherence thought confidence, and socio-computational iterative collaboration, which removes computational ambiguities, thereby cultivating depth-coherence thought confidence. Extending self-validation theory, we argue that when thought confidence manifests, expression becomes a means to preserve consistency between validated reasoning and action. In this view, voice represents a metacognitive obligation: a necessity to articulate reasoning that has achieved internal warrant through iterative refinement, such that not expressing the idea produces cognitive–behavioral dissonance, while expression restores alignment, enabling the employee to maintain consistency between their validated thought and behavior. We thus position interpretive-validated voice as the behavioural consequences of employee reliance on interpretive-coherence thought confidence, and depth-validated voice as the behavioural consequences of reliance on depth-coherence thought confidence. Thus, in our theoretical account, voice emerges as the behavioral culmination of the metacognitive closure of the concept-outcome causal gap.
