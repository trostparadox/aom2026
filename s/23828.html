From Unethical Pro-Organizational to Unethical Pro-Family Behavior: When and How It Occurs

OB
Mian Muhammad Rameez (The Islamia University of Bahawalpur); Muhammad Naeem (The Islamia University of Bahawalpur); Jawad Iqbal (The Islamia University of Bahawalpur); Usman Ghani (Xiamen University)
In the existing literature on unethical behavior, research has primarily focused on examining how leaders' and employees' unethical pro-organizational behavior may benefit the organization. However, limited attention has been given to understanding how unethical pro-organization behaviors may create cross-domain spillover effects and lead to unethical pro-family behaviors, acts intended to benefit their family but potentially detrimental to the organization. Drawing on social learning theory, this study investigates how employees learn from leaders’ unethical pro-organizational behavior and indulge in moral disengagement, which subsequently drives employees to exhibit unethical pro-family behavior. Additionally, this study proposes that leader-member guanxi moderates the hypothesized relationships. Data were collected using a two-wave lagged method. The findings support the hypothesized relationships. This study concludes with the theoretical and practical implications of the findings.
