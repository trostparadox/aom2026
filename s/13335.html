An Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) Approach to Corporate Financial Distress Prediction

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Authors: Jane Chan Washington University in St. Louis; Zewei Li University of Macau

Session overview

This research addresses the trade-off between predictive accuracy and transparency in corporate financial distress prediction by developing an integrated framework that combines high-performance machine learning with Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques. Utilizing a comprehensive dataset of S&P 500 firms from 2013 to 2023, the study implements an XGBoost model optimized via SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) value analysis to predict distress risk while elucidating individual feature contributions. Results demonstrate that XGBoost significantly outperforms traditional methods like the Altman Z-score and logistic regression, achieving a superior AUC-ROC of 0.892. Beyond mere accuracy, the SHAP analysis identifies the debt-to-equity ratio as the primary risk driver and reveals critical nonlinear threshold effects, such as a sharp increase in risk when leverage exceeds 2.5 and diminishing returns on liquidity beyond a current ratio of 1.8. By bridging the "interpretability gap," this study provides a mathematically rigorous yet managerially actionable tool for early warning systems, offering strategic benchmarks for capital structure and working capital management while advancing the theoretical discourse on XAI applications in corporate finance.
