Session overview

Collaboration plays a central role in healthcare organizations, where effective care delivery depends on complex knowledge integration, robust structural arrangements, and in situ performance coordination. To extend existing research on organizational collaboration, this symposium brings together four studies that examine healthcare collaboration in high-stakes contexts, including emergency work, near miss events, resource constraints, and organizational change. These high-stakes settings serve as ideal sites for advancing theory, as collaboration in these settings is both highly consequential and continuously tested. The four contributions are methodologically and analytically diverse, drawing on both quantitative approaches (e.g., social network analysis, quantitative analysis based on large electronic health record datasets) and qualitative methods (e.g., in-depth ethnography), and operating across multiple levels of analysis (team, inter-unit, and organization). By engaging their empirical findings and theoretical implications, the symposium fosters dialogue on theoretical and methodological approaches to studying healthcare collaboration in dynamic, high-stakes environments.
