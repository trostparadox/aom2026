Session overview

The proposed panel symposium examines how intergenerational transmission shapes management and organizational life by integrating research ranging from organizational socialization, cultural persistence, and succession to tradition, legacy, and generational relations. Intergenerational transmission refers to the processes through which organizations (as well as other social systems such as families, communities, and societies) reproduce and reshape themselves over time. It is the bridge between past, present, and future generations. However, this bridge is under strain as organizations and other systems struggle to survive, generational divides deepen, trust erodes, and inherited systems reproduce environmental harm, bias, and inequality. Yet, despite these challenges, organizations must find ways to successfully manage processes of intergenerational transmission in order for societies to not only survive but also preserve traditional knowledge, keep long-cherished values, and extend the higher social purposes of institutions over time. This panel convenes scholars to explore together the role of intergenerational transmission in organizational life and to, thereby, reimagine organizations as places where people are both inheritors and ancestors—finding ways to reproduce the best of humanity while leaving behind its worst.
