Unpacking Mystery of Imposters: Trait Activation Perspective

OB
Tasneem Fatima (Brock University); Asma Gul (Riphah International University)
Research on the positive aspects of personality is extensive, yet dysfunctional dispositions remain relatively underexplored, highlighting the critical need to examine the negative aspects of employee personality. Imposterism is a complex phenomenon and its importance at workplace is much higher due to its silent presence. Drawing on trait activation theory, this study investigates how impostorism, as a dispositional factor, manifests in defensive and opportunistic silence through the parallel mediating role of three types of avoidance behaviors: interaction avoidance, challenge avoidance, and feedback avoidance. Utilizing a moderated mediation model, the study further examines the moderating effects of inclusive and competitive organizational climates on the relationship between impostorism and avoidance behaviors. Data was collected through three-wave, time-lagged surveys from 453 service sector employees. Results supported the mediating mechanisms and revealed that imposters indulge more in three avoidance behaviors while working in a highly competitive climate. Although a highly inclusive climate plays a buffering role, imposters’ indulgence in interaction and feedback avoidance becomes weaker. However, the inclusive climate could not be supportive in case of challenge avoidance. Theoretical and managerial implications have been discussed along with future directions.
