Session overview

This workshop equips management scholars with concrete tools to navigate the unwritten curriculum of academic life—the tacit rules that shape careers, well being, and identity but are rarely addressed in formal training. Rather than focusing on theory or methods, the session tackles seven intertwined domains that determine whether an academic life is sustainable: (1) building a research stream after the dissertation or tenure; (2) writing and revising, including how to respond to reviewers and use revision to clarify one’s thinking; (3) moving beyond the fiction of “work–life balance” toward sustainable integration of work, family, and caregiving; (4) creating and maintaining a support network to survive Reviewer 2 and other shocks; (5) sustaining physical and mental health over the long haul; (6) navigating gender, race, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, and other fault lines that shape access to the hidden curriculum; and (7) understanding how AI is reshaping research, teaching, and the economics of academic work. The workshop uses pre conference Breakout Learning sessions, lightning “war stories,” roundtables, and structured exercises to help participants co create tangible artifacts such as a personal support map, an R&R and feedback response playbook, and a sustainability code tailored to their own constraints and aspirations. The PDW grew out of conversations among a diverse group of scholars—ranging from assistant professors to chaired professors, across institutions, disciplines, and countries—who observed that, despite abundant sessions on theory and empirics, there are too few venues where academics can candidly examine how to build, protect, and adapt their lives in academia.
