Session overview

Scholars and practitioners alike have long promoted the notion that work is—or should be—meaningful. Yet cultural discourse (e.g., "quiet quitting," "anti-work") suggests workers are rethinking positive conceptualizations of work and actively questioning whether contemporary work can even be meaningful. Existing theories about the beliefs workers hold about work may not be sufficient to explain why and how workers are now grappling with (and sometimes against) work itself. This symposium thus convenes scholars from organizational behavior, organizational theory, and sociology to examine how workers respond when the promise of work falls short. Drawing on diverse theories and qualitative and quantitative approaches, the papers reveal the often existential questions workers are asking themselves about the proper role of work in life. Spanning the life and career course, examinations include how outright rejection of work as a source of worth may lead to collective action, the strategies career entrants employ to avoid meaningless work, the abandonment of callings, the development of new work orientations in approaching retirement, and how workers reconstruct moral beliefs to sustain meaningfulness.
