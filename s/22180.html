The Critical Role of Leaders in the Development of Psychological Safety Climate Strength

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12

People

Authors: Tarek Benedict Carls University of St. Gallen; Mahshid Khademi University of St. Gallen; Louisa Antonia Riess University of St. Gallen; Nicola Vivienne Glumann University of St. Gallen

Session overview

Psychological safety is critical for the functioning of hybrid teams. Research has previously shown that both level and strength (i.e., within-team agreement) are crucial for harvesting the positive effects of psychological safety. Building on social learning theory, we propose that leader–member (mis)alignment in psychological safety perceptions shapes how climate strength develops over time in hybrid teams. We argue that leaders function as salient social cues. When leaders’ perceptions align with members’ experiences, leader signals provide consistent guidance that fosters convergence in members’ perceptions. When perceptions diverge, leader signals become less reliable, promoting within-team divergence and weakening climate strength. We test these ideas across two studies. Study 1 employs response surface analysis on four-wave field data from 522 employees nested within 88 hybrid teams, demonstrating that greater leader–member misalignment predicts weaker subsequent development of psychological safety climate strength. Study 2 utilizes data from 570 individuals in 64 teams to investigate a mechanism and finds that the negative consequences of misalignment are particularly pronounced for members experiencing social isolation in hybrid work. We discuss implications for theory and practice.
