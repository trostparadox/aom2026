Stratified Decomposition to Support Complex Strategic Decision Making

OSCM
Mehdi Rajabi Asadabadi (The University of Western Australia)
Reasoning under uncertainty is a central theme in the decision-theory literature. Despite the extensive earlier research, an ongoing challenge faced by senior executives in many organisations is to make decisions in uncertain scenarios This paper, in alignment with the existing literature on normative decision theory, explores the reasoning structures that underpin rational decision making and proposes a straightforward decision-making framework, thereby contributing to the existing literature on prescriptive decision theory. Existing mathematical frameworks in prescriptive decision theory literature, despite being solid and effective, are overly complex for adoption by decision makers in practice. This disconnect has contributed to a persistent gap between academic innovation and practical implementation and hence highlights the need for the development of new frameworks. The proposed framework, while being straightforward to emphasise its employability perspective to practitioners, is based on solid concepts and is sufficiently reliable to handle complex decision-making problems. The case that this paper utilises to illustrate the framework is the ongoing submarine project in Australia. The results indicate that the proposed framework may have a positive impact on decision-makers’ biases by helping them recognise and correct deviations from rational decision making, often influenced by psychological factors.
