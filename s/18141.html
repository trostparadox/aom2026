Session overview

Organizational slack (OS) and sticky costs (SC) are two closely related concepts that address the management of excess resources within organizations, yet they have largely evolved in parallel streams of literature. While organizational theory has examined OS as both a burden and a strategic buffer enabling adaptation, management accounting research has primarily framed excess resources through the lens of cost asymmetry, emphasizing inefficiencies arising from sticky costs. This study aims to bridge these perspectives by systematically analyzing the relationship between OS and SC and by exploring whether OS contributes strategically to organizational performance through its influence on SC. Drawing on the Behavioral Theory of the Firm and the cost asymmetry framework, this article adopts a qualitative methodology based on a systematic literature review. Following the protocols proposed by Tranfield et al. (2003) and recent advances in AI-assisted literature reviews, the study employs Elicit (Plus) to identify and refine relevant academic contributions. Only articles published in journals listed in the 2024 Academic Journal Guide are included to ensure quality. The findings suggest that OS and SC may represent complementary interpretations of the same underlying phenomenon: managerial decisions to retain resources under uncertainty. The review highlights three dominant explanatory mechanisms—agency problems, managerial expectations, and structural misfit—and shows how OS can either exacerbate inefficiencies or serve as a strategic resource that stabilizes operations during adverse conditions. By integrating OS into the SC literature, this study contributes a novel management accounting perspective that reframes excess resources not solely as a problem, but also as a potential strategic opportunity.
