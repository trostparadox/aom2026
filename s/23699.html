Dynamic Model Between Job Search Procrastination and Job Search Progress: A Longitudinal Study

CAR
WENG Qingxiong (University of Science and Technology of China); Jinke Qu (University of Science and Technology of China)
Self-regulation is a central element of job search activity, however, procrastination, as a typical self-regulatory failure, has received relatively little attention in job search literature. Drawing upon self-regulation theory, we propose that there is reciprocal causality between job search procrastination and job search progress, and employment self-efficacy play a mediating role. Results from a 3-wave cross-lagged longitudinal study of 137 job seekers showed that job seekers’ procrastination had a negative time-lagged effect on job search progress, and employment self-efficacy mediates this relationship. However, there was no relationship from job search progress to procrastination, and therefore, no mediating effect of employment self-efficacy. Theoretical and practical implications were discussed.
