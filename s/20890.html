Session overview

Organizations are increasingly deploying AI-based systems not only to support individual work, but to participate directly in team- and group-level coordination. While prior research has generated important insights into AI-assisted decision-making and human–AI interaction, less attention has been paid to how these systems reshape coordination, learning, and collaboration across levels of organizing. At the same time, existing research remains fragmented across disciplinary silos, with limited opportunities for sustained dialogue among scholars studying AI in organizational contexts. This PDW builds on prior interdisciplinary conversations within the CTO Division while advancing a more focused agenda centered on coordination as a core organizational challenge in human–AI teams. As AI systems increasingly participate in collective work, long-standing assumptions about how coordination, learning, and collaboration are communicatively accomplished are being unsettled. For scholars in CTO, this raises foundational questions about how organizing happens when non-human agents are embedded in everyday interaction, decision-making, and workflow. By bringing together researchers from Communication, Information Systems (IS), Organizational Behavior (OB), and Psychology, the workshop creates a space to interrogate these assumptions, surface theoretical tensions, and explore alternative ways of conceptualizing coordination that move beyond exclusively human-centered models. The PDW is designed to generate research questions, methodological approaches, and collaborative connections that speak directly to CTO’s core concerns with communication, technology, and organizing in contemporary organizational settings.
