Assistant or Shackle? The Impact of Algorithmic Management on Work- Family Conflict

OB
Long Chen (Hohai University); Die Yu (Hohai University); Feisi Yao (Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics)
With the widespread application of intelligent technology in organizational management, algorithmic management is profoundly affecting employees' work experience and behavior patterns. Based on the job demands-resources model, this study discusses the different effects of algorithmic recording and automated analysis on employees' work-family conflict. It also investigates the mediating role of bottom-line mentality, as well as the interaction between algorithmic recording and automated analysis. By collecting data from 604 traditional employees at three time points, the hypothesis is verified by using a structural equation model. The results indicate that algorithmic monitoring increases employees’ work–family conflict, whereas automated analysis decreases it. Furthermore, automated analysis weakens the positive impact of algorithmic recording on bottom-line mentality and mitigates the indirect impact of algorithmic recording on work-family conflict through bottom-line mentality. The above findings provide empirical evidence for understanding the spillover effects of algorithm management on employees' family life.
