Session overview

Our planet, and its inhabitants, are in an existential climate crisis. Human-created climate catastrophes of ever more threatening heat waves, droughts, floods, wildfires, rising sea levels, melting glaciers, thawing permafrost, and ocean acidification, among other many other climate-related catastrophes are beginning to overwhelm human efforts, across multiple levels and multiple systems, to reduce their deadly impacts on both humans, economic systems, and the rest of nature. So, what are the practical ways we can integrate research, teaching, and service with climate action? Many signs point to continued and worsening disastrous climate effects such that the world’s educators, students, and institutions and all of their widely varying stakeholders need to study, plan, and take a wide variety of planet-saving actions at multiple levels (macro through micro) involving many different human and natural systems, to reduce these climate risks, both their occurrence and their impacts, as soon as possible.
