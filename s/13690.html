Session overview

This panel symposium examines a longstanding tension in management and organization science: whether researchers should prioritize generalizability for academic impact or usefulness for real-world application. Drawing on philosophical foundations and contemporary research traditions, the panel challenges this dichotomy as a false binary. Across diverse methodological approaches, including intervention-research, dialogic inquiry, action research, and case-based theory building, context is shown not as a limitation to science but as a scientific imperative that shapes rigor, validity, and theory evolution. The symposium advances a contextualized science stance, arguing that when scientific methods are applied through immersion in real organizational settings, contextual knowledge becomes the basis, rather than the opposite, of generalizability. Framed through the lens of management as a prescriptive science, the panel explores how rigor resides in method, while relevance reflects rigor in impact assessment. Interactive activities and structured dialogue invite participants to reflect on their own scholarly and practitioner identities, examine assumptions about generalization, and reconsider the role of context as a mediator or moderator of scientific production. By integrating perspectives from management consulting, organization development, and research methods, this panel offers conceptual clarity and practical guidance for producing research that is both credible and usable, without compromising scholarly standards.
