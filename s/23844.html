Emotional Regulation as a Buffer of Customer Mistreatment and Counterproductive Work Performance

OB
PABLO RUIZ-PALOMINO; JORGE LINUESA-LANGREO (University of Castilla-La Mancha); Vicente Peñarroja (Universitat Oberta de Catalunya)
The hospitality industry is highly labour-intensive, with organizational success largely dependent on employees’ productive behaviours. However, hotels are also characterized by frequent customer mistreatment, which can foster work alienation and increase counterproductive work performance. Drawing on Conservation of Resources theory and the transactional theory of psychological stress, this study examines the effect of customer mistreatment on counterproductive performance, the mediating role of work alienation, and the moderating role of emotional regulation. Using PLS-SEM on data from 242 hotel employees in the Canary Islands (Spain), results indicate that customer mistreatment significantly increases counterproductive performance through heightened work alienation. Moreover, strong emotional regulation skills weaken the positive relationship between customer mistreatment and work alienation, thereby reducing counterproductive behaviours. These findings contribute to the hospitality and occupational stress literature by clarifying the psychological mechanisms linking mistreatment to harmful behaviours and by highlighting emotional regulation as a critical personal resource to protect employee well-being and enhance hotel productivity and competitiveness.
