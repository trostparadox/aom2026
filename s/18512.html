Post-Divorce Trajectories: Economic Consequences and Adaptation Mechanisms

Session overview

Divorce has emerged as a pivotal trend in global demographic restructuring, and its rising prevalence has elevated the economic repercussions of divorce to a core research agenda within the social sciences. To advance the understanding of divorce’s economic consequences, this study first employs CiteSpace analysis to examine the intellectual structure and evolutionary trajectory of the existing literature. It then synthesizes the theoretical underpinnings of this research domain and systematically reviews relevant studies. Considering beyond shaping individuals’ economic outcomes and altering the long-term economic performance of their offspring, divorce’s impacts extend to the organizational level—particularly manifesting in financial volatility and corporate governance risks triggered by the divorce of top executives. We focus on four key themes: the effects of parental divorce on adult children’s economic outcomes, the economic implications for divorced individuals, the corporate-level economic impacts of CEO divorces, and gender disparities in the economic costs of divorce. Finally, the study summarizes actionable strategies to mitigate adverse economic consequences and offers targeted recommendations for future empirical research on this topic.
