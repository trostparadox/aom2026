Self-Concept Clarity and Work Anxiety: A Multi-Method Examination

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

Authors: Xueting Zhang Peking University; Shuwei Hao Peking University

Session overview

Workplace anxiety has emerged as a critical occupational health challenge, yet its cognitive origins rooted in the structural properties of the self remain underexplored. Integrating the entropy model of uncertainty with self-discrepancy theory, this research proposes a dynamic model to elucidate how self-concept clarity (SCC)—the extent to which self-beliefs are clearly and confidently defined—influences work anxiety. We conceptualize low SCC as a state of “psychological entropy” that impairs an employee’s ability to manage uncertainty, thereby triggering workplace imposter thoughts and subsequent anxiety. Furthermore, we posit that goal orientation serves as a critical boundary condition: performance goal orientation exacerbates the negative effects of low SCC, while mastery goal orientation buffers them. We propose a comprehensive multi-method investigation comprising a three-wave longitudinal study, an experimental study, and a randomized controlled intervention trial to test these hypotheses rigorously. This research contributes to the literature by framing work anxiety as a consequence of cognitive energy metabolism failure and offering a verified, low-cost intervention for organizational practice.
