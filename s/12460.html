Session overview

Loneliness is a serious problem facing organizations and their members. It affects potentially 80% of employees (Twaronite, 2022) and has detrimental outcomes on employee attitudes, wellbeing, and performance (McCarthy et al., 2025). A recent review by McCarthy and colleagues (2025) underscores these negative outcomes and begins to unpack the determinants of work loneliness, highlighting the critical need for research to uncover the causes of and interventions for work loneliness and to consider the role that context plays in creating the phenomenon. Accordingly, this symposium addresses both antecedents and context to advance theory and practice around work loneliness. The papers in this symposium analyze how various behaviors, including emotional self-disclosure, empowering leadership, and participation in social opportunities at work, may determine an employee’s work loneliness. The authors additionally unpack the interplay between behaviors and social cognition, highlighting how both factors shape loneliness and behavioral responses to loneliness. Finally, the papers examine loneliness across diverse contexts, including digital nomads, leaders, and knowledge workers. Together, the four papers in this symposium offer critical insights to better understand the phenomenon of loneliness, how it emerges, and how it might be prevented in organizations.
