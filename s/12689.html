The AI in the Room: How Algorithmic Advice Shifts Equity Splits

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Authors: Ece Tuncel Webster University; Ugur Uygur Loyola University Chicago

Session overview

Entrepreneurs increasingly turn to AI systems for guidance on morally complex decisions, yet we know little about how AI advice shapes the moral tradeoffs inherent in venture formation. This study examines AI influence on equity allocation, where founders must balance self-interest against partnership sustainability. We propose that AI advisors can shift these morally consequential decisions, and that structured AI advice amplifies this influence by offloading moral reasoning. We test these predictions using a modified ultimatum game in which participants chat with an LLM as startup co-founders negotiating an equity split. In a 2 × 2 experiment with 351 participants allocating real money payouts, we find support for both predictions: AI advice orientation significantly shifts equity allocation, and advice structure amplifies this effect, pushing decisions further in the direction the AI favors. Notably, participants shifted their decisions toward the AI's recommendation without endorsing the AI advisor as fair or competent. These findings position AI as a powerful new actor in the entrepreneurial advice ecosystem, one capable of not only informing founder decisions but potentially substituting for the moral reasoning those decisions typically require.
