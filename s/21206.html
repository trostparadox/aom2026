Session overview

This symposium examines contemporary antisemitism in organizational contexts. Despite increased attention to antisemitism in broader social and political discourse, there has been limited organizational research examining antisemitism at work. One reason for this gap is that antisemitism at work is often difficult to recognize: it may take subtle forms, be contested in meaning, and be embedded in everyday interactions or organizational practices rather than expressed through overt acts. The presentations in this symposium are intentionally ordered to provide a structured examination of antisemitism at work, moving from conceptual foundations to empirical examination and professional reflection. The symposium begins by examining why antisemitism in organizational settings is often difficult to see or challenge, focusing on paradoxes and interpretive dynamics that shape how antisemitic experiences are recognized and responded to at work. It then turns to how antisemitism shows up in workplace contexts and why it matters for people at work, drawing on conceptual models and empirical evidence to examine associations with employee well-being, social inclusion, and workplace outcomes. The symposium concludes with a facilitated action learning session that engages participants in collective reflection on how antisemitism is understood, discussed, and navigated in professional and organizational settings.
