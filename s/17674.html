Session overview

This symposium examines how contemporary social movements, especially conservative social movements, generate and exercise power through indirect, elite-driven, and cumulative processes that span multiple organizational and societal domains. Moving beyond research that emphasizes direct activist-organization interactions, the papers foreground less visible actors, business elites, political elites, think tanks, and employee coalitions, whose evolving alignments restructure the opportunity space for mobilization over time. Conceptualizing power as relational and dynamic, the four papers use historical, quantitative, and qualitative methods to show how shifting elite alliances, political targeting, idea production, and coalition-building compound over time, reshaping patterns of power, contention, and organizational change across society.
