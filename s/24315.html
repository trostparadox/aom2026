Enacting Responsible Leadership Through Collective Care Practices

PNP
Minna Marinova Paunova (ESCP Business School); Silviya Svejenova (Copenhagen Business School)
Amid institutional strain and ecological risk, stakeholders expect transparency and dialogue about how organizations act. We conceptualize responsible leadership (RL) as stakeholder-relational practice, treating responsibility as something designed, enacted, and publicly interpreted in interaction. Conceptually, we offer a minimal process—Roles, Rhythms, Reception—that renders RL analytically tractable: Roles specify expectations of responsibility— who holds what, when, how; Rhythms are relational routines for enactment of responsibility that make intent observable; Reception captures how insiders and publics validate or contest expressions of responsibility, shaping relevant Outcomes (payoffs, costs, misalignments). Empirically, we elaborate this process with a longitudinal, multi-source study of Spira mirabilis, a conductor-less orchestra that organizes as co-laboratory, rotates stewardship, and invites audiences into rehearsal and dialogue. Across rehearsals, small-hall dialogues, and stage performance, care is enacted and judged within instances of Roles– Rhythms–Reception; Outcomes surface risks such as paternalism, gatekeeping, and burden, prompting recalibration of roles and routines. We specify time and space as boundary conditions that qualify enactment and judgement: responsibility is rhythmically recomposed through temporal patterns and spatial configurations as care is negotiated. Our contribution is to conceptualize leadership as a rhythm-based, collective process that is shaped by, and renders visible, the taking up of responsibility.
