Being a Leader Is All About Growing Others: Leader-Follower Learning Imbalance and Leader Identity

OB
Anders Friis Marstand (Durham University Business School); Ilias Kapoutsis (Athens University of Economics and Business)
Leaders experience a stronger leader identity when they demonstrate characteristics and enact behavior of a leader. Followers can also show this, however, we do not know how leader identity is influenced when leaders see their followers display characteristics and behavior associated with leadership. One such way of acting leader-like is for leaders to take the role of teacher in relation to their followers. Leaders can also learn from followers, however, putting followers in the role of teacher that would be associated with leadership. By integrating self-categorization theory and leader identity theory, we investigate how leader-follower learning imbalance (when followers learn more from the leader than the leader learns from followers or vice versa) influences leader identity and leader time spent leading and how this relationship is moderated by leader power distance orientation. Using one experience sampling investigation of leaders across 10 workdays and one scenario-based experiment, we found that leaders view themselves more as leaders when followers learn more from the leader than vice versa. This effect was stronger for leaders higher (vs. lower) in power distance orientation. Moreover, we found that power distance orientation moderated the indirect effect of leader-follower learning imbalance on time spent leading via leader identity.
