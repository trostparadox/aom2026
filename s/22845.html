Job Crafting Faultlines: How Subgroup Dynamics Shape Inertia, Concealment, and Collective Crafting

OB
Limei Zhang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Matej Cerne (School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana); Sut I Wong (BI Norwegian Business School); Pengcheng Zhang; Xuhong Zhao (Huazhong Agricultural University)
Job crafting enables employees to proactively reshape their jobs to better claim their work identity and fit their talents, yet little is known about its social processes and consequences within teams. Integrating job crafting and faultline perspectives, this study introduces the concept of job crafting faultlines— team divisions arising from divergent individual job crafting approaches. Drawing on social identity and threat-rigidity theory, we conceptualize a model explaining how job crafting faultlines predict individual and collective behaviors. Particularly, faultlines heighten perceived identity threat, fostering defensive reactions such as crafting inertia and hidden crafting, which undermine collective job crafting. We further propose that subgroup status imbalance shapes these dynamics by dampening perceived identity threat. Using multisource, two-wave data from 76 teams (243 members) in a multinational industrial firm, we found general support for our theorized model. This research extends job crafting and faultline literatures by showing that proactive role redesign can divide and harm collective job redesign, and by clarifying when subgroup dynamics exacerbate or mitigate these effects. Practically, our findings highlight the need for managers to foster transparency and cohesion when encouraging employees to personalize their jobs.

Prior-Day Job Crafting Volatility, Morning Affect, and the Arousal Pathway to Creative Performance

OB
PINKO YIQING WANG (National University of Singapore); Haichen Dai (Lingnan University); Nancy Chen (Lingnan University)
Research on job crafting has focused primarily on how much employees craft, reinforcing a dominant “more is better” narrative. Yet because promotion-focused and prevention-focused crafting can yield different outcomes, the pattern with which employees shift between these strategies may matter in its own right. We introduce within-day job crafting volatility, defined as the extent of regulatory reorientation from morning to afternoon, and examine whether such reorientation creates costs that persist overnight and constrain next-day creative performance. Integrating regulatory focus theory with effort-recovery theory, we argue that substantial regulatory reorientation consumes self-regulatory capacity across the workday, and that overnight sleep quality conditions whether the costs of this crafting reorientation dissipate or persist into the next morning, manifesting as reduced activated positive affect and subsequently impairing creativity. An experience sampling study of 373 employees was conducted to track participants across 10 workdays with three daily surveys. Results show that prior-day volatility negatively predicted next-morning positive affect, which then led to reduced afternoon creativity, yielding a significant mediated pathway. Sleep quality moderated this relationship, such that the negative volatility-affect association was weaker. The negative affect pathway was nonsignificant. Together, these findings identify within-day job crafting volatility as a distinct dimension beyond crafting levels, demonstrate a sleep-contingent cross-day cost of regulatory reorientation, and suggest that arousal is central for understanding when affect predicts creativity.

Customer Variability and Employees’ Job Crafting: The Role of AI Usage

OB
Haibo Wu (Macau University of Science and Technology); Mengsang Chen (Guangdong Academy of Social Sciences); lijia Si (Macau University of Science and Technology)
This study aims to explore how and when customer variability affects job crafting through employees’ affective rumination and problem-solving pondering, in which AI usage could be a moderator. The results demonstrate that customer variability positively predicts avoidance job crafting via affective rumination and also positively prompts approach job crafting via positive-solving pondering. Meanwhile, AI usage enhanced the mediating effect of the cognitive pathway and weakened the mediating effect of the affective pathway. The studies have unfolded the processes of affective rumination and problem-solving pondering through which customer variability influences employees’ different job crafting behaviors.

Linking Work Stressors to Proactive Behaviors: A Resource-Conservation Perspective

OB
shihui Zhang (Nanchang University); Changya Hu (National Chengchi University); Rong Zhou (Nanchang University)
Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) theory and the Challenge-Hindrance Stressor framework, we propose a dual-pathway model to examine how distinct workplace stressors, namely workload and interpersonal stressors, influence job crafting through different motivational mechanisms. Based on data from 184 employees at two time points, we found differential effects. Workload, acting as a challenge stressor, positively fueled employees’ motivation for both relational and skill acquisition, subsequently driving promotion-oriented job crafting. In contrast, interpersonal stressors, acting as hindrance stressors, suppressed these acquisition-oriented motivations. Specifically, skill acquisition-oriented motivation mediated the positive relationship between workload and promotion-oriented skill crafting, as well as the negative relationship between interpersonal stressors and promotion-oriented relational crafting. However, the “resource conservation” pathway was not supported; neither stressor triggered a significant conservation motivation, nor did this motivation predict prevention-oriented crafting. Findings suggest that job crafting is primarily driven by the motivation to grow and acquire resources rather than a defensive motivation to conserve them. This study contributes to the literature by unpacking the “black box” between stressors and proactive behaviors.
