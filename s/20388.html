Social Finance in Social Enterprises: A Systematic Review and Research Agenda

Session overview

Social finance has emerged as a global phenomenon with the widespread adoption of financing tools such as impact investment, social impact bonds, and venture philanthropy. While these mechanisms aim at creating social impact along with the financial return, the users of these financing options, particularly the social enterprises, are still facing challenges in getting access to these financing options, primarily due to misalignment between investor and investee expectations. There is rarely an engagement within these domains, leaving us with a partial understanding of how financing logics, financing structure, and institutional contexts shapes the strategies, identities and long terms sustainability of social enterprises. To address this gap, this study undertakes a systematic review of literature at the intersection of social finance and social enterprises to examine the intricacies that exists between these two domains. The study brings together insights from multiple strands of research and develops an integrative framework that maps the factors and pathways through which social finance influences the development of social enterprises. Further, this review contributes to advancing theory and practice by offering an integrative perspective that highlights the dual role of social finance – as a resource for scaling social innovation and as a potential source of tension that may threaten mission integrity.
