Climate Resource View, Nature-Based Entrepreneurship, and River Recreation

ONE
Bert Seither; Christopher Craig (Murray State University)
Climate resources are a unique class of resources that have performance implications for a variety of outdoor-centric ventures. According to Climate Resource View (CRV), climate resources are the bundles—or combinations—of naturally occurring variables (e.g., meteorologic, hydrologic) that are of consequence to organizational performance. Ventures with exclusive access to valuable, rare, and inimitable climate resource are at an advantage. Nature-based entrepreneurs are organized to financially exploit some element of the natural environment (e.g., climate resources) while acting environmentally sustainable. The study’s focal entrepreneurship-type is commercial river outfitters on the Ozark National Riverways in Missouri, United States. The ventures have exclusive access to earn revenues charging for river entry and rental vessels (e.g., canoes, kayaks). The independent variable is river recreation traffic observed by the National Park Service at three known river access points. Study findings indicate that lower river flows and warming temperatures correspond with recreational river traffic (i.e., climate resources). Traffic is constrained by extremely high river flows (90th percentile). Traffic is not constrained by extreme maximum temperature, suggesting the riverways provide a cooling micro-climate for recreationalist during the hot summer meteorological months. Managerial implications and limitations are provided.

When Nature Is Next Door: How Spatial Exposure to Nature Reserves Affects Biodiversity Disclosure

ONE
Wenzhuo Guo; Du Yongzhi (Harbin Institute of Technology)
As global concern over biodiversity loss intensifies, corporate biodiversity disclosure has emerged as a key means for firms to maintain legitimacy. Drawing on legitimacy theory, this study examines whether spatial exposure to national nature reserves influences firms’ biodiversity-related disclosures. Using panel data of 3,124 Chinese listed firms from 2015-2023, we calculate firm-level distances to the nearest national protected areas and construct a unique dataset integrating geospatial metrics with textual disclosure data. The findings reveal that firms with higher spatial exposure to national nature reserves face stronger legitimacy pressures, which encourage them to disclose more biodiversity-related information. The effect is more pronounced among firms with executives’ environmental background and in regions with stricter government environmental regulations. This study contributes to the literature by introducing a spatial legitimacy perspective into the study of corporate biodiversity disclosure and highlighting how both internal governance characteristics and regional institutional environments shape firm responses to ecological exposure.

From Owning to Owing – Kincentricity and Reciprocal Organizing with Nature

ONE
Martta Aune Inkeri Nieminen (Aalto University); Claudia Meri Kovács (Aalto University); Francois Bastien (University of Victoria); Natalie Slawinski (University of Victoria)
This conceptual paper contributes to emerging conversations in management and organization studies on organizing with nature by exploring how organizations, and organizational scholars, might learn from and with kincentric worldviews grounded in Indigenous knowledge systems. Kincentricity offers a relational understanding of the more-than-human world as kin, where interdependence and reciprocity are not only values but are enacted through situated practices. While much of the sustainability discourse in management has focused on decentering the human, prevailing paradigms often remain actor-centric and transactional. We argue that a shift toward kincentricity reorients organizing around relational qualities rather than fixed positions, enabling organizations to recognize, restore, and sustain relationships with the land. Through theoretical development and illustrative vignettes, including the St. Lawrence River in Eastern Canada, and the Indigenous-led social enterprise Nawalakw in British Columbia, we reflect on how organizations might begin to shift away from extractive modes of relating toward reciprocal engagements with land. We offer reciprocal organizing with nature as a concept-in-development that seeks to respectfully engage with kincentric teachings and relational responsibilities. We propose that embracing a kincentric paradigm is not only a promising path, but a necessary foundation for regeneration and organizational transformation.

An Integrative Systems-Level Biodiversity-Relationship Management Framework

ONE
Jason Good (Aquinas College); Andrea Stevenson Thorpe (Universidade Católica Portuguesa, Católica Porto Business School)
There is an emerging body of work in the management and organizations literature on novel, ‘nature positive’ approaches that businesses can take to managing their relationship with biodiversity. A smaller body of work, however, has discussed the role that the inveterate mitigation hierarchy plays in managing relationships between business activities and biodiversity. Nature positive approaches aim for increases in biodiversity levels, while the mitigation hierarchy is oriented to achieving net-zero impact. Though these different approaches are deeply interrelated, we lack an understanding of they can work together as part of an integrative biodiversity-relationship management strategy. In this paper we articulate a system-level view of biodiversity-relationship management strategies, which lays the foundation for the proposal of an integrative, net-zero + nature-positive, framework to help guide managers in creating such a strategy. Our particular system-level view draws on the loosely-coupled systems literature to elucidate types of relationships both within businesses and across businesses and ecological systems, and from there the system-level properties that emerge from, yet also influence, such relationships. Our resulting framework involves both tightly-coupled and loosely-coupled biodiversity-relationship management strategies, which, we argue, are joined by a decoupling process. Within the framework the biodiversity mitigation hierarchy is the tightly-coupled strategy, while biodiversity credits exemplify a loosely-coupled strategy. As we argue, critical to both strategies are biodiversity-relationship management tactics aimed at biodiversity uplift, biodiversity maintenance, and avoided biodiversity loss. In incorporating these strategies, tactics, and net-zero and nature positive approaches into one framework, our paper aims to provide managers with a comprehensive guide to formulating their firm’s biodiversity-relationship management strategy.

The Nexus Between Climate Governance and Eco-Innovation: A Natural-Resource-Based View

ONE
Muzhar Javed (Rabat Business School, International University of Rabat); Ammar Ali Gull (Léonard de Vinci Business School)
Drawing on the natural-resource-based-view (NRBV) complemented by the dynamic capability perspective (DCP), this research investigates the impact of climate governance mechanisms on eco-innovation across diverse organizational landscapes. Utilizing a robust dataset spanning 42 countries over the period 2002-2019, the findings reveal a positive relation between climate governance and eco-innovation, with a more pronounced influence observed in stakeholder-oriented nations. Surprisingly, we found that climate governance mechanisms have a stronger impact on eco-innovation in environmentally non-sensitive industries than in sensitive ones and during the pre- than the post-Paris agreement period. This empirical inquiry not only broadens the geographical and temporal scope of investigation but also enriches theoretical frameworks by integrating insights from NRBV and DCP, thus contributing to a comprehensive understanding of the dynamics between climate governance and eco-innovation. This study also furthers the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal of 'Climate Action' by emphasizing the pivotal role of climate governance in fostering eco-innovation.
