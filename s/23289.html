An Intersectional Analysis of Fintech Funding Access and Entrepreneurial Outcomes

ENT
Kanhaiya Kumar Sinha (University of Minnesota Duluth); Leila Soleimani (Bucknell University); Nga Nguyen (Universite de Quebec a Montreal)
FinTech lenders have become an important alternative to traditional banks in small business financing, yet little is known about who relies on FinTech and with what consequences. Drawing on information asymmetry and discouraged borrower theories, this study examines whether BIPOC-owned firms are more likely to obtain financing from FinTech lenders than from banks, and how entrepreneurial readiness conditions this relationship. Using nationally representative data on early-stage firms, we estimate logistic regression models of financing choice, subsequent funding, and venture survival, and ordinary least squares models of revenue performance. The results show that BIPOC-owned firms are more likely to rely on FinTech lenders, but this tendency weakens when owners have formal business training or prior entrepreneurial experience. Initial FinTech funding increases the likelihood of subsequent FinTech financing, yet FinTech-funded firms exhibit higher failure risk due to financing constraints and lower revenue performance. Overall, the findings reveal a trade-off between financial access and long-term venture sustainability.

From "I Need Everything" to "I Need Compliance": Ecosystem Demand Across the Entrepreneurial Journey

ENT
Joao Gabriel Rosa (HEC Montréal); Fernando Agredano (He University); Jorge Humberto Mejia; Dafna Kariv (Reichman University); Luis Felipe Cisneros (HEC Montréal)
While research on entrepreneurial ecosystems has extensively mapped the supply of institutional resources, we know little about the microfoundations of ecosystem demand. Specifically, how do entrepreneurs' articulated needs vary by motivation, gender, and venture phase? Using a unique longitudinal dataset of 6,517 individuals (2018–2024) comprising nascent, active, and intending entrepreneurs, we employ clustering and multinomial logistic regression to identify distinct demand profiles. We identify three robust taxonomies of support-seeking: "Basic/Low-Support," "Fiscal-and-Guidance," and "High-Intensity." Results indicate that ecosystem demand is not monolithic. The "High-Intensity" profile—seeking comprehensive financial and social capital—is overwhelmingly populated by intention-stage individuals and driven by passion-related motivations. Conversely, established owners migrate toward targeted fiscal compliance or low-support profiles. Furthermore, we find that passion serves as a behavioral mechanism increasing resource mobilization efforts, while gender exerts a significant influence on cluster membership. These findings contribute to a microfoundational view of ecosystems, demonstrating that "ecosystem quality" is subjective to the entrepreneur’s lifecycle stage and motivational drive, challenging one-size-fits-all policy assumptions.

Problem-Oriented Ecosystem Support for Entrepreneurs: An Intersectional Analysis

ENT
Taoting Li (University of Calgary); Chad Saunders; Karen D. Hughes (University of Alberta)
While many entrepreneurial support initiatives have been implemented worldwide, relatively little research has examined whether these interventions account for heterogeneity across diverse entrepreneurial groups or effectively address their distinct support needs. In this study, we focus on problem-oriented ecosystem support—interventions targeting problem diagnosis, solution generation, and solution selection—as a mechanism for supporting entrepreneurs with intersecting identities and improving entrepreneurial problem-solving outcomes. Drawing on a nested multiple-case study, we analyze 15 entrepreneurs and 44 business problems discussed with ecosystem advisors as part of a publicly funded support initiative in Canada. The sample is stratified by gender and immigrant status, enabling comparative analysis across groups. Our findings reveal intersectional differences rooted in entrepreneurs’ configurations of human and social capital and demonstrate that problem-oriented ecosystem support serves distinct functions across groups, reflecting variation in entrepreneurs’ social and human capital endowments. This study contributes to the literature by foregrounding entrepreneurial problem-solving as a micro-level process through which ecosystem support generates outcomes and by demonstrating the analytical value of intersectionality for understanding heterogeneity among entrepreneurs. It further shows how problem-oriented ecosystem support can play a compensatory role in promoting inclusion and reducing inequality within entrepreneurial ecosystems.

The Entrepreneurial Ecosystem's Support for Sustainability Ventures

ENT
Giannina Fabiola LOPEZ ALTAMIRANO
Sustainability ventures are essential for promoting environmental and social sustainability while generating economic benefits. However, these ventures encounter various institutional challenges that affect their implementation within the entrepreneurial ecosystem. Therefore, it is crucial to understand how the entrepreneurial ecosystem supports the implementation of sustainability ventures in an emerging economy. This study conducted multiple case studies involving 16 sustainability ventures and held semi-structured interviews with sixteen startup founders and nine participants from five key actors in the Peruvian entrepreneurial ecosystem. The findings indicate that Peru’s entrepreneurial ecosystem is still in its initial stages. This study developed a conceptual framework that identifies the institutional challenges within the ecosystem and highlights the strategic responsiveness practices that sustainability ventures adopt to transform these challenges into advantages that aid their implementation. The Peruvian government and its agencies play a crucial role in developing the entrepreneurial ecosystem through regulations, the provision of public funds, and legitimizing sustainability ventures in the eyes of ecosystem actors. This study provides a basis for policymakers to design strategies that foster sustainable entrepreneurial ecosystems and support sustainability ventures.
