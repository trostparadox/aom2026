Empowerment Paradox: How Support Creates Dependency Cycles in a Global North-South Social Enterprise

ENT
Giang Tra Nguyen (ESCP Business School); Sönke Mestwerdt (Alliance Manchester Business School, University of Manchester); Jill R. Kickul (EGADE Business School)
International social enterprises (ISEs) increasingly bridge Global North and South partners to address societal challenges. Yet paradoxically, these partnerships often become trapped in dependency loops where efforts to achieve autonomy inadvertently reinforce dependence. Drawing on resource dependence theory (RDT), we examine how dependencies evolve dynamically through organizational phases in a German-Nepali social enterprise partnership. Through 33 interviews, archival data, and observations spanning a decade, our in-depth case study reveals a cyclical pattern of reinforced dependence and independence building across multiple organizational dimensions. We identify four key constraining mechanisms that perpetuate dependency loops: routinized resource flows, entrenched emotional attachments, efforts to prevent mission drift, and empowerment dilemmas. Escaping these loops requires significant shifts in resource flows and emotional detachments, through which both partners collaboratively construct capacity scaffolding in the Global South and the Global North engages in strategic relinquishment. We contribute to RDT by introducing a dynamic, processual perspective on intraorganizational evolution that reveals dependency as a mechanism for organizational transition requiring bilateral engagement and continuous renegotiation. We extend the theory to social entrepreneurship, showing that pursuing autonomy is the ultimate goal, with social dimensions like emotional attachment, mission protection concerns, and empowerment dilemmas unexpectedly constraining this process. Paradoxically, the characteristics that define social enterprises can systematically constrain their evolution toward autonomy. Our findings also reveal that independence is not merely an endpoint but a redefined form of interdependence, where the evolution from asymmetric dependence to mutual interdependence represents a more realistic and effective model for Global North-South partnerships.

From Local Ties to Global Reach: Uncovering Internationalization in Entrepreneurial Ecosystems

ENT
Caio Sousa (FGV EBAPE); Juliana Bonomi Santos (FGV EAESP)
This research contributes to the understanding of the internationalization of entrepreneurial ecosystems (EEs), combining insights from the literature on international entrepreneurship with the growing field of EE studies. The results reveal that, although the literature on the internationalization of EEs is expanding, it remains conceptually fragmented and methodologically diverse. This qualitative study, based on content analysis, conducted a systematic review and identified and categorized the main antecedents and mechanisms that can drive local ecosystems towards global integration. The results generated by these elements include the accelerated internationalization of startups, access to international capital, global visibility, and talent attraction. A conceptual framework is proposed, integrating three main dimensions and outlining a future research agenda to address existing gaps in the literature. This study contributes to the emerging discourse on the internationalization of entrepreneurial ecosystems, offering guidance to academics, policymakers, and ecosystem builders seeking to expand the international reach and impact of entrepreneurial environments.

Intentional and Attentional Dynamics: Rethinking South-to-North Internationalization of Family Firms

ENT
Naïma Cherchem (Toronto Metropolitan University); Sofiane Baba (University of Sherbrooke); Taieb Hafsi (HEC Montréal)
Family firms (FFs) internationalization has become central to international business research because multigenerational ownership, socio-emotional wealth concerns, and long-term orientation distinguish them from non-family firms. Yet the literature is largely based on developed-economy settings and primarily treats internationalization as the execution of intentions formed before strategic action. What remains underexamined are the micro-cognitive dynamics through which internationalization intentions emerge, shift and evolve into actions over time, particularly in volatile institutional environments common in Africa. Drawing on Husserlian phenomenology, we conceptualize internationalization intentionality as a dynamic cognitive process shaped by attentionality, that is, the selective focus that links beliefs to action, rather than merely a fixed precondition. Empirically, we analyze a unique 15-year longitudinal qualitative case of an Algerian family conglomerate, supported by extensive data (interviews, internal archives, and observations). Our contribution lies in theorizing internationalization as a co-evolving process of attention, intention, and action, which challenges the rational model of internationalization. In particular, we show how internationalization under volatility, this sequencing produces two contrasting pathways: an outside-in pathway, where North– South partnerships are pursued to import capabilities and compensate for local ecosystem deficits, and an inside-out pathway, where this north–south scheme is rejected because of its downsides shifts toward south–north projection to consolidate capabilities locally through socially embedded projects and only then expand abroad to leverage home-developed capabilities, treating the North less as a partner to learn from and more as a new product-market to pursue.

SEW International Recalibration: Family Firm Internationalization in the Global South

ENT
Sara Urizar Espinosa (IE Business School); Luiz F. Mesquita (Arizona State University); Luis R. Gomez-Mejia
Existing international business (IB) and socioemotional wealth (SEW) theories predict that family firms internationalize cautiously. Because family owners prioritize non-financial endowments such as control, identity, and legacy, international expansion is typically framed as a SEW-threatening gamble, best approached gradually as uncertainty declines through experience. We propose that this logic changes under persistent institutional volatility. Drawing on behavioral agency theory, we develop the concept of SEW international recalibration to explain how volatile environments can alter how family firm leaders interpret what it means to preserve SEW. When domestic concentration becomes a source of vulnerability, internationalization may be reinterpreted as protective rather than threatening. We develop a process model showing how recalibration reshapes internationalization speed, geographic scope, and entry mode, and how action-oriented responses enable commitment under unresolved uncertainty. This theory explains why family firms operating in volatile institutional contexts—particularly in the Global South—often internationalize in ways that depart systematically from traditional predictions.

Toward Ethical Green Entrepreneurial Internationalization: A Framework for the Generative AI Era

ENT
Vaibhav Chaudhary (University of Campinas); Thomas Long (EDHEC Business School); Cristiano Morini
The convergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen AI), Sustainable Entrepreneurship (SE), and Cross-Border E-Commerce (CBEC) represents a transformative yet underexplored nexus in the evolution of international business. While Gen AI tools offer new capabilities for creativity, personalization, and automation, their role in augmenting the internationalization processes of sustainability-driven ventures remains conceptually fragmented and empirically unsubstantiated. This review synthesizes emerging scholarship across these domains using a semi-systematic approach grounded in peer-reviewed articles from Scopus and Web of Science. It identifies critical knowledge gaps, including the absence of integrative theoretical frameworks, under-theorized AI-enabled platform affordances, and limited research from emerging economies and Global South contexts. Building on dynamic capabilities, stakeholder theory, effectuation, and platform theory, we propose the “AI-Augmented Sustainable Internationalization Framework”, which seeks to explain how entrepreneurs leverage Gen AI and CBEC platforms to navigate institutional voids, resource constraints, and ethical dilemmas while pursuing triple bottom line objectives. The study contributes to the theorization of digitally mediated internationalization, offers a research agenda spanning empirical validation, algorithmic governance, and digital sustainability metrics, and provides actionable insights for policymakers and platform designers aiming to support inclusive and ethical global entrepreneurship.
