Session overview

CEO succession remains one of the most consequential processes boards manage. Although boards are formally responsible for identifying, developing, selecting, and transitioning CEOs, scholarship offers conflicting conclusions about whether boards possess the capabilities necessary to manage this process effectively. Some scholars view boards as effectively planning and managing CEO successions, while others highlight social, political, and cognitive constraints that limit boards’ ability to do so. To engage this enduring debate, this symposium adopts an Oxford-style debate format in which prominent scholars argue for and against the motion: “This house believes that boards have the capabilities to effectively manage CEO succession.” Through a structured exchange featuring opening statements, rebuttals, and audience participation, the session aims to surface competing theoretical assumptions and identify promising directions for future research on board governance and CEO succession.
