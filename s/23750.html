Breaking In, Not Through: How Scaling Friction Constrains Women Entrepreneurs in Emerging Markets

ENT
Preeti Subhashchandra Adhav (Indian Institute of Management Tiruchirappalli); Mukundhan K.V. (Indian Institute of Management Tiruchirappalli)
While digital technologies have dramatically lowered entry barriers for women entrepreneurs in emerging markets, few successfully scale beyond startup. Existing scholarship adopts a static view of constraints, treating barriers as uniform across the venture lifecycle. We address this limitation by introducing scaling friction – the cumulative and transformative constraints that intensify during the startup-to-scaleup transition. Drawing on Resource-Based View, Institutional Theory, Social Capital Theory, and Gender Role Theory, we theorize three mechanisms: temporal resource brittleness (resources valuable at startup become inadequate during growth), institutional exclusion intensification (navigable voids convert to discriminatory friction during formalization), and gendered legitimacy taxation (credibility requirements escalate as venture stakes increase). These mechanisms interact cumulatively, creating non-linear effects that disproportionately constrain women-led ventures. Through conceptual development illustrated with cases from Indian women entrepreneurs, we develop six testable hypotheses and a dynamic model. Our framework introduces scaling friction as a stage-sensitive construct, extends existing theories with insights into resource-stage fit and legitimacy taxation, and provides an integrative model showing how constraints compound across growth transitions. For policy, we demonstrate why entry-focused interventions are insufficient, articulating the need for stage-sensitive support providing growth capital, formalization assistance, and legitimacy scaffolding.
