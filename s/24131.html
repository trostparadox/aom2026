Integrative Literature Review on the Use of Logos, Pathos, and Ethos Appeals

OMT
Sara Fontanet (University of St.Gallen)
As the most fundamental elements in rhetorical theory, logos, pathos, and ethos appeals have been widely adopted by management and organizational scholars (Aristotle, 1991; Hartelius & Browning, 2008). Spanning multiple disciplines and research contexts, these appeals appear as established and well-understood concepts. Yet, a closer investigation reveals a complex and fragmented picture. While scholars draw on the same terminology, they often do so with divergent – and sometimes incompatible – assumptions about what Aristotelian appeals are, how they operate, and why they matter. These inconsistencies are problematic as they hinder theoretical clarity and limit the development of cumulative knowledge on the topic. We address these issues by conducting an integrative literature review that critically and systematically examines how logos, pathos, and ethos appeals have been defined, interpreted, and operationalized across management and organization studies. Our review reveals that these appeals are widely used but inconsistently understood, reflecting two dominant, yet largely unacknowledged, paradigms – the virtue-ethics and the rational-world paradigms. Building on these insights, we develop an integrative rhetorical framework that clarifies the ontological essence of these appeals, provides guidance for their systematic analysis, and introduces three additional rhetorical categories – logo-pathetic, etho-logical, and etho-pathetic appeals (Gottweiss, 2006). In doing so, we pave the way for a more coherent and rigorous future use of Aristotle’s (1991) theory of rhetoric for explaining complex management and organizational phenomena.
