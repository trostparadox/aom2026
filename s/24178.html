Food, Stress, and Algorithms: Unveiling Social Identity Change Through Self-Regulatory Eating

MOC
Sana Mumtaz (University of Niagara Falls, Canada)
The integration of artificial intelligence in work processes has facilitated most organizations in effectively managing employees’ work performance during and after the Covid period. However, non-work outcomes of artificial intelligence have been insufficiently explored in the existing literature. Therefore, this research explored the linkage between stressors of artificial intelligence and changes in the eating behavior of employees. Using the guidance from the literature, an initial framework has been developed for understanding the outcomes of individual vs. collective eating behaviors on the health of employees. Following this, qualitative research methods were integrated for conceptualizing the socio-psychological change experiences of both categories of employees using the social identity theory. The findings suggested that collective eating behavior is likely to encourage employees in becoming more resilient and in developing creative self-efficacy over time because of trust development. Contrary to the above, individual eating behavior might lead to negative interactions and social identity threats in individuals. Such individuals might experience workplace discrimination and burnout in the long run because of the lack of social and emotional support. Overall, this model provides a comprehensive understanding of the essence of daily routine and socialization process for employees, and is one of the first few studies to explore the unique process model which links eating patterns with change experiences of individuals.
