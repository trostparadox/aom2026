Digital Voice Concerns in the Workplace

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Mavis Yi-Ching Chen National Taiwan Normal University; Ryan Shuwei Hsu National Chengchi University

Session overview

Social media has emerged as an alternative communication channel, and voicing through social media differs from traditional organizational communication channels. Prior research indicates that individuals often avoid expressing ideas on social media and prefer face-to-face communication when speaking up. Accordingly, this study aims to understand why individuals refrain from expressing suggestions on social media, referred to as digital voice concerns. Following Hinkin’s (1998) scale development procedures, including item generation, exploratory factor analysis (EFA), and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), this study developed and validated a measure of digital voice concerns. Data were collected from two samples of full-time employees in Taiwan: one for the EFA (N = 156) and one for the CFA (N = 374). Results indicated that digital voice concerns constitute a unidimensional construct, measured by 12 items, which capture employees’ concerns regarding incomplete information, lack of feedback, uncertainty about the audience, and role inconsistency when voicing ideas digitally. The scale demonstrated reliability and construct validity, and digital voice concerns were negatively associated with digital voice behavior, providing evidence of criterion-related validity.
