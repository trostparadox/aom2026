Semiosis in Institutional Work: A Strong Process Framework from Pierce’s Semiotics Lens

OMT
Jiawen Wu (University of Jyväskylä); Kai Huang (Henley Business School)
Institutional work scholarship recognises symbols as outcomes, meanwhile, often treating them as relatively static cultural scenery. This paper asks how signs can be theorized so that the recursive coupling of agency and institutions is rendered explicitly processual. Drawing on Peircean semiosis, we develop a strong-process framework in which meaning is continually made, circulated, and developed through the triadic relation of representamen, object, and interpretant. We locate institutional work in a collective field of meaning, where semiotic traces and artefacts invite abductive inquiry, orient ends-in-view, and mobilise motivation. Across three interlinked processes, dialogue creates actionable interpretations, practice mediates their circulation between distributed agency and the institutional environment, and trans-actional adjustment enables institutional substance (telos) to be re-specified over time. The framework contributes a process ontology of symbols for institutional work, clarifies the mechanics of distributed agency, and offers a vocabulary for tracing fine-grained meaning-in-motion in contemporary empirical studies of institutional change.
