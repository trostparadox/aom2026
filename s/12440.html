Session overview

This symposium brings together a set of papers that examine the social dynamics of gratitude by highlighting how expressions of gratitude are shaped by— and, in turn, shape—social contexts, relationships, and interpretive processes at work. A growing body of research has documented the benefits of gratitude in the workplace, demonstrating, for example, that receiving gratitude from colleagues can enhance employees’ subsequent engagement (Lee et al., 2019). However, much of this work has conceptualized gratitude primarily as an intrapersonal experience with largely positive effects (see Locklear et al., 2023, for a review). More recent scholarship has begun to emphasize the social nature of gratitude, highlighting how expressions of gratitude convey information, shape relational dynamics, and influence social evaluation (Algoe et al., 2020). Building on this, Fehr et al. (2025b) argue that identifying the boundary conditions under which gratitude is expressed is critical for understanding its effects at work. Moving beyond views of gratitude as a uniformly positive emotional experience, this symposium advances a more nuanced understanding of gratitude as a socially embedded signal whose meaning and consequences depend on who expresses gratitude to whom, what is expressed, how it is expressed and interpreted. Together, these studies show that gratitude operates as a socially embedded process that shapes affective, cognitive, and behavioral outcomes in both beneficial and unintended ways. By drawing on pre-registered experiment, multi-source, experience-sampling, and social network, the symposium advances understanding of gratitude not simply as an individual emotional experience, but as a socially situated process that plays a consequential role in shaping prosocial outcome, relational dynamics, and career outcomes. In doing so, this symposium advances a more precise understanding of how the impact of gratitude can be shaped, amplified, or constrained within organizational contexts.
