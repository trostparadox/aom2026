Network Distance in Cross-Border R&D Teams and Patent Quality

IM
Ribuga Kang (Sogang University)
This study examines how cross-border R&D teams should be composed for high-quality innovation, focusing on inventor tie structure. Teams formed through repeated ties or ties connected via mutual partners gain relational stability, but the knowledge exchanged within these ties often overlaps. In contrast, teams composed of relationally distant ties access novel knowledge, yet face higher relational uncertainty, which can hinder knowledge integration. Cross-border R&D teams encounter elevated integration challenges due to the cultural, institutional, and geographical distances. We propose an inverted U-shaped relationship between team relational distance and patent quality in cross-border R&D teams and examine how team network centrality and intra-team patent variance moderate this curvature. Using 45,593 granted U.S. patents, we find support for all three hypotheses. This study contributes to the literature by shifting attention from nationality mix to relational configurations within cross-border R&D teams.
