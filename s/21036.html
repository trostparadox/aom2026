Decolonizing Digital Dialogues: A Critical Theory Framework for Emancipatory Prompting in LLMs

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

Authors: Jens Joachim Marga WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management; Fabian Tingelhoff University of St. Gallen

Session overview

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate knowledge work, yet their provider-controlled infrastructures can institutionalize epistemic hegemony. We theorize four mutually reinforcing oppressive features: epistemic data hegemony in training corpora, proprietary alignment and gatekeeping, scarcity-driven efficiency optimization, and dual recursive entrenchment via interaction and self-consumption feedback. Together, these features compress stakeholders’ freedoms to think, act, belong, and express, with disproportionate burdens on marginalized communities and under-resourced language groups. Because upstream levers are largely inaccessible to adopters, we argue the prompt layer is the most actionable locus for emancipation. Drawing on design science research, participatory design as a kernel theory, and critical-emancipatory information systems scholarship, we develop Emancipatory Participatory Prompt Engineering (EPPE), reframing prompting from an individual craft to a governable institutional capability. We specify four emancipatory tools, agency, dialogue, inclusion, and rationality, and derive eight actionable design principles that promote and protect these freedoms under persistent power asymmetry. We further articulate realization conditions, advancing DEI-oriented governance by operationalizing how organizations and communities can build participatory, contestable prompt infrastructures that preserve epistemic pluralism while retaining the practical benefits of LLMs.
