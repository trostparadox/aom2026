Shareholder Litigation Risk and the Boundaries of Acquisition Strategy

STR
Kwangjun An (Waseda University)
Acquisitions are a central mechanism through which firms pursue growth, yet little is known about how legal institutions shape which targets managers perceive as feasible to pursue. We examine how shareholder litigation risk influences the composition of acquisition strategies. Building on the pressure view of governance, we theorize that litigation risk operates as a blunt institutional constraint that discourages managers from considering informationally or jurisdictionally complex targets that are difficult to defend under ex post legal scrutiny. We test this argument using the staggered adoption of Universal Demand statutes across U.S. states, which reduced exposure to shareholder derivative lawsuits. Using a difference-in-differences design with coarsened exact matching and a comprehensive panel of U.S. acquisitions from 1986 to 2018, we find that reductions in litigation risk significantly reshape acquisition strategies. Firms become more likely to acquire private targets, out-of-state targets, and especially private out-of-state targets. Temporal estimates show that these effects emerge immediately following adoption. Together, the findings reconceptualize shareholder litigation as a structural constraint on exploration and show that legal institutions shape not only whether firms acquire, but also which types of risks managers are willing to assume.
