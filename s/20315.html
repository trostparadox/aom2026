Session overview

Escalating social and ecological challenges globally underscore the urgency of systemic transformations that extend accountability beyond the conventional boundaries of organizations to encompass sustainability of ecosystems and society. Given that organizations traditionally prioritize profit maximization over ecological or social well-being, demands for sustainability generate a need to navigate tensions between organizations’ legal obligations and their normative responsibilities for socioecological stewardship, which are often based in Indigenous normative foundations. The tensions intensify long-standing debates in management and organization scholarship concerning the limits of organizational accountability and the normative foundations upon which these limits are justified. By foregrounding historically- and Indigenous-informed perspectives on socio-ecological (ir)responsibilities in organizational stewardship, this PDW invites scholars to critically interrogate the tensions. Drawing on litigated cases involving multimillion-dollar claims, especially in Indigenous contexts, the workshop examines how responsibility is constructed, contested, or denied across legal, organizational, and community interfaces. The goal is to rethink management theory and practice beyond the regulatory rhetorics of compliance to engage with pluriversal normative conceptions of responsibility rooted in Indigenous ontologies, relational ethics, and intergenerational accountability. The session format will stimulate thought-provoking conversations to explore how sustainability is shaped by histories of dispossession, environmental degradation, and contested claims of stewardship.
