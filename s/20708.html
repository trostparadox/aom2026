HRD Strategies for Ethical and Human-Centered Adaptation in the Age of AI: A Systematic Review

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Author: Mengying Cao The George Washington University

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping job design, skills, and decision processes while raising concerns about transparency, bias, surveillance, and employee well-being. Organizations therefore need clear guidance on how Human Resource Development (HRD) can support ethical and human-centered AI adaptation. This study examines how HRD (1) aligns AI with organizational ethics and values, (2) facilitates effective human–AI collaboration and trust, and (3) supports employee well-being and resilience during AI-enabled change. A PRISMA-guided review of peer-reviewed research published between January 2015 and October 2025 was conducted across Scopus, Web of Science, Business Source Ultimate, and PsycINFO. Sixty-two studies met inclusion criteria and were thematically coded using an integrated framework combining Transformative Learning Theory and Diffusion of Innovations. Three strategy clusters emerged. HRD advances ethical governance and value alignment by embedding AI ethics into competency frameworks, role design, and participatory policy development. HRD strengthens collaborative intelligence, explainability, and trust through transparency training, data stewardship, boundary objects, and cross-functional learning networks. HRD also fosters reflective learning, psychological safety, and capability resilience by institutionalizing reflective practice, self-regulated learning, adaptive feedback, and leadership programs linking ethics with psychological safety. Across these themes, HRD functions as both a capability builder and an ethical steward, offering an evidence-based strategy toolkit for aligning AI adoption with organizational values, enabling trustworthy collaboration, and supporting human-centered adaptation.
