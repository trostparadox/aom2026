Session overview

Organizations face intensifying pressures to address environmental degradation while simultaneously responding to persistent social and economic inequality. Circular economy research has advanced the understanding of resource efficiency, regeneration, and closed-loop systems (Bocken et al., 2014; Ludeke-Freund et al., 2019), while inclusive business and innovation scholarship emphasizes stakeholder participation, access, and social value creation (George et al., 2012). Despite shared sustainability ambitions, these literatures remain insufficiently integrated, limiting insight into how organizations design business models that jointly address environmental and social challenges. This poster advances an emerging programmatic research stream on Inclusive Circular Business Models (ICBMs); organizational configurations that integrate circular value creation with inclusive stakeholder engagement as core design principles. Rather than presenting a single empirical study, the poster synthesizes insights from a multiple comparative case study and articulates a forward-looking research agenda. Empirical foundations include a comparative analysis of energy-sector firms in India and Ghana and an extension into the U.S. lumber industry in Arkansas, enabling cross-contextual and cross-sectoral comparison across emerging and developed economies. Drawing on cumulative empirical insights, the poster proposes an integrative conceptual framework that highlights interactions among circular value mechanisms, inclusive stakeholder engagement, and enabling ecosystem conditions, mediated by entrepreneurial and organizational agency (Bocken & Ritala, 2022; Schaltegger et al., 2016). The poster further outlines future research directions related to scaling inclusive circular models, institutional dynamics shaping sustainability transitions, measurement of dual value creation, and organizational learning over time. By positioning ICBMs as a cumulative research agenda rather than a discrete intervention, this work contributes to ONE scholarship by advancing theory on how organizations engage environmental challenges through socially embedded, context-sensitive business model innovation.
