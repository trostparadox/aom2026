Unpacking the Dynamics of Multi-level Paradox Evolution in the Process of Digital Transformation

ODC
Jingyu Yang (sichuan agricultural university); Zhuoxian Lin (University of Glasgow)
During digital transformation, firms face complex multi-level paradoxes, yet research lacks systematic understanding of how these paradoxes manifest differently across contexts and evolve dynamically. Through qualitative meta-analysis of 28 high-quality case studies, we develop an integrative framework linking context, paradox, response mechanisms, and outcomes. We identify three distinct patterns. Under internal drivers, digital servitization and efficiency imperatives trigger learning and organizing paradoxes—firms achieve efficiency and innovation gains through collaborative participation and organizational restructuring. Under external pressure, market competition and ecosystem pressures generate organizing paradoxes around cooperation-competition tensions—firms balance competition and collaboration through structural separation and differentiated governance. Under combined internal-external drivers, interacting factors trigger triple overlays of performing, learning, and belonging paradoxes—firms achieve multi-value synergy through dynamic equilibrium and cognitive reframing. Cross-pattern comparison reveals differentiated context triggers, dominant paradoxes, and response mechanisms, alongside consistent underlying mechanisms: paradox persistence, strategy-context fit, and ambidextrous outcomes. Our findings extend paradox theory's contextualized understanding and provide systematic guidance for managing multi-level paradoxes during digital transformation.
