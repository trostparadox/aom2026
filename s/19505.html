VCCD: Re-Politicizing Organizational Values and Culture through Critical Cultural Ethnography

Author: Youcef Bousalham

Session overview

Alternative organizations occupy a central place in Critical Management Studies (CMS). They are frequently mobilized as empirical and political counterpoints to capitalist, managerial and instrumental rationalities (Parker et al., 2007; Cheney et al., 2014; Parker & Parker, 2017). These organizations are characterized by strong normative commitments and social values (solidarity, democracy, responsibility, non-lucrativity) which are not peripheral but constitutive of their legitimacy and collective action. Yet, a growing body of critical research shows that the presence of explicit alternative values does not guarantee their enactment in everyday organizational practices (Cheney, 2002; Atzeni & Vieta, 2014; Parker et al., 2014; Bousalham and Vidaillet, 2018a). Alternative organizations are exposed to managerial constraints, competitive pressures and pragmatic adjustments that may undermine the cultural consistency between values and practices. Despite this, CMS has tended either to assume the emancipatory power of alternative values or to disqualify values altogether as ideological artefacts. This extended abstract introduces VCCD (Values–Culture–Consistency–Discussion), a framework for critical cultural ethnography developed from sustained fieldwork in alternative organizations. Drawing on Critical Studies on Organizational Culture (CSOC), VCCD approaches values as cultural objects publicly claimed, symbolically enacted and practically tested rather than as abstract principles or managerial tools. The framework articulates four analytical steps: taking espoused values seriously as emic commitments; ethnographing practices and cultural manifestations; examining cultural consistency between values and practices (Martin, 1992, 2002); and confronting interpretations with organizational members to foster situated reflexivity. Beyond its academic contribution, VCCD aims to equip practitioners within alternative organizations with an ethnographic and critical gaze on values, enabling them to articulate and politicize the tensions they experience in everyday work. The framework also opens analytical extensions toward capitalist organizations increasingly mobilizing ethical and humanistic values.
