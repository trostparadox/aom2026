Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI), automation, and robotics are rapidly becoming core infrastructures of work, innovation, and organizational decision-making. These technologies increasingly influence how people are evaluated, how resources are allocated, how careers unfold, and how new ideas are generated. While emerging technologies (e.g., AI) are often framed as objective and efficiency-enhancing tools, a growing body of research suggests these systems are shaped by the social structures, cultural assumptions, and behavioral patterns embedded in their training data, design choices, and usage. As a result, AI and automation systems often reproduce, and sometimes even amplify, existing inequalities. Understanding who benefits from these technologies, whose preferences the technologies learn, and whose authority they legitimize has therefore become a central challenge for scholars of organizations, innovation, and work. This symposium brings together complementary perspectives to examine how gendered meaning systems and technological infrastructures jointly shape power, opportunity, and inequality in the modern workplace. The included research leverages computational methods, including natural language processing, audit experiments, large-scale platform data, controlled AI training studies, and in-depth fieldwork to uncover the deep cognitive and data-driven structures through which gender stereotypes and gaps become embedded in technology. It further examines how AI and robotics influence real-world organizational practices, from career negotiation and managerial decision-making to the direction of innovation itself. By integrating insights from management, psychology, sociology, and computational social science, these studies advance theory on technology and inequality while offering actionable guidance for designing AI systems and organizational processes that promote more equitable, inclusive, and socially responsible futures.
