Session overview

Organizations invest heavily in diversity yet struggle to realize its promised benefits. This symposium reveals why: diversity fails not through prejudice, but through structural absence—missing shared communication codes, inadequate institutional support, invisible career pathways, inequitable resource distribution, and insufficient psychological safety. Through five rigorous studies spanning experimental economics, longitudinal analysis of millions of employees, media content analysis, multi-team field research, and conceptual modeling, we demonstrate that scalable structural interventions eliminate diversity gaps that cultural approaches cannot touch. A brief linguistic training completely erases racial diversity performance deficits by reducing communicative friction. Formal inclusion policies—not positive climate perceptions—prevent minority turnover cascades triggered by minority leader departures. Media representation shapes whether neurodivergent individuals even aspire to leadership roles. Capital-conscious leadership frameworks make millions of working poor employees visible in organizational theory. Inclusive leadership creates universal psychological safety that enables performance regardless of team composition. Examining diversity across race, neurodiversity, and social class, this symposium moves beyond documenting diversity's paradox to providing evidence-based, implementable solutions. We deliver what organizations urgently need: the infrastructure-building roadmap that transforms diversity from aspiration to measurable competitive advantage.
