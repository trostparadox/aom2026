Agentic Digital Artifacts and the Production of Strain in Platform-Based Fraud

Authors: Mijalche Santa Ss Cyril and Methodius University in Skopje, Faculty of Economics; Jean-Loup Richet

Session overview

Recent years have seen a sharp increase in so-called gamified job or task scams, a form of platform-based fraud that mimics legitimate digital work through repetitive micro-tasks, staged rewards, and escalating investment requirements. While prior research on online fraud and cybercrime emphasizes social manipulation, individual vulnerability, or external economic strain, less attention has been paid to the role of the information system (IS) artifact itself. Drawing on General Strain Theory (GST) and recent IS research on agentic digital artifacts, this study theorizes how strain can be endogenously produced through platform design rather than merely imported from users’ external circumstances. Based on semi-structured interviews with participants in a task-based scam application in North Macedonia, we identify design mechanisms through which the artifact progressively constructs strain and normalizes continued engagement. These mechanisms include escalating commitment ladders, rigid temporal structures that create chronic obligation, manufactured exit barriers that reframe additional investment as recovery, and professionalized gamified interfaces that minimize perceived moral and legal risk. Together, these affordances increase strain magnitude, duration, and centrality while narrowing perceived exit options, making continued participation appear subjectively rational despite mounting losses. By conceptualizing scam applications as agentic cybercrime artifacts, this study extends GST by showing how strain can be actively assembled through design. The findings contribute to IS and cybercrime research by shifting attention from technology as a passive conduit to technology as a generative source of strain. The paper also highlights the risks posed by increasingly adaptive and AI-enabled systems, which may intensify technology-induced strain through personalization and dynamic escalation.
