Reinvigorating Constructive-Development Theory-Based Measurement in Organization Development

Participants: Helen Parker UNSW Sydney; Andrea E Brownlow University of Sydney Business School

Session overview

Organization Development (OD) scholars and practitioners have long been concerned with how leaders and change agents develop the capacity to navigate complexity and achieve ongoing transformation. Constructive-developmental theory (CDT) offers a powerful lens on this capacity, yet CDT-informed work remains relatively marginal in OD and leadership development research. This is partly because traditional CDT measures (e.g., Kegan’s Subject–Object interviews, ego-development sentence completion tests) are time-intensive, require specialized accreditation and are difficult to scale (Brownlow, 2021). At the same time, newer work on collective leadership, leadership culture and interdependent leadership (e.g., McCauley et al., 2006; McGuire & Palus, 2018; McCauley & Palus, 2021) calls for developmental tools that can operate at both individual and system levels. This workshop introduces OD participants to a new generation of CDT-aligned measurement approaches. We will (1) frame CDT as a way of understanding how individuals and collectives construct meaning during change; (2) showcase newer quantitative instruments (e.g., Brownlow’s Meaning-Making Questionnaire (MMQi), Coker’s Constructive-Developmental Self-Report (CDSR)) that are more scalable for OD and HR contexts; and (3) explore the opportunities and limitations of AI-assisted scoring of developmental data (Bronlet, 2025; Hilbert, 2025). The workshop is highly interactive. Participants can complete a short confidential “developmental measurement taster,” interpret anonymized profiles and leadership data in small groups, and engage in a low-stakes AI-assisted scoring demonstration using anonymized vignettes from prior CDT interviews. We close by considering a practical agenda for integrating CDT-informed measurement into leadership development, culture change and evaluation work in organizations.
