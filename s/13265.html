Session overview

In an environment saturated with speed, noise, and polarization, the capacity to slow down and listen well is an increasingly strategic leadership competency. Yet listening is often treated as intuitive rather than trainable. This PDW develops participants’ listening skills through a research-informed, practice-forward design rooted in adult learning principles (active participation, repeated practice, reflection, and application). Listening in interpersonal contexts involves paying attention (presence and responsiveness), comprehending (curiosity and understanding), and relating (support, respect, empathy, and non-judgment). Consistent with foundational listening scholarship, we also emphasize that listening includes internal processes, observable behaviors, and—critically—the speaker’s experience of being listened to, which helps explain why listening produces meaningful interpersonal and organizational outcomes. Participants will (1) build a clear rationale for why listening matters; (2) engage in structured, scalable listening exercises; and (3) leave with classroom-ready tools to teach listening in business school and university courses. As a party favor/parting gift, we will provide attendees with links to online resources (exercise instructions, debrief prompts, and teaching adaptations) to support transfer into their collaborations and classrooms.
