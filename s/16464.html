Session overview

This symposium examines sport as a consequential and under-theorized arena of contemporary organizing in which manifold societal dynamics intersect with unusual visibility and intensity. It addresses the persistent divide between organization and management studies and sport management research, arguing that these fields have evolved in parallel despite overlapping empirical interests and conceptual foundations. The symposium analyzes how divergent incentive structures, evaluation regimes, and methodological preferences shape fragmented knowledge production, theoretical blind spots, and siloed portrayals of sport as an organizational field. Drawing on domains such as governance, digitalization, geopolitics, fan communities, misconduct, and sustainability, it positions sport as a critical case capable of challenging and refining core organizational theories rather than merely illustrating them. Overall, the symposium advances a reflexive agenda for cross-field collaboration that seeks to improve theoretical integration, methodological pluralism, and cumulative knowledge on organizing in high-stakes, morally charged contexts.
