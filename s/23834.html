Decent Work and Meaningful Work in Blue-Collar Jobs (WITHDRAWN)

OB
Roxana Mohammadi (University of Bremen); Julia Kensbock (University of Bremen)
How meaningful work is experienced in highly constrained blue-collar contexts remains insufficiently understood. Dominant theories emphasize autonomy and self-expression as key sources of meaningful work, yet such conditions are often limited in highly structured jobs. We argue that in blue-collar contexts, meaningful work emerges from the quality of basic working conditions—captured by the concept of decent work—which may function not only protectively but also promotively through distinct motivational processes. Drawing on self-determination theory and Herzberg’s two-factor model, we develop a dual-pathway framework in which the relationship between decent work and meaningful work is mediated by psychological need satisfaction and need frustration. Survey data from blue-collar workers (N = 376) in a German automotive manufacturing plant support this framework. Decent work is positively associated with meaningful work, primarily through psychological need satisfaction (reflecting Herzberg’s motivator function), whereas reduced need frustration (reflecting Herzberg’s hygiene function) plays a weaker role. Facet-level analyses reveal distinct motivational profiles across components of decent work. Overall, our findings suggest that supportive, dignifying, and value-consistent work systems are associated with higher levels of meaningful work, highlighting the relevance of decent work as more than a protective baseline in structurally constrained occupations.
