Session overview

As organizational research has increasingly emphasized observable structures, practices, and outcomes, the covert psychological dynamics that shape behavior beneath the surface of organizational life have received comparatively little empirical attention. Systems psychodynamic theory offers a framework for understanding how anxiety, authority relations, projection, dependency, and social defenses influence leadership, teamwork, inclusion, and inequality, yet its application within mainstream organizational research remains limited. We know relatively little about how these covert dynamics operate across diverse organizational contexts, how they are experienced by individuals occupying different roles and identities, or how unconscious processes sustain patterns of dysfunction that resist conventional interventions. Another key challenge concerns how systems psychodynamic insights can be translated into rigorous research designs and practical diagnostic tools. Through four papers, this presenter symposium addresses these gaps by examining covert dynamics in top management teams, gender-diverse workplaces, elite sport teams, and leadership evaluation processes. Collectively, the papers advance both theoretical and applied developments in systems psychodynamics by demonstrating its continued relevance for understanding performance, harm, and change in contemporary organizations.
