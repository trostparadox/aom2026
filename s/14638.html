Family First? The Institutional Consequences of the Two-Child Policy on Women Entrepreneurship

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Authors: ZHU MENG City University of Hong Kong; Jane Lu City University of Hong Kong

Session overview

The persistent gender gap in entrepreneurship remains a critical challenge globally, with women consistently underrepresented compared to men. This study examines the unintended consequences of China’s Two-Child Policy on women’s entrepreneurship through the lens of institutional theory. Although originally implemented to address demographic concerns, the policy inadvertently reinforces traditional gender roles and imposes practical constraints on women’s ability to engage in entrepreneurial activities. Using a Difference-in-Differences (DID) approach and panel data from seven waves of the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS), we find that the gender gap in entrepreneurship widened following the policy’s enactment. Furthermore, we explore the moderating effects of regional internet penetration and Confucian cultural norms. This study contributes to institutional theory by demonstrating how non-business policies intersect with informal institutions to influence gendered economic outcomes and how digital infrastructure can mitigate these constraints.
