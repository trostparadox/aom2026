Embedded but Constrained: Ecosystem Tenure and Business Model Innovation

TIM
Hannah Fabry (RWTH Aachen University); Patrick Pollok (RWTH Aachen University); Dirk Luettgens (RWTH Aachen University); André Amft (RWTH Aachen University)
Business ecosystems enable firms to create complex value propositions through interorganizational collaboration, but they also fundamentally alter the conditions under which business model innovation occurs. While prior research has highlighted the coordination challenges and interdependencies inherent in ecosystem-based business models, we know little about how ecosystem participation over time shapes firms’ ability to innovate their own business model. Building on an activity-system perspective, we examine how ecosystem tenure influences business model innovation and how this relationship is moderated by ecosystem deviance. Using data from cooperative banks embedded in a long-established business ecosystem, we find a curvilinear relationship between ecosystem tenure and business model innovation: innovation increases with tenure at early stages but declines as ecosystem embeddedness deepens. Moreover, we show that ecosystem deviance attenuates this constraining effect, enabling firms to sustain business model innovation despite long ecosystem tenure. Our findings contribute to research on business models and ecosystems by highlighting the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of innovation under ecosystem constraints and by extending tenure-based arguments to interorganizational contexts.
