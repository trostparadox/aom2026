Social Innovation for Social Change: How to Leverage Responsible Governance in the Public Sector

SIM
Katrin Bauer (TU Dortmund University)
Against the backdrop of grand challenges, public sector organizations are reckoned among the central players in the social innovation (SI) ecosystem. However, research so far has rarely focused on these organizations’ and chiefly their internal strategic actors’ readiness and drive to promote SI, but rather points at aspects –rooted in bureaucracy– that bar these actors from flexibly creating opportunities and SI. Therefore, our research delivers and then leaps from a literature review and clustered synthesis of such barriers to generate a framework of public sector SI orientation. Our analysis spans from the macro-to the micro-level and reveals a set of four main dimensions —environmental, intermediate, organizational, and individual— for public sector actors to consider. We thereby draw upon the concept of strategic orientations and the resource-based view and take the inside-out perspective to explain public sector SI orientation. Specifically, we reveal this orientation as a novel strategic leverage point both for theory and practice, a vital intangible capability. We thus also offer a new understanding towards predictors of public sector driven SI and reveal a critical framework and factor to be included in research on SI relationships. Thus, we provide guidelines for public managers, as strategic actors, and ultimately their organizations, to contemplate in their day-to-day responsible behavior when setting the frame for SI, against the grand challenges of today.
