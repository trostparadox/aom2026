Beyond Bandura: Enhancing Organizational Cloud Competency Using Connectivism

ODC
Mayukh Mukhopadhyay (Indian Institute of Management Indore)
Moving beyond social learning perspectives associated with Bandura, this article examines connectivism as an emerging theory of learning and evaluates its relevance for developing organizational cloud competence in digitally connected workplaces. The study adopts a three-stage methodological approach. First, it undertakes a seminal-work-driven analysis of Siemens’ (2005) formulation of connectivism, outlining its core assumptions, theoretical contributions, and limitations. Second, using a snowballing review of workplace learning research, the article situates connectivism alongside established and contemporary learning theories, highlighting its distinctive capacity to explain learning under conditions of networked work and digital abundance. Third, the study draws on a qualitative, case-based organizational analysis of a large-scale cloud migration initiative conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, involving three focal actors across two organizations and geographically distributed settings. Data were generated through direct observation and analysis of organizational artifacts, including meeting minutes, team chat transcripts, and solution design documents, capturing learning as it unfolded in the flow of everyday work. The findings suggest that connectivism explains how cloud competence emerges through dynamic interactions among individuals, social networks, and non-human knowledge repositories. The article extends connectivism beyond its predominant application in student learning to workplace contexts and offers managerial guidance on designing learning arrangements that foster advanced technological competencies without disrupting ongoing work demands. By translating connectivism into an organizational setting, the study contributes to debates on learning, digital transformation, and cloud-based capability development.
