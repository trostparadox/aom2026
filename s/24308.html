The Bright Side of Evil: How and When Leader Bottom-Line Mentality Activates Employee Ethical Voice?

OB
Longjun Liu (Anhui University); Qing Fan (Nanjing University); Jing Long (Nanjing University); Wenhai Wan (Huaqiao University); Ruhong Liu (Yantai University)
Little attention has been paid to how and when leader bottom-line mentality (LBLM) encourages employees’ ethical behaviors. Grounded in trait activation theory and conservation of resources theory, this study examines the mechanisms and conditions under which LBLM promotes employee ethical voice (EV). A three-wave multisource survey was conducted online and offline on 151 leader–subordinate dyads in China. Multi-level structural equation modeling was adopted to test the proposed hypotheses. LBLM does not directly affect promotive or prohibitive EV; instead, its effects are transmitted via employee moral disgust sensitivity (MDS), a mediator between LBLM and both forms of EV. Leader brand equity negatively moderates the relationship between LBLM and MDS, weakening the indirect effects on EV. Conversely, employee psychological capital positively moderates this relationship, enhancing the indirect effects. This study refreshes understanding of LBLM by revealing its potential to foster ethical (not just unethical) behavior and offers implications for ethical leadership and organizational voice behavior.
