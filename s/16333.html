Corporate Governance as a Behavioral Filter: Performance Feedback and Environmental Sustainability

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4
OMT

People

Authors: Minji Kim Sungkyunkwan University; Hye Kyung Yu Dongduk Women's University; Tohyun Kim Sungkyunkwan University

Session overview

Firms face growing pressure to pursue environmental sustainability while remaining financially accountable to shareholders. Yet it remains theoretically and empirically unclear how corporate governance shapes firms’ sustainability responses to performance deviations from aspiration levels. Drawing on the Behavioral Theory of the Firm, this study examines how corporate governance moderates firms’ responses to financial performance feedback in the domain of environmental sustainability. We argue that governance systems shape managerial attention and resource allocation between financial and environmental objectives in response to performance signals. Using panel data on 1,263 U.S. manufacturing firms (1991–2018), we combine Compustat and KLD data. Financial performance feedback is measured as the deviation between return on assets and aspiration levels based on historical and peer benchmarks, and is decomposed into negative and positive components. Environmental sustainability is captured using firms’ environmental strengths and concerns. We find that firms with stronger corporate governance maintain higher sustainability engagement when performance is close to aspiration levels, but reduce sustainability investments when performance deviates either below or above those levels, revealing a governance dilemma between financial accountability and sustainability.
