Sustainability Talk as Strategy: Inertia, Convergence, and Ecological Differentiation in Disclosure

STR
Hamidreza Harati (RMIT University)
How do firms change their sustainability disclosure over time, and why do some firms become environmental specialists while others remain balanced? We develop a strategic trajectory perspective that treats sustainability talk as a dynamic allocation problem shaped by three forces: attention inertia in pillar allocation, convergence toward field norms in total disclosure, and asymmetric environmental differentiation. Using a panel of Chinese listed firms from the China Sustainability Disclosure Index (2010 to 2019), we show that pillar shares are highly persistent, while overall disclosure converges rapidly toward the industry year norm. Environmental disclosure differentiates more than economic or social disclosure, with substantially larger dispersion and heavier tails. Firms with higher conformity sensitivity exhibit less concentrated and less environmentally tilted disclosure portfolios, consistent with a trade off between conformity and differentiation. We formalise these dynamics in an agent based model calibrated to empirical moments. The calibrated model reproduces inertia and convergence closely and generates concentration dynamics in the right direction, while the baseline calibration slightly overstates specialisation in the upper tail. Together, the findings link micro level attention dynamics to macro level field convergence and clarify why environmental disclosure becomes a dominant dimension of strategic differentiation.
