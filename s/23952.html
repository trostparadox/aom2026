The Ambiguity Tradeoff: How Categorical Positioning Shapes the Returns to Learning-by-Hiring

TIM
Alireza Mahmoodzadeh (ESADE Business School - Ramon Llull University); Ivanka Visnjic
Firms hire inventors to acquire valuable knowledge, yet the returns to these investments vary. We draw on categorization theory to explain how an inventor's categorical positioning shapes post-move outcomes. We argue that newcomers with ambiguous categorical positioning trigger incumbent scrutiny as evaluators work to resolve uncertainty about where the newcomer belongs. This scrutiny produces opposing consequences: incumbents engage more deeply with the newcomer's prior work, increasing knowledge spillovers, while newcomers interpret scrutiny as signaling provisional standing and respond by conforming, reducing their innovation impact. Newcomers with clear categorical positioning receive less scrutiny, resulting in fewer spillovers but greater latitude to pursue distinctive, high-impact work. We test these arguments using 144,081 inventor moves between U.S. firms from 2000 to 2021, leveraging state-level adoption of Renewable Portfolio Standards as exogenous shocks that heighten the salience of green-brown categorical boundaries. Consistent with our predictions, we find an inverted U-shaped relationship between inventor greenness and knowledge spillovers, and a U-shaped relationship with innovation impact. These findings reveal a hidden tradeoff in learning-by-hiring such that categorical ambiguity maximizes knowledge reuse while simultaneously minimizing inventive impact
