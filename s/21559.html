Session overview

Mental health concerns among college and university students have escalated significantly over the past two decades. Rising academic pressures, financial strain, socio-political stressors, and post-pandemic adjustments have contributed to heightened levels of anxiety, depression, and even suicide among students. Institutions of higher education face increasing demand for comprehensive mental health services and evidence-based interventions to support student well-being. This PDW highlights attachment theory as a powerful, evidence-based lens for addressing mental health disparities and supporting student success in diverse higher education settings. We examine how attachment theory shapes the mental health experiences of multigenerational learners. Attachment theory examines an individual’s behavior over time based upon the emotional bonds they form as a child. It assumes that certain attachment models are detrimental to the career and workplace functioning of an individual. Attachment patterns, originally formed in early caregiver relationships, continue to influence coping, help-seeking, stress responses, and academic adjustment throughout adulthood. This PDW will develop best practices for professors and/or managers to utilize attachment models as a tool to support the mental health of multigenerational learners. It will equip educators with generation-specific and attachment-informed tools to support students’ mental health, enhance performance, and promote positive classroom dynamics. As mental-health concerns escalate and student populations become increasingly diverse in age and experience, understanding attachment theory and generational influences will help educators create environments where all students can learn, cope, lead, and thrive.
