Session overview

The resurgence of interest in corporate purpose has shifted scholarly attention toward the fundamental question of why organizations exist. However, as organizations increasingly integrate prosocial, value-laden commitments, investigating the tensions, dynamics, and implications of organizational repurposing becomes critical. Organizations are becoming arenas where new purposes are being shaped and contested, with evident repercussions for markets and fields, as they become moralized. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) seeks to understand organizational purpose as dynamic and evolving rather than a static constraint, focusing specifically on the process of repurposing at both the organizational and field levels. We organize the PDW around two central themes. First, we examine the organizational and field-level implications of integrating new purposes. Second, we propose that purpose serves as a generative force that imagines new possibilities and opportunities and shapes organizations and markets. The workshop features an intergenerational panel (V. Rindova, R. Durand, A. DeSantola, J. C. Verhaal) presenting cutting-edge research that connects the concept of purpose to research streams such as imagination, market emergence, authenticity in organizational forms, and organizational design. Structured roundtables will provide PhD students and early-career researchers with developmental feedback on their research, fostering intellectual community among researchers. This workshop supports the OMT, STR, and MOC Divisions by integrating theoretical traditions, providing developmental feedback, and fostering a collaborative network.
