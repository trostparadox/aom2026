Constructing Director Evaluation Framework for Overseas Subsidiaries of State-Owned Enterprises

IM
Xuhong Xu; Gudian Zeng (Queen Mary University of London); Lutao Ning
Overseas subsidiaries of state-owned enterprises (SOEs) face complex governance challenges due to conflicting economic, national strategic, and host-country demands. Existing director evaluation systems are inadequate for transnational contexts, as Western models overlook distinctive Chinese institutional features such as Party leadership, while domestic frameworks lack adaptability to diverse host environments. This study conceptualizes a culturally adaptive “Virtue–Competence” evaluation framework of overseas directors based on corporate governance theories. Through in-depth semi-structured interviews with 30 directors, virtue elements, especially integrity, compliance, and self-regulation, are identified as essential in mitigating agency risks and protecting state assets. Competence dimensions emphasize strategic capability, cultural adaptability, and policy sensitivity. The refined system introduces weighted indicators tailored to host-country governance risks, integrating Eastern ethics with Western governance principles to support effective and compliant overseas operations.Overseas subsidiaries of state-owned enterprises (SOEs) face complex governance challenges due to conflicting economic, national strategic, and host-country demands. How Top Management Team Disciplinary Diversity Drives Subsidiary Innovation via Autonomy

IM
Dennis Gleissner (HHL Leipzig Graduate School of Management); Piotr Trapczynski (Poznan University of Economics and Business)
Top management team (TMT) diversity is widely linked to innovation, but we know less about how this relationship unfolds in foreign subsidiaries. This study unpacks the link between subsidiary TMT disciplinary diversity and innovation by investigating the path through operational and strategic autonomy. Drawing on upper echelons theory and the headquarters–subsidiary literature, we argue that TMTs with diverse disciplinary backgrounds broaden problem framing and knowledge integration, which strengthens subsidiary decision-making and builds headquarters’ trust. This trust, in turn, translates into greater operational and strategic autonomy, enabling subsidiaries to sense local opportunities and convert ideas into concrete innovation outcomes. Using subsidiary-level survey data from multinational enterprises, we find that subsidiary TMT disciplinary diversity is positively associated with innovation performance. The two autonomy dimensions, however, play asymmetric roles. Operational autonomy partially mediates the effect of TMT diversity on innovation, whereas strategic autonomy, although beneficial in isolation, becomes less effective when operational discretion is constrained. We demonstrate these patterns across product, process, and administrative innovation. The study contributes to research on TMT diversity and subsidiary strategy by clarifying when and how different forms of autonomy allow diverse TMTs to leverage their potential for innovation in global settings.

Codified Board Monitoring and Family Firm Innovation in an Emerging Economy

IM
Joyce Wang (Texas State University); Yiyi Zhao (University of International Business and Economics); Jigao Zhu (University of International Business and Economics); Mike W. Peng (The University of Texas at Dallas)
Family firm innovators in emerging economies are replete with challenges, ranging from managerial conservatism stemming from socioemotional goals to underdeveloped institutional environments constraining resource availability, all of which can overload CEOs’ cognition that ultimately stymies firm innovation. We propose that codified board monitoring—explicitly bestowing boards with approval authority for major resource allocation decisions—can alleviate CEO cognitive constraints and thus enhance innovation. The innovation implication of codified board monitoring is more potent for family MNEs, in which explicit communication of board involvement in resource allocations can effectively offload CEOs’ cognitive burden, enabling their innovation pursuits. In addition, we posit that the number of codified categories of board monitoring has an inverted U-shaped effect on innovation, and the curvilinear relationship is more pronounced for family MNEs. With a sample of publicly listed family firms in China, we find empirical support. Overall, we advance CEO cognition as a bottleneck and codified board monitoring as an enabler to family firm innovation in an emerging economy. Our investigation of underexplored microfoundational factors underpinning family firm innovators in the Global South has the potential to strengthen the IB field.

Antecedents of Top Management Team Composition in International Joint Ventures

IM
Weitong Wang (The University of Sydney); Chinmay Pattnaik (The University of Sydney); Anish Purkayastha (The University of Sydney)
Formation and appointment of Top Management Teams have been an intriguing topic for managerial research. Given the indispensable role of TMTs in the strategic and performance outcomes of firms, understanding the formation of TMTs provides pivotal insights into such decision-making process. While studies have looked into executive appointment and turnover in conventional organizational forms, this has rarely been studied in the International Joint Venture (IJV) context, where two or more parent firms will continuously monitor the operations of a new entity. In such circumstances, whether parent firms will agree on internal promotions or external hires becomes a thought-provoking question. Using the Social Exchange Theory (SET), we argue that ownership structures will have a crucial effect on TMT insider-outsider composition, mainly that IJVs with more similar ownership shares will hire outsiders as a means of conflict avoidance. However, we believe that under conditions where mutual trust decreases, the main effect will diminish. Using a sample of 84 IJVs, we demonstrate preliminary results on how a more similar ownership structure will increase the ratio of external hires in an IJV TMT.
