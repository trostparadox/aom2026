Dialogic Co-Crafting of Metaphors: Toward a Dynamic Theory of Generative Learning

ODC
Makoto Nagaishi (Chukyo University); Maki Nishimori
This study examines how metaphors of work and leadership are co-crafted through dialogue and how such co-crafting enables generative learning. While prior research has highlighted metaphors as powerful cognitive and rhetorical devices, much of the literature treats them as relatively stable frames deployed by leaders. Drawing on a qualitative single-case study of a Japanese YouTube interview with an experienced OD/HRD practitioner, this article reconceptualizes metaphors as evolving interactional accomplishments that emerge through empathetic engagement and interdiscursive reframing. Methodologically, the study combines life story and reflective interviewing to analyze how metaphors are introduced, elaborated, and transformed over the course of an extended dialogic interaction. The findings show that generativity does not arise from specific metaphors alone, but from a recursive process in which interviewer empathy enables metaphors to become speakable, while interdiscursive reframing allows them to reorganize participants’ understandings of experiences. The article makes three contributions. First, it advances metaphor theory by elaborating a processual view of metaphor-as-co-crafting. Second, it reframes leadership as dialogic sensemaking rather than metaphorical sensegiving. Third, it demonstrates how SNS platforms can support reflexive generative learning through metaphor-mediated interaction. Together, these insights deepen understanding of how meaning, learning, and leadership co-evolve in contemporary environments.
