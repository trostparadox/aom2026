From Telling to Seeing: Observing Leadership Learning Under Paradox

People

Author: Duijnisveld Sabrina Antwerp Management School

Session overview

Leadership research increasingly recognizes paradox, tension, and ambiguity as central conditions of contemporary organizing. Yet leadership development research continues to rely predominantly on self-report measures and retrospective reflection, implicitly assuming that learning can be inferred rather than directly observed. This epistemological limitation becomes particularly consequential in contexts where leaders must act under persistent tension and without stable reference points. Drawing on paradox theory and process research, this study reframes leadership learning as a process of paradox enactment rather than a change in individual capability over time. We argue that leadership learning becomes visible only when leaders are required to hold competing demands in action, revealing micro-level behavioral adjustments as they navigate moments of imbalance. Empirically, the study is situated in a neuro-experiential learning environment in which leaders engage in embodied, actor-based scenarios designed to surface paradox through uncertainty, relational tension, and conflicting expectations. Rather than assessing outcomes or perceived competence, the research relies on real-time behavioral observation and facilitated sensemaking moments to capture how learning unfolds as it happens. Preliminary insights suggest that leadership learning under paradox is marked by cycles of imbalance and rebalancing, in which embodied responses and interactional shifts precede cognitive articulation. As an OB-focused work-in-progress, this poster invites dialogue on how leadership learning can be theorized and studied as an observable process rather than an assumed outcome.
