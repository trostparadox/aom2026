The Role of Financial Dependence in Gig Workers’ Responses to Algorithmic Silencing

CTO
Yong ZHANG; Yixin Zhang
Algorithmic silencing represents a critical yet underexplored phenomenon within the gig economy context. Existing research remains nascent, predominantly emphasizing homogeneous responses and overlooking individual differences in gig workers’ reactions to algorithmic silencing. Our study systematically conceptualizes algorithmic silencing and develops a measurement scale for it. Subsequently, we integrate psychological reactance theory and the insights from the gig worker literature to empirically examine individual differences in gig workers’ reactions to algorithmic silencing. Results from a three-wave survey study show that algorithmic silencing elicits psychological reactance among gig workers with low financial dependence, which subsequently facilitates their engagement in manipulating algorithm behavior. By contrast, algorithmic silencing does not significantly predict psychological reactance in gig workers with high financial dependence, and consequently, it does not show a significant positive relationship with their manipulating algorithm behavior. Our findings advance research on algorithmic silencing, offer a balanced and holistic understanding of gig workers’ reactions to algorithmic silencing, and broaden the discussion on antecedents of gig workers’ resistance behaviors.
