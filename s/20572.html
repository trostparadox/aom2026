Session overview

Contemporary leadership theories predominantly emphasise cognitive, behavioural, and instrumental approaches to leadership development, often prioritising skills, competencies, and observable behaviours. While these frameworks contribute to performance and relational effectiveness, they offer limited guidance on cultivating inner stability, ethical resilience, and sustained meaning under conditions of complexity and uncertainty. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) introduces devotion at work as a complementary developmental lens that addresses this gap. Drawing on Indian Knowledge Systems, the Narada Bhakti Sutra is presented as a non-religious, developmental framework for leadership that conceptualises devotion not as belief or ritual, but as deep, value-centred commitment and wholehearted engagement with purpose, people, and practice. Building on our prior work on pilgrimage as a form of management development, this PDW positions devotion as an inner strength that supports meaningful leadership journeys for both scholars and practitioners. From a Western perspective, devotion parallels constructs such as calling, purpose, and identity-based leadership, echoing scholarship that emphasises the importance of inner coherence in navigating leadership challenges (Palmer, 1993; Campbell, 2008). By engaging the Narada Bhakti Sutra, this workshop invites participants to reconceptualise leadership as a practice of devotion capable of fostering sustainable, ethical, and compassionate organisational cultures. Keywords: Devotion-based leadership; Indian Knowledge Systems; Narada Bhakti Sutra; leadership development; purpose and meaning; ethical resilience
