The Counterintuitive Role of Social Support in LMX Ambivalence, Burnout and Turnover Intention

OB
Alina Haider (Victoria University of Wellington); Geoff Plimmer (Victoria University)
LMX ambivalence reflects followers’ conflicted thoughts about their relationship with a leader and represents a form of relational uncertainty. Despite growing evidence linking LMX ambivalence to adverse outcomes, little is known about how followers engage in sensemaking of relational uncertainty. Drawing on uncertainty management theory, this research examines the role of social sensemaking through followers’ behavior of support-seeking and its influence on the relationship between LMX ambivalence, burnout, and turnover intention. Using a mixed-methods design, we first test a moderated mediation model in a three-wave time-lagged survey of knowledge workers in India (N=242). Results show that LMX ambivalence is positively related to turnover intention through burnout. Contrary to prevailing assumptions, seeking social support does not buffer this relationship; instead, it amplifies burnout and, consequently, turnover intention. To explain this counterintuitive finding, we conduct a follow-up qualitative study based on interviews (N=19). Thematic analysis reveals that seeking social support functions as a social sensemaking process that can reinforce mistrust, negative affect, and withdrawal through cognitive, affective, and behavioral pathways. Together, these findings advance understanding of how social support functions in practice, how sensemaking occurs in uncertainty-laden relational contexts, and how LMX ambivalence undermines follower well-being and retention.
