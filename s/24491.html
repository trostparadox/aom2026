How and When AI-powered Virtual CEOs Influence Middle Managers’ Precaution Taking

TIM
Yating Hu; Zhe Zhang
With the rise and maturity of artificial intelligence (AI)-powered virtual humans, organizations have begun to introduce virtual CEOs into their operations. The original intention of adopting a virtual CEO is to enhance the organization’s performance. However, it often backfires, especially regarding the negative influence on middle managers’ precaution taking, which is a typical kind of ethical and responsible behavior to safeguard their organizations’ information security in the workplace. With more and more practitioners and scholars calling for responsible AI, it is essential to explore how and when AI-powered virtual CEOs affect middle managers’ precaution taking. This study draws on leader’s function view of a secure base to argue that AI-powered virtual CEOs (vs. human CEOs) may negatively influence middle managers’ precaution taking via decreased psychological safety. We also propose that clan culture strengthens the negative relationship between AI-powered virtual CEOs and middle managers’ precaution taking. Results from two experiments substantiate our claims. Our study echoes the recently warmly called-for research on AI ethics and gives a timely hint to scholars and practitioners that adopting AI-powered virtual CEOs is a positive thing, but in some situations, it may not be as good as it seems, which aims to make efforts to promote responsible AI.
