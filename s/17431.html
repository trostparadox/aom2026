Session overview

The video and board game industries are among the largest and fastest-growing in the world, generating billions of dollars in annual revenues and engaging hundreds of millions of active players across the globe. Case in point is Grand Theft Auto 6, whose development is expected to cost between $1 billion and $2 billion. Beyond their economic magnitude, video and board games provide rich, interactive environments that mirror many of the dynamics central to management research, including competition, innovation, cooperation, and adaptation. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) invites scholars to explore how games can serve as research contexts for advancing theories of strategy, innovation, and organizational theory. From the ancient strategic traditions embedded in Chess and Go to modern board games like Settlers of Catan, board games have long served as laboratories for studying competition, resource allocation, and negotiation. Video games extend these opportunities by offering immersive experiences and vibrant online communities numbering in the millions. Importantly, digital games offer access to granular, high-frequency data and the opportunity to leverage big data tools and techniques due to very large sample sizes. Both video and board games take place in clearly defined and replicable settings, enabling rigorous empirical analysis of strategic and innovative behavior. The workshop aims to create a community of scholars interested in harnessing the potential of games to advance strategy, innovation, and organization theory and test them in novel settings. The workshop is targeted towards anybody who is interested in games as context and those who are using or planning to use game data for their research.
