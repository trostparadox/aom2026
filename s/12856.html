Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly embedding itself in today’s work, yet research has primarily focused on AI adoption and outcomes with less attention to the self-initiated actions employees take. In addition, proactive behavior literature so far predominantly focused on human interaction. This gap leaves us without an integrated understanding of what proactivity becomes when AI changes job demands and resources and introduces new targets for proactive regulation. This presenter symposium brings together five papers that develop and test different views of employees’ proactive behavior with AI. Starting from a meta-analysis that conceptualizes AI as a double-edged job characteristic, the symposium zooms in on four concrete manifestations of proactivity in AI-enabled work: (a) AI-enabled job crafting; (b) proactive signaling under AI-caused job insecurity; (c) feedback-seeking from chatbots as an alternative to manager feedback; and (d) newcomer help seeking from AI versus colleagues as proactive socialization. Across presentations, the symposium highlights how AI influences motives for proactivity, the targets of proactivity and the interpersonal tradeoffs of proactivity with AI. The session concludes with a discussion on a research and practice agenda.
