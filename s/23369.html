Beyond Trade-Offs: Sustainability Orientation as a Legitimacy Buffer for Distinctive Ventures

STR
Kai He (University of Bayreuth); Ricarda B. Bouncken (University of Bayreuth)
Challenging classical trade-off assumptions in optimal distinctiveness theory, we argue that a sustainability orientation functions as a normative legitimacy buffer, redefining the distinctiveness-performance relationship. While distinctiveness typically imposes legitimacy penalties on new ventures, sustainability orientation signals alignment with societal values, appealing to backers' normative evaluations and reducing liabilities of newness. Crucially, sustainability orientation steepens the inverted U-shaped curve: it shields ventures from legitimacy deficits at high distinctiveness while amplifying competitive benefits, elevating peak performance, and enabling synergies over trade-offs. We analyze 27,116 technological Kickstarter campaigns from 2009 to 2023 using computer-aided text analysis, confirming a positive main effect of sustainability orientation on funding performance and the moderating role of sustainability orientation in transforming the optimal distinctiveness curve. Results remain mainly robust across categories (e.g., technology/crafts), as well as across time periods and project characteristics. This study advances theory by revealing sustainability orientation as a legitimacy buffer that reconfigures optimal distinctiveness, offering practical insights for sustainable positioning in platform economies.

Above-Target Environmental Performance and Market Response After Pollution Incidents

STR
Luca Berchicci; Xin Cheng (Renmin University of China); Ruxi Wang (Renmin University of China); Cheng Yin (Tsinghua University)
While exceeding financial performance targets typically garners positive investor responses, the market’s reaction to above-target environmental performance (ATEP)—firms surpassing their self-set pollution reduction goals—remains theoretically ambiguous. This study addresses this gap through an abductive, exploratory analysis of U.S. publicly traded firms from 2007 to 2019. We find investors largely ignore ATEP under routine conditions. However, during pollution incidents, ATEP backfires—firms with higher ATEP face harsher market penalties. The negative effect is amplified for firms held by socially responsible investors and those with executive-certified ATEP disclosures. Additionally, penalized firms subsequently reduce reported ATEP and increase target disclosure opacity. These findings reveal context-dependent investor attention to environmental performance and highlight a dynamic feedback loop between market reactions and corporate environmental governance, offering implications for managers and policymakers.

Do Business Groups Shape Firm-Level Decarbonisation in Indian Manufacturing?

STR
Bhaskar Shandilya (Indian Institute of Management Sambalpur); Pushpesh Pant (Institute of Management Technology Hyderabad); Diptiranjan Mahapatra (Indian Institute of Management Sambalpur)
Climate strategy research typically conceptualises decarbonisation as a firm-level trajectory. However, in emerging economies where diversified business groups (BGs) dominate, group-level net zero pledges can obscure substantial variation in affiliate-level action. This paper develops a theory of translated decarbonisation. We argue that internationalisation generates both disciplining pressures, through cross-border comparability, stakeholder scrutiny, and enforcement, and capability upgrading, through access to technology, learning, and practice transfer. At the same time, BG organisational structure shapes whether these forces translate into measurable emissions reductions or dissipate into symbolic alignment. We theorise three boundary conditions. First, BG affiliation strengthens the relationship between degree of internationalisation (DOI) and degree of decarbonisation (DOD) by enabling pooled capital and coordinated routines. Second, BG network size amplifies this relationship by expanding internal channels for mobilisation and diffusion. Third, BG industry diversification weakens it by increasing information asymmetries, monitoring costs, and intra-group bargaining over long-horizon low-carbon investment. We test this framework on Indian manufacturing firms from 2015 to 2025 by integrating CMIE Prowess data on BG ownership with LSEG operational emissions data. Using firm fixed effects and within-group comparisons under a common group apex, we isolate translation effects. Conceptually and empirically, the analysis shows that heterogeneity in decarbonisation outcomes reflects organisational design and internal resource allocation logics, not only average firm responses to global climate pressures. In doing so, the study reframes debates on globalisation and climate performance by locating a meaningful share of variation in intra-group governance of decarbonisation rather than in international exposure alone.

The Green Path to Export Competitiveness: Strategic Resource Allocation in Emerging Markets

STR
Ruosu GAO (The University of Sydney)
This study explores the relationship between environmental expenditure and export growth among Indian manufacturing firms. As environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations gain prominence, firms face growing pressure to invest in sustainability initiatives to comply with regulations and sustain competitiveness. Using data from listed Indian manufacturing firms between 2003 and 2023, we investigate how environmental spending influences export performance. Our findings indicate a U-shaped relationship. Initially, increases in environmental expenditure may hinder export growth by diverting finite resources from core market activities, but beyond a certain threshold, these investments foster dynamic capabilities in resource management, leading to improved export intensity. Further, foreign ownership moderates this relationship by providing firms with greater resources and capabilities, thereby flattening the U-shaped curve. This study contributes to the resource-based view (RBV) by empirically demonstrating how resource-constrained firms in emerging markets navigate the trade-offs of sustainability investments, emphasizing the non-linear path firms face in balancing these investments with international competitiveness. The results highlight the importance of strategic resource management and dynamic capabilities in aligning environmental commitments with export growth over the long term.

Credibility, Execution Risk, and Governance While Sequencing Sustainability as Strategy

STR
ANN MARY VARGHESE
Firms increasingly make highly visible sustainability commitments, yet many struggle to translate these commitments into reliable execution. This challenge is especially acute when sustainability initiatives intersect with mission-critical services and depend on complex external ecosystems. While prior strategic management research has emphasized the signaling and legitimacy benefits of sustainability ambition, it has paid limited attention to how public commitments reshape execution risk, constrain strategic choice, and alter governance requirements under conditions of interdependence. This paper develops a conceptual, process-oriented framework that theorizes sustainability transitions as a corporate strategy execution problem rather than a purely environmental initiative. Integrating insights from institutional theory, signaling theory, resource dependence theory, ecosystem strategy, dynamic capabilities, and innovation research, the paper explains how public commitments escalate expectations, increase exposure to ecosystem bottlenecks, and create credibility risks when implementation lags. Using the electrification of student transportation as a motivating context, the framework advances propositions explaining how firms govern large-scale strategic transitions through sequencing, readiness-gated deployment, and conditional commitments. The paper contributes to strategic management theory by showing how disciplined sequencing functions as a governance mechanism that preserves credibility, manages execution risk, and enables firms to pursue long-term strategic transformation without undermining operational reliability or stakeholder trust.
