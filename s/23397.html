Symbolic or Substantive? Sustainability Committee and ESG Controversies

ONE
Ammar Ali Gull (Léonard de Vinci Business School); Tanveer Ahsan (Rennes School of Business); Muhammad Azeem Qureshi
The emergence of board committees prompts a fundamental normative question into whether their existence is merely symbolic or genuinely substantive. To this end, this paper investigates the impact of the presence and composition of a sustainability committee on ESG-related controversies. Drawing on legitimacy theory and normative stakeholder theory, we propose that the existence of a sustainability committee, inherently guided by ethical orientation, would help firms manage their ESG controversies. Using a sample of listed firms drawn from 38 countries, we found counter-intuitive results that the presence of a sustainability committee is associated with an increase in ESG controversies. Further analysis demonstrates that ESG performance-based compensation weakens the sustainability committee and ESG controversies erratic relationship. Concerning the committee composition, we note that the longer tenure of the sustainability committee members helps firms reduce ESG controversies, supporting the ‘expertise-hypothesis’. The results remain robust to several subsample analyses and identification strategies and offer important research, managerial, and ethical implications.

Defensive vs. Aggressive Responses to ESG Disagreement: Asymmetric Effects on R&D and Tax Avoidance

ONE
Wenzhuo Guo; Du Yongzhi (Harbin Institute of Technology)
This study examines how ESG rating disagreement (ERD) reshapes corporate strategic allocation between research and development (R&D) and tax avoidance, conceptualizing these choices through the lens of defensive versus aggressive strategic responses. Using a panel sample of 4,138 Chinese A-share firms from 2018 to 2023, we find that ERD significantly reduces R&D investment and raises tax avoidance. The magnitude of the R&D contraction exceeds the increase in tax avoidance, revealing an asymmetry between defensive–aggressive strategies. Further analyses show that managerial myopia and economic policy uncertainty both intensify ERD’s impact on each strategy and widen the asymmetry effect between them. By exploring the asymmetric effect of ERD on firms’ R&D and tax avoidance, the study clarifies how firms respond to ERD by a trade-off between defensive and aggressive strategies.

Mandatory ESG Disclosure Regulations' Deterrent Effect on Corporate ESG Incidents: A Global Study

ONE
Jan Hohberger (ESADE Business School - Ramon Llull University); Dimitri Simonin (University of Neuchâtel); Emmanuelle Reuter (University of Neuchâtel)
While mandatory information disclosure regulations, such as Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) disclosure regulations, seek to modify organizations’ behaviors, little is known about the role of institutional heterogeneity in reducing real-risk outcomes. We study the deterrent effect of mandatory disclosure regulations on the risk of corporate incidents, and the role of three regulatory institutional contingencies -types of regulatory authority (public or private), regulatory logic (coercive or normative), regulatory institutional environment (strong or weak)- in either strengthening or weakening this relationship. We test our hypotheses using a comprehensive global panel of firm-level ESG incidents following the introduction of mandatory ESG disclosure regulations across 38 countries that require organizations to disclose ESG activities that pose risk. By developing novel arguments that blend deterrence with information asymmetry, we seek to make key contributions to literature on firm responses to institutional pressures, information disclosure, and sustainability governance and derive implications for policymaking.

Dressing Up for the Wedding: Acquirors’ Short-Term ESG Score Management as a Legitimacy Seeking

ONE
Xinyu (Celia) Yang (Tsinghua University); Qianzhi Wei (Tsinghua University)
This study investigates how firms strategically manage their Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) score as a cost-effective tool for risk mitigation and stakeholder alignment during the announcement of corporate acquisition. Under conditions of heightened legitimacy pressure, firms increasingly employ ESG activities as a form of strategic communication—reliable, visible, and relatively low-cost. Drawing on a dataset of 3,955 acquisitions by 2,933 publicly listed firms in China from 2018 to 2022, we document a significant abnormal increase in ESG scores around acquisition announcements. This ESG enhancement is shaped by both internal and external legitimacy pressures. Using monthly ESG data, we examine the determinants of these abnormal score changes and identify two key drivers: lower divergence among ESG ratings and stronger corporate commitment to environmental and social goals are both associated with greater pre-acquisition ESG score management. Furthermore, the presence of a sustainability committee moderates these effects—mitigating the negative influence of rating divergence while amplifying the positive impact of internal ESG commitment. Our findings highlight ESG management as a strategic, legitimacy-driven behavior in the context of high-stakes corporate events.

ESG and Firm Performance: The Mediating Roles of Green Innovation and Accounting Conservatism

ONE
Ranran Li (Yanshan University); Ruicong Lu (Yanshan University); Jinghui Tang (Yanshan University); Yan Wang (Nottingham Trent University)
This study investigates the relationship between environmental, social and governance (ESG) performance and corporate financial performance, focusing on the mediating roles of green innovation and accounting conservatism. Using a panel dataset of Chinese listed companies from 2010 to 2023, we demonstrate that ESG performance significantly improves financial performance. Firms with strong ESG performance are more effective in enhancing corporate value through two mediating channels: the stimulation of green innovation and the reinforcement of accounting conservatism. The results remain robust across a range of robustness tests, including alternative variable specifications, two-way fixed effects models and Difference-in-Differences (DID) model. Furthermore, heterogeneity analyses reveal that the positive effect of ESG performance is more pronounced among non-state-owned enterprises (non-SOEs), non-polluting firms and smaller-sized firms. By uncovering these dynamics, the study offers valuable insights for companies aiming to integrate ESG principles into their operations, highlighting how such practices can drive both financial and sustainable development goals.
