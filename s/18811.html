Behavioral Phases Shape Mobility Innovation Needs: Evidence from Sustainable Commuting

Authors: Maris GAILIS Riga Technical University; Inguna Jurgelane-Kaldava Riga Technical University

Session overview

Urban mobility transitions depend on individuals’ willingness to adopt sustainable commuting practices, yet strong pro-environmental intentions often fail to translate into observable behavior. This poster examines the intention behavior gap by integrating behavioral action phase theory with organizational responses to mobility innovation. Drawing on the Rubicon Model of Action Phases and the COM B framework, the study analyzes how perceived barriers and transport modality relate to intention formation and behavioral enactment. Empirical evidence is drawn from a survey of university students and staff with 2,192 respondents. Using PLS-SEM, a two-stage model distinguishes between pre actional volitional planning and actional behavioral execution. Results show that individuals with stronger sustainable commuting intentions report higher salience of multiple perceived barriers, particularly commuting time, financial cost, and public transport quality. This pattern reflects cognitive engagement during planning rather than resistance to change. In contrast, only a limited subset of barriers, notably commuting time, electric vehicle accessibility, and habitual behavior, directly constrain sustainable commuting behavior in the actional phase. Transport modality is positively associated with sustainable commuting behavior but does not significantly moderate the intention behavior relationship. These findings indicate that barrier salience signals active goal engagement rather than behavioral inertia. The study contributes to discovery-oriented research by showing that sustainable mobility behavior is shaped by phase specific mechanisms rather than uniform determinants. For organizations and mobility authorities, the results highlight the importance of aligning innovation strategies with behavioral phases, emphasizing motivational and informational interventions during pre-actional stages and infrastructural and operational improvements during actional stages.
