Identity-Based Transition Pathways in GenAI Implementations

Authors: Sam Senanayake ESCP Business School; Petros Chamakiotis ESCP Business School

Session overview

With the pervasive adoption of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in the workplace, practitioners may need to rethink in previously unimagined ways how they accomplish their organizational tasks. Recent literature has identified Information Technology Identity (ITID) archetypes which capture different reactions to Information Systems (IS) adoption. We expand this literature by examining the process through which ITID archetype changes occur in the context of GenAI implementations. We do so by focusing on three case organizations and interviewing 27 practitioners twice (before and after a GenAI implementation), totaling 54 interviews. Our analysis reveals three distinct transition pathways through which workers shift between archetypal positions, suggesting that successful GenAI adoption occurs when the following are present: (a) cognitive restructuring, (b) organizational sensegiving; and (c) boundary-spanning adoption. These findings contribute a process-based model for understanding and supporting GenAI adoption, with actionable insights on when and how organizational interventions can shift adoption trajectories toward sustained technology integration and organizational value creation.
