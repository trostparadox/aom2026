A Slam Dunk or a Step Back? Performance Effects of Temporary Downward Reassignments

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Cyrus Khalvati University of Missouri-Columbia; Xuejiao (Shelly) Zheng University of Missouri-Columbia; Rysa Khalvati The University of British Columbia

Session overview

Temporary downward reassignments are becoming increasingly common in organizations, yet research has rarely examined them as a tool for improving job performance. We develop a learning-based framework that conceptualizes temporary downward reassignment as a form of internal mobility that preserves core job content while reducing performance pressure and evaluative scrutiny. This combination creates a setting in which employees continue working on closely related tasks to their original role, enabling them to strengthen task-specific skills and build human capital that enhances performance. We propose that performance improves after reinstatement to the original role and that age attenuates this improvement. We evaluate these predictions in professional basketball, where players are frequently reassigned from the NBA to the NBA G League following substandard performance and subsequently reinstated. Our results demonstrate that temporary downward reassignments can serve as an effective tool for correcting underperformance in organizations.
