Sustaining Employee Well-Being in Digital Transformation: The Role of Leadership and Self-Efficacy

TIM
Samuel Gyamerah (RMIT University); Leila Afshari (RMIT University); Samuel Asante Gyamerah (Toronto Metropolitan University); Enock Mintah Ampaw (Koforidua Technical University)
As digital transformation (DT) accelerates, employees increasingly confront technological changes that can either support thriving or lead to psychological strain. This study examines how DT shapes employee well-being in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and identifies conditions under which employees are more likely to thrive rather than merely survive technological change. Drawing on self-determination theory and the challenge–hindrance stressor framework, we develop a curvilinear model proposing that DT has both enabling and depleting effects on employee well-being, depending on its intensity. Using two-wave survey data from 342 employees across 121 Ghanaian SMEs, we find support for an inverted U-shaped relationship between DT and employee well-being. Moderate levels of transformation are associated with higher well-being, whereas intensive transformation predicts declining well-being. Perceived leadership support and digital self-efficacy each buffer these negative effects, with their joint presence offering the strongest protection. The findings clarify when DT promotes thriving and highlight how leadership and employee agency shape well-being in technologically transforming SMEs.
