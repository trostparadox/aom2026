Transformational, Authentic, Shared? How Female and Male Entrepreneurs See Their Own Leadership

ENT
Veronika Kneip (Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences); Melanie Slavici (Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences)
The literature frequently links entrepreneurial leadership to agentic characteristics aligned with traditional male stereotypes, while also framing it as closely related to transformational, shared, or authentic leadership styles that emphasize communal characteristics commonly associated with female stereotypes. Reflecting this theoretical tension and the largely inconclusive evidence in prior research, this study examines entrepreneurial leadership from a self-perception perspective. We ask which leadership traits and styles male and female entrepreneurs attribute to themselves and whether gender-related patterns can be identified in these self-images. The empirical analysis is based on qualitative data from 30 technology-oriented startups, including nine female-led, ten male-led, and eleven mixed-gender founding teams. Applying the Gioia methodology, we inductively derive first-order concepts, second-order themes, and aggregate dimensions related to leadership traits and leadership styles. This approach enables a fine-grained understanding of how entrepreneurial leaders perceive and articulate their leadership roles. With regard to leadership traits, cooperation, inspiration, and self-regulation emerge as central characteristics across male and female founders. Although some references to stereotypical notions of femininity and masculinity are present, they do not dominate entrepreneurs’ self-perceptions. In particular, the widely cited association summarized as “think entrepreneurial leadership—think male” is not reflected in the self-image of the founders in our sample. In terms of leadership styles, we identify shared leadership and strong team orientation as core elements of entrepreneurial leadership regardless of gender. Beyond these established styles, the study recognizes human-centered leadership as a third, previously underexplored dimension in entrepreneurial leadership research. Our findings show that female founders play a significant role in shaping and promoting this leadership style, both in all-female and mixed-gender teams. Human-centered leadership seems to be a valuable asset, contributing to founders’ well-being and enhancing the long-term success prospects of startups. Overall, this study contributes to the discourse on gender and entrepreneurship by offering gender-sensitive empirical insights into entrepreneurial leadership. By challenging stereotypical assumptions and extending existing leadership frameworks, it fosters a more nuanced understanding of leadership in entrepreneurial contexts.
