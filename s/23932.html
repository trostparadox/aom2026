Knowledge Co-Creation by Human–Machine Social Interaction: Based on Decision Field Theory

TIM
Xin Shen (Wenzhou University); Ying Yi (Wenzhou University); Zongqiang Ren (Wenzhou University)
Generative AI is reshaping the paradigm of knowledge creation from mono-creation of human beings to human–machine co-creation. However, cognitive differences, fragmented interactions and lack of contextual resonance between humans and machines prevent classical anthropocentric knowledge theories from explaining knowledge co-creation by heterogeneous agents. AI strengthens its human-like centrality as such technology becomes increasingly embedded and embodied. Knowledge co-creation through socialised human–AI interaction is expected to offer safer and more efficient solutions to complex problems, thereby advancing augmented intelligence. This study aims, through descriptive analysis and scientific mapping techniques, to integrates decision field theory and Nonaka’s socialisation–externalisation–combination–internalisation (SECI) model to demonstrate the human–machine social interaction for knowledge co-creation.
