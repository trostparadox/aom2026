Session overview

The Financial Times list of “50 journals used in FT Research Rank” has become one of the most powerful reference points in business and management research. It now shapes how business schools are evaluated and how scholars plan their careers across the world. Being on the FT50 list has concrete consequences, because it can influence promotion and tenure decisions, the allocation of internal resources, and the perceived status of institutions in global rankings. However, there is still considerable debate about whether this list genuinely reflects research excellence and broader societal impact. Taking the FT50 list and the Financial Times article that formalized it as a starting point, this paper provides an integrative and critical examination of the FT50 as a proxy for research quality, with particular relevance for readers of the Journal of Documentation and Research Policy.
