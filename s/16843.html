Session overview

Recent policy-driven disruptions of critical input materials have created new challenges for forecasting and inventory management in global supply chains. In response to United States (US) tariffs, China announced new export control measures on rare earth elements (REEs) in February 2025, affecting upstream inputs essential for semiconductor manufacturing. This policy shock provides a timely empirical setting for studying how firms adjust demand forecasts, inventory policies, and post-shock recovery expectations under sudden supply-side uncertainty. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is a tool-oriented and interactive session that introduces a counterfactual research framework treating export controls and critical mineral restrictions as exogenous forced supply chain de-scaling shocks. The workshop illustrates how researchers can measure short-term operational losses, medium-term buffering responses, and long-term recovery and forecastability using publicly available firm-level data. Participants engage in guided demonstrations, breakout discussions, and template-based exercises, and leave with (i) a replicable research design template for studying upstream input shocks, (ii) operational measures linking forecasting uncertainty, inventory policy, and recovery dynamics, and (iii) guidance on implementing dynamic empirical designs using publicly available data. As a focused extension, the PDW introduces AI-assisted forecasting and text-based tools to extract recovery-related signals from firm disclosures and assess long-run forecastability and persistent uncertainty. While motivated by rare earth export controls, the tools developed here apply broadly to supply chain disruptions involving critical materials and strategic resources.
