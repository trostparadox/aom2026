Session overview

This symposium examines how artificial intelligence is reshaping competition, innovation, and global organization under growing geopolitical constraint. While generative AI is driving technological convergence through highly concentrated infrastructures, states are simultaneously fragmenting the global technology landscape through regulation, data sovereignty, and security-driven industrial policy. Firms therefore face a central tension between ecosystem-level dependence and geopolitical decoupling. Panelists in this symposium approach this tension from complementary perspectives. Cennamo analyzes how reliance on concentrated GenAI infrastructures induces strategic convergence across firms and ecosystems. Nambisan examines how AI-driven geopolitical dynamics reshape managerial agency, learning, and interaction within multinational enterprises. Sokol investigates how AI regulation moderates technological shocks to entrepreneurial finance, drawing evidence from China. Verbeke explores how MNEs adapt their strategies and governance to a multipolar world characterized by regulatory divergence and technological bifurcation. Zahra situates these dynamics within a broader transformation of innovation systems, arguing that AI increasingly functions as an instrument of geopolitical power rather than purely economic growth. Together, the symposium advances understanding of how geopolitics, regulation, and technological infrastructure jointly shape firm strategy, innovation, and global organization in the age of AI.
