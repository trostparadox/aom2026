Unemployment Across 60 Countries: Affective Distress is Universal, Pain is Not

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

People

Authors: Lisa E. Baranik University at Albany, State University of New York; Dev K Dalal University at Albany-State University of New York; Brandon Gorman

Session overview

Unemployment is a well-known stressor linked to poor employee health outcomes. While prior research has consistently shown associations between unemployment and affective distress, less is known about whether unemployment also predicts other health symptoms, particularly those associated with central sensitization, such as sleep disturbance, pain, cognitive dysfunction, and fatigue. In this study, we examine the relationship between unemployment and SPACE symptoms (Sleep, Pain, Affective distress, Cognitive difficulties, and low Energy) across 60 countries using data from the World Health Survey (N = 149,260). We first assess whether the structure of SPACE symptoms is consistent across countries using network psychometrics. Results reveal a consistent four-factor structure, with S and E loading together, yielding four dimensions: S/E, P, A, and C. We then estimate multilevel models (MLMs) to examine the effects of unemployment on each outcome and whether these effects vary cross-nationally. Unemployment significantly predicted A on average; however, the other outcomes were less consistent, especially P. These findings suggest that while affective distress is a common consequence of unemployment globally, other symptoms emerge more selectively depending on national context. Our results provide empirical support for a hierarchy of stress responses, where affective symptoms are more proximal and universal, and somatic symptoms more context-dependent. These findings offer theoretical and practical implications for how institutions and employers might better support unemployed individuals across diverse global settings.
