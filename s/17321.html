Resilience as Orchestration: How Ventures Respond to AI-Native Failure- and Capability-Shocks

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6
ENT

People

Authors: Yuki Xueqi Yuan; Jingwei Ma Imperial College London

Session overview

Resilience research has largely theorised shocks as adverse disruptions, locating resilience within organisational routines or technical infrastructures. Yet, AI-native entrepreneurial ventures increasingly operate through external, fast-moving AI infrastructures (e.g., model APIs, toolchains, and proprietary platforms) that generate two qualitatively different classes of shocks: (1) failure shocks (e.g., outages, regressions, policy changes) that threaten continuity, and (2) capability shocks (e.g., major model releases) that expand what is possible but destabilise workflows, architectures, and value propositions. Drawing on a longitudinal, multi-case study of 13 London-based AI-native entrepreneurial ventures (small business with less than 50 people and established less than 3 years), we develop a Dual-Process Model of Resilience-as-Orchestration. We show how founders and technical leads govern resilience by shifting between homeostatic resilience (restoring operational coherence after failure shocks) and transformative resilience (reconfiguring socio-technical systems to capture and survive capability shocks). Critically, this orchestration produces a distributional “price”: resilience at the venture level is secured through intensified founder attention, cognitive strain, and change fatigue. The study extends resilience theory by (a) reconceptualising innovation-driven capability upgrades as shocks, (b) theorising resilience as agentic orchestration in human-AI systems, and (c) identifying the micro-level costs through which resilience is produced.
