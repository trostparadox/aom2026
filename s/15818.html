A Couple Shared Reality Perspective on Female Employees’ Family Work Conflict

Authors: Joy Obioma Ibedionu The University of Texas at Arlington; Ariane Froidevaux The University of Texas at Arlington; Wendy J. Casper The University of Texas at Arlington; Lars Uriah Johnson The University of Texas at Arlington

Session overview

Over the years, although women’s participation in the labor force has increased steadily, they continue to assume the primary responsibility for household management. Hence, women in dual-earner couples often juggle the demands of both home and work roles, thereby experiencing increased family-work conflict (FWC). In this study, we develop and investigate a model of heterosexual dual-earner couples’ perceptions of congruence regarding female employees’ FWC. Building on and extending shared reality and spill-over theories, we argue that couples’ congruence perceptions regarding female employees’ FWC have downstream effects on their proximal and distal outcomes, namely, interpersonal conflict at work and physical health symptoms, respectively. We find support for our model with a dataset of 224 heterosexual dual-earner couples recruited across the United States, which was analyzed using polynomial regression with response surface analysis. Specifically, our results show that couples’ congruence on female partners’ FWC initially increases their interpersonal conflict at work. However, at higher levels of congruence, this relationship becomes negative, resulting in lower interpersonal conflict at work. Lastly, we found an indirect relationship between couple congruence on female partners’ FWC and their physical health symptoms, such that as couples align, the female partner’s interpersonal conflict at work declines and subsequently her physical health symptoms.
