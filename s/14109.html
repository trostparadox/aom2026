Circular Innovation: Old Wine in New Bottles or New Paradigm? An Integrative Literature Review

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5
TIM

People

Authors: Marcelo Francisco De La Cruz Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich; Nadine Gurtner Bern University of Applied Sciences

Session overview

The circular economy (CE) has gained traction across scholarship, practice, and policy makers. Yet, its scope ranges from macro-level visions of societal transformation to micro-level operational practices, creating definitional and conceptual ambiguity about innovation and its role in CE. We address this challenge by asking: What is circular innovation, how does it differ from adjacent notions, what theoretical foundations and mechanisms underpin it, and which avenues should future research pursue? To answer our research questions, we conduct an integrative literature review of 231 articles that critically synthesizes a representative body of evidence on circular innovation across streams of literature. Our preliminary results suggest circular innovation is configurational, combining innovation principles (e.g., minimize resource use) and multiple innovation approaches (e.g., open innovation) within an intervention system that comprises activities, actors, and structures that create, deliver, and capture value at the micro level (e.g., product design), meso level (e.g., business models), and macro level (e.g., supply chains). We also derive a research agenda around four major areas: (1) circular innovation as a configurational, multi-level phenomenon, (2) technological and digital enablers of circularity, (3) circular entrepreneurship, and (4) anticipatory and systems-thinking capabilities in circular innovation. Our integrative review advances the field by providing a clear, theoretically grounded concept that establishes a shared grammar for cumulative theory building. We articulate an innovation perspective fully aligned with circular principles and synthesize dispersed research streams into a unifying model and research agenda that reshape how circular innovation is understood and studied.
