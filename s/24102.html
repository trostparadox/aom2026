Multinational Enterprises as Ceremonial Intermediaries for Energy Transitions

IM
Huong Lan Hoang (University College Dublin); Shuna Shu Ham Ho (University College Dublin)
In the global energy transition, transition intermediaries – actors that connect stakeholders, mobilise resources, and support innovation diffusion to support the sustainable transition – play a pivotal role. While multinational enterprises (MNEs) possess the scale, technological capacity, and global reach to act as transition intermediaries, their effectiveness remains uncertain due to both internal constraints and external barriers. This study integrates institutional theory with the transition intermediary concept to examine the degree to which MNEs act as intermediaries that transmit home-country climate policy pressures abroad. Using the European Union (EU) Emission Emissions Trading (ETS) System as a quasi-natural experiment, we apply a dynamic difference-in-differences design to a panel of MNEs and their foreign suppliers with 7,556 firm-year observations from 2006 to 2020 across 40 countries. We find that home-country policy pressures transmitted through MNEs generate measurable yet temporary decarbonisation spillovers among suppliers, thereby extending the notion of ceremonial adoption to MNE–supplier relationships and introducing temporal dimension to it. The effect is strongest where host-country transition readiness is low and suppliers’ absorptive capacity is limited. The results highlight that MNEs can extend domestic policy reach beyond borders, yet relying on MNEs in national energy transition is a short-term strategy with selective applicability. Instead, policy should enhance national energy transition readiness, improve sustainability commitment and capabilities of local firms.
