Author: Minseok Kim Temple University

Session overview

This study examines how alignment between preferred and actual disclosure of financial idiosyncratic deals (i-deals)—conceptualized as disclosure fit— affects turnover intention. Drawing on person–environment fit and self-determination theories, this study propose that disclosure fit enhances autonomy need satisfaction, thereby reducing turnover intention. Integrating prospect theory, this study argues that psychological ownership strengthens reactions to disclosure fit. Using a three-wave survey of employees with financial i-deals, this study tests a moderated mediation model. The study contributes by shifting attention from coworkers to recipients and by identifying disclosure fit as a key dimension of i-deals communication.
