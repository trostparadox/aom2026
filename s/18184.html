Session overview

This study examines the gendered dynamics of precarious labor in South Korea, a context largely underexplored in international scholarship despite its unique sociocultural and economic characteristics. Specifically, the paper demonstrates how South Korea’s patriarchal norms, deeply embedded in Confucian ideologies, shape and perpetuate women’s exposure to precarious employment even as their labor force participation rises. Challenging human capital theory, this study identifies a paradox in which South Korean women, despite having higher educational attainment, disproportionately experience precarious labor. Employing a multidimensional framework based on hedonic weighting, we define and operationalize precarious labor through eight distinct configurations that capture intersecting insecurities in employment, wages, and social protection. Using longitudinal panel data, an analysis of occupational stratification further reveals gendered patters of precarity across occupational strata, highlighting the systemic devaluation of women’s human capital. The study also investigates how individual characteristics, such as age, education, and marital and parental status, differentially influence precarious labor by gender. Overall, this research contributes to scholarship on gender and precarious labor by contextualizing economic disparities within South Korea’s unique sociocultural regime.
