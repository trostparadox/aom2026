Wage Theft in the Platform Economy? A Study on Ridehailing Sector

OSCM
Souradeep Koley (Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee); Arnab Bisi (Johns Hopkins University); Mukesh Barua (Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee)
There has been a dramatic upsurge in the platform-based economy, where workers interact with algorithmic management systems to perform various tasks. While these organizations provide employment in the form of ‘independent contractors,’ reports outline that these platforms indulge in various forms of wage theft without having direct contact with workers. Taking a multi-methodological approach with the ridehailing sector as a case study, we conduct a qualitative study in the first phase, involving interviews with 32 drivers, cab unions, and firm managers, to develop a hypothesis using the inductive theory approach. Following this, a quantitative empirical analysis was performed in the second phase based on the weekly financial statements of 704 drivers. Our findings highlight that the variability in commission is attributed to the platform’s algorithmic management systems (AMS) that dynamically adjust commission based on drivers’ prior trip modalities, such as customer payment methods. The actual commission, tax, and total percentage deduction by the firm are 12%, 8%, and 20%, respectively. Our study makes novel contributions to the intersection of the digital labor market, algorithmic management systems, and on-demand transportation. Additionally, we offer actionable implications for firms’ managers and policymakers in the ridehailing ecosystem.
