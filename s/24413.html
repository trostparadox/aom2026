Does Government AI Attention Foster Corporate AI Innovation: Evidence from Chinese Listed Firms

PNP
Huimin Zhou (Central South University)
How governments steer the development of emerging general-purpose technologies through soft governance mechanisms remains insufficiently understood. Drawing on attention-based theory, this study examines how government attention to artificial intelligence (AI) influences firm-level AI innovation. We conceptualize government AI attention as a form of agenda-setting that signals policy priorities to market actors and shapes their behavioral responses. Using panel data on Chinese A-share listed firms from 2010 to 2023, we find that higher levels of government AI attention significantly promote firms’ AI innovation outcomes. Mechanism analyses show that this effect operates through firms’ internal AI attention, suggesting that government agenda-setting affects corporate behavior by influencing how firms allocate attention to emerging technologies. Moreover, the impact of government AI attention is contingent on executive characteristics. The positive effect is strengthened when firms have a higher proportion of executives with digital backgrounds or stronger compensation incentives, but weakened when the proportion of female executives is higher. Additional analyses indicate that the effect is more pronounced among growth-stage firms and firms located in regions with stronger innovation infrastructures. This study contributes to public and nonprofit management research by demonstrating that government attention functions as a soft governance tool shaping firm behavior beyond formal regulations. It further advances agenda-setting research by identifying a multilevel attention transmission mechanism linking public policy priorities to firm-level innovation outcomes in the context of AI governance.
