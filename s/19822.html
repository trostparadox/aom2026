An Experimental Study on the Role of Literacy in Human-AI Interactions

Authors: Moritz T. Bruckner University of Augsburg; Felix Büsching University of Augsburg; Julian Michael Stöcklein University of Augsburg; Daniel J Veit University of Augsburg

Session overview

Information technology (IT) has an enduring effect on transforming work routines, role profiles, and technology use. Those factors are once again fundamentally transformed by the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into organizational processes. As a result, competencies expected from employees without any IT background, so-called non-experts, are shifting, extending far beyond traditional concepts of digital literacy. At the same time, individuals integrating IT capabilities in their identity formation process is an accompanying phenomenon. Consequently, when identities are being threatened, effects are known to be severe. This study examines the interaction of AI literacy in a professional context and analyzes the extent to which literacy is suitable in mitigating psychological burdens on individuals from IT identity threats. In this study, we employed a quantitative research approach with 184 participants. Results indicate that AI literacy serves as a significant protective factor in reducing perceived identity-related threats. Moreover, AI literacy increases the level of AI self-management, which nevertheless does not mediate the effect on IT identity threat. With this investigation, we contribute to the ongoing theoretical development of the AI literacy construct and introduce a novel, interdisciplinary perspective by connecting it with IT identity. For managers, to reduce fears of AI we promote educational initiatives for critical reflection, emotional self-regulation and ethical awareness alongside the technical training of employees.
