Empower or Constrain: How AI Affordance Shapes Firm Innovation Distinctiveness

TIM
Jingwen Liang (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Yawen Li (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Mengyu Zhuang (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a key general-purpose technology reshaping industry competition. Although frequent AI algorithmic breakthroughs occur at the technical level, firms' AI innovations at the application level exhibit significant convergence, and prior research remains inconclusive regarding whether these breakthroughs effectively enhance firm-level innovation distinctiveness. Drawing on Optimal Distinctiveness Theory and Technology Affordance Theory, this study constructs "artificial intelligence affordance" using new model release events at the algorithmic level. Using data from Chinese firms with AI patent accumulation between 2013 and 2024, this study systematically examines the nonlinear effect of AI affordance on innovation distinctiveness. The study further investigates the moderating roles of two key technological ecosystem features: technological open-sourcing and commercialization. The results indicate a significant inverted-U relationship between AI affordance and innovation distinctiveness, while open-sourcing and commercialization both attenuate this inverted-U effect, rendering the overall influence of AI affordance on innovation distinctiveness more moderate. By adopting a technology affordance perspective, this study reconciles conflicting findings on AI breakthroughs' impact on innovation distinctiveness, extends Optimal Distinctiveness Theory to fast-evolving technological contexts, and provides guidance for firms to seize technological windows and sustain long-term innovation distinctiveness in AI ecosystems.
