Session overview

Workplace mistreatment remains a persistent and costly challenge for organizations, warranting sustained scholarly attention and dialogue. This symposium marks the third consecutive year devoted to this important topic. In this iteration, we advance a perpetrator-centered perspective by examining why and how employees or leaders engage in workplace mistreatment across diverse contexts and motivations. Building on a growing body of work, the symposium brings together five papers that investigate varied antecedents of mistreatment enactment, including leaders’ experiences of ostracism, employees’ appraisals of coworkers’ goal sharing, the interpersonal consequences of holding an underdog mindset, the emergence of malicious compliance as a rule-abiding form of resistance, and motivational orientations toward undermining high-performing peers. Drawing on experience-sampling, field experiments, laboratory experiments, and qualitative methods, these studies illuminate both maladaptive and adaptive pathways through which mistreatment arises. By shifting attention from victim characteristics to perpetrators’ motives, emotional processes, and situational constraints, this symposium advances theoretical understanding of workplace mistreatment and offers actionable insights for managing and preventing harmful interpersonal behavior in organizations.
