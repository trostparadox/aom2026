From Knowledge Sharing to Performance: Innovativeness as Mechanism, Centralization as Constraint

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12

Authors: Nadja Laban; Sonja Muratovic University of Montenegro, Faculty of Economics; Renata Grgurevic; Jovana Cabarkapa University of Montenegro

Session overview

Knowledge sharing is widely portrayed as a key driver of organizational performance, yet organizations often experience uneven returns from initiatives designed to stimulate sharing. Integrating the knowledge-based view with organizational design arguments, we propose that knowledge sharing contributes to perceived organizational performance primarily by fostering organizational innovativeness, and that this mechanism depends on organizational centralization. When decision authority and communication are concentrated at higher hierarchical levels, employees may have fewer opportunities to experiment, adapt ideas locally, and implement improvements, weakening the extent to which shared knowledge is transformed into realized innovation. We examine this conditional process using probability-based survey data from a representative sample of 1,016 employed individuals across public and private sector organizations in Montenegro. To test our model, we estimate a moderated mediation framework linking knowledge sharing to performance through innovativeness, while accounting for key demographic and work-related controls. Findings align with the theorized process: knowledge sharing is associated with greater innovativeness, innovativeness is associated with higher perceived performance, and centralization functions as a meaningful boundary condition that dampens the conversion of shared knowledge into innovation. These insights contribute to research on knowledge management by specifying a structural contingency for when knowledge sharing “pays off,” and they suggest that organizations may realize stronger benefits from knowledge sharing when supported by structures that enable discretion, responsiveness, and bottom-up implementation.
