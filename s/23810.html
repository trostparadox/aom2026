The Double-Edged Effects of Daily Unfinished Tasks on Next-Day Speed of Engagement

OB
Junchao Huang (Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences); Waiseng Lou (Peking University); Yongjuan Li; Yaoshan Xu
Employees often leave work with unfinished tasks—tasks they intended to finish but remained incomplete at the end of the workday. While prior research has primarily examined how unfinished tasks impair employees’ nonwork experiences, we propose that these tasks may have double-edged effects on subsequent work functioning. Integrating the Zeigarnik effect with cognitive theories of rumination, we hypothesize that daily unfinished tasks trigger evening affect-focused rumination, which then influences next-day speed of morning engagement through two distinct mechanisms: by amplifying morning negative affect, which decreases speed of engagement, and by increasing morning anticipated workload, which in turn enhances speed of engagement. Results from a 10-day experience sampling study provide support for our hypotheses. Our findings challenge the unilateral negative view of unfinished tasks by revealing their potential motivational benefits for work, providing a more balanced understanding of this common workplace phenomenon.
