Session overview

The PDW will highlight applications of computational methods including natural language processing, image analysis, and agent-based modeling to answer theoretical questions germane to management and organizations. Across diverse empirical settings including online microfinance marketplaces, visual art markets, historical cultural production, and evaluations of cultural products the papers demonstrate how large-scale text and image data can be leveraged to operationalize complex social and cultural concepts that have traditionally resisted systematic measurement. The presentations illustrate complementary strategies for moving from unstructured data to theoretically meaningful constructs: embedding-based representations of justificatory logics and evaluative criteria; image-based measures of product distinctiveness grounded in visual content rather than categorical labels; and agent-based and generative models that capture the tension between conformity and differentiation in cultural production.The PDW speaks to key themes across several AOM divisions. In their presentations and subsequent Q&A with the audiences, the panelists will share their insights on methodological best practices, the role of computational techniques in advancing theory and their experiences going through the review process.
