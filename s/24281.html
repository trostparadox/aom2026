Sustaining Calling Amid Digitalization: The Intersection of Paradoxical Leadership and Technostress

ODC
Hang Ngoc Dieu Doan (National Sun Yat-Sen University); Hao-Chieh Lin (National Sun Yat-Sen University)
The accelerating utilization of artificial intelligence and social media has transformed how people work on the one hand but cast doubt on how they envisage calling in their work on the other hand. Yet, despite growing interest in occupational calling as a source of motivation and purpose, little is known about how leadership sustains this sense of calling under the psychological pressures of digitalization. This study addresses this gap by drawing on Self-Determination Theory to propose and test a model in which paradoxical leadership—a “both-and” style that balances control with empowerment and distance with closeness—enhances employees’ occupational calling through psychological safety. Furthermore, technostress from AI and social media undermines this pathway by frustrating the satisfaction of autonomy, competence, and relatedness needs. Using three-wave, time-lagged data from the final sample of 406 full-time employees across industries in Taiwan, results support a moderated mediation model showing that paradoxical leadership fosters calling by enhancing psychological safety, yet this positive process diminishes under high technostress. By situating occupational calling within the realities of the digital era, this study repositions occupational calling as a dynamic, context-sensitive experience and highlights leadership’s crucial role in preserving meaning and human motivation in an increasingly digitalized world.
