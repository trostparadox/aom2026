Job Coaches as Workflow Improvers: How Embedded Support Roles Create Organizational Value

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12
ODC

People

Authors: Reeti Patnaik Temple University; Thomas V. Edwards

Session overview

This study examines how job coaches, external support professionals who work alongside neurodivergent employees during onboarding, generate operational value inside organizations. Although job coaches are formally positioned as employee-side supports, interview data from 11 coaches and 4 managers show that their embedded and observational training practices often introduce workflow clarity that extends beyond the individual being supported. Across hospitality, retail, food service, and manufacturing settings, job coaches improved organizational functioning through four consistent mechanisms: (1) breaking complex jobs into teachable task sequences, (2) developing visual tools and checklists that standardize routines, (3) modeling communication practices that reduce ambiguity, and (4) identifying workflow blind spots that employees and managers had normalized. These actions stabilized early onboarding, reduced training variability, and improved performance across shifts. Several tools created for a single employee, such as closing checklists or task rotations, were later adopted throughout the organization. We propose a preliminary model in which job coach involvement promotes workflow clarity, with two conditions shaping whether improvements spread more broadly: the consistency of onboarding practices and the degree of managerial transparency around expectations and processes. When these conditions were strong, job coach interventions produced lasting operational changes. When weak, improvements remained isolated. The findings contribute to research on organizational development and change by positioning job coaches as informal process improvers whose ground-level visibility reveals operational issues that formal systems tend to overlook. This reframing shows how inclusive hiring can unintentionally prompt small but meaningful forms of process redesign and generate repeatable organizational value.
