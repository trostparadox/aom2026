How and When Does Remote Working Benefits Both Family and Work Outcomes?

OB
Xiaobei Li (UNSW Sydney); Lei Wang (Dongbei University of Finance and Economics)
In this research we examine the conditions to make remote work beneficial for both families and workplaces. Integrating literature of remote work and information system research, we propose that technical support provision enhances employee job autonomy, which in turn increases employee family role performance and task performance, and reduces employee deviance behavior. We further argue that the influences of technical support provision on job autonomy will be moderated by remote work intensity, and that the three-way interaction of technical support, remote work intensity, and individual intrinsic work value will jointly influence family and work outcomes and the effect will be mediated by job autonomy. The analysis of a two-wave dataset (N=272) supported the hypotheses related to family role performance and task performance and partially supported the hypotheses related to organizational deviance. These results suggest the effectiveness of flexible work arrangements is likely to be determined by both the technical support provision at the institutional level, and the intrinsic work value at the individual level.
