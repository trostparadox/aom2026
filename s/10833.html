Session overview

Peer coaching groups (PCGs) are increasingly used in organizations as a developmental approach that offers leaders a psychologically safe space for reflection, learning, and mutual support in complex and demanding work environments. As leadership roles become more uncertain and emotionally taxing, PCGs can function as a “safe harbor” where leaders openly share challenges, experiment with new perspectives, and learn from peers facing similar pressures. Despite their growing adoption across corporate, entrepreneurial, and professional settings, scholarly understanding of how different PCG designs create such conditions and shape developmental outcomes remains limited. This panel symposium brings together leading scholars and experienced practitioners to examine PCGs from multiple theoretical and practical perspectives. Drawing on research on psychological safety, identity and meaning, power and status dynamics, perspective-taking, vicarious learning, and career barriers, the panel explores how PCGs cultivate relational climates that support learning, connection, and leadership development. Practitioners from established peer-based organizations will share insights into how PCGs are designed and sustained in practice, while scholars engage these designs to surface the relational and cognitive mechanisms that underlie group processes and outcomes. By integrating research and practice, the symposium offers a comparative view of peer coaching group designs and highlights key conditions that enable PCGs to serve as a safe harbor for leaders. The discussion aims to advance theoretical understanding of peer-based developmental groups while providing scholars and practitioners with a clearer framework for studying and designing PCGs in organizational contexts.
