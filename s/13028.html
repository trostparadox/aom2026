Session overview

“To be or not to be” is an enduring question of existence and moral agency which captures the dilemma confronting contemporary business schools in the face of mounting contextual turmoil: can we carry on as before or is it time to rethink? This PDW equips participants with a refined research agenda, collaboratively generated research questions, and interdisciplinary pathways for contemplating this choice, and reimagining business schools in future as institutions for the public good. Once founded to cultivate the professional stewardship of managers, business schools have gradually become powerhouses for discourses that put profits first. Now measured and managed by rankings, revenues, and reputational metrics rather than contributions to professions, communities and collective well-being, business schools are locked in a managerialist mindset that emphasizes financial performance and marginalizes alternative thought. This orientation, and its negative impact on wider society, is increasingly questioned in light of institutional demands (see AACSB Standard 9) and scholarly critique (e.g., Colombo, 2023; Hoffman, 2025) which urges business schools to reimagine their role as civic institutions devoted to the public good. This PDW begins from that tension: what it means to continue “to be” as we are, or to pursue another future and decide “not to be” with six leading academics in three scholarly dialogues, each focused on a point of provocation around moral purpose, performative knowledge and institutional design. These interactions aim to prompt visions for how business schools flip-the-script to serve, and care for, a tumulted society. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
