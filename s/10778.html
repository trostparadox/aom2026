Session overview

This panel symposium proposes an exploration of the shifting definitions of responsibility and wrongdoing. While research in management and organization studies (MOS) has shown how societal stakeholders such as the media, judicial courts, and regulators construct wrongdoing by evaluating ambiguous or dubious practices, far less is known about what happens when practices long regarded as responsible become newly problematized. Emerging work in corporate social responsibility, legitimacy, and institutional theory demonstrates that responsibility is historically and politically contingent. Practices such as ESG reporting, carbon offsetting, and DEI initiatives, once celebrated, are increasingly reframed as symbolic, insufficient, or harmful through evolving debates in business and society. Bringing together behavioural, organizational, and institutional perspectives, this symposium examines how organizations committed to “doing the right thing” navigate these contested environments. We invite reflection on how responsibility norms are produced, institutionalized, and challenged, and how organizations can act with integrity amid uncertainty.
