Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes increasingly adopted and integrated into the workplace, concerns regarding potential bias in AI and the ethical implications have intensified. Although AI is often viewed as more objective and capable of reducing human bias in decision-making, it is shaped by socially situated data and may reflect and reproduce existing inequalities. These biases disproportionately impact underrepresented and marginalized groups, raising critical questions for diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI). This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) explores biases in AI and their ethical implications, recognizing diversity both as a driver of bias and as a receiver of its consequences. By emphasizing the uneven distribution of risks and benefits across individuals and groups, the workshop responds to a growing need for DEI-centered frameworks that move beyond technical solutions to address the human and social consequences of AI use at work. The workshop is structured in two parts. In the first part, four expert scholars present research and perspectives on bias in AI across organizational contexts. In the second part, participants engage in a facilitated, interactive brainstorming session to develop future research directions and methodological approaches. This PDW aims to foster critical dialogue and generate actionable research agendas that support more inclusive, responsible, and human-centered approaches to AI in organizations.
