When Young Employees Encounter Setbacks: The Moderating Effect of Forgone Identity Dwelling

CAR
Hang Yang (Renmin University of China); Shiyong Xu (Renmin University of China); Zheyuan Wang (Renmin University of China)
Previous research has primarily examined the effects of perceived underqualification on job behaviors from resource-based, person–job fit, and social exchange perspectives, paying limited attention to its impact for individuals’ career-related behaviors through the lens of identity research. Drawing upon the self-affirmation theory, this study investigates how young employees’ perceived underqualification influences their job search behaviors and examines the moderating role of forgone identity dwelling. We conducted a three-wave survey of 281 young employees. Our findings indicate that young employees’ perceived underqualification negatively affected state self-esteem, which in turn increased job search behavior. Moreover, forgone identity dwelling weakened the negative effect of perceived underqualification on state self-esteem. These findings enhance our understanding of the career-related consequences of perceived underqualification, extend self-affirmation theory by incorporating the concept of the forgone self, and contribute to job search behavior research by establishing perceived underqualification as a significant antecedent.
