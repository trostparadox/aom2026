How Reflective Practice Generates Absorptive Capacity in Strategic Leadership

STR
Adebayo Serge Francois KOUKPAKI (Heriot-Watt University Dubai Campus); Chidozie Umeh (University of York); Thomas Garavan; Manzoor Khan (Khansaheb Group); Cliodhna Mackenzie (University College Cork)
Strategic leaders increasingly operate in volatile environments that demand cognitive flexibility, political acumen, and adaptive learning. Reflective practice is widely recognised as an important component of leadership development, yet its role in the development of strategic leaders remains underexplored. This study examines how reflective practice shapes leaders’ absorptive capacity and the enactment of strategic insight in senior leadership roles. Drawing on teleological theory and a qualitative longitudinal design, we analyse data from interviews and reflective narratives with 32 strategic leaders across 18 countries, collected over three waves spanning 24 months of an executive education programme embedding structured reflection. Our findings show that reflective practice operates as a teleological process through which strategic leaders iteratively revise goals, reinterpret their role, and test new courses of action under conditions of uncertainty. We identify four empirically grounded phases, awakening, strategic reasoning, enactment, and polemical divergence, that capture how reflection moves from sensemaking to action and political confrontation. Together, they explain how absorptive capacity is cultivated through recursive cycles of reflection, rather than functioning as a stable individual trait. It thus emerges as a non-linear, recursive, and emotionally charged practice that deepens self-awareness, challenges entrenched logics, and enables leaders to translate insight into strategic action. We develop a staged, teleological process model of reflective practice that clarifies how strategic leaders build absorptive capacity over time and mobilise it in navigating complex and contested organisational environments.
