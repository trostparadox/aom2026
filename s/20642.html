Human-AI Symbiosis in Supply Chain Decision Making: Dynamic and Differentiated Roles

Author: Youssra Riahi Rabat Business School, International University of Rabat

Session overview

As supply chains (SCs) increasingly implement artificial intelligence (AI) across processes, the decision-making environment is undergoing a profound transformation. While AI contributes to the development of multiple SC capabilities, it remains limited in ambiguous, emergent, and ethically complex situations; contexts that demand distinctly human capabilities. Despite the growing interest in human-AI collaboration, the literature remains fragmented and lacks a systematic understanding of how human cognitive and social capabilities complement AI across SC decision environments and how a symbiosis between the two agents can emerge. This study addresses this gap by conceptualizing Human-AI Symbiosis (HAIS) as a dynamic, co-evolutionary socio-technical capability that enables SCs to continuously adapt, learn, and evolve. Grounded in cognitive and social psychology, decision theory and naturalistic decision-making, this paper identifies human-specific capabilities that complement AI in SC decision contexts. These capabilities are mapped onto different decision environments using the Cynefin framework, consequently shaping different AI and huma roles, varying across domains characterized by differing levels of structure, uncertainty, and time pressure. This synergy consequently shapes different SC states contingent upon AI and human involvement in decision-making presented in a human-AI symbiosis matrix. By framing HAIS as a recursive learning process that enables dynamic reconfiguration of human and AI roles, this study advances organization and management theory beyond task-based or episodic human-AI collaboration. The proposed conceptualization highlights how aligned human-AI roles support capability renewal and adaptive decision architectures, offering a pathway toward more resilient and context-sensitive SCs.
