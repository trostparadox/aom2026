Gender Stereotypes and Job Insecurity in an AI-Driven Future

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Yuanyuan (Yoyo) Huo University of Surrey

Session overview

The rapid diffusion of generative artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping contemporary careers, altering how employability is defined, developed, and evaluated. Yet the psychological and social processes through which individuals experience these shifts remain under-theorized. At the same time, persistent gender stereotypes surrounding technical competence continue to shape career self-concept, learning engagement, and perceptions of future opportunity. This conceptual paper integrates careers scholarship with organizational psychology to develop a theoretical framework explaining how gender stereotypes contribute to job insecurity in the context of AI-mediated careers. Drawing on social cognitive theory and conservation of resources theory, we propose that gender stereotypes undermine perceived employability by eroding career self-efficacy and perceptions of access to valued career resources. Reduced perceived employability, in turn, heightens subjective job insecurity during the education-to-work transition. Extending contemporary models of employability and career adaptability, we theorize that generative AI competence and trust in AI function as critical career resources that shape individuals’ sense of agency, adaptability, and career control. These resources, we argue, can buffer the negative effects of stereotype-induced doubts on career-related insecurity. This paper contributes to careers research by reconceptualizing employability in the AI era as jointly shaped by technological capability and social identity dynamics, and by offering a theoretically grounded agenda for understanding inequality and resilience in increasingly technology-mediated careers.
