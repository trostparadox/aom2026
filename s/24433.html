When Control Becomes Invisible: Fear Under Algorithmic Management

HR
Qinyan Gong (RMIT University); Di Fan (RMIT University); Timothy Bartram (RMIT University)
Algorithmic management reshapes workers’ experiences by altering how work is monitored and evaluated, yet its emotional consequences remain underexplored. Drawing on appraisal theory of emotions, we develop a framework explaining how workers’ interpretations of algorithmically structured environments generate fear. We test our arguments with two datasets: 10,922 online posts from U.S. Uber drivers and 227 interview videos with food-delivery couriers in China. The findings show that constraining work contexts and heightened stress intensify work-related fear, whereas perceived fairness in compensation, job autonomy, and social diversity mitigate fear under algorithmic control. By foregrounding fear as a systematic outcome of algorithmic management, this study highlights the importance of considering emotional consequences in the governance of workplace technologies.
