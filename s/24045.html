An Adopter-Centered Theory of Social Innovation Diffusion

SIM
Jeffrey Gauthier (State University of New York College at Plattsburgh); David Gordon Cohen (Skidmore College); Chris Meyer (Baruch College, CUNY)
Research on social innovation has given limited attention to the role of adopters in influencing diffusion outcomes. Addressing this gap, this conceptual paper develops an adopter-centered theory of social innovation diffusion grounded in multilevel legitimacy processes. Integrating diffusion research with theories of micro-level propriety and macro-level validity judgments, we argue that adoption is not a passive response to innovation characteristics but an active, political process of legitimation. We present a typology of social innovations based on institutional challenge and resource requirements, and develop propositions linking adopter characteristics – societal entity, familiarity, status, economic well-being, network position, and psychological strength – to diffusion across innovation types. We show how community-based collectives, peer familiarity, lower-status actors, and independent propriety judgments play central roles in diffusing institutionally challenging social innovations, while higher-status actors facilitate scaling with risks of co-optation. Our research advances theory on social innovation diffusion and offers directions for future empirical work.
