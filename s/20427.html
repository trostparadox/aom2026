Session overview

Understanding modern gender bias requires attention to how macro-institutional forces shape organizational life. In the United States, recent political and cultural shifts--the rollback of reproductive rights, intensifying attacks on DEI initiatives, and the resurgence of sexist rhetoric--function as transformative mega-events. These events serve as powerful institutional signals that legitimize a restrictive gendered logic, constraining how women are evaluated, treated, and expected to behave in the workplace. This symposium traces how these institutional signals can penetrate organizations, shape individual psychology, become embedded in systems and technologies, and produce downstream gendered outcomes. We present five papers analyzing the drivers and consequences of these shifts, each employing unique theoretical frameworks and diverse methodologies, including large-scale surveys, panel data, archival analyses, experiments, and computational models. Together, these papers examine the complex ways in which gendered hierarchies, norms, and stereotypes are reproduced across political, organizational, psychological, and technological domains—from the campaign trail and ballot box to the c-suite and boardroom and into algorithmic and evaluative systems.
