Session overview

This panel symposium will argue that the emergence of polycrisis, or the intersecting, civilization-threatening array of crises that now exist, highlights the need for transformative change not only in business, economic, and other institutional practices, but also in how we as scholars approach, write-up, and disseminate our work. Despite years of calls for more impactful, meaningful, and systemic research that matters, business as usual largely continues—both in practice and in our scholarly lives. To address these issues, starting with the big picture, Sandra Waddock will outline the issue of polycrisis, the need for holistic eco-social research, scholarly, and teaching perspectives, a shift away from neoliberalism, and the ways in which management research appears to be avoiding dealing with these issues. Following on, Amanda Williams (Em Lyon Business School) will explore the concept of the Anthropocene and Planetary Boundaries, which demonstrate how humans are contributing to the environmental crisis and the current state of corporate action. Harry Hummels (U. Maastricht) will then emphasize the need for a fundamental philosophical and ethical shift in the foundation of societal and economic thinking. Michael Barnett (Rutgers U.) will bust the myth that impactful research destroys careers, demonstrating the need for a fundamental philosophical and ethical shift in the foundation of our societal and economic thinking. Finally, Ralph Hamann (U. Cape Town) will propose a broadening of the repertoire of what it means to be a scholar, recognizing and celebrating scholars’ relational embeddedness in communities and places. This constellation of perspectives will shed light on the need to deal with problems that are already creating global crises here on Planet Earth. Management research, and we as researchers, we argue, need to shift towards really understanding and dealing with the significant civilizational threats that already exist.
