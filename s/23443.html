AI-Driven Excellence: Unlocking Better Technological Innovation Through AI Orientation

TIM
Annika Arbitter (TU Dortmund University); Steffen Strese (TU Dortmund University)
While firms increasingly deploy Artificial Intelligence (AI) to improve their performance, not all are able to translate their AI efforts to impact. This study examines how and under what conditions a firm’s AI orientation enhances their technological innovation quality. Drawing on the dynamic capabilities theory, we conceptualize AI orientation as a higher-order capability that enables firms to sense, seize, and transform in response to change. Using a panel of 489 technology-active firms from the S&P 1500 index spanning 2015 to 2024 (3,643 firm-year observations), we apply a generalized estimating equations model to examine our hypotheses. The results show that AI orientation is positively associated with technological innovation quality and that this effect is stronger when firms face performance shortfalls relative to competitors and when technological uncertainty is high. These findings advance research on AI-driven innovation by positioning AI orientation as a dynamic capability and by introducing performance shortfalls and technological uncertainty as contextual boundaries for its effectiveness. Practically, the results suggest that managers can leverage AI orientation to transform pressure and uncertainty into superior innovation outcomes.

Artificial Intelligence Orientation and Firm Innovation: The Critical Role of Strategic Emphasis

TIM
Yu Huang (Fudan University); Xinchun Wang (West Virginia University); Yuanxu Li (Fudan University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a transformative force reshaping firms’ innovation landscapes. While top management increasingly adopts AI to enhance competitiveness, it remains unclear whether and how its strategic orientation in AI (AIO) translates into improved innovation performance. Drawing on the attention-based view (ABV), this study theorizes that AIO channels managerial attention toward value creation rather than value appropriation, thereby fostering innovation. We further argue that this effect is contingent on the firm’s search context, which shapes how AI-oriented attention is allocated and enacted. Using a multiple-source dataset from 1,680 Chinese manufacturing firms, we find that AIO positively influences firm innovation performance through a stronger emphasis on value creation, and that this relationship is amplified when performance attainment is low or organizational slack is high. These results offer new insights into how emerging technologies shape resource allocation, strategic cognition, and sustainable competitive advantage.

How Does AI Orientation Affect Disruptive Innovation? A Resource Orchestration Perspective

TIM
Hanshu Liu (Tianjin University); Zhaoli Song (National University of Singapore); Feng Guo (Tianjin University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly adopted by firms to support disruptive innovation and sustain competitive advantage. Yet, despite its growing prominence, both theory and empirical research have devoted limited attention to the mechanisms through which AI enables disruptive outcomes. To address this gap, drawing on resource orchestration theory (ROT), we advance the framework to the domain of AI-enabled disruption by theorizing that AI orientation influences disruptive innovation both directly and indirectly through two complementary IT–human resource pathways: AI technology investment and AI talent recruitment. In addition, we hypothesize that the effectiveness of these pathways depends on key internal and external contingencies—top management team (TMT) consistency and technical uncertainty—which shape how successfully firms mobilize, coordinate, and integrate AI-related resources. Using an unbalanced panel of 7,736 firm-year observations from Chinese manufacturing firms between 2016 and 2024, we find broad support for the proposed framework. Our study extends ROT to AI-enabled disruption and clarifies how cognitive orientations, contextual conditions, and resource-allocation processes jointly shape disruptive outcomes, offering a novel theoretical account of how firms mobilize AI for transformative innovation.

Racing Ahead: How AI Orientation Drives Innovation Speed in Competitive Environments

TIM
Annika Arbitter (TU Dortmund University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a transformative force, changing and accelerating how firms operate. To benefit from the advantages of AI, it is not sufficient for firms to simply implement the technology. Instead, they need to align their strategy, to form an AI orientation. Yet, little is known about how firms with an AI orientation stay ahead of competitors. To address this gap, we draw on the dynamic capabilities view and investigate how a firm’s AI orientation influences their innovation speed, as innovation speed is one way to secure competitive advantage. Additionally, we show how internal and external boundary conditions moderate this relationship. Using a generalized estimating equations (GEE) model on longitudinal panel data from S&P 500 firms (2015–2024), we find that a higher AI orientation is associated with faster innovation speed. Our results indicate that competitive intensity further strengthens this relationship, while higher levels of working capital weaken it. We contribute to management research by extending the dynamic capabilities view and AI orientation literature. Additionally, we expand research on innovation speed, highlighting key boundary conditions, such as competitive intensity and working capital. Our findings also offer practical insights for managers navigating AI-driven transformation in competitive environments.

Shaping Innovation Resilience with Artificial Intelligence Adoption

TIM
Yong Xu (China University of Mining and Technology (Xuzhou)); Yichen Yao (Ocean University of China); Liangliang Zhang (Shanghai University of International Business and Economics)
Although the benefits of artificial intelligence (AI) adoption in innovation are widely recognized, its influence on innovation resilience (IR) remains insufficient understood, especially for high-tech firms. Drawing on the motivation-opportunity-ability framework and using Chinese high-tech firms from 2010 to 2023, we conceptualize IR by capturing its distinctive characteristics in high-tech firms and empirically examines how AI adoption (AIA) shapes IR. The results indicate that AIA significantly enhances IR, such effect is magnified among non-loss-making firms, those operating in competitive industries, and firms are involved with technology blockade. Moreover, innovation culture, information asymmetry, and human capital play positive mediating roles in such relationship, while marketing capability exerts a negative moderating effect. This study advances the understanding of AI-enabled innovation, providing both theoretical insights and practical implications for high-tech firms.
