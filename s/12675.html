Session overview

The organization of work is undergoing significant transformation (Barley et al. 2017). Organizations are reconsidering where work and when work takes place, and how it is supervised and coordinated. Alternative work arrangements such as remote work, algorithmic management systems, and applications of artificial intelligence have become increasingly prominent (Katz and Krueger 2019, Von Krogh 2018). Scholarly debates surrounding these changes are fundamentally concerned with whether new forms of work are beneficial or detrimental, typically evaluated along key dimensions of employee well-being and organizational performance. This focus reflects employee demand for meaningful and satisfying work (Ward and King 2017, Chan and Jiang 2025) and evidence on the interconnections between employee well-being and organizational performance (Bellet et al. 2024). Yet debates framed around benefits versus detriments have yielded mixed and often contradictory evidence on autonomy, satisfaction, and productivity (Bloom et al. 2015, Rahman et al. 2023, Giuntella et al. 2025), or displacement, eroded work–life boundaries, and burnout (Boswell and Olson-Buchanan 2007, Mazmanian et al. 2013, Chung 2022). Perhaps less well-understood is the role of institutional and structural transformations underlying the changing organization of work. Omitting such factors risks overlooking inequality and power structures as access to flexibility, discretion, and valued skills reshapes opportunity, economic security, and well-being (Bidwell et al. 2013, Spreitzer et al. 2017). This panel symposium brings together scholars examining organizational structures, HR systems, management practices, and new technologies to advance forward-looking approaches to studying the changing organization of work.
