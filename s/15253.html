Session overview

The growing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in management and organizing leads to digital visibility and invisibility in organizations. AI systems do not merely render activities visible; they actively construct new objects of visibility through inference, filter what becomes opaque, translate visibility into automated action, and provoke new forms of resistance. Across organizations, platforms, online communities, and emerging decentralized forms of organizing, AI reshapes what counts as observable work, who can see whom, and how visibility translates into authority, control, and value capture. Work is increasingly assessed through algorithmic judgments rather than direct observation, producing asymmetric and often opaque visibility regimes with significant managerial, ethical, and societal implications. Yet, our theoretical understanding of these dynamics remains fragmented. The goal of this Symposium is to systematically explore how research and practice should address the emerging prevalence of AI in the generation of digital visibility and its consequences. Bringing together scholars working on AI, digital surveillance, platforms, communities, and algorithmic governance, the Symposium provides a space to take stock of existing knowledge and define avenues for future research. Speakers will present findings from their research on the interplay between AI and digital visibility, followed by an interactive panel discussion with the audience. The intended outcomes are twofold. First, the Symposium develops a multi-perspective understanding of AI-enabled visibility across different forms of organizing, with particular attention to how AI simultaneously creates new possibilities for visibility and invisibility. Second, it identifies key research questions and collaborative opportunities that position AI-enabled digital visibility as a central construct for understanding contemporary organizing.
