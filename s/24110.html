Dynamic Capabilities, Entry Modes and SCA: Theory and Evidence

IM
Christos Pitelis (University of Cambridge); Hongyi Yang (London South Bank University); Eleni Piteli
The theory of the multinational enterprise (MNEs) focuses on firm level sustainable competitive advantage (SCA). The choice of cross-border entry mode is an important part of the theory and the MNEs’ global strategy. Numerous studies have investigated entry modes from different perspectives such as internalization of transaction costs, the reduction of competition and the leveraging of resources. More recently the dynamic capabilities (DCs) view has explored the way in which DCs help inform MNE global strategy, including entry modes. Despite extensive literature, little is known so far about the specific types of DCs needed for different entry modes and the way in which they impact upon MNEs’ SCA. To address these limitations, we conduct a systematic literature review. Our findings suggest that equity-based, non-equity and inter-firm collaboration modes require different levels of sensing, seizing and reconfiguration DCs, and consequently affect MNEs’ SCA, alongside environmental and organizational factors.
