Organizational Forms in the Platform Economy: Reconfiguring Model-Value Relations?

OMT
Dominika Wruk (University of Mannheim); Sahin Dündar (University of Mannheim)
Questions of how economic activity should be organized and governed have long accompanied debates on alternative forms of organizing. In the context of persistent ecological and societal crises and limited prospects for state-led solutions, these questions have gained renewed urgency. Building on work on prospective theorizing and real utopias, this paper examines how alternative organizational futures are enacted from within existing economic arrangements, without collapsing into advocacy or prediction. We study the platform economy as a salient domain in which organizational choices around ownership, governance, and value distribution crystallize. Drawing on an exploratory analysis of 138 digital platforms, we identify four organizational forms—grassroots alternative platforms, worker-oriented cooperative platforms, corporate physical platforms, and corporate digital platforms—and examine the value orientations they articulate. Our findings show that alternative platforms advance distinct model-value configurations that challenge assumptions of dominant platforms. By mapping organizational diversity and model–value configurations in the platform economy, this study advances prospective theorizing by showing how real utopias take the form of empirically identifiable organizational models that structure ownership, governance, and value distribution in alternative ways.
