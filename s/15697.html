Project Society Meets ESG: LPA and the Microfoundations of Sustainability Reporting Capacity

Session overview

Projects increasingly constitute the core organizational arenas through which environmental, social, and governance (ESG) strategies are translated into operational practices and institutionalized as credible sustainability performance. Framed by the notion of the “project society,” this research proposes to examine how ESG integration is enabled by the microfoundations of professional competence in project-based organizations and how these micro-level configurations shape organizational capacity for sustainability reporting under emerging regulatory regimes (e.g., CSRD). Building on institutional theory, the project-as-practice perspective, and sociotechnical systems theory, the study conceptualizes ESG reporting not merely as a compliance exercise, but as an outcome of patterned professional practices embedded in project work. The core research objective is to identify distinct latent configurations of ESG-related competencies among project professionals and to theorize how these configurations condition organizations’ ability to generate reliable environmental, social, and governance data through projects. Methodologically, the study is designed as a large-scale empirical investigation using survey data from project professionals across multiple European industries and countries. Latent Profile Analysis (LPA) will be employed to uncover heterogeneous professional profiles based on dual assessments of the perceived importance and enacted performance of ESG-oriented competencies. These profiles will then be analytically linked to an original composite construct - the ESG Transformation Capacity Index - intended to capture the extent to which project portfolios are structurally capable of producing credible inputs for corporate sustainability reporting. By foregrounding the microfoundations of ESG in project-based work, this research positions professional competence configurations as the missing link between regulatory ambition and organizations’ actual capacity to produce credible sustainability outcomes and reporting.
