Affect and Belonging in the Workplace: Dynamics of Sensemaking in Hybrid Work

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Authors: Endi Guza University of Lugano; Alessandra Zamparini University of Lugano

Session overview

Digital organizing and hybrid work are reshaping how organizing unfolds and how work is enacted. As the boundaries between physical and digital spaces blur, the socio-affective and material foundations of sensemaking are being reconfigured. This study employs an ethnographic approach to explore how members of a non-profit organization, which transitioned from a physical to a hybrid model of organizing, make sense of their work through affective– identificatory processes. Preliminary insights suggest that sensemaking in hybrid environments is supported by the integration of physical and digital affective practices such as shared office rituals, digital exchanges, and mediated encounters, that sustain emotional connection and belonging. These findings highlight how hybrid work transforms affective sensemaking, as belonging becomes simultaneously mediated and materially distributed across physical and digital spaces. Ongoing analysis will further explore how affect unfolds across digital communication and collaboration platforms, offering new insights into the emotional foundations of sensemaking in digitally mediated organizational life.
