Beyond Anonymity: Users’ Linguistic Framing and Gatekeepers’ Idea Evaluation in OUICs

TIM
Xuemei Xie (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); Lei Yu (Tongji University); Chenjian Zhang (The University of Western Australia)
Online user innovation communities (OUICs) enable firms to harvest user-generated ideas, yet gatekeepers may be biased by how ideas are presented rather than by their substantive merit. This study investigates how two primary forms of linguistic framing—cognitive framing (logical arguments, factual information) and emotional framing (emotive language, personal appeals)—bias gatekeepers’ idea evaluation in OUICs. Drawing on competence-warmth attribution literature, we propose that cognitive and emotional framing inflate evaluation scores through distinct pathways: competence and warmth attributions, respectively. Employing a mixed-methods design, we analyzed 179,376 user ideas from Xiaomi’s OUIC and conducted two randomized controlled vignette experiments manipulating framing while holding content constant. Results demonstrate that both cognitive and emotional framing lead to significantly higher evaluations—rewarding linguistic presentation independent of idea quality. Our experiments also demonstrate the underlying mediation mechanisms. These findings challenge assumptions that the anonymity of digital innovation platforms inherently reduces evaluation bias, revealing that content-based biases emerge when traditional social cues disappear. Our study contributes to the literature by identifying linguistic framing as a systematic source of evaluation bias in digital innovation contexts.
