Critical Mass, Board AI Literacy, and AI Adoption in Strategy, Risk, and Workforce Transformation

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Authors: Shuai L. Ren McMaster University; Michael Hartmann McMaster University, Canada; Daniel Hartmann

Session overview

Corporate boards are increasingly expected to oversee firms’ adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) in strategy, risk governance, and workforce transformation. Yet boards vary widely in whether investments in AI translate into meaningful strategic action. Drawing on critical mass theory and upper echelons theory, we develop and test a moderated mediation model that explains when and how board-level AI training and AI literacy shape firms’ strategic AI adoption. We theorize that AI training enhances boards’ collective AI literacy, which in turn enables more active engagement in AI-related strategic, risk, and workforce transformation. However, we argue that this indirect effect is contingent on whether the board reaches a critical mass of AI expertise. Using survey data based on 123 Canadian board directors from various organizations, we find support for a moderated mediation model, with effects concentrated in strategic engagement. Our findings highlight the importance of board-level AI training and collective AI literacy for the strategic governance of AI adoption.
