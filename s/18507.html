Session overview

The ownership structure of a firm is a central determinant of who holds power, which stakeholders are included in decision-making, and the extent to which non-financial goals are prioritised. This PDW explores the radical potential of alternatives to capitalist ownership by introducing participants to alternatives to the dominant capitalist model of corporate ownership—including worker ownership, steward ownership, and mutual ownership—and invites them to join in a critical reflection on how to evaluate the impact of alternative ownership forms in facilitating a greater balance of power and inclusion in the economy, while supporting human and planetary values. This workshop utilizes a panel of scholars responding to the question “What is the radical potential of alternatives to capitalist ownership?” to open a dialogue with their discussion followed by an audience plenary to advance an egalitarian exploration of the topic from a diversity of perspectives. This PDW addresses important aspects of alternative ownership underrepresented in the literature by simultaneously engaging the potential of these models while also wrestling with the practical challenges of adopting and implementing alternative models, emphasizing the normative aspects of alternative ownership, and giving credence to radical approaches that question the foundational assumptions—such as profit maximization—of the dominant capitalist ownership model.
