Session overview

Festivals have a long history and have taken place in virtually all human cultures for millennia. These ritualized, recurrent “time-out-of-time” moments interrupt ordinary life, providing opportunities to come together and celebrate life, our relation to nature, to each other and to spiritual aspects, including deities and gods. Festivals worldwide continue to attract billions of people annually, generating hundreds of billions of dollars in revenues. Their contemporary forms span religious calendars, cultural and arts, as well as music- and movement-based gatherings, each assembling distinctive value for participants, hosts, and organizers. While the business impact is global, festivals also create and maintain social and communal infrastructure. Many festivals are explicitly oriented to religious and/or spiritual themes, ranging from longstanding devotional traditions to contemporary spiritual movements and intentional transformational gatherings. Festivals surface spiritual dynamics as patterned ritual work, where embodied practices organize attention, emotion, and interaction. Another aspect that makes festivals of special interest for Management Spirituality & Religion members is that they serve as fertile ground for the emergence of direct spiritual experiences and deep individual psychological processes, including pilgrimage-like pre-festival journeys and meaningful post-attendance transformation. These intentionally curated liminal/liminoid containers form temporary holding environments where transformative qualities are encountered across pre-, during-, and post-festival phases. They also engender collective effervescence, a heightened shared affective energy which participants often interpret as sacred or healing. Our aim is to introduce the topic to MSR (and other Academy) members and explore the rich potential that festivals hold as a focus of research and teaching.
