How Corporate Green Attention Shapes Green Innovation Network Embeddedness

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 2
ONE

People

Authors: Yuanting Zhao Zhejiang University; Jun Jin Zhejiang University

Session overview

Amid global carbon peaking and neutrality transitions, green innovation inherently demands inter-firm collaboration, yet Chinese enterprises face an isolated innovation predicament with insufficient collaborative engagement. Drawing on the Attention-Based View (ABV) and Network Embeddedness Theory (NET), this study investigates how corporate green attention shapes green innovation network embeddedness and the moderating role of executives with environmental backgrounds, using panel data of 1,301 Chinese A-share listed firms (2007–2021). Results reveal that corporate green attention exerts a significantly positive impact on network embeddedness through resource allocation guidance, strategic signal transmission, and network power accumulation. The ratio of executives with environmental backgrounds negatively moderates this relationship, as such executives tend to prioritize internal environmental compliance over external network expansion. Heterogeneity analysis shows stronger main effects in large firms and state-owned enterprises. This study supplements the cognitive antecedents of green innovation networks, extends the application of ABV and NET to green transformation contexts, and offers practical insights for firms to optimize executive configurations and governments to promote green collaboration.
