Understanding How and When Despotic Leadership Hinders Workplace Thriving

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Authors: Darryl B. Rice Miami University; Antoine Busby Stephen F. Austin State University; Alana Bell Hampton University; Richard "Mason" Sloan Miami University

Session overview

The goal of this manuscript is to extend the literature that examines the impact of leadership styles on workplace thriving. Whereas prior research has predominantly focused on bright-side (i.e., positive) leadership styles, we focus on dark-side (i.e., negative) leadership style, namely despotic leadership. We ground our study in organizational resentment theory. Given the cruel mistreatment, as well as the disregard for the well-being of others, inherent in despotic leadership, this type of leadership style should elicit organizational resentment. In turn as employees develop organizational resentment, this should undermine their ability to thrive at work. This adverse reaction to despotic leadership is amplified by employee consciousness. This is because conscious employees react more negatively when they experience despotic leadership. Across a three-wave, time-lagged study, we find support for our model. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.
