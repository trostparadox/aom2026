Generational Effects, Socio-Emotional Burden, and Foreign Divestment in Family Firms

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3

People

Author: Gaurav Kumar XLRI Xavier School of Management

Session overview

Research on socioemotional wealth (SEW) suggests that emotional attachment to the firm diminishes across generations, leading later-generation family CEOs to make more economically rational decisions such as divesting underperforming foreign subsidiaries. Yet recent evidence shows inconsistent generational effects. This study argues that these inconsistencies stem from an overlooked mechanism - Socio-Emotional Burden (SEB)—the heightened symbolic and legitimacy pressures descendants face to preserve the founder’s legacy, especially in culturally embedded family firms. Using panel data on Indian family firms from 2014–2024, we examine how principal generation influences foreign divestment and how performance below aspirations and foreign competition moderate this relationship. Random-effects negative binomial models reveal a significant negative main effect, indicating that later-generation CEOs are less likely to divest, consistent with SEB rather than declining-SEW logic. Performance shortfalls reverse this tendency: when firms perform below aspiration levels, descendants become significantly more likely to divest. Conversely, foreign competition strengthens legacy-preservation pressures, further reducing divestment among later-generation leaders. Our findings introduce SEB as a complementary emotional mechanism shaping generational decision-making in family firms and offer new insights into why foreign divestment patterns vary across generations.
