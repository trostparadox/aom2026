Rebound Effects as Organizational Outcomes: A Territorial Scoping Review of Circular Economy Transit

Session overview

The circular economy (CE) is widely promoted as a strategic response to environmental degradation, promising resource decoupling through efficiency, innovation, and circular business models. However, a growing body of research documents the persistence of rebound effects (RE), whereby circular interventions generate countervailing environmental outcomes that offset expected sustainability gains. Existing studies predominantly frame RE as unintended side effects of otherwise desirable efficiency-oriented strategies, often analyzed through decontextualized and techno-economic lenses. This study challenges this dominant framing by conceptualizing circular economy rebound effects as structurally produced organizational outcomes, rather than residual anomalies. Drawing on a PRISMA-ScR-aligned scoping review of peer-reviewed literature published between 2019 and 2025, the study systematically examines how RE are theorized, operationalized, and governed across CE research. Building on insights from organization theory and sustainability transitions scholarship, the review pays particular attention to the role of territorial configurations, governance arrangements, and organizing processes in shaping rebound dynamics. The analysis reveals a persistent analytical gap between the territorially grounded implementation of circular strategies and the spatially abstract models used to assess their impacts, resulting in the systematic under-theorization of territorially mediated RE. By reframing rebound as an outcome of how circularity is organized, coordinated, and scaled across territories, this contribution advances a more reflexive and context-sensitive understanding of circular transitions. The study contributes to management and organization studies by positioning RE as diagnostic signals of structural tensions within CE initiatives, with implications for governance, strategy, and transition design.
