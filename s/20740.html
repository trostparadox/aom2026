Session overview

Hypes have become a central concern in management and organization theory because of their power to reshape organizations, technologies, and entire industries. Over the past two decades, scholars from a wide range of theoretical traditions have examined hype and related phenomena (such as bubbles, fads, and fashions), producing a large but dispersed body of research. Across these literatures, hypes can be understood as the collective amplification of speculative expectations about a novel phenomenon—widely believed to generate rapid growth and transformative impact, yet often prone to subsequent disappointment or decline. Despite growing interest, research on hypes remains fragmented and relatively undertheorized. Fundamental questions persist regarding what distinguishes hypes from adjacent concepts, how they originate and evolve, how organizations navigate internal–external alignment under hype pressures, and what consequences hypes generate for organizational fields and markets. This panel symposium seeks to advance a more integrated and theoretically grounded understanding of hypes by bringing together leading scholars working on entrepreneurship, innovation, social evaluations, narrative, and market emergence. The panel addresses four interrelated themes: (1) the defining characteristics of hypes; (2) the origins and drivers of hypes; (3) the dynamics of hypes and the strategic trade-offs organizations face when aligning with or resisting hype narratives; and (4) the short- and long-term consequences of hypes. Through theoretical and empirical illustrations, the symposium positions hypes as a pervasive organizing force in contemporary economic and social life.
