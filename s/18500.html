Should I S(t)ay or Should I Go? MNE Responses to Competing Home–Host Market Institutional Pressures

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1

Authors: Konrad Albert Graf Vienna University of Economics and Business; Evelyn Reithofer; Thomas Lindner Copenhagen Business School; Matevz Raskovic Auckland University of Technology

Session overview

The Russo-Ukrainian war since February 2022 has generated evolving institutional pressures on MNEs. While many firms initially announced withdrawals or suspensions, subsequent responses ranged from full exits to partial exits, continued presence, public “intent to exit” alongside ongoing operations, and even consolidation. This paper examines how MNEs respond when institutional pressures arise simultaneously from home and host markets. We argue that these responses are best understood by recognizing the dichotomy created by dual-market pressures and the three sets of interests MNEs balance: firm-level, home-market, and host-market interests. We test the effects of regulatory, normative, and mimetic pressures from home and host markets and the conditions under which MNEs assign greater or lower weight to them. Our research comprises a prior qualitative study and a subsequent quantitative study. Exploratory, semi-structured interviews with industry experts inform six hypotheses and identify firm-level moderators, including market revenue share, market footprint, media exposure, and legal capabilities. In the quantitative study, we compile three datasets and analyse 847 MNEs using Russia-related status information from the Yale CELI list and public disclosures. Using regression analysis, we relate home- and host-market pressures and their moderators to observable response categories such as exit, partial exit, stay, dig-in, and say-go-stay/defer. We contribute by jointly modelling home and host market pressures and clarifying how MNEs balance competing institutional demands under geopolitical crisis.
