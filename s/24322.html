How Knowledge Scope Shapes Inventor Response to Discontinuous Change

TIM
Mina Karami (Oregon State University)
This paper revisits the generalist versus specialist debate by studying how inventors respond when a major technological shift disrupts the field. I argue that after a technological discontinuity disrupts, generalist inventors are more likely to shift into the new regime and to produce more inventions there, because their broader background helps them make sense of the shift, learn quickly, and combine ideas, leading to higher participation and higher output in the new regime. I test these ideas in the context of the 2017 Transformer breakthrough and its diffusion in natural language processing using a large inventor-year panel of AI-active patenting from 2013 to 2021. Using inventor and year fixed effects models, I compare within-inventor changes in NLP participation before versus after the discontinuity while distinguishing participation from intensity. I argue that generalists increase NLP inventive productivity more than specialists and become more likely to participate in NLP field in a given AI-active year. My findings show that knowledge scope shapes both whether inventors participate in the new regime and how much inventive effort they devote to it once they do.
