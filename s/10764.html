Design Challenge as Boundary Object: How Wording Shapes Ambition and Scope in AI Innovation

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Franziska Anna Roeckel University of Liechtenstein; Benjamin van Giffen University of Liechtenstein; Andreas Janson University of St. Gallen; Jennifer Hehn Bern University of Applied Sciences

Session overview

AI in corporate innovation processes is challenging. AI is simultaneously designing material and transformative force for innovation activities. While prior research has focused on AI innovation processes and methods, the role of artefacts that initiate and guide these processes remains undertheorized. In particular, the Design Challenge – the starting point of innovation activities – has received little scholarly attention. This study conceptualizes the Design Challenge as a boundary object that enables collaboration across interdisciplinary stakeholders in corporate AI innovation. Drawing on a theory-guided content analysis of 42 Design Challenges from corporate AI innovation projects, we identify core elements of Design Challenges and show how specific wordings systematically shape the ambition (radical vs. incremental) and scope (performance vs. expansion) of innovation activities. We develop a typology of four Design Challenge types, each associated with distinct innovation and AI profiles. By theorizing the Design Challenge as an artifact-based boundary object, this research contributes to AI innovation theory and offers actionable guidance for practitioners on formulating Design Challenges aligned with intended innovation ambitions. By offering this framework, this research contributes both to theory and practice. It provides a theoretical conceptualization of the Design Challenges as an artifact by using the Boundary Object while providing guidelines to practitioners on how to formulate the Design Challenge to be aligned with the ambition for innovation activities.
