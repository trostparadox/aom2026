From Legitimacy to Executive Authority: A Process Model of Racialised Women’s Leadership Progression

DEI
Huong Nguyen (La Trobe University); Suzanne Young (La Trobe University)
Despite increasing attention to diversity and inclusion in leadership pipelines, ethnically diverse women remain underrepresented in executive authority and succession roles. While prior research documents barriers to advancement, we know less about how leadership inequality unfolds over time when formal inclusion is present. Drawing on 21 in-depth interviews with ethnically diverse women in senior leadership roles in corporate Australia, this study develops an empirically grounded process model that conceptualises leadership progression as a relational and temporal process of gaining legitimate executive authority. Informed by institutional logics and embedded agency, the model shows how shared identity tensions are navigated within organisational contexts characterised by strong inclusion rhetoric yet narrow architectures of power. We identify four interrelated career mechanisms—precarious legitimacy, relational authorisation, strategic containment, and carved-out inclusion—through which competence is unevenly translated into authority across career transitions. These mechanisms generate four patterned leadership trajectories—Surfers, Strategists, Strivers, and Standbys—representing distinct configurations of mechanism activation over time. By shifting attention from promotion to authority, this study reframes leadership progression as an ongoing accomplishment shaped by recognition, relationships, and institutional norms, and explains why inclusion so often stops short of executive power.
