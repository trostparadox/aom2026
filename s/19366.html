Does Online Entrepreneurship Education Build Entrepreneurial Competencies?

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6
ENT

People

Authors: Kana Tomita The University of Tokyo; Katsufumi Matsui Kansai University

Session overview

Entrepreneurship education (EE) has expanded in higher education, and since COVID-19 many programs have shifted from face-to-face delivery to online formats. While online EE may improve access and scalability, evidence remains mixed due to variation in design rigor and outcome measurement. This study evaluates a synchronous online EE program using entrepreneurial competencies as learning outcomes (Morris et al., 2013) in a comparative quasi-experimental design. We examine an eight-session, two-month synchronous online program delivered nationwide in Japan. Using pre- and post-program surveys with a comparison group, we estimate program effects with difference-in-differences (DID) regression models. Outcomes are measured with five subscales from Morris et al.’s (2013) entrepreneurial competency instrument: Opportunity Recognition, Opportunity Assessment, Creative Problem-Solving, Value Creation with New Products, Services, Business Models, and Building and Using Networks. In the DID with controls, participants exhibit statistically significant additional gains in Opportunity Assessment, Value Creation with New Products, Services, Business Models, and Building and Using Networks, whereas effects for Opportunity Recognition and Creative Problem-Solving are positive but not statistically significant. These findings suggest that synchronous online EE can build specific entrepreneurial competency subscales—particularly Opportunity Assessment, Value Creation with New Products, Services, Business Models, and Building and Using Networks—while gains are less evident for Opportunity Recognition and Creative Problem-Solving. The study offers implications for designing online EE to strengthen outward-facing engagement and feedback-driven iteration.
