Between Illusion and Impact: Navigating the Dual Nature of Innovation Theater

TIM
Constance Dumalanède; Cristian Granados (EGADE Business School)
Innovation theater, i.e. high-visibility corporate venturing initiatives (CVIs) that signal innovativeness without commensurate outcomes, is often treated as inherently harmful. Drawing on paradox theory, we examine the tensions it produces and how organizations can manage them. We conducted an inductive qualitative study of CVIs in 12 organizations in Mexico and Spain (2022–2023), combining 50 interviews, observations of 20 CVI events, and secondary data. Using the Gioia methodology, we show that innovation theater shapes stakeholders’ emotions, perceptions, engagement, and firms’ innovation reputation. These effects cluster into three paradoxes: Commitment (motivation vs. frustration), Ambition (innovation mindset vs. exhaustion), and Image (visibility gains vs. credibility erosion). We further identify three strategies—voluntary engagement, divergent collaboration spaces, and transparent communication —that help amplify upsides while mitigating downsides, creating a Paradox-Driven Innovation Environment. Our framework reframes innovation theater as an ambivalent phenomenon and offers practical guidance for designing CVIs that convert symbolic momentum into durable innovation capability in resource-constrained settings.

Signaling Through Standard Setting: Evidence from China’s Capital Markets

TIM
Ziyu Liu (Zhejiang University)
This study examines how capital markets respond to firms’ participation in standard setting. Drawing on signaling theory, we conceptualize standard-setting participation as a credible and observable signal that conveys firms’ technological and strategic capabilities to investors. Using an event study design combined with propensity score matching and multivariate regressions, we analyze stock market reactions to the public release of ICT-related national standards involving Chinese listed firms from 2012 to 2024. We find a positive and statistically significant short-term market reaction to standard-setting participation, indicating that investors perceive such involvement as value-relevant information. Importantly, this effect exhibits systematic heterogeneity. The market reaction is weaker for firms with greater analyst coverage and higher institutional ownership, consistent with a richer information environment. Moreover, the signaling effect depends on the availability of alternative signals: it is stronger for first-time participants and attenuated for firms with strong recent patenting activity or high and new technology enterprise certification. We further show that standard-setting participation is associated with increased trading activity and improved market liquidity, suggesting active information processing by market participants. Overall, our findings extend the literatures on standardization and market signaling by highlighting the informational role of standard setting in shaping investor beliefs and capital market outcomes.

Intellectual Capital to AI enabled Digital Marketing Cap.: Exploitative Innovation & Tech Turbulence

TIM
Chi-Cheng Wu (National Sun Yat-Sen University); Chunhsien Wang (National Chiayi University); Chin-Chia Ou (Asia University)
How do firms convert intellectual capital (IC) into AI enabled digital marketing capability (DMC)? Integrating dynamic capabilities and agile marketing, we theorize that IC fuels DMC primarily through AI capability (AIC) a higher order orchestration capability enabling rapid sensing, experimentation, and iterative decision making. We further posit that this conversion is contingent on exploitative innovation (EI) and technological turbulence (TT). Evidence from a high quality key informant, cross regional survey of Taiwanese firms spanning ten plus industries including semiconductors and with a majority of middle to senior executives and owner managers shows that AIC fully mediates the IC and DMC relationship: once AIC is accounted for, the direct IC effect becomes non-significant. TT significantly relates to EI and, critically, moderates the AIC to DMC linkage, indicating that AI enabled marketing payoffs are environment dependent. Moreover, the joint EI×TT interplay yields a significant three way contingency that governs the strength of the mediated conversion. Conditional indirect effect analyses reveal that the IC to AIC to DMC pathway is strongest under lower TT, but erodes sharply when TT is high and EI is weak. By identifying AIC as the pivotal conduit through which knowledge resources are translated into agile, data driven marketing capability and by specifying when this conduit breaks down this study advances theorizing on AI enabled capability formation under technological volatility.

When Investors Speak, Green Ideas Grow: How Online Investor Interaction Catalyzes Green Innovation

TIM
Sheng Liu (Guangdong University of Foreign Studies); Xiuying Chen (Guangdong University of Finance); Haiming Lan (Guangxi University)
The green innovation behavior of listed companies is not only influenced by its own internal factors, but also by the interaction between external investors and stakeholders. In digital economy, the online exchange interaction platform, as a new mode of interaction and communication between investors and listed companies, deserves in-depth research on whether it can motivate listed companies to fulfill their ecological social responsibilities and strengthen their green innovation incentives. This paper applies the panel data of Chinese A-share listed companies and the internet interaction data on "Easy Interaction" and " Shanghai Stock Exchange E-Interaction" as observation samples to identify the impact of the interactive mechanism between listed companies and investors on the enterprise’s green innovation activities. The results show that investors' attention to environment protection will be transmitted to listed companies through effective online interaction, which helps to suppress the earnings management and short-sighted behavior of senior management, improve the enterprises’ awareness of environmental information disclosure and strengthen the inputs of green research & development resources. This highly disclosed and real-time online information interaction form can help to induce enterprises to improve the quantity and quality of their green innovation. The conclusion is valid after the robustness tests including dynamic game analysis, difference-in-difference method, placebo test and instrumental variables test. In addition, the effect of green innovation induced by the enhanced online interaction between listed companies and investors is heterogeneous due to the differences in high-tech and ownership, the effectiveness of product market and factor market, the policy sensitivity of enterprise and the internal environmental management level.
