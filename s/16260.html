CEO Narcissism and ESG Inconsistency

Author: Yi Tang The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

Prior research on environmental, social, and governance (ESG) strategies has predominantly emphasized aggregate levels of ESG engagement, devoting comparatively limited attention to the internal coherence and consistency of firms’ ESG activities. Yet firms frequently allocate attention and resources unevenly across ESG domains and over time, resulting in internally inconsistent ESG profiles. Such ESG inconsistency has important implications for stakeholder trust and the effectiveness of ESG initiatives, but its organizational and leadership antecedents remain underexplored. Drawing on upper echelons theory and research on executive narcissism, this study develops a conceptual framework to explain why and when firms exhibit ESG inconsistency. I argue that CEO narcissism—a personality trait characterized by grandiosity, entitlement, and a strong desire for admiration—predisposes firms toward uneven ESG engagement. ESG initiatives differ in their visibility, symbolic value, and reputational payoff across stakeholder domains, allowing narcissistic CEOs to selectively emphasize ESG activities that best serve self-enhancement motives while neglecting less visible dimensions. As a result, narcissistic CEOs are more likely to generate internally inconsistent ESG profiles rather than balanced ESG engagement. The framework further identifies corporate governance conditions that shape this relationship. CEO duality amplifies ESG inconsistency by expanding executive discretion and weakening board oversight, whereas board gender diversity constrains inconsistency by enhancing stakeholder sensitivity and monitoring effectiveness. By shifting attention from ESG magnitude to ESG configuration, this study contributes to ESG research by theorizing ESG inconsistency as a distinct strategic outcome and to leadership research by highlighting how executive psychological traits shape firms’ nonmarket strategies.
