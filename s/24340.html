Leadership Consensus Emergence: Development, Antecedents, and Implications on Team Performance

OB
Xiaoyu Li (China University of Mining and Technology (Xuzhou)); Chu-Ding Ling (Renmin University of China)
Despite theoretical recognition of leadership emergence as a dynamic process, empirical research has largely overlooked how team members develop a shared consensus on a nominal leader’s influence over time. Drawing on a dynamic, process-oriented framework and interpersonal perception theory, this study examines the development of leadership consensus emergence, its antecedents (competence and warmth), and its implications on team performance. We tested our hypotheses using longitudinal data from 143 teams participating in a multi-round business simulation competition. Results of consensus emergence model indicate that team members’ evaluations of nominal leaders converge over time, establishing a clear consensus. Furthermore, initial perceptions of a nominal leader’s competence and warmth facilitate this convergence. Crucially, we find that the interaction between emergence consensus and the final level of leadership emergence positively predicts team performance. These findings advance understanding of the dynamic nature of leadership emergence, identifying competence and warmth as drivers of consensus and clarifying when collective endorsement enhances performance.
