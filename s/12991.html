When Conversational Agents Lead: A Review of Leadership Behaviors in Psychosocial Interventions

Authors: Sebastian Moder University of Liechtenstein; Türkü Erengin Maastricht University; Simon Liegl University of St. Gallen; Markus Langer

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is moving beyond process automation to engage in adaptive, relational dialogues. This development makes leadership behaviors enacted by nonhuman agents a central concern for leadership research, challenging the field’s human-centered assumptions. While few studies have examined AI systems in organizational leadership, evidence from psychosocial interventions (e.g., therapy, coaching, health management) offers a large rigorously evaluated corpus of automated processes functionally equivalent to leadership behaviors. Drawing on Yukl’s taxonomy, we systematically review how conversational agents (CAs) in these contexts enact task-, relationship-, change-, and externally oriented leadership behaviors. We summarize which leadership-related outcomes they affect, including engagement and adherence (as proxies for followership), affective and cognitive shifts (e.g., stress reduction, adaptive thinking), and performance indicators (e.g., goal attainment, behavioral change). We integrate this evidence and (1) map identified CA functions onto leadership behaviors, (2) evaluate their transferability to organizational theory by highlighting differences compared to human leadership, (3) identify design patterns linking CA features to outcomes, and (4) derive methodological implications for studying AI leadership. By transferring lessons from fields in which these influences have already been rigorously examined, our work helps leadership research to build on existing evidence rather than repeat avoidable missteps. In doing so, we advance leadership theory in the age of AI and outline an evidence-based agenda for studying AI leadership.
