Employee Sense of Humor and Career Success in Hospitality: A Dual-Path Social Capital Perspective

OB
Meihong Chen (Xiamen University); Weiwei Lin (Xiamen University)
Drawing on social capital theory, this study investigates whether, how and when frontline employees’ sense of humor influences their career success. To test our hypotheses, we conducted a three-wave survey of 248 supervisor–subordinate dyads in 5 hotels in China. The results indicate that employees’ sense of humor has a significant positive effect on frontline service employees’ career success. Moreover, popularity in the organization and job resources sequentially mediate the relationship between employees’ sense of humor and career success, while popularity among customers and customer resources constitute an additional mediating pathway. Furthermore, task interdependence strengthens the positive effect of employees’ sense of humor on popularity in the organization, whereas customer contact frequency amplifies the positive effect of employees’ sense of humor on popularity among customers. These two contextual factors further enhance the indirect effects of employees’ sense of humor on career success through the dual sequential mediation mechanisms. Finally, we discuss the theoretical and practical implications of these findings.
