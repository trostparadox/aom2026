“It’s Like We’ve Lost Our Connective Tissue”: Employee Sensemaking in the Wake of DEI Retrenchment

DEI
Laxmikant Manroop (Eastern Michigan University); Huimiao Zheng; William E. Donald (University of Southampton)
This study examines how employees experience and respond to the retrenchment of organizational diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) initiatives amid intensifying political and legal pushback. Addressing a critical gap, we center the employee perspective to explore DEI retrenchment as a perceived breach of trust, a site of sensemaking, and a catalyst for quiet, values-driven advocacy. Drawing on 57 semi-structured interviews across U.S. sectors and diverse identity groups, we applied reflexive thematic analysis, guided by psychological contract breach, sensemaking, and tempered radicalism, to reveal how employees interpret DEI retrenchment and sustain inclusion under constrained conditions. We identify seven themes that reveal how DEI retrenchment ruptures expectations, erodes organizational trust, and reconfigures inclusion as a moral and emotional burden. While some participants framed retrenchment as procedural, others viewed it as a betrayal of institutional promises and personal values. Notably, many employees continued inclusion efforts informally through discreet acts of resistance. A comparative analysis reveals racialized divergences, with non-White employees more likely to experience emotional exhaustion and view retrenchment through a moral lens. Our findings contribute to scholarship on psychological contracts, organizational sensemaking, and inclusion resilience, while offering practical insights into how organizations can ethically navigate DEI change in polarized environments.
