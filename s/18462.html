The Effects of Witnessed Workplace Ostracism on Organizational Commitment

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 10

Authors: Ishraque Hossain Chowdhury The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley; Md Parvez Alam The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley

Session overview

Third-party observation (TPO) of deviance is a phenomenon that has begun to be studied by researchers with increased interest. We look at the effects of one such observation named workplace ostracism. Drawing on Affective Events Theory, we develop a conceptual model explaining how witnessed workplace ostracism shapes observers’ affective organizational commitment. We argue that witnessing ostracism by a third party may lead the witness to perceive injustice in the workplace, which will trigger other sympathizing and other condemning behavior towards the victim and the, perpetrator respectively. We also argue that this relationship is moderated by the moral identity of the witnessing employee and the corrective actions that the organizations take regarding deviance towards individuals, such that they will help to reduce the effect size of the event. Finally, I argue that these other sympathizing emotions and other condemning emotions will lead to the reduction of organizational commitment of the employee observing ostracism. These findings extend the TPO of deviance and workplace ostracism literature by looking at ostracism as deviance and looking at the effects of witnessed ostracism. Furthermore, the arguments present practical implications for managers to focus their attention on the witnessed ostracism as well.
