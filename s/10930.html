Open Strategy in Hidden Regions: An Ecosystem Perspective on Openness

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
SAP

People

Author: Kristina Stoiber Technical University of Munich

Session overview

Open Strategy has broadened our understanding of how strategic processes can be made more inclusive and transparent, yet it remains theoretically anchored in firm-centric assumptions, offering limited guidance for understanding how openness unfolds when the relevant strategic unit is not a single organization but an interdependent, multi-actor ecosystem. Drawing on the context of hidden regions, we advance an ecosystem-level conceptualization of open strategy. Hidden regions represent peripheral regional ecosystems marked by strategic closure, manifested in deep local embeddedness, inward-oriented networks, limited visibility, and persistent legitimacy deficits. Specifically, we build on a case vignette of the Heilbronn-Franken region. Heilbronn-Franken exemplifies such a hidden region, yet interestingly also exhibits concerted efforts to overcome closure through the establishment of a TUM Campus in a peripheral location, substantial philanthropic investment, and the expansion of digital and AI-related infrastructures. Applying open strategy practices to this context allows us to translate practices commonly examined at the individual or firm level into ecosystem-level openness. We offer a framework that conceptualizes how inclusion and transparency can broaden stakeholder involvement, strengthen access to information and shared infrastructures, and support more distributed governance across ecosystem actors. In doing so, we provide new insights into open strategizing at ecosystem-level.
