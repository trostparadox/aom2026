Session overview

The relationship between social movements and markets has entered a period of heightened uncertainty, with activism increasingly occurring under conditions of political turmoil. While previous scholarship has examined how movements target firms through contentious tactics (Bartley & Child, 2014; King, 2008) and how corporations respond to social challenges (McDonnell, King, & Soule, 2015), the current political landscape presents distinct challenges. Rising populism, democratic backsliding, anti-ESG backlash, and polarized regulatory environments have fundamentally altered the opportunity structures available to activists and the strategic calculus facing corporations (Briscoe, Chin, & Hambrick, 2014; Werner, 2017). These conditions demand theoretical and empirical attention to how activism adapts, persists, or transforms when operating under political instability. Research has shown that political hazards shape firm strategy in consequential ways (Henisz, 2000; Henisz & Zelner, 2010), while activist hedge funds increasingly target firms for their social positioning (DesJardine, Marti, & Durand, 2021). Building on our previous examination of collaborative activism, this year's PDW addresses how social movements navigate hostile political environments, how firms respond to activism amid regulatory uncertainty (Dorobantu, Kaul, & Zelner, 2017), and how movement-market dynamics shift when political institutions are contested or unstable. The workshop examines questions including how activists maintain effectiveness when facing political hostility (Walker & Rea, 2014), how firms balance stakeholder demands against politicized backlash (Cai, Yue, Lin, Yan, & Yang, 2025), and what mechanisms enable or constrain movement influence during periods of institutional disruption (Schneiberg & Lounsbury, 2017). Through expert perspectives spanning political strategy, corporate activism, and cross-sector dynamics, the workshop aims to advance theory on activism under adverse political conditions while fostering an inclusive scholarly community examining these pressing dynamics between movements and markets.
