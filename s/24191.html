Servant Leadership Buffering Contextual Performance and Procedural Justice from Coworker Undermining

MSR
PABLO RUIZ-PALOMINO; JORGE LINUESA-LANGREO (University of Castilla-La Mancha); Vicente Peñarroja (Universitat Oberta de Catalunya)
In service-intensive sectors such as hospitality, coworker undermining has emerged as a significant workplace stressor with negative implications for employee performance. This study examines how coworker undermining reduces contextual performance by eroding employees’ perceptions of procedural justice and explores the buffering role of servant leadership. Using Partial Least Squares analysis on survey data from 242 hotel employees in the Canary Islands (Spain), results indicate that coworker undermining indirectly diminishes contextual performance through lowered procedural justice. Servant leadership mitigates the negative effects of undermining on procedural justice and contextual performance, as well as its indirect impact through this mediating mechanism. These findings contribute to the literature on workplace mistreatment and leadership by identifying procedural justice as a key explanatory process and by highlighting servant leadership as an effective organizational resource for sustaining contextual performance, productivity, and competitiveness in hospitality settings.
