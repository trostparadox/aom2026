How Causal Ambiguity Sustains Competitive Advantage in Family Firms

ENT
Mohammad Jasim Uddin; Erhan Atay (Abu Dhabi University)
This conceptual paper explains how family firms sustain competitive advantages through the mechanism of causal ambiguity. While prior studies highlight the role of family-specific resources in driving performance, the persistence of these advantages remains underexplored. Drawing on the resource-based view (RBV), this paper proposes that sustainable advantage emerges from the coexistence of high inter-firm and low intra-firm causal ambiguity. The model explains how family firms’ unique characteristics—such as shared identity, long-term orientation, and intergenerational resource management—create complex, tacit, and path-dependent capabilities that are difficult to imitate. By articulating three theoretical propositions, the paper extends RBV by identifying causal ambiguity as a dual mechanism that both protects family resources from imitation and enables internal coherence. The framework advances theory in family business and strategic management by clarifying the causal processes underlying sustained advantage and offers future research directions for empirically testing how family dynamics influence resource inimitability.
