Session overview

This professional development workshop (PDW) is driven by a quest to bridge the gap between entrepreneurial ecosystem (EE) research and entrepreneurial behaviour (EB) studies. It aims to explore theoretical and empirical ways to integrate micro-level behavioural insights in entrepreneurship with macro-level ecosystem frameworks. Both streams of literature have advanced considerably in the last few decades, albeit as separate from each other. That is, one focused on the entrepreneur, whilst the other on the systemic factors that influence entrepreneurial activity realised in new firms. While culture, institutions and governance-driven approach to the making of an EE is important, the shaping of an EE by entrepreneurs (with different traits, cognition, behaviour) is not a trivial issue. Literature on entrepreneurial behaviour offers fertile background to incorporate the entrepreneur’s behavioural and decision-making processes into the ecosystem approach placing the entrepreneur at the core of the system as the primary agent. Studying individual level behaviour in an ecosystem is crucial because it paves the way for understanding the co-evolving top-down and bottom-up approaches that underpin the development of an EE. Using bite-size expert presentations, group brainstorming and idea sharing formats led by experts, this PDW seeks to bring together junior and senior entrepreneurship scholars in a setting designed to create opportunities for co-learning, collaboration and subsequently delivering a scholarly output in a Special Issue dedicated to this topic in the International Journal of Entrepreneurial Behavior & Research (IJEBR).
