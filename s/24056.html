The Engagement Paradox: How Stakeholder Relations Mitigate and Create Tensions in Social Enterprises

SIM
Filippe Barros; Victor Gomes (University of Bath School of Management); Adalberto Fischmann
Social enterprises face tensions balancing social mission with financial viability, yet existing frameworks inadequately address contexts where beneficiaries are structurally marginalized populations lacking voice and resources to articulate demands. We ask: How and why is stakeholder engagement associated with tensions in social enterprises serving marginalized populations? Through a constructivist single case study of Central da Visão, a Brazilian social enterprise providing affordable ophthalmological treatments to low-income populations, we conducted 17 in-depth interviews with shareholders, employees, physicians, investors, and clients. Our findings introduce the concept of involuntary demands: tensions arising from marginalized stakeholders' structural circumstances rather than from communicated preferences. We identify a paradoxical relationship: deeper stakeholder engagement simultaneously increases social impact and organizational tensions, as the investment required to engage marginalized populations conflicts with efficiency expectations. We develop six propositions explaining how engagement mitigates tensions through progressive expectation alignment while also creating new tensions through resource requirements and expectation misalignment. This research extends stakeholder theory beyond managing competing interests to understanding how structural marginalization alters tension dynamics, and contributes to decolonizing management studies by positioning the Global South as a site for theory building.
