What Facilitates Task Performance? A Causal Chain from Person-Organization Fit

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Authors: Jee Young Jeeny Seong Jeonbuk National University; Doo-Seung Hong Seoul National University; Jun Woo Lee Hanbat National University

Session overview

Drawing on person–environment fit and leadership theories, this study examines when and how person–organization (PO) fit translates into task performance. We propose that organization-directed organizational citizenship behavior (OCBO) mediates the relationship between PO fit and task performance and that charismatic leadership moderates the first stage of this mediation. Specifically, charismatic leadership strengthens the extent to which PO fit is enacted as OCBO, thereby amplifying its indirect effect on task performance. Using multi-source data, we find support for a first-stage moderated mediation model. These findings advance fit research by identifying leadership as a behavioral activation mechanism and clarify the role of OCBO as a pathway linking fit perceptions to task performance.
