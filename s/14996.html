Session overview

The extant strategic management literature has explored the effect of CEO Background on organizational outcomes in a variety of settings. This research study posits into the literature by exploring how variation in CEO characteristics affects corporate performance through human rights records. We argue that the background of executives matters regarding decision making on human rights issues, specifically social class. Since corporate social responsibility often involves redistribution of resources to those in need, upper-class members ever being part of this group, it is arguable that the upper-social class offspring will be reluctant to proactively engage in CSR since it will imply a shift of resources to their relative disadvantage (Kemayou, 2011). Specifically, the intended contribution is clarification of the mechanism which explains this CEO behavior. Our empirical setting is South Africa and through testing of our hypotheses we ascertain CSR Performance from the firm’s relations with humans proximal to the firm.
