Did We Succeed? Incentive Systems as Facilitative Symbolic Artifacts

HR
Sarri Nykänen (University of Turku); Juha S. Laurila (University of Turku)
At the same time as incentive systems align the interests of principals and agents, they also define the value of different organizational goals and offer the incentivized individuals an opportunity to feel and be seen as making a valuable contribution. Prior research offers a limited understanding of the origins of organizations’ incentive systems’ inherent goals and their meanings to organizational actors, especially in multi-goal and collaborative organizations. Therefore, we conducted a longitudinal single-case study on the design and influence of an incentive system in a large construction project carried out as a collaborative alliance. We find that, given the multiple goals and professional perspectives involved, the project’s incentive system’s design required the actors to negotiate a shared understanding of its success. To avoid retrospective disputes over the obtained performance, these actors excluded unmeasurable, although otherwise important goals. Both financially affected and not-affected actors were primarily motivated by the performance measures’ symbolic meaning as an overall criterion of success or failure, which then influenced their professional pride. By highlighting the origins of incentivized goals, practices to attain procedural fairness, and the symbolic meanings of incentive systems, we extend goal-framing and recognition theories of incentive systems.
