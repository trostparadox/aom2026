From Hard and Soft to Technical, People, and Mindful: A Three-Layer Architecture for Executive Skill

MED
David Matta (Lebanese American University)
Executive education, and education in general, has traditionally distinguished between "hard" and "soft" skills—a binary framework that, while useful, presents limitations. "Hard" suggests domain specificity, yet technical competencies encompass related technical skills that could be generic; "soft" confounds interpersonal and intrapersonal skills, yet they overlap. This paper proposes a three-layer architecture that reframes these categories. Technical skills encompass both domain-specific and general competencies—what one knows and can do. People skills address interpersonal effectiveness—how one relates and works with others. Mindful skills address the intrapersonal dimension—how one relates to oneself, including one's own mind, emotions, patterns of attention, and sources of meaning, as well as the metacognitive skills needed to reflect and reorient oneself. Drawing on phenomenology, transformative learning theory, and mindfulness research, we introduce the M5 framework comprising five dimensions: Perception, Feeling, Thinking, Acting, and Awareness. These interdependent capacities enable leaders to perceive clearly, regulate emotions, think reflectively, act intentionally, and maintain integrated presence. Without this intrapersonal foundation, executives may achieve success without satisfaction, performance without presence, and competence without consciousness. The paper provides conceptual differentiation of the three skill layers, developmental readiness mapping, cross-cultural adaptations, implementation guidance, and a research agenda.
