Session overview

The rapid diffusion of generative AI (GenAI) has intensified debate about whether qualitative researchers should engage these tools at all. Critics warn that GenAI can deskill interpretive work and flatten contextual richness; proponents suggest it can complement human understanding when researchers actively guide its contributions. This Professional Development Workshop addresses the current gap in practical guidance by focusing on abductive theorizing as a process of cultivating surprise through movement between data and theory. We convene scholars actively experimenting with GenAI in qualitative research to comparatively show how different tools and prompting strategies shape what becomes noticeable, questionable, and theoretically generative in qualitative datasets. The session combines panel reflections on (1) data familiarity and interpretive authority, (2) emerging craft skills and reflexive practice, and (3) pedagogy and training, with live demonstrations of multiple AI tools applied to qualitative materials. Across components, we treat AI as an epistemic provocateur and emphasize interpretive vigilance: maintaining analytic responsibility by using AI outputs as proposals to interrogate, contextualize, and refine, rather than as evidence to accept. Participants will leave with concrete workflows, reflexive checkpoints, and shared language for transparency and review standards in AI-assisted qualitative research.
