When Decentralization Helps—and Hurts: Governance Domains and the Distribution of Decision Rights

TIM
Moritz Otto Nafeh Dalheimer (University of St. Gallen)
Decentralized platforms use protocol rules and token-weighted voting instead of managerial hierarchy, but we still know little about when decentralization changes governance outcomes. We argue that the effect of decentralization depends on the governance domain. We analyze 3,202 governance proposals (July 2020–October 2025) from 10 token-governed decentralized exchange protocols on Snapshot and merge them with protocol-level ownership concentration in voting power (Gini) from Boardroom. Using unsupervised topic modeling of proposal text, we classify proposals into governance domains and relate ownership concentration to proposal-level weighted consensus. Ownership concentration is associated with higher consensus for proposals that delegate exceptional control rights and for proposals that allocate shared resources. Owner-ship concentration is associated with lower consensus for proposals that distribute broad-based participation incentives. These results show that decentralization is not a single governance pre-scription: its relationship with consensus differs systematically across governance domains.
