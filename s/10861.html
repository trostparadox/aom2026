Vulnerability and Adaptive Resilience in Platform Work Across Emerging and Developed Economies

Authors: Julian Dario Diaz Avendano CENTRUM PUCP; Juan Velez-Ocampo

Session overview

This study examines how individuals working in platform-mediated work navigate vulnerability and construct adaptive resilience across culturally and economically diverse contexts. Drawing on grounded theory and semi-structured interviews with platform-based tutors from emerging and developed economies, we develop a process model that explains how individuals respond to persistent structural vulnerability, build adaptive resilience, and sustain a coherent professional identity in the absence of formal organizational structures. The findings show that resilience is enacted through ongoing emotional regulation, cultural mediation, and relational engagement, enabling individuals to manage systemic demands while maintaining agency and meaning in their work. While the core adaptive processes are shared across contexts, their meaning, motivation, and consequences differ. Individuals in emerging economies emphasize economic continuity and social positioning, whereas those in developed economies prioritize autonomy and intrinsic purpose. By theorizing resilience and identity as culturally situated and relational processes, this study advances organizational behavior research on agency, identity work, and adaptation beyond traditional organizational settings and highlights how institutional and cultural contexts shape individual responses to platform vulnerability.
