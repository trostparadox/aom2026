From Agency to Context: Towards a Holistic Framework of Career Self-Management Behaviors.

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM

Authors: Irina Sergeeva University of Vaasa; Qingyang Xu University of Southampton; Mostafa Ayoobzadeh Université du Québec à Montréal (UQAM)

Session overview

Contemporary careers evolve in an increasingly uncertain work environment, with career research taking a turn from emphasis on individual agency towards as the primary means of coping with such conditions. Career self-management (CSM) has emerged as a central concept in this discourse; however, existing research largely conceptualizes CSM as a self-initiated, linear process shaped unidirectionally by contextual factors. This assumption may limit a holistic understanding of CSM and overlook the multifaceted nature of individual career experiences within organizational contexts. Therefore, informed by a sensemaking perspective, this study unpacks how individuals actively construct meaning from organizational cues and how this shapes their ongoing CSM processes. Grounded in the social constructivist paradigm, we adopted a qualitative approach. Drawing on 23 semi-structured interviews conducted within the ICT industry, we thematically analyzed it and found that organizational cues simultaneously enable and constrain CSM through individuals’ sense-making filters. Our findings reconceptualize CSM as a continuous, dynamic process emerging through ongoing interactions between individuals and their organizational context, thereby challenging dominant agentic and proactive assumptions of CSM in the field. Our study contributes to the CMS literature by revealing the interplay between organizational context and individual agency through sense-making processes, responding to calls for more holistic approaches in work and organizational psychology. It also offers practical insights for multiple stakeholders by helping organizations develop more supportive systems and enabling employees to manage their careers more consciously within an uncertainty-laden work landscape.
