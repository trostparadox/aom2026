From Filing to Monetization: Does Patent Exchange Establishment Raise Green Patent Output?

SIM
Ying Zhang (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Yuxuan Zhao (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Yi Xiang (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
This study examines how the establishment of a patent exchange market affects corporate green innovation from two distinct perspectives: transaction efficiency versus strategic value. The transaction efficiency perspective suggests that the positive effect of patent exchanges stems from minimizing search and transfer costs for standardized assets, thereby primarily incentivizing control-based green innovation that is modular, and codified. By contrast, the strategic value perspective posits that patent exchanges principally function to resolve information asymmetry through certification, thereby driving prevention-based green innovation by unlocking the liquidity of complex, high-value assets. Using a dataset of Chinese listed firms and a quasi-natural experiment involving the staggered establishment of provincial patent exchanges, we find that patent exchange establishment increases firms’ green patent output. Moreover, this increase is stronger and more robust for control-based green innovation than for prevention-based green innovation. These findings align with the transaction-cost reduction perspective and challenge the strategic-value perspective.
