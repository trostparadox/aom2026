Orchestrating Stakeholders for Public Value: Multi-Level Governance in DPG Ecosystems

Authors: Fabien Rezac Aarhus University; Kyeyoung Shin University of Oxford

Session overview

Digital public goods (DPGs) such as open-source digital identity systems, interoperable payment platforms, and civil registration software are increasingly recognized as critical enablers of inclusive public services and economic participation. Actors adopting DPG-based systems frequently present these initiatives as mechanisms for reducing exclusion, improving service delivery, and enhancing transparency. These ecosystems are organizationally complex: their global architectures are designed and maintained by transnational communities, foundations, or public-technology partnerships, while implementation and governance occur within national regulatory and institutional contexts. This reflects a multi-level governance environment in which decision-making authority is shared across actors rather than concentrated within a single entity. Because no single actor possesses hierarchical control, stakeholders must coordinate technical design, governance arrangements, and operational processes under conditions of distributed authority. Existing research highlights how interdependent actors coordinate through shared architectures and governance mechanisms, but it focuses predominantly on commercial ecosystems anchored by focal firms, whereas DPG ecosystems are oriented toward public value and must balance diverse institutional mandates while aligning global design with national implementation. This inductive multiple-case study examines how orchestrators coordinate stakeholders across global, national, and local levels in DPG ecosystems. Drawing on evidence from MOSIP, Mojaloop, OpenCRVS, OpenG2P, and GovStack, we identify four phases of coordination —architectural foundation, stakeholder mobilization, national localization, and institutionalization—together with five recurring governance tensions that shape how these systems scale. The study contributes to research on ecosystem governance, stakeholder alignment, and multi-level public-sector innovation by showing how public value is advanced through distributed orchestration rather than hierarchical control.
