How Cultural Orientations Shape Trust in AI

Authors: huang feng CEIBS; Ke Michael Mai China Europe International Business School; Peiran Wen Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University

Session overview

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly integrated into creative and organizational work, yet public attitudes toward AI vary substantially across countries. Existing research on AI acceptance has relied largely on single-country evidence and has paid limited attention to national culture. Integrating the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) with Hofstede’s cultural dimensions, this study examines how national cultural values are associated with societal attitudes toward AI. Using two waves of global tracking surveys conducted in 2024 and 2025 (N = 46,032 across 31 countries), we find that cultural effects are selective rather than universal: lower power distance and higher individualism are consistently associated with greater AI understanding, trust, and acceptance, whereas masculinity, uncertainty avoidance, long-term orientation, and indulgence show weak or non-significant relationships. Moreover, individual-level perceptions particularly perceived understanding of AI and anticipated performance improvement are more strongly linked to trust and acceptance than institutional orientations toward AI governance, with these patterns remaining stable across waves. Overall, the findings highlight cultural heterogeneity in AI attitudes and underscore the importance of culturally differentiated strategies for AI governance, public communication, and policy design.
