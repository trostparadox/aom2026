Session overview

“Big Tech” platforms are increasingly entering established industries, creating new challenges for incumbents. Existing research addresses incumbent responses from two perspectives. One focuses on firm adaptation, examining how incumbents reduce distance to environmental change by adjusting cognitive frames, reconfiguring routines, and innovating business models in response to platform entrants. The second stream centers on platform competition, analyzing how Big Tech platforms compete through envelopment, diversification, and acquisitions. These perspectives, however, remain weakly connected, limiting our understanding of how incumbents experience and respond to platform-driven change. This symposium brings together five papers from different empirical settings to examine incumbent responses to Big Tech, including platform creation, experimentation, and rhetorical strategies. Drawing on multiple theoretical lenses and methodological approaches, the symposium fosters dialogue on the cognitive, social, and organizational processes shaping these responses and seeks to inspire new research on how incumbents manage dependencies, safeguard competitiveness, and adapt to technological change.
