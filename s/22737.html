How and When AI-Related Technological Uncertainty Implicates Quality of Work Life

OB
YUAN LIANG(Leong) (Tianjin University of Commerce); Yu-Ming Wan (Harbin Institute of Technology); Ruicong Xiao (Harbin Institute of Technology); Xuan-xuan Cui
Scholars have confirmed the significance of AI and its essential role in human life and work domains. However, limited research focuses on the influence of AI-related technological uncertainty (AI-TU) on employees’ work and life and explain the potential mechanism. Based on the uncertainty management theory, we hypothesized a moderated mediation model to explore how and when AI-TU implicates employees’ quality of work life via work meaningfulness and meaning in life. Three time-lagged surveys (N1=271; N2=210; N3=357) were conducted with participants from different industries (i.e., education, service, and IT). Results indicated that AI-TU was negatively related to work meaningfulness and meaning in life, then disrupted quality of work life. Further, we found the buffering role of job crafting and leisure crafting. Our research is among the first empirical studies to provide the impact of AI-TU and suggests employees conduct crafting behavior both in life and on the job.

Melodies, Microphones, and Machine Learning? Musicians’ Sensemaking About Artificial Intelligence

OB
Kirsten Marie-Paule Robertson (Brock University); Todd Green (Brock University)
The hype and fear associated with artificial intelligence (AI) is creating unprecedented challenges for creative industries and the workers within them. Our research explores how professional musicians are navigating this shift in the ways that music is produced and consumed. We ask: (1) how are musicians using AI in their work? And (2) how do they make sense of their AI integration decisions? Through 60 in-depth interviews, we found that there is widespread experimentation with AI tools in the industry. We categorize these uses into a framework that considers both how central it is to core (musical) output and the extent of AI execution of the task. By combining this framework with a sensemaking lens, we identify four profiles of creative worker-AI co-existence: conscientious AI resistors, ancillary AI absolutists, targeted AI integrators, and holistic AI experimenters. The musicians belonging to each profile engage in distinct meaning making activities to justify their choices around AI use. A central consideration in their sensemaking is the meaning of art and hence their occupational identities as artists. The creation and adherence to rules around ‘acceptable’ AI use is a means of reconciling the discomfort that can be triggered by AI tools with the potential to produce unique musical outputs. Our findings contribute to the emerging literature on human-AI interaction and points to future research directions for this evolving area of scholarship.

Algorithmic Anticipation and Constrained Sensegiving in Organizational Change

OB
Muhammad Farrukh Iqbal
Organizations are increasingly introducing algorithmic systems in a gradual and often unclear way, rather than as fully implemented and well explained interventions. Most existing research on algorithmic management focuses on systems that are already in use and examines their outcomes. Much less attention has been given to how employees start making sense of algorithmic change at very early stages, when systems are visible but not yet clearly defined. Drawing on sensemaking theory, this study examines how employees interpret early algorithmic cues and how managerial sensegiving is experienced in relation to these interpretations. The study is based on a qualitative analysis of interviews with 21 employees from Chinese organizations that were at early stages of algorithmic adoption. The findings show that material and symbolic cues, such as pilot dashboards, announced systems, and trial metrics, lead employees to engage in collective and forward looking sensemaking. In many cases, these interpretations become relatively stable before managers provide sustained explanations. As a result, later managerial communication is often experienced as limited in its ability to shape meaning and is interpreted through already established frames. This study contributes to research on sensemaking and algorithmic management by showing how meaning can stabilize early under conditions of algorithmic ambiguity. It highlights temporal compression as an important process in early algorithmic change and suggests that the timing of communication plays a critical role in shaping how algorithmic systems are understood in organizations

Unravelling Employee AI Awareness (AIA): A Review, Conceptual Framework and Future Research Agenda

OB
Harish Yadav (Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee); Rajib Lochan Dhar (Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee)
Employee awareness of artificial intelligence (AIA) has gained prominence in service research as scholars seek to understand how employees make sense of AI and emotionally respond to its presence in their work environments. However, prior research on AIA is scattered across disciplines and lacks conceptual coherence. Addressing this shortcoming, the present study undertakes a systematic review of the literature to bring structure and clarity to the field and to develop a coherent, process-oriented framework. Drawing on a rigorous five-stage search and screening procedure, 78 peer-reviewed articles were identified and examined using the TCCM framework to integrate insights across theories, contexts, constructs, and methods. The synthesis suggests that AIA is triggered by contextual and organizational signals, shaped through intertwined cognitive and affective processes, and gives rise to varied employee responses, depending on the resources available to individuals and organizations. In addition to advancing theory, the review provides practical guidance for service firms regarding leadership approaches, workforce planning, capability building, and the design of supportive interventions in AI-enabled work settings, while outlining a clear agenda for future research.

From Discrete Events to Lived Employee Experience: An Event-Based Process Model of AI Adoption

OB
Harish Yadav (Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee); Rajib Lochan Dhar (Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee)
Although artificial intelligence (AI) adoption is widely recognized as transformative for organizations, existing research has largely examined its employee-level consequences through static, snapshot-based perspectives. Most studies capture AI awareness, appraisals, and outcomes at a single point in time, implicitly treating AI adoption as a discrete event rather than an unfolding organizational process. As a result, little is known about how employees come to experience AI adoption in their everyday work lives, how awareness is shaped by successive AI-related events, and why similar adoption initiatives evoke divergent employee responses. To address this gap, this conceptual paper reframes organizational AI adoption as an event cluster, a sequence of interconnected events that vary in novelty, disruption, and criticality. Drawing on event system theory and the transactional model, we develop a process model that explains how these event characteristics disrupt employees’ psychological equilibrium and trigger cognitive appraisals. We position appraisal processes as the central psychological mechanism linking AI-related events to employees’ emotional and behavioral responses. Further, we introduce additive, exacerbation, and inurement effects to explain how multiple event characteristics interact and evolve over time, shaping appraisal intensity and direction. By integrating event-oriented change research with subjective experience and stress–coping perspectives, this paper advances a dynamic, human-centered understanding of AI adoption as a lived organizational experience. The model offers novel theoretical insights and practical guidance for managing employee responses during ongoing AI-enabled transformations.
