Job Applicants’ Reactions to AI-Driven Decisions: The Effect of Personalization and Objectivity

HR
Guofan Li (Curtin University - Perth); Fangfang Zhang (Curtin University - Perth); Patrick Dunlop (Curtin University - Perth); Sharon Parker (Curtin University - Perth)
The role of AI in recruitment has received considerable attention from scholars and practitioners. However, how applicants respond to AI-driven decisions across levels of evaluation objectivity remains unclear. Drawing on organizational fairness and capability-personalization theories, this study examines how decision agents (AI vs. human vs. augmented) interact with evaluation objectivity to shape applicants’ perceived decision fairness, dehumanization, and behavioural intentions. An online vignette experiment with 220 participants showed that AI and augmented decisions, compared to human decisions, reduced perceived personalization (i.e., whether the agent was tailored to individuals’ characteristics, and context), which decreased fairness and increased dehumanization. The net effect was decreased decision acceptability and job acceptance intention. These indirect effects remained significant across both evaluation conditions but were significantly weaker in high than in low objectivity. Exploratory analyses reveal that AI and augmented decisions reduced perceived personalization compared to human decisions. This reduction decreased fairness and increased dehumanization, and further weakened decision acceptability and job acceptance intention. These indirect effects via dehumanization remained significant across both conditions but were weaker in high objectivity in AI decisions. The findings highlight that acceptance of AI-driven recruitment is likely to require integrating human roles into decision processes especially when assessing candidates against less objective criteria.
