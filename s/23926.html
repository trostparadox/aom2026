How Can Technology Innovation’s Accelerated Pace Align with Older Users’ decelerated Rhythms?

TIM
Fabio James Petani (Burgundy School of Business); Philippe Naccache (INSEEC); Andri Georgiadou (University of Nottingham); Helmi Issa (ESSCA School of Management)
Management studies may understand better how firms attune to the multiple rhythms of their environment (in B2B, B2G, B2C or intra-organizational BU relationships), to forecast technological and social challenges related to innovation. The notion of entrainment focuses on temporal alignment between activities and has theorized on the acceleration of decision making and innovation cycles, set by the intensive competition of high velocity environments. Speed is often unquestioned in technology development, but we need a rhythmanalysis of how existing users are lost, when substitute technologies replace old ones too rapidly, or when potential users, in organizations and society at large, fail to adopt available innovations, because of misalignments, or what we call arrythmia. Technological innovations are not fully adopted during their life cycle, and a temporally ill-programmed obsolescence of adopted products could erode the growing, slower demography of older consumers. Gerontechnology and silver economy-related innovations partially address the temporality of technology development. We contribute to this reflection by integrating rhythmanalysis and entrainment theory, exploring how organizations may manage opposed rhythms of innovation, paradoxically both accelerating technology innovation (to remain competitive) and decelerating product life cycle termination (to entrain to older users’ long-term adoption and use rhythms).
