When the Future Is Known but Not Acted Upon!

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
SAP

People

Author: ANN MARY VARGHESE

Session overview

Organizational crises are often explained as failures of prediction or as responses to unexpected shocks. This paper advances a practice-based alternative by examining crises that emerge when futures are widely known yet repeatedly deferred through everyday strategizing. Drawing on strategy-as-practice research on temporality, future-making, and discursive and sociomaterial practices, the study develops the concept of strategic non-anticipation, a patterned accomplishment through which organizations recognize emerging futures while rendering them non-actionable in routine strategic work. The paper adopts an abductive, process-oriented case study of IndiGo Airlines’ December 2025 operational breakdown following the implementation of revised flight duty time limitation regulations. Despite prolonged advance notice, repeated regulatory engagement, and institutional flexibility, the organization failed to reorganize strategy and capacity in anticipation of the known future. The analysis shows how strategic non-anticipation was enacted through temporal structuring, metric-centered planning, sociomaterial scheduling systems, and discursive framing that stabilized deferral as a legitimate strategizing outcome. The paper further introduces the concept of forced foresight to describe moments when deferred futures return through crisis, producing temporal compression, legitimacy loss, and institutional intervention. By reframing crisis as an outcome of everyday strategizing practices rather than surprise events, the paper contributes to SAP research on temporality, power, and future-making by explaining how known futures are actively deferred in practice and why they re-emerge through crisis.
