Session overview

Organizations depend on clear communication, yet people routinely speak past one another at work. Colleagues assume agreement that was never expressed, interpret polite rejection as ambiguous, or experience communication tools very differently depending on their role in the interaction. This symposium brings together four papers that examine how such miscommunication gaps arise and why they matter. Across a range of workplace interactions, including rejecting proposals, making requests, listening to others, and collaborating in groups, the papers show that people’s interpretations of communication depend systematically on the role they occupy. What feels clear and appropriate to one party often carries a very different meaning for another. The presentations highlight experimental findings on how these role-based misinterpretations shape trust, coordination, persistence, and the use of effective collaboration practices. Together, the session offers a unifying perspective on everyday workplace communication, showing how small mismatches between communicative intent and interpretation can accumulate into meaningful relational and organizational consequences.
