Session overview

Entrepreneurship, as a field, is often viewed in terms of how businesses are started and how they scale up to obtain venture capital funding, initial public offerings, and the oft touted “Unicorn” status. Yet, high growth “unicorns” (> one billion dollar valuation) are more the exception than the normal entrepreneurial outcome. This PDW takes stock of the entrepreneurial efforts to remain small by choice. These entrepreneurial ventures choose not to scale by means of: not hiring, limiting their hiring, limiting their geographic expansion, limiting the diversification of their offerings, resisting debt financing, resisting equity financing and so on. Ventures that resist scaling in such ways I term small-scale entrepreneurship. Academic interest in small-scale entrepreneurship has grown in recent years with calls for papers in Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice on Hybrid Entrepreneurship (Folta et al., 2024), the Journal of Management Studies on Lifestyle Entrepreneurship (Schulze et al., 2025), and a recent special issue in the Journal of Business Venturing that questions what we know about scaling (Autio et al., 2021). Paradoxically, many businesses feel they must physically grow to have a large impact, but in growing they lose control of the impact they do have. In this PDW, we will discuss small-scale entrepreneurship and why they may actively resist scaling their businesses through the perspectives of entrepreneurial well-being, family businesses, resourcefulness, social entrepreneurship, and lifestyle entrepreneurship. In so doing, we will hope to further research into the reticence to scale, predominately around solo ventures, lifestyle ventures, and other small-scale ventures.
