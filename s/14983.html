How People Perform in Context: The Factor-Based Model

People

Presenter: Frederic Lucas-Conwell

Session overview

The workshop will demonstrate how people's performance can be measured and represented, using a factor-based model. This model differs substantially from other models commonly used in recruitment, coaching, and research that rely on personality traits or typologies. It introduces nuances that enable new applications at the managerial, organizational, and strategic levels. The survey you will complete uses a two-question adjective list format. The output is an adaptive profile, composed of graphs and scales, that reflects how a person functions, adapts, and engages with the environment. The results are work-related, meet psychometric standards, and are concise enough to be readily used alongside other variables to improve decision-making about people and communication, as well as for job and group analyses. The model, which we are excited to present for the first time to academic audiences, originated from a research project analyzing how executives' use of personality assessments affects organizational performance in the early 2000s. The framework was later expanded to encompass any assessment techniques and their users. Developed in accordance with social science standards, the framework has helped set new benchmarks for assessment techniques, identify methods to improve organizational performance, and develop innovative approaches to social skill development. During the workshop, through activities beginning with your adaptive profile, we will explore the model's value in advancing research in organizations and management, with practical implications for the effective development of managerial skills across all organizational levels.
