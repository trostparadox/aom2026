Who Is “Us”? Incumbent’s Spousal and Affinal Involvement and Within-Family Ownership Reallocation

ENT
Sen Chen (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Yang Li (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Ye Yao (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); YIRONG CAI (University of Nottingham Ningbo China)
Family ownership constitutes is the central control mechanism of family firms, yet existing research often treats the owning family as a unified and stable entity, overlooking how evolving kinship ties reshape control allocations within the family itself. In practice, incumbent’s spouse and affinal kins may gain decision influence through marriage and formal governance roles, despite remaining outside the bloodline that traditionally defines family membership and legitimacy. Drawing on social identity theory, we argue that this ambiguous status of spousal and affinal actors destabilizes taken-for-granted boundaries of “the family” in the firm. Their involvement prompts families to renegotiate who can legitimately represent the family’s interests and exercise control, leading to within-family ownership adjustments. We hypothesize that incumbent spousal involvement reduces aggregate family ownership while increasing incumbent’s ownership stake, and that affinal involvement amplifies these effects by legitimizing marriage-based participation in top decision roles. Using panel data on 1,663 Chinese A-share listed family firms from 2011 to 2022, we provide evidence supporting these arguments. This study advances family business research by theorizing ownership reallocation as an identity-driven outcome shaped by kinship ties and governance involvement.
