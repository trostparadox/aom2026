Session overview

People, including workers in organizations, are constantly inundated and influenced by popular narratives in society. In this symposium, we explore a range of popular narratives that workers and managers hold and the way in which these stories challenge and complicate workers’ experiences and well-being. Papers in this session present compelling theoretical and empirical research that focuses on a diverse set of popular narratives, ranging from the enduring narrative that “no one wants to work anymore” to the competing clock narrative faced by VC-backed female entrepreneurs, and employing a wide array of methodological approaches. Our five papers highlight the often unexpected ways in which popular narratives about work in society shape the attitudes, emotions, and behaviors of workers. Through exploring research questions related to the complicated influence of popular narratives, this session will provide a holistic perspective on the intersection of work and narratives, as well as how employees and organizations can navigate these dynamics.
