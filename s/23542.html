First a Ruler, Then a Wand: Nonlinear Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Green Innovation Rhythm

TIM
Yufeiyang Zeng (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); Heyu Lu (Tongji University); Zongfa Wu (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University)
As digital and intelligent technologies diffuse rapidly and the green transition advances, green innovation has become a key source through which firms build long-term competitive advantage. Prior research has largely focused on the static level of green innovation, paying limited attention to the temporal dynamics that characterize innovation processes. This study employs panel data of Chinese A-share listed firms from 2011 to 2022 to empirically examine the nonlinear effect of artificial intelligence (AI) on green innovation rhythm. Our results reveal a significant U-shaped relationship between AI and green innovation rhythm. Climate policy uncertainty and industry competition respectively attenuate and intensify the curvature of this U-shaped relationship. Heterogeneity analysis further shows that the nonlinear effect above is more pronounced among young firms, firms in high-technology industries, and firms located in low-carbon pilot cities. By adopting a dynamic rhythm perspective, our findings extend existing discussions on AI and green innovation and provide new empirical evidence on how firms adjust the temporal pattern of green innovation activities as AI adoption deepens.

Green AI and Sustainable Innovation: The Role of Planning Capabilities and Societal Responsibility

TIM
Muhammad Waqas (Jiangsu University of Science and Technology); Usman Ahmad Qadri; Chenhui Zhai (Zhongnan University of Economics and Law)
This study examines how Green Artificial Intelligence (Green AI) contributes to sustainable innovation in technology-intensive firms. Building on dynamic capabilities theory, we conceptualize Green Innovation Planning (GIP) as the organizational routine that mediates the relationship between Green AI practices and sustainable innovation outcomes. We further test the moderating roles of societal accountability pressure and technological turbulence. Two empirical studies were conducted with Chinese firms. Study 1, a multi-informant triad survey of 190 firms, shows that GIP fully mediates the relationship between Green AI and sustainable innovation. Study 2, a three-wave time-lagged survey of 309 firms, finds a significant direct effect of Green AI on sustainable innovation, with GIP as a partial mediator. Across both studies, societal accountability pressure and technological turbulence strengthen the Green AI–GIP link, yielding conditional indirect effects on sustainable innovation. These findings demonstrate that planning routines are essential mechanisms through which Green AI produces measurable sustainability outcomes, and that external scrutiny and rapid technological change amplify the value of such routines. The study advances research on the impact and evolution of technology by specifying when and how Green AI creates sustainable innovation and offers guidance for embedding carbon-aware planning in AI project governance

How AI-Based Organizational Learning Stabilizes Firms’ Green Innovation Rhythm

TIM
Rixiao Cui (Beijing Institute of Technology); Mingxuan Wang (Chongqing University); Xiaohui Zhao
Firms increasingly adopt artificial intelligence (AI) to support green innovation, yet little is known about how AI influences the temporal stability of innovation processes. To address this issue, this study introduces green innovation rhythm (GIR) as an indicator of innovation process stability and examines how AI-based organizational learning (AIOL), grounded in organizational learning theory, relates to GIR. The analysis further draws on the dynamic capability perspective to explain how this relationship operates through digital agility. Using panel data on Chinese A-share manufacturing firms from 2009–2023, we find that AIOL is associated with a smoother and less variable GIR. Moreover, digital agility—through its sensing, integrating, and restructuring dimensions—mediates this relationship. Heterogeneity analyses show that the stabilizing effect of AIOL is more pronounced in state-owned firms, firms in less developed regions, and firms operating under higher economic uncertainty and competitive pressure. Overall, the findings show that AIOL supports sustained green innovation under technological and institutional uncertainty by embedding AI into organizational processes that structure innovation over time.

When Does Green Bond Issuance Benefit Expected Firm Performance?

TIM
Francesco Cappa (Roma Tre University); Francesco Fasano; Raffaele Oriani; Samuel Vigne (Luiss university)
As firms have begun to offer green bonds in response to the growing emphasis on environmental sustainability factors. In this study, we examine the potential overall impact of green bonds on expected performance based on contingency factors. Our findings, which are based on signaling theory, imply that the influence of green bond issuance on expected performance is positively impacted by firm sustainability performance. This beneficial effect, however, is only applicable up to a certain point, after which investors may view an excessive focus on environmentally friendly initiatives as diverting a company from its financial objectives, which would have a negative impact on future profits. In addition, it is shown that this inverted U-shaped effect is moderated by both the use or non-use of revenues from green bonds for green technological advancements and business profitability levels, both of which contribute to a firm’s overall legitimacy in green bond issuance. When issuing green bonds, these factors should be carefully taken into account. In light of the ongoing discussion on sustainable investments, our research helps clarify the precise circumstances in which green bonds might be positive for firms, which is also relevant to policymakers.
