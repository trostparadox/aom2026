AI Rhetoric, AI Substance, and Firm Valuation: Evidence from Capital Markets

STR
Jun Yang (University of North Carolina at Greensboro); Yonghong Liu (University of North Carolina at Greensboro); Lei Ju (Dongbei University of Finance and Economics)
This study examines whether and how capital markets interpret and respond to firms’ artificial intelligence (AI) related signals. Drawing on signaling theory and the attention-based view of the firm, we distinguish between hard AI substance (e.g., AI patents), soft AI substance (e.g., voluntary disclosure of AI investment), and AI rhetoric (e.g., firm narratives regarding AI). Using a panel of 31,053 firm-years of Chinese A-share listed firms between 2018 and 2024, we examine how the interaction between AI rhetoric and different types of AI substance influences firm valuation. We find that the interaction between AI rhetoric and soft AI substance is positively related to future firm valuation, indicating that AI rhetoric enhances firm valuation only when accompanied by high voluntary disclosure of AI investments. The interaction between AI rhetoric and hard AI substance on firm valuation is not significant. Our findings suggest that, in complex and difficult-to-verify technological domains such as AI integration, capital markets rely on internally consistent and easily accessible signals rather than costly verification of underlying capabilities. This study contributes to research on strategic signaling by demonstrating how the form of technological signals determines firm valuation.
