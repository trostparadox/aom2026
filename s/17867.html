Inclusive Work Environments: A Dance Between Structure and Flexibility

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Dustin T Weilbach George Mason University; Brian Petrucci George Mason University; Sarah Wittman George Mason University

Session overview

The key to workplace inclusion is an equilibrium between organizational structure and flexibility, yet naturally emerged structures could be at odds with the ideal work environments for optimal performance as organizations seek to increase consistency at the expense of individual expression. We examined the approaches taken to navigate the tension between structure and flexibility for neurodiverse employees, a group that has historically faced disproportionate challenges in the workplace and therefore often benefit from extensive adaptation and adjustment of organizational structure. In the context of an AI data-labelling company that has a disproportionately large number of neurodivergent employees, 60%, and recently grown from approximately 20 to 120 employees, we observed onboarding for all new employees for six weeks, and conducted interviews with legacy employees including management to understand how an organization with accelerated growth with a unique population handles the dance between structure and flexibility. Ongoing data analysis has suggested that the tension and balance between structure and flexibility stretch across multiple domains of the work environment, such as time, tasks, technology, information, and social domains.
