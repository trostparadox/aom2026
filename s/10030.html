To Stay or to Leave? Prolonged Pendency and Inventor Enthusiasm

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Dohun Lee Seoul National University; Hyunwoo Park Seoul National University

Session overview

Does prolonged patent examination discourage inventors from pursuing further innovation? Using disambiguated inventor data from PatentsView, we examined how pendency duration affects subsequent patenting behavior. Ordinary least squares estimates suggested a negative association, but this may reflect selection bias if more experienced inventors receive faster examination while being more productive. To address endogeneity, we employed art unit (technology-specific examination group) leave-one-out mean pendency as an instrumental variable, exploiting quasi-random variation in examination workloads. Our first-stage confirmed strong instrument relevance, and exclusion restriction is supported by conditioning on technology fixed effects. Two-stage least squares estimates revealed heterogeneous effects: for novice inventors, one standard deviation increase in pendency reduces subsequent patents by 10.1% of mean output; for early-career inventors, 10.4%; and for veterans, 6.1%. These findings suggest that examination delays disproportionately discourage early-career inventors who lack resilience or financial resources to buffer against prolonged uncertainty. Our study contributes to innovation policy by highlighting differential impacts of patent office efficiency on inventor motivation across career stages.
