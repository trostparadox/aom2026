Vulnerability at Work and Human Resource Management

HR
Mónica Santana (Pablo de Olavide University); Susana Pasamar (Pablo de Olavide University); Alvaro Lopez Cabrales
Research on work and employment has increasingly engaged with vulnerability as a central concern, yet the human resource management (HRM) literature addressing vulnerable workers remains theoretically fragmented and dispersed across multiple themes and perspectives. This study provides an integrative analysis of how vulnerability has been conceptualized and examined within HRM research over time. Grounded in a relational and dynamic understanding of vulnerability, we conceptualize it as a condition shaped by interactions between labor market structures, organizational practices, and individual resources. We adopt a mixed-method research design that combines bibliometric performance analysis and science mapping using SciMAT with a period-based qualitative content analysis of key themes and clusters. This approach enables a longitudinal examination of publication trends, citation impact, thematic structures, and conceptual evolution, while also offering qualitative insight into shifts in theoretical framing and substantive focus. Our findings reveal a rapidly expanding literature increasingly structured around HRM as an integrative core, with dominant themes related to migration, aging, disability, diversity, precarious work, and emerging forms of employment such as platform and gig work. Over time, the field has shifted from group-based and descriptive approaches toward more relational, institutional, and practice-oriented analyses. By clarifying the intellectual structure and evolution of HRM research on vulnerable workers, this study advances theory on vulnerability at work and highlights promising directions for future management research.
