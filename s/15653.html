Five Carriers, One Strategy? Vertical Integration and Isomorphism in Container Shipping

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3
OMT

People

Author: Murat Atalay Akdeniz University

Session overview

While the digital age is often associated with organizational anisomorphism and strategic uniqueness, the global container shipping industry tells a markedly different story. This study examines a striking paradox in which the world’s top five container carriers converge on highly similar vertical integration strategies despite fundamentally different organizational origins and governance structures. Drawing on a qualitative multiple case study of COSCO SHIPPING, MSC, CMA CGM, Maersk, and Hapag-Lloyd, the paper challenges the assumption that efficiency considerations alone explain this strategic shift. Instead, it advances the argument that the industry is experiencing a contemporary revival of institutional convergence, conceptualized as a Renaissance of the Iron Cage. The findings demonstrate equifinality, showing how structurally distinct firms arrive at comparable strategic outcomes through different institutional pathways. While Maersk’s integration strategy reflects normative pressures to redefine professional standards, COSCO’s expansion is shaped by coercive, state-driven mandates linked to national supply chain security. In contrast, Hapag-Lloyd exhibits mimetic behavior, engaging in strategic imitation under conditions of heightened uncertainty. Overall, the study argues that vertical integration in container shipping has evolved from a discretionary strategic option into an institutionalized requirement for legitimacy and resilience in an increasingly volatile global environment.
