Session overview

MSR Scholars are often drawn to questions of meaning, flourishing, consciousness, ethics, and transformation, questions that are complex and multi-level. While MSR scholarship engages spiritual, philosophical, and experiential dimensions of organizing, some scholars find that existing methodological frameworks do not fully support the inquiry these questions invite, particularly when research spans inner experience, relational dynamics, organizational systems, and broader societal contexts. Transdisciplinary approaches are increasingly attractive, yet often remain loosely specified or difficult to enact in concrete research practice. This PDW introduces an operationalized approach emerging from Transformative Studies, a research lineage grounded in systems and complexity thinking that frames inquiry as creative, reflexive, and integrative. Transformative Studies emphasizes developing researcher capacity to engage complex phenomena by integrating multiple ways of knowing while remaining open to emergence. The workshop offers MSR scholars a structured yet flexible inquiry method supporting transdisciplinary research at the intersection of spirituality, management, and societal transformation. This methodology is being developed through doctoral research at the California Institute of Integral Studies, and this workshop serves as collaborative space for exploration and reflexive engagement. Participants experience the foundational practice of writing about phenomena as lived experience before engaging literature. This demonstrates how genuine inquiry emerges from curiosity rather than literature gaps and how positionality enriches rather than biases transdisciplinary research. Small groups work with participants' actual research questions, followed by facilitated reflection.
