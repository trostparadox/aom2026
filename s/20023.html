Session overview

Markets are often modeled as dependent coordination devices built on formal infrastructures: contracts, regulation, disclosure regimes, and price systems that aggregate information into efficient outcomes. Yet, we have good reasons to doubt that these formal tools constitute the full story. Many recent episodes of substantial economic consequence—from meme-stock surges and platform monetization pivots to the rise, expansion, and backlash of ESG and the boom-and-bust cycles of digital assets—cannot be easily understood without grappling with informal market infrastructure. Even in markets that appear decentralized and information-rich—anonymous trading environments, platform-mediated exchange, and diffuse investment fields—coordination depends on informal market infrastructure: the relational and meaning systems that enable collective action when hierarchy, contracts, and formal authority are weak, absent, or contested. This symposium advances a perspective on the durability and fragility of coordination through social infrastructure in markets. Informal structures such as cohesive networks, embedded relationships, and shared values can sustain coordinated behavior that departs from market fundamentals for extended periods of time, even in settings where incentives to defect are strong and information is abundant. At the same time, because these forms of coordination tend to rely on continual reinforcement rather than formal enforcement, they are vulnerable to disruption. Small shocks can unravel networks, strain relationships, or destabilize shared meanings, producing rapid cascades of defection, breakdown, or backlash. For us, this presents the opportunity to bridge this data- and observation-driven view on finance joined with relational data on the networks constituting informal markets.
