Expertise Evaluation: A Dimensional Framework for Broad-Based Theory Buildling

OB
Maartje E. Schouten (Erasmus University Rotterdam); Aman Kabra (Oklahoma State University); Stephen Humphrey (The Pennsylvania State University)
Expertise evaluation – the interpersonal assessment of others’ domain-specific knowledge, skills, and abilities – is a critical mechanism for leveraging knowledge in organizations. Despite its importance, research on expertise evaluation is fragmented across disciplines, plagued by construct proliferation, and hampered by inconsistent terminology, limiting cumulative theory building. To address this challenge, we synthesize operationalizations from 106 studies and propose a three-dimensional framework that captures core assumptions underlying expertise evaluation: Verification (the degree to which evaluations are corroborated by objective indicators), Reference (the explicitness of the comparison frame), and Interdependence (whether evaluations are individual or shared). This framework provides a common language for scholars, clarifies conceptual tensions (e.g., perception sufficiency vs. recognition necessity; portability of evaluations; individual vs. collective assessments), and enables examination of contingent relationships among dimensions. By situating expertise evaluation within this multidimensional space, we advance theory development, improve measurement precision, and offer practical guidance for designing evaluation systems that enhance the utilization of expertise in organizations.

Why Needs Matter? From Psychological Needs to Knowledge-Management Behaviors and Task Performance

OB
Zhuofan Kan (Nanjing University); Sun Young Sung (Nanjing University)
Knowledge is a critical resource to be exchanged and managed among employees for effective functioning and performance in increasingly knowledge-intensive workplaces. Considering the social dilemmas inherent in knowledge sharing, this study adopts a multifaceted approach to employees’ knowledge management behaviors (KMBs). We propose that employees employ differentiated knowledge management strategies (i.e., sharing, hiding, and manipulation) driven by underlying psychological needs, and that the implications of these strategies for task performance may vary depending on contextual factors like task ambiguity. The findings suggest that distinct psychological needs are linked to different knowledge management tendencies, which in turn have varying consequences for employee task performance under different conditions of task uncertainty. This study advances understanding of the complex dynamics of KMBs at the individual level and provides theoretical and practical insights for managing knowledge in real-world work settings.

The Impact of Workplace Interpersonal Capitalization on Knowledge Sharing Behavior

OB
Huiying Jia (Wuhan Textile University); Chunran Lang (Wuhan Textile University)
We based on self-consistency theory explores how workplace interpersonal capitalization influences knowledge sharing behavior through organization-based self-esteem, and examines the moderating effects of competitive psychological climate and perceived organizational support. We conducted a three-wave survey with 274 employees from Chinese companies. The results show that workplace interpersonal capitalization significantly enhances employees' organization-based self-esteem and positively promotes knowledge sharing behavior. Organization-based self-esteem mediates the relationship between workplace interpersonal capitalization and knowledge sharing behavior. Competitive psychological climate negatively moderates the effect of workplace interpersonal capitalization on both organization-based self-esteem and knowledge sharing behavior, while perceived organizational support strengthens the impact of organization-based self-esteem on knowledge sharing behavior. We uncovers a new pathway through which workplace interpersonal capitalization drives knowledge sharing behavior via organization-based self-esteem. Furthermore, we extend the understanding of positive events and individual-level effects by confirming the boundary role of competitive psychological climate and perceived organizational support in shaping the impact of workplace interpersonal capitalization. In organizations with a high competitive climate, internal knowledge flow is hindered. Therefore, reducing internal competition intensity and fostering a supportive environment are necessary to activate employees' willingness to share knowledge, thereby facilitating knowledge flow.

When Expertise is Voluntarily Applied: Navigating Social Validation Through Identity Work (WITHDRAWN)

OB
Veronika Kentošová (Aarhus University)
While identity work has been well studied in organizational settings characterized by formalized roles, less is known about how individuals manage their identities in voluntary, self-selected evaluative contexts in which participation is optional and activities extend beyond organizational or role boundaries, often offering opportunities for professional enrichment. To address this gap, this study examines how experts navigate alignment and misalignment between self-verification and social validation of their expertise in public innovation contests in Canada. Drawing on 22 semi-structured interviews with contest participants, triangulated with interviews with contest jurors, departmental representatives, and contest documents, I show how pre-contest social validation of solvers’ expertise, or its absence, shapes distinct forms of identity work through which experts navigate alignment or misalignment with the validation received during the contest. Contributing to identity and social identity theory, this study reconceptualizes social validation not merely as an outcome that legitimizes or challenges expert identity, but as a lens through which experts engage evaluative contexts via distinct identity work processes. Beyond its theoretical contribution, the paper offers practical insights into the design of feedback mechanisms that support both idea or solution development and identity validation.
