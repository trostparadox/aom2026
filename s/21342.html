When GenAI Breaks the Innovation Process: Stage-Contingent Failure Modes in Team-Based Ideation

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5
TIM

People

Authors: Laura Bischoff Philipps-University of Marburg; Michael Stephan

Session overview

Generative AI (GenAI) is rapidly entering innovation work, yet most accounts emphasize productivity gains rather than the breakdowns that shape adoption and outcomes. This study asks: What failure modes emerge when teams embed GenAI across stages of a structured innovation process? We draw on an in-depth qualitative case from a German university seminar in which student teams completed a staged product-development project spanning market analysis, idea generation, and prototyping using both text and image generation tools. We analyze process documentation, intermediate artifacts, and reflective reports, applying inductive qualitative coding to identify recurring patterns. We develop a taxonomy of six failure-mode families: (1) epistemic failures (e.g., unreliable sources, shallow or biased insights), (2) IP and legitimacy failures (e.g., trademark or logo leakage), (3) context, reasoning, and control failures (e.g., misinterpretations, prompt sensitivity, output volatility), (4) creativity failures (e.g., derivative ideas, stereotype-driven imagery, novelty–feasibility tradeoffs), (5) process failures (e.g., uneven evaluation rigor, choice overload), and (6) affective failures (e.g., frustration, demotivation, reduced ownership). We derive recommendations that improve the GenAI-integration in innovation processes while limiting hidden costs. The study contributes a failure-centered lens on GenAI-enabled innovation and offers implications for organizing and governing GenAI-augmented ideation work.
