Ethnic Population and Ecosystem Responses: A Spatial Perspective on Entrepreneurship

ENT
Dongge Zhou (Kean University); Joseph J. Cabral (Louisiana State University); Han Liu; Eunah Jung (Brown University)
Research on ethnicity and entrepreneurship has largely focused on ethnic population as founders, with limited attention to how ethnicity reshapes entrepreneurial activity among other groups. Relatedly, existing studies often treat ethnic populations as static and spatially bounded attributes, overlooking how immigration patterns evolve over time and across space. Drawing on the entrepreneurial ecosystem perspective, we reconceptualize ethnic population as a contextual force that alters ecosystem components, and the ecosystems’ responses depend on historical immigration patterns and geographic boundary connectedness. Using U.S. county-level Census Bureau data from 2012 to 2017, we examine how Hispanic immigration influences non-Hispanic entrepreneurship across traditional, new, and minor immigrant destinations. Our results reveal pronounced spatial heterogeneity. Immigration has no significant effect in traditional destinations, a positive effect in new destinations, and a negative effect in minor destinations. Furthermore, among new destinations, proximity and connectedness to traditional immigrant gateways significantly amplify the positive association between immigration and entrepreneurship. These findings reveal that ethnic population functions not only as a source of entrepreneurship but also as an enabler of entrepreneurial activity of others. More broadly, the study demonstrates that entrepreneurial consequences are not uniform but contingent on the spatial and social structure of local ecosystems.
