Session overview

The rapid diffusion of data-driven systems and algorithmic tools is transforming how organisations coordinate work, allocate tasks, evaluate performance, and govern systems. While iconic cases such as Uber and other online labour platforms have dominated much of the early debate, algorithmic management now moves beyond platforms and into core organisational processes, deeply embedded in “ordinary” organisational contexts, such as internal HR analytics, performance dashboards, scheduling systems, workflow tools, and a growing family of AI-based assistants and decision-support tools. This shift raises difficult questions about agency, autonomy, control, trust, and human dignity—not only for gig workers, but also for employees working in teams, projects, and knowledge-intensive environments. It also foregrounds the intricate data work that makes algorithmic management possible: the collection, curation, integration, and repurposing of trace data, communication data, and performance data, alongside the increasingly sensitive biometric data about employees. Recent advances in AI, including generative AI (GenAI), intensifies these concerns, particularly regarding how human judgment, automated decision-making, and data interact in governing employees and organising work. This Professional Development Workshop brings together scholars from multiple perspectives to discuss how algorithmic management unfolds in organisational settings, and how data work and AI-intensive modelling may evolve going forward. The session combines lightning talks from leading researchers, interactive small-group breakouts, and a reflective panel discussion, unpacking the complexities, risks, and governance challenges of studying algorithmic management in employee environments.
