Session overview

The global automotive industry is undergoing its most profound transformation in over a century. The convergence of connectivity, autonomy, sharing, and electrification (CASE) has disrupted incumbent business models, reshaped ecosystem relationships, and attracted new entrants from technology giants to startups. This presenter symposium showcases how the global automotive sector can be used as a powerful empirical setting for examining foundational questions in strategic management. The session will begin with five paper presentations examining questions including how incumbents design corporate strategy portfolios under technological discontinuity, why innovation unfolds through ecosystem-level interdependence rather than simple displacement, how governance emerges when intellectual property is fragmented, how industry-wide learning occurs vicariously through salient safety failures, and when managerial foresight succeeds given different problem structures. The presentations will be followed by comments from the symposium discussant before general Q&A and group discussion. By engaging these issues across multiple levels of analysis, this session advances core theories of strategy under deep uncertainty, with implications for other sectors confronting turbulent technological change.
