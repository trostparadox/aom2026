From Employee Job Demands to Supervisor Meaninglessness: Role of Psychological Detachment

HR
Yunita Sofyan; Ivan Radevic (University of Montenegro)
Over the past decade, meaningful work has gained increasing attention from researchers and practitioners. However, prior studies have primarily focused on how individuals derive meaning from their work, often overlooking the conditions that erode meaningless work. Using Conservation of Resources (COR) theory and managerial identity work perspectives, we develop a novel spill over model elucidating how employee job demands indirectly increase supervisor work meaninglessness. Analysing 527 matched employee-family-supervisor triads from diverse Montenegrin organizations, we find that employees facing high job demands engage in counterproductive work behaviours, which in turn heighten supervisors’ work meaninglessness. However, this effect weakens when employees psychologically detach from work after hours. Our findings suggest that employees under significant job demands not only exhibit harmful behaviours but also undermine their supervisors’ well-being. This study contributes to research on meaningless work, managerial identity work, and work-life spill-overs by elucidating how and when job demands negatively affect organizations.
