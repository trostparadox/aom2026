How Startups Balance Novel and Efficient Business Model Design Though GenAI: A Paradox Perspective

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Deping Ye South China University of Technology; Feng Zhang South China University of Technology; Jing A. Zhang University of Otago; Ting XU Southern University of Science and Technology

Session overview

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is rapidly diffusing in entrepreneurial work, promising gains in creativity and productivity while also triggering paradoxical tensions rather than uniformly positive outcomes. Drawing on paradox theory, we examine how GenAI influences business model design (BMD) in startups, where firms must balance novelty and efficiency under resource constraints and legitimacy pressures. We further theorize that the outcomes of GenAI-enabled BMD depend on top management-based conditions that shape how such tensions are enacted. Specifically, we focus on paradoxical leadership as a socio-discursive process and ethical dilemmas as a mental template that delineates moral boundaries for innovation exploration. We test our arguments using a scenario-based laboratory experiment with 154 participants and 56 independent experts, and the data were analyzed using mixed effects models. The results show that GenAI is positively associated with both novel and efficient BMD. Moreover, paradoxical leadership exhibits a U-shaped moderating effect on the relationship between GenAI use and novel BMD, and a positive moderating effect on efficient BMD, whereas ethical dilemmas weaken the relationship between GenAI use and novel BMD and show no significant effect on efficient BMD. This study advances research on GenAI, business model innovation, and paradox theory by explaining how top management-based boundary conditions shape paradoxical tensions, thereby leading to divergent BMD outcomes in startups.
