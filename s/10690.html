Authors: Jia Wun Chen Soochow University; Ling-Yu Tsai

Session overview

Drawing on the Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, this study examines how middle-aged and older employees’ perceptions of a developmental climate for older workers influence their retention intentions. A longitudinal study was conducted with 265 full-time employees aged 45–65 in Taiwan, collecting data across two waves. Results show that elder employees’ perceptions of a developmental climate are positively associated with retention intentions, and this relationship is fully mediated by relational psychological contracts. Furthermore, the moderated mediation effect of future time perspective on this indirect relationship is significant. For employees with a longer future time perspective, the mediated relationship via relational psychological contracts is significant, whereas it is not for those with a shorter perspective. These findings clarify the mechanisms by which perceived developmental climate enhances retention intentions among middle-aged and older employees, with practical implications for organizations seeking to build age-friendly workplaces and promote sustainable workforce participation.
