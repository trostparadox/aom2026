Making Books Global: How Trade Intermediaries Construct Prestige Hierarchies in Publishing

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
OMT

People

Author: Kwelina Thompson Sloan MIT

Session overview

Cultural goods are produced in local languages, institutions, and markets, yet many cultural industries are increasingly organized around claims about “global” success, “world-class” quality, and international prestige. How do fields construct and stabilize global status hierarchies when products and audiences remain fundamentally national and culturally heterogeneous? This paper examines how trade intermediaries in the publishing industry actively produce a sense of global comparability and hierarchy through discourse, categorization, and brokerage. Focusing on the U.S. trade magazine Publishers Weekly and its extensive coverage of international markets, international book fairs, and translation, the study analyzes how regions, languages, and literatures are narrated and positioned within a global publishing field. I argue that global prestige orders are constructed through intermediary practices that translate cultural specificity into commensurable status signals. Drawing on a qualitative analysis of several decades of trade discourse, the paper identifies recurring narrative frames. The study contributes to research on status, valuation, and fields by theorizing how intermediaries construct global status orders in markets characterized by deep cultural and linguistic fragmentation.
