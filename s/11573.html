Session overview

Neurodivergent individuals comprise 15–20% of the global workforce yet face unemployment rates of 30–40%—a gap reflecting not capability deficits but misalignment between conventional HR systems and diverse cognitive processing styles. This 90-minute professional development workshop addresses this critical equity and business opportunity by equipping HR professionals, educators, and organizational leaders with evidence-based strategies to support neurodivergent employees across the entire employee lifecycle. Grounded in experiential learning and design thinking methodologies, the workshop engages participants in interactive problem-solving centered on realistic workplace scenarios: inclusive hiring practices, neuroinclusive onboarding, retention and advancement strategies, workplace accommodations, performance management approaches, and leadership development pathways. Through an extended icebreaker highlighting cognitive diversity, participants explore how different ways of thinking—detail-focused, big-picture, pattern-seeking, narrative-driven, and sensory-sensitive—strengthen collaboration and innovation. The workshop's centerpiece features facilitated small-group design thinking activities where participants progress through the full Empathize-Define-Ideate-Prototype-Test cycle to develop practical, research-informed solutions. Following group report-outs, facilitators synthesize participant insights with evidence-based best practices, connecting neurodiversity scholarship to actionable HR strategies across recruitment, onboarding, culture, accommodations, and leadership. The session concludes with comprehensive individual and organizational action planning, enabling participants to identify quick wins, short-term initiatives (2–3 months), and longer-term goals (6–12 months) for translating workshop insights into concrete organizational change. By integrating HR research, inclusive design principles, and organizational change practices, this workshop reframes neurodiversity not as a limitation to manage but as a strategic asset for innovation, resilience, and organizational strength. Participants leave with deepened understanding of neurodiversity, practical tools for inclusive HR system design, and renewed commitment to advancing neuroinclusive workplaces.
