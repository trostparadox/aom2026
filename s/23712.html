Labyrinth of Work: Autistic Employees’ Career Development

DEI
Subhashini Poornima Abeysekara (La Trobe University); Jennifer R. Spoor (La Trobe University); Shalinka Jayatilleke (La Trobe University); Susan Hayward (Swinburne University of Technology)
Autistic adults tend to have poor employment outcomes, including fragile and unstable careers. Stereotypes, stigma, and normative expectations in the world of work often shape these outcomes. This study examines how autistic employees define and experience career development and how organizational systems shape their career trajectories. We analyzed 31 semi-structured interviews with autistic and non-autistic employees and managers/supervisors of autistic employees in Australia using abductive thematic analysis. All participants valued opportunities for growth, recognition, and meaningful work. However, autistic employees described career pathways marked by inconsistency, unclear expectations, and limited access to advancement. Their progress often depended on chance access to supportive environments rather than on structured opportunities, described by one participant as “luck of the draw.” These findings reframe career development from individual responsibility to an indicator of an organization's readiness to support diverse talent. The study contributes conceptually by applying the misfit lens to career development, spotlighting the organizational role that either enables or constrains autistic employees' career development. This helps create an inclusive organizational environment that enables all employees to flourish in the workplace.
