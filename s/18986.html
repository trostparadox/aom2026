Session overview

This PDW explores who gets recognized as a theorist in management scholarship and how we might broaden both the people and practices involved in theorizing. Although our field publicly celebrates theoretical pluralism, prevailing norms and publishing practices continue to privilege masculinized, abstract, and procedural forms of theory-building. These norms distance us from lived experience and from the many practitioners, participants, and communities who already engage in sophisticated, situated theorizing outside universities. Drawing on virtue epistemology and recent critiques of theorizing in OMT, we examine how intellectual virtues such as open-mindedness, care, courage, and reflectiveness sit uneasily with dominant expectations of “strong theory,” construct clarity, and standardized templates. We outline four recurring dynamics—ontological blindness, epistemological defensiveness, hegemonic masculinity, and myopic self-referentiality—together with an underlying assumption that theory is the exclusive domain of professional academics. The PDW combines brief provocations from scholars thinking through alternative means of theorizing with small-group work in which participants map who counts as a theorist in their own projects and design concrete experiments for more inclusive, human-centered theorizing. The session will culminate in a draft “Manifesto for Human Theorizing” and a network of scholars committed to expanding theorizing practices within OMT and beyond.
