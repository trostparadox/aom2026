Indigenous Entrepreneurship as a Decolonial innovation System

Author: GERTRUDE MENSAH University of Ghana

Session overview

Mainstream entrepreneurship and innovation theories are largely rooted in Western, individualistic paradigms, leaving a critical gap in understanding how innovation operates in indigenous contexts of the Global South. This conceptual paper proposes “indigenous entrepreneurship as a decolonial innovation system” as a new theoretical lens to address this gap. We begin by critiquing dominant frameworks such as Schumpeterian disruption and resource-based views, highlighting their limitations and colonial blind spot in non-Western contexts. We then build a decolonial foundation drawing on indigenous epistemologies, community-centred knowledge systems, non-extractive innovation logics, and relational worldviews. This foundation informs a novel conceptual framework illustrating how indigenous innovation systems function through communal values, cultural knowledge, and sustainable practices. Illustrative examples from Ghanaian, Kenyan, and Andean indigenous entrepreneurship demonstrate these principles in action. We further propose key theoretical propositions to guide future research. The paper highlights contributions to innovation and entrepreneurship theory by reframing value creation, opportunity, and development from a Global South perspective. It argues that embracing a decolonial innovation system framework is urgently needed for scholars to enrich theory with diverse epistemologies, and for policymakers to design inclusive, context-sensitive innovation strategies.
