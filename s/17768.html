Psychological Contract Breach and Critical Organizational Outcomes in the Hotel Sector

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9

Authors: Olivia Mensah Louisiana State University; Anthony Sarkodie Amankwah Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology

Session overview

Hotel employees’ positive service behavior is essential to organizational success. This is because the customer impressions of service quality are intrinsically tied to the employee-customer interactions, which forms an integral part of the service provided. This study explores the relationships between psychological contract breach, on-the-job embeddedness affective commitment service sabotage, and turnover intention with a focus on the sequential mediation of on-the-job embeddedness and affective commitment in the hotel sector. Data were gathered from 411 respondents in a three-phase survey and PROCESS macro was used to test the proposed hypotheses. The findings revealed that psychological contract breach positively influences service sabotage but negatively influences on-the-job embeddedness and affective commitment and turnover intention. Moreover, on the-JE and affective commitment were found to directly reduce service sabotage and turnover intention. On-the-job embeddedness and affective commitment sequentially mediated the psychological contract breach-service sabotage and the psychological contract breach-turnover intention relationships. Implications for research and practice will be discussed in the poster presentation.
