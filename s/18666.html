What Are You Up to This Week? How Couple Planning Affects Work and Family Outcomes for Dual Earners

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 10

People

Authors: Sophia Miri Yoo University of Minnesota; Seola Kim University of Minnesota; YoungAh Park University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign; Theresa M. Glomb University of Minnesota

Session overview

Although dual-earner couples often manage their work and family lives as interdependent partners, research has paid limited attention to how couples jointly engage in active management of daily work and family responsibilities. Drawing on transactive goal dynamics theory, we develop and test a theoretical model examining how couples’ joint planning of their weekly schedules (i.e., couple planning) influences dual earners’ work and family outcomes. We propose that weekly couple planning enhances coordination with partner, which in turn facilitates more effective management of work and family demands. Using a five-week experience sampling study, we found that weekly couple planning was positively associated with coordination with partner. Enhanced coordination, in turn, predicted better work-family balance effectiveness, more quality time with partner, and higher relationship satisfaction. We also examined attachment avoidance as a moderator of these relationships. Contrary to expectations, attachment avoidance strengthened—rather than weakened—the effects of couple planning on coordination and subsequent outcomes. By conceptualizing couple planning as a dyadic regulatory process, this study advances a relational perspective on work–family management.
