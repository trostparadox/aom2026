Audience-Specific Strategic Framing of Digitalization Processes Across Knowledge Divergences

TIM
Rebecca Alguera Kleine (Witten/Herdecke University); Marc André Scheffler (Witten/Herdecke University); Felix Lorenz (Witten/Herdecke University); Matthias Waldkirch (EBS University of Business and Law); Thomas Clauss (Witten/Herdecke University)
Organizations undergoing digitalization often experience an expansion of knowledge, introducing distinct knowledge types into the organization, which refers to the domain-specific knowledge of actors associated with their roles, experiences, and functional backgrounds. Consequently, firms have to deal with knowledge divergences among various actors involved in digitalization, who hold different roles ranging from strategic decision-making to implementation. As a solution, the strategic framing literature suggests that actors use strategic framing to achieve knowledge convergence. We challenge this view, as it overlooks the beneficial effects of knowledge divergence, which facilitates complementary perspectives and skills. This study investigates how actors use strategic framing to manage persistent knowledge divergence during the digitalization process. Through a multiple-case study of 15 incumbent firms engaged in digitalization processes, we identify strategic framing tactics: leveraging, bridging, and equalizing knowledge divergence. Actors shape three distinct outcomes of knowledge coordination: they instigate unidirectional actions, foster polyphonic actions, or cultivate shared understandings, without needing to converge. Our findings extend research on strategic framing by shifting the focus from achieving convergence to coordinating knowledge polyphony. Therefore, we deepen the understanding of micro-strategies that enable action across different knowledge types. Additionally, we enrich digitalization research by highlighting the symbolic and cognitive challenges associated with expanding the scope of knowledge.

Digital Business Strategy as a Catalyst for Knowledge Digitization: Moderating Role of Risk

TIM
Abdul Hameed Pitafi (Sir Syed University of Engineering and Technology); Jahangir Ahsan (Sir Syed University of Engineering and Technology); Muhammad Athar Nadeem (University of Science and Technology of China)
The impact of the digital industry and firm digitization on innovation has become a significant focus in research. Prior studies have focused on how digital business strategies (DBS) affect value creation, financial performance, and operational efficiency; however, fewer studies has been conducted to examine how these approaches affect product innovation and strategic competitive performance. By investigating the ways in which digital business strategies impact both strategic competitive performance and product innovation, this research attempts to address this knowledge gap. Employing the resource-based approach, the study investigates the fundamental processes by which these tactics produce these results. Data from 481 Chinese enterprises was acquired and structural equation modeling was used for analysis. The findings indicate that a DBS positively influences knowledge digitization, but does not significantly impact digital knowledge transfer management. Additionally, both knowledge digitization and digital knowledge transfer management are shown to positively affect strategic competitive performance and product innovation. Organizational innovation intensity mediates the relationship between DBS and both strategic competitive and product innovation performance. Furthermore, digital platform risk is found to dampen the effect of DBS on knowledge digitization. This study provides valuable insights into how DBS impact strategic competitive performance and product innovation, mediated by organizational innovation intensity and digital transformation management

Digital Knowledge Sharing Capability : Pathway to Sustainable Competitive Edge

TIM
Shazia Nauman (Riphah International University); Mahreen Malik (Ghent University); Sadaf Noor (Riphah International University); Muhammad Zain Tahir (Riphah International University)
Employing knowledge-based dynamic capabilities theoretical framing, this research investigates the effect of digital knowledge sharing capability on sustainable competitive advantage directly and through business model innovativeness. This study also investigates digital business capability as a first stage boundary condition in digital knowledge sharing capability—business model innovativeness relationship. This study used quantitative time-lagged research design to collect data at two points in time. The authors collected data from 208 employees working in IT organizations of Pakistan. The results confirm that digital knowledge-sharing capability is positively linked to sustainable competitive advantage via business model innovativeness. The results also reveal that digital business capability strengthens the digital knowledge-sharing capability—business model innovativeness relationship. The originality of this paper lies in integrating knowledge-based view and dynamic capabilities theory through the novel KBDC framework to explain sustainable competitive advantage in the digital era. Moreover, the study uniquely examines business model innovativeness as a mediating mechanism and digital business capability as a boundary condition within Pakistan’s IT context.

SDK Portfolios as Learning Strategies: Evidence from Mobile App Developers

TIM
Dadian Qu (University of Georgia)
Developers increasingly rely on software development kits (SDKs) to build and maintain mobile applications, yet little is known about how SDK adoption choices shape learning and performance outcomes. Drawing on organizational learning theory, we conceptualize developers’ SDK portfolios as learning strategies along an exploration–exploitation continuum. Using a large-scale dataset of mobile app developers, we examine how similarity in SDK portfolios across apps relates to app performance. We find that SDK similarity is positively associated with app performance, indicating benefits from exploitative SDK portfolio strategies, but that this relationship is nonlinear. Beyond moderate levels, higher SDK similarity yields diminishing and eventually negative performance returns. We further show that developer experience and environmental dynamics condition this relationship, such that very high SDK similarity is less detrimental for more experienced developers and in more dynamic environments. Finally, we explore update intensity as a behavioral mechanism linking SDK portfolio configurations to performance. Together, our findings highlight how SDK portfolio configurations function as learning strategies in digital development and inform the management of technological reuse and diversity.

Setting the Beat: Knowledge Characteristics and Strategic Rhythms of Digitalization

TIM
Fangyan Niu (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Yaqun Yi (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Penghao Qian (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
Despite recent evidence emphasize the process view of digitalization and its implication for firm performance, understanding of how firms organize digitalization over time remains limited. Drawing on the attention-based view, this study theorize that a firm’s knowledge structure shapes managerial attention allocation, thereby producing distinct digitalization rhythms, with these effects contingent on market leadership. Using panel data from Chinese A-share listed manufacturing firms, we find that knowledge variety is associated with increased frequency and variability in digitalization while knowledge complexity reduces both, with these impacts moderated by managerial myopia. Collectively, our study enriches the process view of digitalization by identifying key antecedents of digitalization rhythm. It also advances the attention-based view by showing how knowledge structural attributes shape strategic attention allocation in digital contexts and by extending attention-based outcomes beyond static choices to the temporal dynamics of strategic behavior.
