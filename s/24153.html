Human-AI Behavioral Convergence; An Equivalence Perspective

CTO
Fabrice Cavarretta (ESSEC Business School)
Artificial intelligence systems now replicate human behavior with undistinguishable fidelity across diverse domains. This behavioral equivalence challenges organizational theory built on human-centric assumptions. We develop a historical-organizational theory explaining how modern AI achieved what three earlier paradigms (1940s-1990s) failed to accomplish. Classical symbolic AI, information processing psychology, and cybernetics encountered fundamental incommensurability between deterministic computation and probabilistic human cognition. Modern AI overcomes this through neural architectures, natural language processing, and experience-based learning—realizing Wiener's cybernetic prophecy. Yet behavioral equivalence creates irreducible paradoxes: organizations face automation-augmentation tensions, professional knowledge reconfiguration, and agency redistribution while AI lacks relational expertise, operates opaquely, and raises alignment challenges.
