Dynamic Links Between Perceived Job Search Progress and Job Search Intensity: Goal Difficulty Change

CAR
Jinke Qu (University of Science and Technology of China); Qingxiong Weng
Drawing on a self-regulation perspective, we develop and test a dual-path model in which perceived job search progress simultaneously activates (a) self-enhancement via upward recalibration of goal difficulty and (b) effort withdrawal via reduced discrepancy-based urgency. Using 10-week weekly panel data from 347 active job seekers and analyzing lagged relations with multilevel structural equation modeling, we find that perceived progress positively predicts subsequent goal difficulty change, which in turn predicts higher job search intensity. This indirect effect is primarily driven by goal difficulty up-regulation. In contrast, after accounting for goal difficulty change, perceived progress shows a negative direct association with subsequent search intensity, consistent with effort withdrawal when discrepancies are perceived as shrinking. Further, between-person differences in long-term perceived employment opportunities strengthen the positive linkage between goal difficulty change and job search intensity, yielding a stronger conditional indirect effect among individuals who perceive more favorable employment opportunities. Implications for dynamic job-search self-regulation and goal management interventions are discussed.
