Rethinking Meaningful Work Under Institutional Fragility in Chile's Public Education

SIM
Francisca Alvarez Figueroa (Pontificia Universidad Católica de Valparaíso)
Research on meaningful work has largely conceptualised it as an individual, positive psychological experience shaped by job design, autonomy, or self-realisation. This presentation advances a sociological reorientation by examining how meaningful work is constructed under conditions of institutional fragility. Drawing on interviews with managers, professionals, and administrative staff across Chile’s territorial education administrations: pre-existing municipal education departments (DAEMs) and newly created Local Public Education Services (SLEPs), the study shows that meaningfulness emerges not despite institutional breakdown, but through workers’ efforts to compensate for it. The findings identify three interconnected processes. First, workers navigate organisational fragility produced by persistent institutional informality in DAEMs and over-formalisation without operational capacity in SLEPs. Second, administrative failures expose workers to sustained moral encounters with social vulnerability, positioning them as the primary interface between the state and marginalised communities. Third, workers construct meaning through compensatory practices in which boundary-stretching, custodial labour, and moral commitment stabilise systems unable to function autonomously. Theoretically, the presentation demonstrates that meaningful work can be generated through institutional absence rather than institutional support, relocating the source of meaningfulness from job characteristics to workers’ socially organised efforts to hold fragile public institutions together. By foregrounding meaningful work as a collective, moral, and structurally induced labour process, the study contributes to sociological debates on work, institutional fragility, and public sector employment under conditions of austerity and reform.
