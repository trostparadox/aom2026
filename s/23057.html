Dynamic Capabilities as Leverage for Slack Resources: Evidence from SMEs’ International Performance (WITHDRAWN)

IM
Tim Heubeck (TU Dortmund University); Patrick Held (University of Bayreuth); Reinhard Meckl (University of Bayreuth)
This study examines whether and under which conditions slack resources enhance the international performance of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Focusing on unconstrained slack as a highly discretionary and readily deployable resource, we argue that its performance implications depend on SMEs’ dynamic capabilities. Drawing on survey data from internationalized German SMEs, we show that (1) unconstrained slack has a positive effect on international performance; (2) dynamic capabilities significantly amplify this relationship; and (3) this amplifying effect differs across capability dimensions, with transforming capabilities exerting the strongest influence. These findings suggest that SMEs’ unconstrained slack resources contribute to their international performance only when supported by sufficiently developed capability structures, thereby identifying an important boundary condition in the slack–performance relationship. By specifying how distinct dynamic capabilities enable SMEs to translate discretionary slack into international performance outcomes, this study contributes to theory and provides actionable guidance for SME managers seeking to deploy slack more effectively in international markets.

Institutional Distance and Alliance Scope

IM
Majid Abdi (The University of Sydney)
Interfirm collaboration across institutional distance involves legal and socio-cultural complexities, arising from the absence of shared formal and informal frameworks. We identify alliance scope—the range of value chain activities included in a partnership—as a critical design lever to manage these complexities. A broader scope facilitates private ordering or self-enforcement, which becomes critical when formal distance compromises external enforcement mechanisms. Conversely, informal distance disrupts shared cognitive frameworks and administrative compatibility, imposing integration constraints that restrict the feasibility of broad collaborations. Furthermore, we find that alliance experience facilitates scope broadening in response to formal distance while offsetting the restrictive effects of informal distance. Examining partnerships of publicly listed U.S. firms over 1988-2020, we provide empirical support for these relationships. Our study positions alliance scope as a mechanism that enables firms to navigate the systematic tensions inherent in institutionally distant cross-border partnerships.

Market-Oriented Resilience and International Marketing Competence

IM
Chenxin Xie (Tsinghua University); Zuohao Hu (Tsinghua University)
Ever-changing global markets are fundamentally transforming the international marketing operations. While building resilience can help firms cope with uncertainty and complexity in external markets, the concept of resilience has received limited attention in the international marketing domain. This study focuses on market-oriented resilience, examining how its behavioral and cognitive dimensions contribute to international marketing competence. Based on empirical analysis, we find that market-oriented behavioral resilience and cognitive resilience both significantly enhance international marketing competence. Our results further reveal that market turbulence attenuates the positive effect of behavioral resilience but strengthens the impact of cognitive resilience. Additionally, employee autonomy reinforces the potency of behavioral resilience but weakens the role of cognitive resilience. This study extends the theoretical perspective of resilience to the research on internationalization strategy while offering practical guidance for firms to pursue sustainable competitive advantage in dynamic international environments.

Brand Management Efficiency of Developed and Emerging MNE Subsidiaries and Domestic Firms in Türkiye

IM
Erkan Bayraktar (Gulf University for Science and Technology); Ekrem Tatoglu (Turkish Academy of Sciences (TÜBA)); Sunil Sahadev (Sheffield Hallam University); Mehmet Demirbag (University of Essex)
This study investigates the efficiency of brand management practices (BMP) among firms with different levels of foreign ownership in emerging countries (EC) markets. Drawing on strategic, cultural, and institutional perspectives, the study focuses on Türkiye and examines domestic firms, EC MNE subsidiaries (represented by Turkish MNE subsidiaries), and developed country MNE (DC MNE) subsidiaries. Using survey data from 176 firms and applying data envelopment analysis (DEA), the study reveals significant differences in BMP efficiency across these firm groups. The findings show that DC MNE subsidiaries, despite exhibiting higher levels of BMP adoption, demonstrate lower efficiency in converting these practices into business, operational, and brand performance outcomes. In contrast, domestic firms and EC MNE subsidiaries display higher efficiency, reflecting stronger alignment between brand management practices and local market conditions. By identifying specific inefficiencies in brand management implementation, the study contributes to a deeper understanding of how foreign ownership influences the effectiveness of branding strategies in institutionally and culturally complex environments. The findings offer important implications for firms operating in Türkiye and other emerging markets facing similar global–local challenges.
