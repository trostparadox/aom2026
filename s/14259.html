Session overview

Social evaluations such as legitimacy, reputation, celebrity, infamy, stigma, and status are constructed through the perceptions of external evaluators and bestowed upon the entities being perceived. Variations both between and within these evaluators and their perceptions naturally lead to differences in their respective social evaluations, which can be impactful for the people and organizations involved. While early work in social evaluations tended to consider evaluators as homogenous, scholars have more recently acknowledged the crucial role of their complexity. Our understanding of their nuances, however, remains to be fully explored. This symposium highlights five novel papers at the frontier of examining differences in evaluators and their perceptions, which contribute to our understanding of their respective social evaluations.
