Trading Justice: How the EU Deforestation Regulation is Reshaping Uganda’s Coffee Sector

ONE
Samuel Tang (UCL - University of London)
Trade-related environmental regulations, such as the European Union Deforestation-free Products Regulation (EUDR), aim to reduce the environmental impacts of the global expansion of commodity supply chains. However, their implementation in smallholder-dominated systems may generate unintended social and economic consequences. Drawing on 67 semi-structured interviews with stakeholders across Uganda’s coffee value chain, this study examines the distributive, procedural, and recognition justice implications of the EUDR. While stakeholders broadly supported the regulation’s goals, they identified challenges in traceability, compliance costs, and sector readiness, particularly for smallholders, intermediaries and SMEs.. Limited consultation during the regulation’s design undermined procedural justice, and the failure to consider smallholders’ agroforestry practices raise recognition justice concerns. These design features risk further market consolidation and marginalisation of vulnerable actors, potentially undermining the regulation’s environmental objectives. We propose context-sensitive approaches that leverage existing intermediary networks, strengthen local capacity, and integrate incentives for sustainable production, highlighting lessons for the design of just and effective trade-related environmental regulations globally.
