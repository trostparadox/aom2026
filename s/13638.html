Session overview

The purpose of this PDW is to help faculty transition into academic leadership roles. Faculty members selected or asked to serve in academic leadership roles have very little formal training and often struggle to resolve challenges and capitalize on opportunities. Whereas leadership in academia is often characterized as a ‘poison chalice’, this PDW explores the benefits of pursuing an academic leadership career and opportunities to ‘make a difference’ for institutional, societal and community stakeholders. Participants will explore how to balance leading others whilst maintaining their own research pipeline and wellbeing. They will also be informed about the global challenges facing academic communities and the role leaders play in shaping institutional cultures. This PDW is open to faculty who are currently serving as chairs, program directors, associate deans, and/or those who will be taking on such leadership roles in the future. This two-hour workshop includes a leadoff plenary session with two speakers highlighting key challenges and opportunities in academic leadership. The plenary session will be followed by breakout sessions moderated by five academic leaders. The PDW also reflects diverse experiences, across several countries. Breakout sessions then explore: (1) Creating and Capitalizing on Career Opportunities in Academic Leadership (2) Leading, Evaluating and Mentoring faculty (3) Managing ‘Up’ and “Down,” and (4) Career Sustainability and Individual Wellbeing in Academic Leadership Roles. The workshop will end with a wrap-up session to recap and summarize emerging issues, explore perceptions of ‘best practice’ in academic leadership, and facilitate an open Q & A session.
