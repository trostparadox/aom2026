Recovery as a Multiteam Phenomenon: Leadership Fragmentation and Employee Well-Being

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12
OB

People

Author: Kayla Nichole Kozak University of South Carolina

Session overview

Multiple team membership (MTM) has become a defining feature of contemporary work, yet existing research offers limited insight into how leadership operates in multiteam structures where employees simultaneously report to multiple supervisors. Prior work on recovery and supervisor support largely assumes a single-leader context, leaving unanswered how recovery-supportive behaviors function when leadership is structurally fragmented across teams. Building on MTM theory and leadership spillover research, this study develops a multilevel framework that conceptualizes recovery support as an inherently multiteam phenomenon shaped by the distribution, salience, and spillover of leadership across team memberships. We argue that recovery is not solely influenced by an employee’s average level of supervisor support, but rather by MTM-specific features such as the presence of highly supportive “core” leaders, the inconsistency of recovery norms across teams, and the extent to which recovery-supportive behaviors spill over from one team context to others. In doing so, the study reframes recovery in MTM as a structurally embedded process rather than an individual-level resource dynamic. This framework advances MTM research by identifying leadership fragmentation as a novel boundary condition for recovery processes and extends leadership theory by demonstrating how supervisor behaviors can differentially shape employee functioning across concurrent team contexts. The study offers implications for designing sustainable multiteam systems that support employee well-being without undermining the productivity benefits of MTM.
