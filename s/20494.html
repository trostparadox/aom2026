Motherhood and Self-Employment Transitions

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Himani Singh University of Mannheim

Session overview

Research has recognized self-employment as a potential career path for mothers seeking to balance professional ambitions with family responsibilities. While existing work has established that motherhood facilitates entry into self-employment, scholars have treated motherhood as a uniform experience, overlooking how family and child characteristics may influence mothers’ self-employment trajectories. This study examines whether child gender influences mothers’ transitions into and out of self-employment. Drawing on perspectives that emphasize time as a resource and research on gendered patterns in household division of labor, this work proposes that child characteristics may affect the temporal resources available to mothers, thereby impacting their likelihood of self-employment entry and exit. We test this proposition using longitudinal data from the German Socio-Economic Panel (1985-2021), analyzing mothers’ self-employment entry and exit decisions over time. Findings suggest that child gender is significantly associated with mothers’ self-employment transitions. These findings contribute to research on gendered career paths by highlighting how household-level dynamics impacts women’s self-employment.
