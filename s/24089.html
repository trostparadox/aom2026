Populism and Sustainable Packaging: Insights from Consumer-Packaged Goods Manufacturers

IM
Martin Heinberg (University of Leeds); Mustapha Belkhouja (Grenoble Ecole de Management); Hyungseok David Yoon (University of York)
While sustainable marketing practices have traditionally been grounded in stakeholder capitalism, the rise of anti-climate-change populism presents new challenges to firms’ sustainable strategies. Drawing on resource dependence theory, this study argues that anti-climate-change populism generates political uncertainty, prompting firms to reduce sustainable marketing practices—specifically, the use of environmentally friendly packaging in new product launches. However, we propose that subnational formal and informal institutions, such as economic freedom and cultural tightness, attenuate this relationship. To test these hypotheses, we employ a quasi-natural experimental design, using the announcement of the United States’ exit from the Paris Agreement to analyse its impact on sustainable marketing practices, based on a global sample of product launches by consumer-packaged goods manufacturers. Our difference-in-differences analysis supports these hypotheses. This study advances the understanding of the dynamic relationship between populism and subnational institutions, highlighting how different institutional pillars shape firms’ sustainable marketing strategies.
