Session overview

The act of teaching DEI in business school in 2025–2026 is unfolding amid intensifying backlash, legislative constraints, and resource pressures which may be creating a chilling environment. At the same time, students are interested in serious engagement with equity, identity, and power in organizations, and we know that managers and organizations need to understand and incorporate DEI principles to be effective. As DEI teachers, we face politically charged classroom environments, and rapidly evolving technologies such as generative AI that can both enable and erode critical, equity-focused learning. The rapid changes are prompting pedagogical innovations to address the current moment of teaching DEI, which is what we plan to discuss. This workshop brings together six teachers from the US, Canada, and Australia to share their innovative approaches highlighting a range of strategies for “making lemonade” by engaging skeptical or conservative audiences through rigorous evidence, incorporating non-Western epistemologies, and reframing backlash. The session will include time for facilitated table discussions to surface participants’ current classroom dilemmas, teaching strategies, and ways they are supporting both students and DEI faculty across global contexts. Participants will leave with clearer strategies, concrete tools, and comparative insights for teaching DEI in turbulent political and institutional contexts. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
