Reputation as a Policy Effectiveness Metric: Machine Learning Insights from Crisis Policy

OMT
Homa Molavi (The University of Manchester)
Global health crises demand policy responses that not only safeguard public health but also maintain institutional credibility across diverse stakeholder groups. This study introduces a comprehensive reputation prediction framework to evaluate the effectiveness of global health policies by analysing how stakeholders perceive and respond to crisis communication strategies. Using a Hybrid Multi-layer Stacked Ensemble Machine Learning model, we assessed 26,644 social media posts and reactions to COVID-19 policy communications from Russell Group universities as a case study for wider global health governance. The methodology integrates advanced natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and behavioural pattern recognition to measure policy effectiveness through reputation indicators and stakeholder engagement metrics. Findings reveal a predominant trend of neutral stakeholder sentiment, signalling communication gaps that undermine the credibility and perceived effectiveness of health policies during crises. This neutrality highlights the reputational risks associated with insufficient transparency and information asymmetry, which may foster environments conducive to misinformation and reduced trust. The study underscores the limitations of traditional reputation measurement methods in real-time policy evaluation, offering instead a data-driven framework that establishes quantifiable metrics for communication effectiveness and predictive modelling for reputation management. By identifying critical communication strategies that either strengthen or weaken stakeholder confidence, this research advances new avenues for assessing global health policy effectiveness. It further introduces AI-driven research directions, including predictive crisis response modelling, stakeholder interaction optimisation, and strategies to address neutral perceptions. The framework thus provides a scalable and interdisciplinary approach to evaluating global health policies, bridging gaps between machine learning, communication science, and health governance.
