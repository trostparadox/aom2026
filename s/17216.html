Speak Up Alone or Stay Silent Together: How Shared Anger Reshapes Voice Under Abusive Supervision

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9

People

Author: Yujun Sha City University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Although anger toward unfair treatment is often theorized as a catalyst for employee voice, empirical findings regarding the anger–voice relationship remain mixed. Drawing on research on collective emotions, this study proposes that collective anger serves as a critical contextual boundary condition that shapes when anger arising from abusive supervision motivates or suppresses voice behavior. We conceptualize collective anger as a shared emotional appraisal within a team and argue that employees interpret their own anger and behavioral options in light of others’ emotional cues. Using three-wave, multilevel data collected from 165 employees and 47 supervisors in a large retail organization, we test a moderated mediation model in which collective anger simultaneously moderates (a) the relationship between abusive supervision and individual anger and (b) the relationship between anger and employee voice. Results show that collective anger strengthens the positive association between abusive supervision and anger, while reversing the behavioral consequences of anger for voice. Specifically, anger is positively related to voice when collective anger is low but negatively related to voice when collective anger is high. Moreover, the indirect effect of abusive supervision on voice through anger is positive under low collective anger and negative under high collective anger. By integrating abusive supervision, anger, and voice literatures through a collective emotion lens, this research resolves long-standing inconsistencies regarding the anger–voice relationship and highlights the importance of shared emotional contexts in shaping employee reactions to mistreatment.
