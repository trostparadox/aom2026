Internal Knowledge Flows: The Transformative Role of AI Agents

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Linda Arben Shehu University of Lausanne; Katherine Tatarinov University of Geneva

Session overview

As generative AI becomes embedded in everyday organizational work, its effects on interhuman collaboration may be as consequential as its productivity gains, yet remain underexamined. AI agents are increasingly deployed across functions, raising questions about how they reshape coordination, expertise, and decision-making. This study investigates how these agents reconfigure internal knowledge flows in four Swiss financial institutions, a setting marked by strong regulation, entrenched hierarchies, and functional silos. Based on a qualitative cross-case analysis, we find that banks initially implemented in-house AI agents to meet privacy and security requirements, but they quickly evolved into centralized knowledge hubs that reorganize how institutional knowledge is produced, accessed, and validated. Vertically, agents reduce dependence on hierarchical channels by enabling direct access to internal documents and procedures. This speeds up onboarding by compressing “time-to-know”, giving employees rapid access to guidance and institutional know-how that previously flowed through senior mediation, while also risking weaker mentorship and contextual judgment. Horizontally, agents make functional boundaries more permeable by enabling employees to retrieve and interpret cross-departmental information without routine peer interaction, streamlining coordination but potentially diminishing informal knowledge exchange. These shifts surface tensions around trust, accountability, and role clarity as responsibility can drift in AI-mediated workflows. We contribute to the literature on Human–AI Collaboration by theorizing a core trade-off between efficiency and the relational mechanisms that sustain knowledge sharing and build on boundary spanning theory by showing how AI agents act as digital boundary spanners, signaling a shift from socially embedded to digitally mediated communication and collaboration.
