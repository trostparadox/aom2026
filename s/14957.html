Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is designed to help educators bring entrepreneurship education into everyday classroom practice through concrete, accessible teaching tools. Moving beyond abstract theory, the workshop emphasizes instructional approaches that can be readily embedded into existing courses to promote entrepreneurial thinking, experimentation, and problem-solving. Participants will engage with a range of practical methods, including effectuation-based activities and a variety of classroom-applied exercises designed to encourage experimentation, reflection, and experiential learning across disciplines and institutional contexts. The session also examines how intentional choices in course design, such as assignments, discussions, assessments, and learning structures, can significantly strengthen entrepreneurial learning outcomes. An expert panel of distinguished entrepreneurship scholars and practitioners, including Saras Sarasvathy (University of Virginia), Satish Nambisan (Case Western Reserve University), Russ Browder (The University of Oklahoma), and Erin Scott (MIT), will share classroom-ready examples, teaching materials, and insights drawn from their academic and entrepreneurial work. The workshop includes interactive teaching demonstrations enabling participants to engage deeply with the content and actively explore and adapt tools they can immediately apply in their own teaching.
