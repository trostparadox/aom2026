Resolution Strategies for Paradoxical Sustainability Tensions from a SC Manager’s Perspective

OSCM
Hashim Refaat Ahmed Mokbel (University of Sussex); Nachiappan Subramanian (University of Sussex); Biao Yang (University of Sussex)
Sustainability tensions are pervasive in supply chain management (SCM), as managers in focal organisations are increasingly expected to pursue economic, environmental, and social objectives simultaneously. Although prior research recognises these tensions, empirical studies rarely operationalise the degree of sustainability tension or rigorously test the effectiveness of resolution strategies such as separation and synthesis. Drawing on paradox theory, this study addresses this gap by examining how perceived sustainability tensions influence the adoption of resolution strategies in SCM. The research proceeds in two stages. First, we develop and validate a novel measurement scale for paradoxical resolution strategies, addressing a critical methodological limitation in the sustainable supply chain management literature. Second, we test the hypothesised relationships using data from a large-scale cross-sectional survey of 193 supply chain managers from pharmaceutical companies in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, and the United Arab Emirates. The results show that sustainability tensions have a positive and significant influence on the adoption of both separation and synthesis strategies, indicating that managers respond to paradoxical demands through complementary differentiation and integration mechanisms rather than a trade-off logic. By extending paradox theory to an under-researched regional and sectoral context, this study advances the empirical understanding of sustainability tensions in SCM and provides robust measurement tools for future quantitative research on resolving sustainability paradoxes.
