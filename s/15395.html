Feeling Uncertain: The Impact of Emotions on Entrepreneurial Action

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

Authors: Dian de Ridder Tilburg University; Lien Denoo Tilburg University; Rene Bakker Tilburg University; Joshua Gabriel Eckblad Tilburg University

Session overview

Entrepreneurs act even when feasibility evaluations are limited or absent. Building on the work of McMullen and Shepherd (2006), we theorize that emotions can influence the desirability of an action and can serve as a substitute when feasibility evaluations are weak or absent. Moreover, we argue that these desires are accompanied by the entrepreneurs' willingness to bear uncertainty. Accordingly, positive emotions, such as hope, increase desirability and tolerance for uncertainty, while negative emotions, like fear, can either attenuate or amplify these effects. Furthermore, we argue that emotional intensity shapes these effects asymmetrically: gains tend to follow a concave pattern, while losses display a convex pattern. To test our theories, we propose two pre-tested vignette experiments that control for feasibility and examine how emotions influence desirability and willingness to bear uncertainty. Our findings would contribute threefold. First, we would advance entrepreneurial action theory by decoupling willingness from uncertainty evaluation and desirability. This allows us to use emotions to demonstrate that entrepreneurs can act under constrained or abandoned feasibility assessments. Second, we would contribute to uncertainty research by theorizing that entrepreneurs navigate uncertainty through their emotions. Lastly, we would add to the understanding of affect by showing that emotions directly influence the willingness to bear uncertainty, rather than merely moderating uncertainty evaluations. From a practical standpoint, the results would suggest that entrepreneurs are motivated to act because it feels compelling and meaningful, using emotion as a reason for action, even when their capabilities and constraints are unclear.
