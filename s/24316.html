How Rivals’ Environmental Penalties Shape Proactive Environmental Disclosure of Focal Firms

STR
Ying Zhang (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Zijun Ye (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
Research in organizational impression management has primarily focused on how firms react to existing negative events that happened to themselves. However, why and how firms take action in response to potential adverse consequences from the outside, such as the environmental penalties imposed by rivals, without being attacked themselves, is less well understood. Focusing on environmental governance, our research is based on the deterrent effect of environmental regulation. We argue that the environmental penalties imposed on rivals can trigger the impression management motivation of the focal firm to avoid potential adverse consequences. Therefore, focal firms will enhance the quality of their environmental information disclosure, and this response is closely associated with the inter-organizational geographic distance. Furthermore, state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and media exposure of rivals will further reinforce the primary effect. We use the sample of Chinese A-share listed companies from 2010 to 2022, to explore the impact of rivals' environmental penalties on the environmental disclosure quality of the focal firms. The results and robustness checks support our predictions.
