Managerial Values and Human-AI Collaboration: A Case Study of Online User Reviews Generation

CTO
Pei Ma (Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
Online user reviews are central to platform governance in service markets, yet their decision value is often diluted by generic and low diagnostic content. As generative AI begins to assist review writing, platforms face an upstream design challenge that prior human–AI collaboration research has largely overlooked: how managers allocate roles and decision rights between users and AI when authenticity and credibility matter. Using a longitudinal single case study of OrderJoy’s AI assisted review system (2023–2025), we trace two contrasting development phases. A technology led team initially pursued automation and ease of use, positioned AI as a substitute for users, and encountered failure due to generic output, hallucinations, and weakened trust. A subsequent business led cross functional team redesigned the system around human–AI complementarity, using AI to prompt decision dimensions while preserving user authorship and grounding design choices in user feedback, which improved review participation and quality. We develop a process model in which occupation based managerial values operate as group level subjective norms that shape AI system design through two mechanisms, goal orientation and technological ethics, thereby determining whether human–AI collaboration becomes substitutive or complementary. The study contributes a design centered account of human–AI complementarity in platform governance and clarifies how managerial values become embedded in AI adoption outcomes.
