Session overview

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have expanded creative capabilities, often suggesting that human–AI collaboration can outperform human creativity alone (e.g., Dellermann et al., 2021). This study revisits the fundamental importance of enhancing human creativity itself by examining how entrepreneurial creativity emerges, deepens, and is sustained over time, and how this process relates to individual well-being. Drawing on Mumford’s (2003) definition of creativity as the generation of ideas that are both novel and useful, this study conceptualizes entrepreneurial creativity as a developmental process grounded in individual knowledge formation. Based on in-depth, semi-structured interviews with nine highly distinctive entrepreneurs across diverse domains, this study adopts a Grounded Theory Approach to trace creative experiences from early life to current entrepreneurial practice. The findings reveal a cyclical process in which early formative experiences foster deep respect for nature, materials, and human practices, leading to enhanced insight, dialogical engagement, and the capacity to materialize ideas. Over time, intrinsic passion evolves into a sense of purpose and social mission, generating sustained creativity and enduring well-being. This study contributes by theorizing a process model of sustained entrepreneurial creativity and highlighting its implications for entrepreneurship education and human development.
