Teaching ESG Through Projects: A Practice-Based Learning Model in Engineering Education

Session overview

As sustainability expectations increasingly shape project-based work, higher education faces the challenge of preparing future engineers and project managers to engage with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations in practical and decision-oriented ways. While ESG is often addressed at the level of institutional strategies or policy frameworks, students frequently struggle to connect sustainability concepts with the realities of project planning, trade-offs, and accountability. This teaching-oriented poster presents a practice-based learning model developed within project management courses at the Faculty of Technical Sciences. The model shifts the focus from institutional ESG integration to course-level teaching design, embedding ESG considerations directly into project-based learning activities. Students engage with sustainability through realistic project scenarios that require balancing cost, risk, stakeholder expectations, and governance constraints alongside traditional project objectives. The poster outlines the structure of the teaching model, key learning activities, and the role of reflection in supporting the development of sustainability-oriented project mindsets. Rather than presenting empirical evaluation results, the contribution lies in offering a transferable teaching design that supports experiential ESG learning in engineering education. The poster aims to support educators seeking practical approaches to teaching sustainability through projects rather than alongside them.
