Prior Relational Imprints and Post-Divestment Performance of Acquired MNE Subsidiaries

IM
Xinyue Zhang (York University)
Multinational enterprises' (MNEs) divestment of operations from a country creates opportunities (i.e., access to resources) and challenges (i.e., effective use of these resources) for local firms that acquire these assets. This study examines the conditions under which local firms can effectively exploit the assets acquired from foreign MNEs by theorizing how distinct relational imprints established through prior interactions with the foreign divesting firm influence post-divestment competitiveness. Empirical results based on a sample of 394 MNE divestment cases in five countries during twenty years (2005-2024) show that cooperative imprints developed through prior joint venture ties enhance post-divestment competitiveness. In contrast, control-oriented imprints formed through prior acquisition ties hinder acquirers' ability to integrate and leverage divested units. When both types of imprints coexist, their conflicting relational legacies generate tensions that partly offset the benefits of cooperative experience.

Addition by Subtraction? Effects of Value Creation Strategic Emphasis on Asset Divestments

IM
Tae Young Park (Yonsei University); Yong-Suhk Pak (Yonsei University)
This study attempts to rethink corporate asset divestments from the perspective of strategic emphasis. Instead of focusing of divestments as a reactionary response to failure, we examine how divestment decisions are proactively pursued depending on internal strategic directions that emphasize opportunistic reconfiguration. Accordingly, we argue that divestment activity increases depending on whether a firm pursues value creation (VC) strategies over value appropriation (VA) strategies, as VC pursuing firms are more likely to reconfigure their assets to further continue their innovative performance. To further distance asset divestments from its concept as a performance-based reaction, we also examine the moderation effects of internationalization and firm financial performance. Based on an analysis of 457 publicly listed firms across 27 countries from 2012 to 2021, we find that firms pursuing a VC strategy exhibit higher levels of asset divestments than firms pursuing VA strategies. In addition, internationalization levels showed negative moderation effects while financial performance showed positive moderation effects. Our results show that firms may pursue divestments much more proactively than previously thought. Further implications are discussed at the end of the manuscript.

Discursive Legitimation Strategies and Cross-Border Acquisition Completion and Duration

IM
Lulu Yan (Zhejiang University of Technology); Cong Cheng (Zhejiang University of Technology); Jian Dai (Zhejiang University of Technology); Changwei Guo (University of Science and Technology of China)
Cross-border acquisitions (CBAs) are increasingly contested in host-country public discourse, yet prior research has predominantly adopted a demand-side perspective that focuses on how external stakeholders raise legitimacy challenges. Much less is known about the supply side of legitimation—namely, how acquiring firms strategically deploy discourse to shape legitimacy judgments and acquisition outcomes. Drawing on a discursive legitimation perspective, we examine how acquirers exercise discursive agency through two distinct strategies—rational and emotional appeals—and how their effectiveness is conditioned by host-country situational contexts. We show that both forms of discursive legitimation are, on average, associated with higher acquisition completion likelihood and shorter completion duration, but their efficacy is contingent on situational characteristics. Situational strength amplifies the effectiveness of rational appeals while diminishing the marginal value of emotional narratives, whereas situational variability introduces interpretive noise that systematically attenuates the impact of firm-led discourse, irrespective of appeal type. By foregrounding the supply-side dynamics of discursive legitimation and identifying the situational conditions under which discursive strategies succeed or fail, this study advances international business research on organizational legitimacy and discourse.

From Divestment to Springboard: Light-Touch Strategies for Foreign-Invested Enterprise Acquisitions

IM
Yuyao Nie (Tsinghua University); Zhenzhen Xie (Sun Yat-Sen University)
Unlike traditional cross-border acquisitions, acquiring divested foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs) provides local firms with a unique, low-cost pathway to access international capabilities within their domestic market. We argue that local acquirers proactively adopt light-touch integration—maintaining target autonomy while providing complementary resources—to preserve potential innovative sources of distant knowledge recombination by capabilities of both target and acquirer. Using propensity score matching and difference-in-differences methods with Chinese listed company data (2008-2023), we find that compared to domestic acquisitions, FIE acquisitions exhibit significantly higher target independent innovation without corresponding increases in collaborative or integrated innovation. This light-touch tendency intensifies when targets possess stronger innovation quality and higher international innovation capabilities, and when acquirers have richer overseas experience and are located in coastal/metropolitan region. Our supplementary tests confirm that the light-touch integration is not passive compromise but informed strategic choice enabling acquirers to effectively learn international knowledge and enhance exploratory innovation while preserving targets’ recombinatory capabilities. Our findings extend the springboard perspective to foreign divestment contexts, revealing that foreign divestment can create strategic opportunities for host country firms’ capability building when managed through appropriate integration strategies.

Foreign Divestment and Domestic-Firm Productivity: A Resource-Redeployment Perspective

IM
Hyewon Ma (Indiana University Bloomington); Joseph Clougherty (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign)
We examine how foreign divestment shapes the productivity dynamics of local businesses. Building on the resource-redeployment perspective, we argue that divestment releases locally-embedded resources that can be reallocated by domestic firms to generate efficiency gains. Employing Orbis data on ownership and financials for Chinese establishments over the 2007–2019 period, we construct a region–industry measure of foreign-divestment intensity and link this construct to firm-level total factor productivity. Using System-GMM estimations, we find higher exposure to foreign divestment to be associated with modest but robust productivity improvements amongst domestic firms. The evidence thus indicates that global disinvestment triggers local renewal rather than uniform decline. In addition to extending IB research beyond the implications of foreign-investment inflows to the often-overlooked consequences of their reversals, our study reframes foreign divestment as a redistributive shock that enables structural adjustment in local economies.
