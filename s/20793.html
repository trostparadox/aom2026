Detecting and Deterring LLM-Based Cheating in Ability Tests via Warnings and Facial Validation

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Tori Bakeman Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University; Louis Hickman Virginia Tech

Session overview

The emergence of large language models (LLMs), such as those accessed through ChatGPT, has raised new concerns for unproctored, high-stakes assessments. Applicants can easily use LLMs to obtain external assistance, potentially undermining test validity. Drawing on ability-motivation-opportunity theories of faking, we examine how integrity warnings and webcam-based facial validation influence test-taker behavior and performance in the LLM era. Using a large field sample of job applicants completing a cognitive ability test for selection, we test whether cheating-related behaviors and test performance changed following the release of chat-based LLMs and whether procedural deterrents mitigate these effects. Mean test scores were modestly higher following ChatGPT’s release, while cheating-related behaviors increased substantially. Integrity warnings reduced cheating-related behaviors without decreasing scores, whereas facial validation reduced both behaviors and performance. Together, these findings suggest that LLM-related validity threats manifest more strongly in behavior than in score inflation and that targeted deterrents can effectively reduce problematic behaviors.
