From Individual Heuristics to Organizational Capabilities: Building AI-Embedded Cancer Diagnosis

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 6

People

Authors: Zhiyu Zhang University of Nottingham; Gongtao Zhang Nottingham Univercity Business School; Kim Hua Tan Nottingham Univercity Business School

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in cancer imaging workflows, yet comparable tools can produce widely different organizational outcomes across hospitals. To explain this variation, we adopt a microfoundational lens, integrating dynamic capabilities with the 'simple rules' perspective. Heuristics are simple rules that are articulated, shared “rules of thumb” that reduce uncertainty and coordinate workflows across organisations. An interpretivist, inductive single-case qualitative research was conducted in a public tertiary hospital in China that has embedded an AI-enabled imaging system into routine cancer diagnosis for over six months. Data comprise 37 semi-structured interviews with radiologists, clinicians, IT specialists, and managers, supplemented by workflow observations and internal documents. Gioia-informed analysis with temporal bracketing traces how AI-related practices and heuristics evolve across diagnostic stages. Findings identify a cross-role heuristic portfolio including case routing rules, trust-and-override rules, double-reading triggers, and accountability and auditability rules. We develop a process model revealing how individual heuristics are selected, codified, and institutionalized into organizational capabilities, ultimately stabilizing AI-embedded workflows. This analysis indicates that AI benefits depend on aligning and institutionalizing heuristic portfolios across units, offering practical levers for safer and more reproducible AI-enabled cancer diagnosis.
