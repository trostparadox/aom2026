Halo Shelter: Low-Carbon City Pilot Policy and Corporate Environmental Information Disclosure

STR
Kaicheng Liao (Chinese Academy of Social Sciences); Jizhen Li (Tsinghua University); Zhibin Lin (Durham University); Guanhua Wang (Tsinghua University)
Governments worldwide increasingly rely on policy interventions to enforce corporate environmental information disclosure (CEID). The prevailing view in institutional theory suggests that such regulations create pressure that compels firms to enhance transparency to secure legitimacy. Challenging this “regulation-as-pressure” paradigm, we posit that high-status regional policy designations can inadvertently create a “legitimacy buffer” that crowds out firm-level disclosure. Exploiting China’s Low-Carbon City Pilot (LCCP) policy as a quasi-natural experiment, we find that the implementation of this command-and-control regulation significantly reduces the level of environmental information disclosure among firms in pilot cities. We conceptualize this phenomenon as a legitimacy substitution effect, where the “green halo” conferred by the regional policy endorsement substitutes for the need for costly private signaling. Mechanism analyses reveal that this substitution is driven by policy-induced managerial moral hazard (manifested as over-investment) and dampened media scrutiny. The inhibitory effect is most pronounced among non-state-owned enterprises and young firms, who have stronger incentives to free-ride on the regional reputation. Our study contributes to the information disclosure literature by identifying a “regulation-as-shelter” mechanism and highlights the unintended consequences of static policy designations in environmental governance.
