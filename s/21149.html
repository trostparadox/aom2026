Session overview

In this symposium, we bring together a set of theoretical and empirical papers that examine the processes underlying the use and perceptions of deception in organizations. Specifically, the papers in this symposium consider how deception and deception perceptions are shaped by intrapersonal and interpersonal processes, including moral disengagement, bounded ethicality, socialization, and ambiguous interpersonal cues. The papers examine these processes across diverse contexts, including negotiations, performance evaluations, third-party representation, and strategic interactions. Thus, this symposium advances the behavioral ethics literature by offering new insights into the psychological and social processes that give rise to a particularly problematic form of unethical behavior in business.
