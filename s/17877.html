Fitting In and Going Beyond: Person-Group Fit, Strivings, and Organizational Citizenship Behavior

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Jacob Whitney Kennesaw State University; Christina Li University of Oklahoma

Session overview

Person-group fit, or perceived compatibility with one’s workgroup, is tied to helping and individual initiative as forms of organizational citizenship behavior. Yet, this link is often treated as uniform, overlooking that person-group fit consists of distinct social and task components that may motivate different forms of contribution toward the group. In this study, we examine how social person-group fit and task person-group fit relate to helping and individual initiative via unique group members’ strivings. Using a time-lagged, multisource survey of 106 employees working in group settings, we find that social fit is positively related to helping via communion striving, while task fit is positively related to individual initiative via accomplishment striving. We also show that task fit weakens the indirect effect of social fit on helping. We briefly discuss implications for fit research and managers leading groups.
