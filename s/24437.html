To Contrast or to Assimilate: The Interpersonal Consequences of Organizational Career Growth

OB
Ying Wang (Anhui University); Jing Zhao (Anhui University); Xiao Yuan (Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics)
While existing research has extensively examined how organizational career growth (OCG) influences employees’ own work outcomes, its paradoxical interpersonal effects on colleagues remain underexplored. This study extends this literature by integrating social comparison theory to examine how employees’ OCG triggers both cooperative and competitive responses from colleagues. Using multi-wave, multi-source data from 121 focal employees and 449 colleagues, we tested our hypotheses. Results from cross-level analysis indicated that (1) employees’ OCG triggers contrastive responses in colleagues, which induces colleague job insecurity and consequently leads to social undermining; (2) employees’ OCG shapes assimilative responses of colleagues, which motivates colleague self-improvement and ultimately enhances task performance. This study advances OCG research beyond an individual-centric framework and offers practical guidance for managing its unintended social consequences.
