Session overview

The Behavioral Theory of the Firm (BTOF) has had a foundational impact on the development of much work in both organization theory and strategy. We seek to build an annual PDW that will be the touchstone of BTOF theoretical development. The PDW will link past research with future opportunities, both narrowly within the BTOF and, more broadly, reaching out to research domains in which the BTOF is a substantive building block. We provide a discussion forum where scholars can develop a shared understanding of BTOF research and insights for developing research to advance theory. AOM 2026 will represent the sixth edition of the PDW. In 2026, the PDW will focus on the role of emotion in the BTOF. Although the BTOF characterizes organizational decision-making as a boundedly rational process in which cognitive and structural constraints often take precedence over explicit consideration of emotions, its core concepts implicitly acknowledge several ways that emotions influence organizational decisions. For example, in Cyert and March's (1963) seminal book, frustration and the drive to eliminate it lurk behind the idea of problemistic search, just as joy and playful exploration underlie the notion of slack-driven search. In later work on how low performance influences decisions (March and Shapira, 1992; Audia and Brion, 2007), threat-induced emotions such as anxiety and fear lie behind shifts of attention across reference points and goals. In recent years, however, we have witnessed a shift from implicit acknowledgment to explicit consideration of emotions' influence—suggesting that current BTOF work may have more to offer than a view of "Tin Man" decision makers devoid of heart (Levinthal and Newark, 2023). We will engage with some of these latest advances that directly consider the integration of cognition and emotion within the BTOF conceptual framework. The Part-I session will highlight four speakers, each addressing various aspects of how emotions contribute to advancing the BTOF. The Part-II session will be roundtable discussions.
