Session overview

Work motivation and performance remain critical challenges for organizations. This symposium presents five empirical investigations examining psychological approaches to enhancing these outcomes across diverse contexts. The first paper tests superordinate framing as a cognitive intervention for creating meaning in routine work, demonstrating that helping workers see how mundane tasks interconnect enhances job meaningfulness. The second paper introduces "progress steadiness" as a novel dimension of goal monitoring, showing that unsteady progress undermines motivation even when total progress is constant. The third paper presents a field experiment demonstrating that emotion regulation training improves newcomer socialization and generates spillover benefits for veteran coworkers through reduced emotional contagion. The fourth paper examines sequential versus parallel goal pursuit through longitudinal experiments, finding that sequential constraints increase completion of high-priority goals, particularly when those goals are difficult. The final paper presents a randomized controlled trial examining what role models should communicate, revealing that combining learning strategies with anti-stereotyping messages produces the largest performance gains but also creates performance-well-being trade-offs. Together, these papers advance understanding of how cognitive reframing, goal structuring, emotion regulation, and message design influence motivation and performance, while revealing important boundary conditions and potential unintended consequences.
