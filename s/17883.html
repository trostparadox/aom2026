Exploring the Effects of Gender on Transactional and Transformational Leadership in College Athletes

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Madison Hintze La Salle University; Patrick Coyle La Salle University

Session overview

In this study, we integrate transformational leadership theory with social role theory to explore how leadership behaviors vary as a function of both coach gender and athlete gender. Using qualitative data from semi-structured interviews with 15 collegiate coaches representing male, female, and mixed-gender teams, results suggest male athletes tend to respond more favorably to transactional leadership approaches emphasizing structure, competition, and performance accountability, whereas female athletes demonstrate stronger engagement under transformational leadership behaviors centered on relational trust, collaboration, and supportive communication. Across both groups, clear expectations and consistent feedback emerged as universally important, though enacted through different leadership mechanisms. These findings highlight the importance of adaptive leadership in collegiate athletics and contribute to leadership research by clarifying how gender shapes leadership effectiveness in applied sport settings.
