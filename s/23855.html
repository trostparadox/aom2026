Effects of Lay Beliefs about Creative Process on Creative Engagement Strategies and Creative Outcome

OB
Tong Wu (Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences); Lin Lin (Central University of Finance and Economics); Xinwen Bai (Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences)
Creativity depends not only on ability or motivation, but also on how individuals organize their engagement in the creative process. Although creative process engagement is a well-established predictor of creative performance, less is known about how people prioritize different creative activities under resource constraints. The present research examines how creative process beliefs—beliefs about how creativity unfolds—shape behavioral prioritization during creative work and, in turn, multidimensional creative outcomes. Across three complementary studies, we focus on two creative process beliefs: the Insight Model and the Production Model. Results indicate that these beliefs systematically guide prioritization between preparation-focused and generation-focused behaviors. Endorsing the Insight Model was associated with higher novelty, fluency, and flexibility, whereas endorsing the Production Model was associated with higher feasibility. Moreover, match between creative process beliefs and behavioral priorities further enhanced performance on creativity dimensions closely tied to creative processing. Together, these findings show that creative process beliefs function as action-guiding frameworks that organize creative process engagement and shape the configuration of creative outcomes. Rather than one belief being universally superior, different beliefs support different creative strengths, underscoring the importance of matching creative strategies with individuals’ underlying understandings of the creative process.
