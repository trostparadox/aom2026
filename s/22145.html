Session overview

The accelerating diffusion of artificial intelligence (AI) across service contexts is profoundly reshaping organizational forms, practices, and value creation processes, thereby challenging foundational assumptions in organization and management theory. While extant research has predominantly conceptualized AI as a technological resource, decision-support tool, or efficiency-enhancing artifact, considerably less attention has been devoted to understanding how AI-based systems reconfigure services as socio-technical arrangements embedded in broader organizational and institutional contexts. As a result, critical theoretical questions regarding agency, coordination, accountability, and organizing in AI-enabled services remain insufficiently explored. This PDW seeks to advance a theory-driven and critically reflexive research agenda on AI in Services that moves beyond instrumental and technology-centric perspectives. We conceptualize AI not merely as an enabling technology, but as an integral socio-material actor that reshapes service practices, alters actor constellations, and redefines boundaries between humans, organizations, and algorithms. Drawing on theoretical lenses such as practice theory, institutional theory, socio-materiality, and process studies, the PDW provides a structured forum for participants to refine conceptual frameworks, articulate novel research questions, and position their work within broader OMT debates. The workshop is designed for both junior and senior scholars interested in theorizing AI-enabled services and aims to foster the development of rigorous, theoretically grounded research projects that contribute to ongoing conversations in organization and management theory.
