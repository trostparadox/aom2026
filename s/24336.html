From Prediction to Insight: Random Forest Evidence on Turnover Antecedents Across Firm Sizes

HR
Sangsuk Oh; Justin Ji (Towson University); Owwon Park; Jong-Seok Cha (Hansung University)
This study uses a prediction-oriented, abductive approach to examine firm-level predictors of collective turnover in South Korea. Using the Human Capital Corporate Panel (HCCP), we train a random forest classifier to categorize firms into low- versus high-turnover groups and identify the predictors that best distinguish them. We evaluate model performance and rank variables using permutation importance to enhance interpretability and robustness. Consistent with Context-Emergent Turnover theory, we estimate separate models for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and large firms. Across models, the four most influential predictors are average employee education, proportion of females, turnover intention, and pay. Partial dependence plots show nonlinear, firm-size-contingent effects. Higher average education reduces the likelihood of belonging to the low-turnover group up to a threshold, especially in large firms. The proportion of females exhibits a U-shaped association, with the highest turnover risk at moderate gender diversity. Turnover intention has a more substantial impact in large firms than in SMEs, whereas pay increases are more effective at reducing turnover in SMEs than in large firms. Overall, the findings underscore the need to tailor HR strategies to firm size and workforce composition and demonstrate how machine-learning evidence can inform theory building in collective turnover research.
