Gendered Entrepreneurial Capabilities in Chinese SMEs: An RBV Perspective

ENT
Jingmin Tao (Royal Holloway, University of London); Maria Simosi (Royal Holloway - University of London)
Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO) is widely viewed as a set of capabilities enabling firms to identify and exploit opportunities, yet little is known about how these capabilities develop differently for men and women in emerging economies. Drawing on the Resource-Based View (RBV), this study examines how gender shapes EO capability development among Chinese SME entrepreneurs. Based on semi-structured interviews with 32 entrepreneurs, the findings reveal both convergence and divergence across EO dimensions. Innovativeness shows capability parity, with male and female entrepreneurs emphasising similar technological and non-technological innovation under shared institutional pressures. In contrast, risk-taking and proactiveness diverge. Male entrepreneurs develop calculative risk capabilities and interdependent proactiveness grounded in experience, guanxi, and state-linked resources, while female entrepreneurs demonstrate empathetic risk capabilities and independent proactiveness informed by emotional resources, resilience, and autonomy. This study conceptualises EO as a selectively gendered capability system and advances gendered entrepreneurship research through a context-sensitive account of capability development in China.
