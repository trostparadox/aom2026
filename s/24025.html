Climate Policy Uncertainty and Corporate Environmental Violation: A Moral Disengagement Perspective

ONE
Caini Yang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Chen Wang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Jianling Wang (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
Based on moral disengagement theory, we develop a framework to reveal the impact of climate policy uncertainty on corporate environmental violation. We argue that climate policy uncertainty leads to ambiguous corporate environmental responsibilities and allows firms to justify their unethical behavior, thus fostering moral disengagement and ultimately committing more environmental violation. We further examine two theoretically grounded moderators to test the moral disengagement mechanism: peer firms’ environmental violations serve as an unethical descriptive norm that reinforces focal firms’ moral disengagement, while local government environmental attention acts as an ethical injunctive norm that constrains it. Furthermore, heterogeneity analysis reveals that the effect of climate policy uncertainty is significantly weaker in firms led by CEOs with foreign experience or financial backgrounds, as well as in those with stronger internal and external governance systems. Our study triggers reflection on corporate moral disengagement and contributes to both research and practices by deepening the understanding of climate policy uncertainty and the psychological mechanisms underlying corporate misconduct.
