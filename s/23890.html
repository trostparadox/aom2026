Linking Servant Leadership to Service-Oriented OCB: The Role of Courage and Prosocial Motivation

OB
Anyi Chung; Szu-Ying Lee (National Changhua University of Education); Szu-Yin Lin (National Kaohsiung University of Science and Technology)
This study explores the impact of servant leadership on service-oriented organizational citizenship behaviors (SOCBs) among frontline service employees. Drawing on the self-concept-based motivational theory, this study examines the mediating effects of prosocial motivation and courage, as well as the moderating effect of formalization. Data were collected from 303 frontline service personnel in a chain supermarket in Taiwan through a three-stage survey. The results indicate that servant leadership is positively related to both prosocial motivation and courage. Both mechanisms mediate the relationship between servant leadership and SOCB. Additionally, formalization positively moderates the relationship between prosocial motivation and SOCB, as well as the indirect effect of servant leadership on SOCB via prosocial motivation. These findings offer theoretical insights into the psychological mechanisms of servant leadership and practical implications for service organizations.
