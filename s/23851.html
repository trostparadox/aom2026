When Good Inside Meets Bad Outside: Understanding Employees’ Moral Responses to CSR Misalignment

OB
Chen QIAN (University of Science and Technology Beijing); Xinran Gu
Drawing on ethical decision-making and moral compensation theories, this study investigates how corporate social responsibility (CSR) misalignment— specifically high internal CSR coupled with external corporate social irresponsibility—affects employee behaviors through distinct moral emotions. Using a mixed-method approach combining scenario-based experiments (N=240) and retrospective surveys (N=336), we find that: (1) external irresponsibility triggers employees’ moral anger and shame; 2) high internal CSR amplifies these emotional responses; 3) these emotions drive different behavioral outcomes—anger facilitates retaliatory responses (turnover and punishment intentions), while shame promotes constructive behaviors (ethical voice and prosocial behavior). This study advances CSR research by revealing employee reactions to CSR misalignment, enriches moral emotion theory by differentiating self- versus organization-oriented emotional pathways, and provides practical insights for managing external irresponsibility through internal CSR practices.
