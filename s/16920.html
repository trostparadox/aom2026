Session overview

Is AI a catalyst or a constraint for developing college students’ critical thinking and problem solving? On one hand, students are tempted to use AI to generate content for class as a substitute for developing their own critical thinking and problem-solving skills. On the other hand, AI presents unprecedented opportunities to stimulate and grow students’ higher-order (analytical, critical, and creative) thinking skills if instructors understand how to leverage it to achieve their learning objectives. The purpose of this PDW is to explore how instructors can deploy AI in the classroom to accomplish three objectives: build students’ AI literacy, improve group-based learning outcomes via active learning, and improve students’ individual-based learning outcomes through immersive and integrative learning experiences. Participants will hear from five seasoned academics who will each facilitate a highly interactive, hands-on application of experiential AI exercises they use to stimulate students’ interest and structure the development of their higher-order thinking skills. Participants will practice how to write effective prompts, explore three types of AI group activities, discuss ethical implications of AI for different course modalities, and experience an AI-facilitated exercise to challenge students’ ability to integrate and apply their knowledge about management to a practical managerial problem. Participants will engage with the presenters in a smaller roundtable format to share questions, feedback, and insights about implementing AI to teach management. Participants will leave the session with a portfolio of AI activities they can immediately deploy in their teaching to improve students’ career readiness and develop their higher-order thinking skills.
