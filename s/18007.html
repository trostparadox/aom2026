When "It's Just Business" Turns Personal

Authors: Wesley Johnson Montclair State University; Jennifer Fin Montclair State University; Alexsis Davis Montclair State University; Afsin Ara Rehman Montclair State University; Manuel F. Gonzalez Montclair State University

Session overview

Research on micro-level corporate social responsibility (CSR) demonstrates that organizational actions toward external stakeholders shapes employees’ attitudes and behaviors. However, less is known about whether CSR, and especially corporate social irresponsibility (CSiR), extends beyond organization-focused outcomes to influence individuals’ private prosocial behavior outside the organization. Drawing on micro-CSR, social identity, and moral emotion perspectives, this study examines how society-directed CSR versus CSiR shapes moral emotions, organizational attachment, and downstream private prosocial action toward a congruent external cause. We submit an ongoing experimental vignette study in which students are exposed to a realistic news article describing their university’s decision to grant community access to athletic facilities under conditions of high CSR, neutral treatment, or intentional CSiR. Following exposure, participants report discrete moral emotions (e.g., moral elevation, gratitude, anger, sympathy, guilt), organizational identification, affective commitment, and are given the opportunity to donate their participation compensation to a related youth organization. Benevolence trustworthiness is examined as a boundary condition. We hypothesize that CSR (vs. neutral and CSiR) will elicit other-praising moral emotions and strengthen organizational identification, thereby increasing private prosocial behavior, whereas CSiR will evoke other-condemning and self-condemning emotions and undermine organizational attachment. By linking CSR and CSiR to prosocial behavior outside the organization, this research extends micro-CSR theory beyond organizational citizenship and highlights moral emotions as critical mechanisms through which organizations shape individuals’ broader prosocial engagement.
