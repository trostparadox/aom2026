Ecosystem Configurations for Gender-Inclusive and Environmental Entrepreneurship

ENT
Ravi Roshan (S.P. Jain Institute of Management and Research); Krishna Chandra Balodi (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Samrat Bagchi (S.P. Jain Institute of Management and Research)
Entrepreneurial ecosystems (EEs) are increasingly being mobilized as policy instruments for fostering productive entrepreneurship; yet, most empirical assessments remain volume-oriented and overlook the societal character of entrepreneurial outcomes. This study examines how ecosystem configurations enable two distinct outcomes of growing policy relevance in emerging economies: gender-inclusive entrepreneurship and environmental entrepreneurship. Using fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) on a panel dataset of 32 Indian states over the period 2020–2023, we analyze how nine ecosystem elements—formal institutions, entrepreneurial culture, physical and digital infrastructure, finance, human capital, knowledge, demand, and intermediate services—combine to generate high levels of inclusion- and sustainability-oriented entrepreneurship. The findings show that knowledge infrastructure is the only structurally necessary condition across both outcomes, while institutional, digital, and financial elements frequently form shared enabling cores. At the same time, outcome-specific complementarities persist: physical infrastructure consistently supports gender inclusion by reducing mobility and access barriers, whereas environmental entrepreneurship is reinforced by institutional steering and digital infrastructure that can compensate for physical constraints. Taken together, these results demonstrate that entrepreneurial ecosystems do not produce high-quality entrepreneurship through a single dominant architecture, but through equifinal pathways that layer selective complementarities onto common systemic foundations. The study advances entrepreneurial ecosystem theory by shifting attention from entrepreneurial magnitude to outcome differentiation and offers policy guidance for designing coordinated yet outcome-specific interventions in emerging economies.
