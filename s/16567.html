Session overview

Research on organizational purpose has attracted significant interest in management scholarship and practice. However, our understanding of how purpose emerges, persists, and evolves is informed by research that focuses almost entirely on long-established organizations. While this context provided important insights, our understanding of the processes through which purpose emerges and becomes embedded in the early history of newly created entrepreneurial ventures is limited. Bringing purpose research into conversation with entrepreneurial contexts, processes, and mechanisms offers scholars new avenues for generating insights into how purpose shapes or becomes shaped in the context of new ventures and entrepreneurial action. For example, how founders’ purpose evolves amidst investors’ expectations and strategic reorientations, how it evolves as ventures grow, or how it shapes the evolutionary trajectory of these new ventures, offer new prospects for understanding purpose and its role in organizing. As entrepreneurs pursue their goals, research on purpose can shed light on how entrepreneurs integrate, reframe, and align goals, intentions, and actions. This PDW brings together leading scholars in entrepreneurship and organization theory from across different geographies to explore perspectives, paths, and possibilities for studying purpose in entrepreneurial settings. The panelists’ presentations will be followed by roundtable discussions with participants to explore and foster ideas for interesting, important, and impactful research.
