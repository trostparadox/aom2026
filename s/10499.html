Author: Natalia Abgaryan University of Houston

Session overview

This study examines how gender and racial dimensions of CEO identity influence full divestment decisions in multinational firms operating in conflict-affected developing countries, and how these effects are shaped by organizational tokenism and institutional maturity. Using 11,306 firm-year observations from the S&P 1500 (2014–2019) and mixed-effects multilevel logistic models with marginal analysis, we find that female CEOs are less likely to withdraw investment, especially in countries with strong institutional frameworks, while gender tokenism weakens this protective effect. In contrast, CEO race does not significantly affect divestment behavior. These findings highlight that leadership under turbulence is contingent on the interplay between executive identity, organizational visibility, and institutional context, illustrating how gendered leadership and governance strength jointly shape firms’ strategic resilience in high-risk environments.
