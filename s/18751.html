Session overview

Recent geopolitical tensions—including U.S.–China decoupling, semiconductor supply chain restructuring, and talent-related security policies—are reshaping global talent mobility and cross-border knowledge transfer. While international business (IB) research has traditionally conceptualized distance as a structural barrier rooted in cultural, institutional, or geographic differences, these developments reveal the limits of static, country-level approaches. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) advances a reorientation toward psychic distance as an individual-level friction experienced and navigated by mobile scientists and engineers in geopolitically sensitive contexts. Rather than treating distance as a uniform constraint, the workshop explores when and how distance hinders knowledge transfer, when it enables novel recombination, and how its effects vary by career stage, technological characteristics, and geopolitical salience. Using global talent mobility in the semiconductor industry as a motivating context, the workshop brings together scholars from international business, strategy, innovation, and labor mobility. Through interactive discussions and small-group activities, participants will refine core concepts, identify key mechanisms linking distance and learning, and explore promising empirical strategies for studying individual-level mobility under geopolitical constraints. The workshop is explicitly designed to support theory development, research design, and positioning for IM-oriented journals.
