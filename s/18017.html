Session overview

Third-party evaluations—such as ratings, rankings, certifications, and awards—play a central role in shaping audience attention, organizational strategies, and market structure. By translating diffuse information into standardized and comparable metrics, evaluations influence how products and organizations are perceived, how categories are defined, and how actors compete and adapt. While prior research has largely conceptualized evaluators as actors external to market exchanges, an increasing share of evaluative activity now unfolds within platform-organized markets. Platforms not only facilitate exchanges among multiple constituencies but also actively evaluate, rank, certify, and curate visibility, positioning them simultaneously as market makers and interested evaluators. This symposium brings together four papers that sit at the intersection of research on third-party evaluations and platform governance. The papers examine how evaluative practices operate when embedded within platform ecosystems and address a unifying integration question: how can platform research inform theories of third-party evaluations, and how can evaluation research, in turn, inform understandings of platform governance and ecosystem dynamics? Collectively, the papers conceptualize evaluations as market-shaping devices and platforms as governance systems, highlighting emerging theoretical issues related to legitimacy, credibility, power, and strategic intent. A concluding discussion synthesizes insights across the four papers and engages the audience in a broader conversation about how platform-based evaluations reshape governance, organizational responses, and long-term market dynamics.
