Regional Entrepreneurship Culture and Entry Rate Disparities in Japan

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Author: Masahiko Hayasaka Tohoku University

Session overview

Persistent regional disparities in entrepreneurial activity remain a central puzzle in entrepreneurship research. While cross-country differences have been widely studied, substantial heterogeneity in entrepreneurial entry persists within countries operating under shared legal and institutional frameworks. This study addresses this puzzle by focusing on regional entrepreneurship culture as a key driver of within-country variation. Drawing on institutional perspectives and entrepreneurship ecosystem research, we conceptualize regional entrepreneurship culture as a localized informal institution that shapes entrepreneurial outcomes indirectly through institutional support mechanisms and entrepreneurial cognition. Using Japan as a stringent test case, we develop a multi-level cultural–institutional framework linking culture, institutions, cognition, and entrepreneurial behavior. To operationalize regional entrepreneurship culture, we construct a novel prefecture-level index (REC-J) based on theoretically grounded proxy indicators. A pilot, exploratory analysis across Japan’s 47 prefectures reveals a moderate positive association between REC-J and business entry rates, alongside notable regional heterogeneity. These patterns suggest that institutional capacity and market conditions may moderate the translation of cultural support into entrepreneurial action. Overall, this study shifts the analysis of entrepreneurship culture from the national to the regional level and lays the foundation for future multi-level and mixed-methods research on regional entrepreneurship disparities.
