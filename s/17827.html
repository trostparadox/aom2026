Avoid, Reduce, or Remove? The Impact of Carbon Credit Portfolios on Supply Chain Emission Trends.

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
OSCM

People

Authors: Chulhwan Kwon Arizona State University; Kevin Dooley Arizona State University

Session overview

As carbon credit markets grow toward a trillion-dollar valuation, businesses increasingly utilize carbon credits to balance carbon emissions. While recent studies explore the link between the use of carbon credits and corporate sustainability, there is a significant research gap regarding their impact on sustainable supply chain management and buyer-supplier relationships. This study investigates how a buying company’s use of voluntary carbon credits affects supplier sustainability performance and overall supply chain emissions. Drawing on signaling theory, this research proposes that voluntary carbon credits signal a buyer’s intent, either compliance driven or sustainability driven, to its suppliers. Buying companies using voluntary carbon credits without internal reduction efforts would negatively impact scope 3 emissions, whereas voluntary carbon credits paired with reduction efforts would foster supplier compliance and positive climate benefits. Utilizing OLS regression on data from the EU’s Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive and Bloomberg, this research aims to provide a decision-making framework for optimizing carbon portfolios and managing scope 3 emissions.
