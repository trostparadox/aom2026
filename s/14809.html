Session overview

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly embedded in organizational routines, professional judgment, and decision infrastructures across diverse sectors. Rather than functioning merely as decision aids or productivity tools, LLMs are becoming active participants in organizing processes by shaping how work is coordinated, how expertise is exercised, and how authority and accountability are constructed. This Professional Development Workshop examines how LLMs become organizational actors through processes of algorithmic organizing that vary across institutional and sectoral contexts. Drawing on comparative empirical research in legal services, healthcare, and manufacturing, the workshop advances a sociomaterial and institutional perspective on LLM integration. It conceptualizes LLMs as components of algorithmic organizing systems that redistribute epistemic labor, reconfigure authorship and responsibility, and mediate institutional logics such as efficiency, compliance, safety, and professional care. The workshop introduces participants to two integrative frameworks: first, a model of algorithmic organizing that explains how LLMs are stabilized through governance, training, and documentation practices; and second, a governance-skill complementarity perspective that shows how expertise and institutional safeguards jointly shape epistemic quality and organizational legitimacy. Through case-based discussion and facilitated research design exercises, participants will develop theoretically grounded approaches to studying generative AI across sectors. The PDW is designed for scholars at all career stages interested in organization theory, digital transformation, professional work, and AI governance. By focusing on how LLMs are enacted differently across industrial and service organizations, the workshop contributes to ongoing debates in OMT and allied divisions about sociomateriality, institutional complexity, and the future of organizing in AI-mediated environments.
