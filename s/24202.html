Oscillating Openness: Managing an Openness Increase Over the Entire Strategy Process Through Closure

SAP
Adriana Wacker (Universität Innsbruck); Daniel Degischer (MCI - The Entrepreneurial School)
While prior research on open strategy acknowledges that openness and closedness coexist, little is known about their dynamics over time, meaning how openness and closedness evolve over the entire strategy process and how these dynamics can be supported. This article explores the dynamics of openness and closedness as well as the adopted practices in an open strategy process. For this reason, we examine the strategy process of a medium-sized organization as a single case study, drawing on 56 semi-structured interviews, 20 observations, and comprehensive secondary data. We identify five phases of the strategy process, to which we link practices of strategy-making and navigation. Our findings highlight that deliberate closure after each sequence of open strategizing is key to manage increasing openness over the entire strategy process—generating oscillating openness. In particular, we find that actors responsible for strategy-making, such as top management, adopt five recurrent practices to navigate the varying degrees of openness. Summarized into a model of oscillating openness for the entire strategy process, our findings contribute to relational literature on openness and closedness in open strategy and provide valuable insights into dynamics that support the development of open qualities and their navigation.
