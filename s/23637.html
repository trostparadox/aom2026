Strategic Management of Community Sports Clubs

MC
MELANIE BOSC (Jean Moulin Lyon 3 University)
Community sports clubs in France are not very professionalized but have a large number of volunteers to coordinate. The number of sports clubs in France has decreased by 9% between 2019 and 2024. Changes in the external environment partly explain this situation: reductions in public funding, shifts in sports demand, and a volunteerism crisis. Our thesis aims to develop management practices in community sports clubs to ensure their sustainability. These involve strengthening the management actions of volunteer leaders and formalizing a collective strategy. Our research results are based on three research interventions in amateur rugby clubs. The first stages of this methodology enable us to describe the issues raised by club stakeholders. The transformative aim of our research leads us to analyze the conditions for change in these structures through three topics: volunteer engagement, strategic practices, and the development of self-financing. Our work thus sheds light on the actions that clubs need to implement in order to evolve in a turbulent environment.

Support Through Research-Intervention as a Process for Recasting a Creative Hub

MC
Isabelle Horvath (Haute Alsace University); Gaelle Dechamp (CERAG); Marc Bonnet (ISEOR)
How can an organization damaged by tensions and conflicts be repaired? Management science research, particularly through methods such as research-intervention, offers answers by bringing together researchers and organization’s actors (Savall, Zardet). These approaches, inspired by Piaget and Morin, consider that transformation—or even metamorphosis—is essential to prevent the disintegration of an organization. The study presented is based on the theory of embeddedness (Granovetter, Polanyi), which links economic, human, and social dimensions, to analyze how research-intervention promotes the reintegration of activities and the coordination of stakeholders. La Friche la Belle de Mai, the case we studied, is a french creative hub facing a loss of coherence. The researchers worked with the actors to redefine their strategy, combining theoretical analysis and transformative actions. The methodology, centered on research-intervention, makes it possible to act “in” and ‘on’ the action, involving the actors in a participatory process of change. Finally, the article proposes the concept of an “embedded ecosystem” to enrich the theory of embedding.

Consultancy for SME’s Recovery, Analysis of Four Small Businesses in Economic Difficulty

MC
Melissa Sanchez; Veronique Zardet; Pierrick Govart (ISEOR); Salomé Marty (ISEOR)
In times of economic crisis, failures of very small businesses abound. Management science research focuses mainly on analyzing the causes of failure in accounting information systems, while generic levels for economic recovery remain to be explored. The approach of deferring expenses or reducing visible costs is dominant in accounting analysis but is not sufficient to guarantee the survival and development of the company. However, involving employees in the hidden costs recycling may be levels of recovery. The methodology of qualimetric research-intervention makes it possible to experiment with and evaluate concrete levers for economic recovery. We compare the results of four qualimetric research-intervention programs carried out in soon-to-be failing VSEs.

Volunteer Engagement as a Mechanism of Organizational Change: An Integrative Literature Review

MC
Bex Takacs-Britz (Western Michigan University)
Volunteer engagement plays a central role in nonprofit mission delivery, yet it is often treated as a discrete management function rather than as a mechanism of organizational change. This integrative literature review synthesizes research on volunteer motivation, engagement systems, leadership, and organizational change to examine how volunteer engagement functions as a system through which organizations learn and adapt. Drawing on scholarship from nonprofit management, organizational behavior, and change leadership, the review examines how shifts in volunteer participation, organizational practices, and leadership behaviors interact over time. Across four themes, including changing volunteer motivations and engagement patterns, management practices shaping engagement and retention, volunteers as agents of change and resistance, and engagement-driven feedback loops, the review highlights how volunteers act as boundary spanners who influence organizational sensemaking, learning, and adaptation. By framing volunteer engagement as a relational and emergent process embedded within organizational systems, this review offers a more integrated understanding of how nonprofits navigate complexity and sustain organizational change.

Corporate Social Responisbility, Innovation and Firm Performance: Case of French SMEs

MC
Taher Lazhar Ayed; Sami EL GOUDDI; Reda GOURINE; Tarek MEJRI (Paris-Saclay University)
Drawing on the Dynamic Capability Theory, this article examines the relationship between Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and firm performance, with a specific focus on both financial and non-financial performance. It investigates the role of firm innovation in mediating the impact of CSR on performance. Utilizing data from 152 valid surveys collected from French companies and employing a Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) approach, the findings reveal that innovation significantly mediates the relationship between CSR and financial performance. However, this mediating effect is not observed in the relationship between CSR and non- financial performance. This discrepancy may be attributed to the tendency of French SMEs to prioritize innovations that yield immediate financial returns over those aligned with long-term sustainability objectives and stakeholder satisfaction. The study underscores the critical role of Innovation in enhancing financial performance while highlighting the need for a more balanced approach that integrates both short-term profitability and long-term sustainability. These insights provide valuable guidance for managers seeking to align CSR initiatives with innovation strategies to optimize overall performance. Sheds light on the relationship between CSR and financial and non-financial firm performance. Singularly, the paper explores the effect of firm’s innovation for understanding CSR and its impact on company performance. The sample is composed of 152 retained valid surveys collected from French companies. Using the PLS-SEM approach, the study finds that firm’s innovation significantly mediates the relationship between CSR and financial performance. However, this mediating role is not significant when it comes to the CSR-non-financial performance relationship. This may be attributed to the tendency of French SMEs to focus on innovations that generate immediate financial benefits, rather than pursuing major innovations that align with long-term sustainability goals and stakeholder satisfaction. This study highlighted the relative importance of innovation and its impact on financial and non-financial performance offering insights for managers on balancing short-term profitability with long-term sustainability.
