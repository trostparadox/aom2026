Session overview

Difficult conversations—involving conflict or disagreement (e.g., political beliefs or team conflict), grievances (e.g., counterproductive work behaviors, intergroup offenses), critical feedback (e.g., performance reviews), high-stakes moral choices (e.g., end of life care), or parties who lack a trusting relationship (e.g., employee and HR)—require complex decision-making. People must choose what to say, how to say it, whether to speak at all, and how to manage interpersonal and tangible risks. Yet the same features that make these conversations consequential also make them hard to study: The relevant “moves” are often subtle, sequential, and embedded in rich organizational contexts. This PDW will guide researchers in developing actionable research plans for studying difficult conversations in organizations by (a) introducing concrete conversation data sources, (b) demonstrating how to identify and operationalize conversational features, link them to outcomes, and choose appropriate designs, and (c) providing a practical toolkit of analytic approaches. The session will also include structured problem pitches and facilitated roundtables to incubate early-stage projects and connect scholars to collaborators, datasets, and methods. The PDW is organized in partnership with the “Conversation Research” micro-community (see links below), an interdisciplinary group network spanning organizational behavior, psychology, sociology, communication, and computational modeling. Participants will leave with a clearer map of viable datasets and designs for conversation research in organizations, plus shared resources and connections to support future work.
