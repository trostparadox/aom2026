All Comparisons Not Equal: How Temporal and Social Performance Comparisons Lead to ESG Greenwashing

STR
Fangliang Zhang (Guangxi University); Huanxin He (Guangxi University)
Firms are constantly evaluated through performance comparisons with both their own past and their peers, yet prior research has largely treated these comparisons as equivalent learning inputs. Adopting an external audience perspective, we argue that temporal and social performance comparisons carry distinct impression meanings and therefore trigger different impression management responses. Drawing on temporal comparison theory and social comparison theory and impression management literature, we explore how different types of performance comparisons lead to ESG greenwashing as an impression management strategy. Using panel data from Chinese listed firms from 2009 to 2023, we find that both positive and negative temporal gaps increase ESG greenwashing, suggesting that deviation from continuity, whether positive or negative, elicits image-stabilizing responses. In contrast, only negative social performance comparisons increase greenwashing, while positive ones reduce it, reflecting firms’ desire to repair status through ESG greenwashing. We further show that CEO tenure strengthens responses to temporal performance comparisons, while institutional ownership amplifies responses to negative social comparisons. This study advances performance feedback research by distinguishing the external meanings of temporal versus social comparisons and extends ESG research by theorizing greenwashing as a cross-domain impression management response.
