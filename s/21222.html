When Advice Becomes Friendship: Attachment Styles and Workplace Loneliness

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Author: Zexi Li Aston University

Session overview

Workplace loneliness reflects not only the absence of interaction, but a perceived gap between relational expectations and experienced connection. In organizations, most interaction is embedded in instrumental interdependence. Employees coordinate work and exchange expertise through advice ties that constitute a central relational infrastructure. Because advice exchange is primarily functional, legitimate, and role-consistent, instrumental embeddedness does not inherently translate into socio-emotional affirmation. Accordingly, employees can be structurally embedded in advice networks without experiencing those relationships as affectively sufficient. Differences in affective needs should therefore shape whether and how employees expand instrumental advice ties into affective connection, with downstream implications for loneliness. Integrating a tie-transition perspective with attachment theory, we propose attachment styles as microfoundations of cross-functional relationship development. Individuals higher in attachment anxiety should be more likely to expand recurrent advice relations into friendships to secure reassurance and belonging, thereby increasing the affective meaning of instrumental interaction and reducing loneliness. In contrast, individuals higher in attachment avoidance should prefer bounded, low-intimacy exchanges and be less inclined to convert advice ties into friendship. We test these propositions using longitudinal complete-network data on advice and friendship analyzed with multivariate stochastic actor-oriented models, and a field study linking advice networks, advice–friendship multiplexity, and attachment styles to workplace loneliness. Findings show that attachment anxiety strengthens the advice-to-friendship developmental pathway, whereas attachment avoidance attenuates tie expansion. This expansion predicts lower subsequent loneliness, particularly among anxiously attached employees.
