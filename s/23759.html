Living the Dream, Managing the Strain: Work-Life Balance and Resilience in Expatriate Tourism Entre

ENT
Rizwan Tahir (Rochester Institute of Technology Dubai); Ioanna Karanikola (Rochester Institute of Technology Dubai); Amir Klincar (Rochester Institute of Technology Dubai)
Entrepreneurship is commonly associated with autonomy and flexibility, yet founders frequently experience persistent work–life balance (WLB) strain. These challenges are intensified for expatriate entrepreneurs operating in tourism-driven global cities, where service intensity, transnational mobility, and digital connectivity intersect. This study explores how expatriate tourism entrepreneurs in Dubai experience WLB pressures, negotiate work–life boundaries, and develop resilience over time under conditions shaped by artificial intelligence (AI). Drawing on Work–Life Border Theory, the study adopts a qualitative research design based on in-depth interviews with 50 expatriate entrepreneurs across diverse tourism subsectors. The findings reveal that WLB is not a stable or attainable state but a fragile, continuously renegotiated process. Digitally intensified service demands, emotional labour, and constant connectivity systematically weaken temporal and psychological boundaries, while expatriate status removes traditional family- and organisation-based border-keepers. At the same time, entrepreneurs actively deploy adaptive boundary-management strategies, including selective use of AI-enabled automation, to cope with pressures and cultivate resilience over time.
