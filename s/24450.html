Co-Opted and Non-Co-Opted Independent Directors: Post-Succession Family Firm Competitive Dynamics

STR
Jaeyoung Cho (University of Wollongong); Sam Garg (ESSEC Business School)
This study examines how co-opted and non-co-opted independent directors affect the post-succession competitive dynamics of family firms over time. In an analysis of public-listed South Korean family firms, we find that both types of directors enhance competitive aggressiveness and complexity immediately following succession. Co-opted directors contribute successor-aligned resources that create new competitive opportunities, while non-co-opted directors provide legacy resources that preserve family legitimacy and continuity, allowing complementarity. Over time, however, legacy resources become outdated and misaligned with successors’ strategic priorities, turning initial complementarity into conflict. Successors thus face a “cleaning the house” dilemma but often cannot easily remove non-co-opted directors due to family symbolic and legacy constraints, requiring them to manage the coexistence of outdated and updated board resources. Importantly, family successors endowed with extended socioemotional wealth are better able than nonfamily successors to reconcile these competing resources and sustain competitive aggressiveness and complexity over time. These findings contribute to research on family firm boards, evolutionary CEO–board relationships, and competitive dynamics.
