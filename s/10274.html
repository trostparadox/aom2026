Session overview

New ventures face a persistent liability of newness, marked by resource constraints, uncertainty, and limited legitimacy. While prior research emphasizes entrepreneurs’ individual social capital as a means of overcoming these challenges, it has paid less attention to the role of community social capital—the shared norms, trust, and recognition structures that shape access to resources within entrepreneurial communities. This symposium advances a community-based perspective on social capital and entrepreneurship, arguing that social capital is often jointly produced, imperfectly excludable, and context dependent, with implications that differ fundamentally from individual-level ties. Across four papers spanning experimental, field, archival, and historical designs, the symposium examines how community social capital shapes entrepreneurial outcomes through multiple mechanisms. The papers explore how local status hierarchies (and thus distributions of social capital) emerge within entrepreneurial cohorts; how access to community-based financial arrangements allows excluded individuals to overcome disadvantages by allowing them access to community social capital ; how community membership signals are interpreted by external evaluators under shifting market conditions; and the conditions under which community social capital is transmitted from one generation to the next. Together, these studies show that community social capital can both enable and constrain entrepreneurship, with uneven benefits across individuals, time periods, and evaluative regimes.
