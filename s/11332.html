Entrepreneurship, Sleep, and Life Satisfaction: Revisiting the ‘Hustle’ Narrative

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Authors: Maximilian Weldert University of Münster; Henrik Heinemann University of Münster; Martin Obschonka; Julian Scharbert; Philipp Schäpers University of Münster

Session overview

Entrepreneurship is often associated with relentless work ethics and minimal sleep, yet empirical evidence of entrepreneurs’ sleep habits and their implications for well-being remain scare. Drawing on a longitudinal daily diary dataset of German entrepreneurs (N = 117) and an equally sized control group of non-entrepreneurs, we examine whether entrepreneurs differ in their sleep quality, duration and variability, and how these sleep characteristics relate to their life satisfaction. Participants recorded their bedtime, wake-up time, and perceived sleep quality on at least five days. Consistent with prior research, entrepreneurs reported significantly higher life satisfaction than the rest of the population. Contrary to frequent assumptions, they also experienced higher sleep quality, while showing no difference in sleep duration or their intra-individual variability of their sleep across days. Our analyses revealed that high sleep quality positively predicted life satisfaction in both groups. Overall, the evidence collected in this study challenges the prevailing narrative of the hustling entrepreneur who sacrifices sleep for success and highlights that entrepreneurs’ well-being benefits from adequate sleep just as much as that of the general population. These results advance our understanding of the biological and behavioral mechanisms underlying entrepreneurial well-being and offer insights for entrepreneurship practice and individual career choices.
