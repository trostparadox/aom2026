Session overview

As artificial intelligence increasingly shapes organizational decisions, moral responsibility becomes difficult to locate. Authority shifts toward data-driven systems, ethical voice is muted by procedure, and leadership risks becoming technical rather than moral. While governance and compliance frameworks for ethical AI continue to expand, moral agency and presence are often shaped before conscious judgment begins. This professional development workshop examines that condition through a disciplined role-based simulation grounded in Positive Leadership, understood as virtue-oriented moral presence under organizational constraint. Spirituality is approached not as belief, but as lived attentiveness, discernment, and moral courage within complex organizational systems. Participants inhabit structured roles in an AI-mediated senior leadership decision to experience how responsibility, voice, and ethical clarity shift with positional location rather than personal intention. Through constrained dialogue and guided reflection, participants observe how integrity, compassion, purpose, and hope are diminished or reclaimed through governance practices. Rather than debating ethical principles or evaluating AI outcomes, the workshop makes visible how moral responsibility is formed, distributed, and constrained prior to articulated judgment.
