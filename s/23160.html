Working With Silos: A Study of Data-Driven Transformation in Urban Policing

CTO
Marjolijn Bruggeling (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Lauren Waardenburg (ESSEC Business School)
Organizations increasingly turn to data-driven initiatives to improve decision-making, yet little is known about how such efforts reshape organizational boundaries over time. Drawing on a four-year qualitative study (2021–2025) of a Large Urban Police Department (LUPD) in the United States, this paper examines how data work evolves as organizations transition from fragmented legacy systems toward integrated data environments. Specifically, we analyze how actors coped with existing technical and social silos while developing now data-driven work practices. Our findings show that data-driven transformation unfolded through the layering of two interdependent forms of data work: shielded data work, in which analysts and technical staff maintained fragile systems within existing boundaries, and shared data work, in which responsibilities for data quality, interpretation, and use were collectively negotiated across roles and hierarchies. Failed early initiatives exposed the limits of working within silos but also created the conditions for sustained cross-boundary collaboration. Over time, these layered practices stabilized into an organizational capacity for “organizing for data,” exemplified by the development of a centralized data repository, i.e., the Data Bodega, that supported routine decision-making and enabled later experimentation with analytics and AI. The study contributes to research on data work and boundary work by showing how maintaining, rather than erasing, boundaries can serve as a foundation for durable shared data practices and long-term data-driven change.

When Elasticity Pays off: Use-Agnostic Properties in the Secondary Use of Clinical Trial Data

CTO
Felix Haas (Freie Universität Berlin); Tim Kilgus (Free University of Berlin); Daniel Fürstenau (Freie Universität Berlin)
The growing availability of research data has elevated secondary data use to a strategic concern for organizations investing in data infrastructures. While prior research emphasizes access, less is known about how the use-agnostic properties of data, elasticity and transparency, facilitate its alignment with new tasks in reuse. This study examines these properties by drawing on data from 6,433 clinical trials across three major infrastructures: Vivli, Clinical Study Data Request (CSDR), and the Yale Open Data Access (YODA) Project. We employ a multilevel mixed-effects logistic regression to identify the determinants of reuse. By differentiating between horizontal and vertical elasticity, the findings show that secondary use is driven primarily by contextual depth (vertical elasticity) rather than contextual breadth (horizontal elasticity). Trials characterized by deeper Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) hierarchies are significantly more likely to be requested, indicating that semantic richness and contextual specificity enhance data–task alignment. In contrast, broader topical coverage exhibits limited explanatory power. The study demonstrates that data infrastructures create value not through volume alone, but by curating semantically coherent environments that reduce interpretive gaps. For managers, this suggests that investments should prioritize semantic infrastructure, such as standardized taxonomies and deep metadata indexing, over simple data accumulation. At the same time, infrastructure-specific preselection practices constrain interoperability and limit the generalizability of reuse patterns.

Computing Infrastructure, Computing-Network Collaboration, and Regional Economic Growth

CTO
Shengjie Cai (Tsinghua University); Jinglei Huang (Tsinghua University); Danxia Xie (Tsinghua University); Rouhan Yin (Tsinghua University)
In the era of the digital economy, computing infrastructure ensures that data, as a new production factor, contributes to economic growth. This paper constructs a theoretical model of cross-regional computing power allocation, illustrating how computing infrastructure enterprises—distributed across nodes of the data transmission network—endogenously decide on computing power allocation and data transmission schemes. At the macro level, this manifests as computing infrastructure fostering local economic growth and exhibiting heterogeneous spillover effects on the economic growth of other regions along the data transmission network. Empirically, this paper first uses ITU communication cables to construct a topology map of China's data transmission network and its spatial network matrix. Then, by proxying regional computing infrastructure levels using the number of operational data center racks at the provincial level in China from 2017 to 2021, panel regression analysis reveals that computing infrastructure unlocks the potential of data to drive local economic growth, with particularly significant effects in regions with scarce computing infrastructure and high network centrality. Spatial autoregressive analysis further shows that while data exhibits negative spillover effects on other regions' economies along the data transmission network, these effects can be significantly mitigated by computing infrastructure. The positive spatial spillover effects of computing infrastructure along the data transmission network are primarily realized through promoting innovation in industries such as information technology and manufacturing in other regions. By uncovering the economic impacts and mechanisms of computing infrastructure and its coordination with data transmission networks, this paper aims to provide a theoretical foundation and policy guidance for the layout and development of computing infrastructure, as well as for China's future digital transformation.

Harnessing Digital Capabilities for Employee-Centric CSR: Evidence from Emerging Economies

CTO
Ijaz Younis (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Muhammad Ahmed (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
The rapid digital transformation of firms in emerging markets is reshaping both operational processes and corporate social responsibility (CSR) practices. This study examines how digitalization influences internal CSR, defined as a firm’s commitment to employee well-being, and explores the moderating roles of media coverage and market competition. Drawing on institutional theory, we argue that digital technologies not only enhance transparency, efficiency, and employee engagement but also interact with external institutional pressures to shape CSR outcomes. Using a panel dataset of 11,789 firm-year observations from Chinese A-share listed companies between 2011 and 2022, we find that higher levels of digitalization significantly improve internal CSR practices. This relationship is strengthened under intensive media scrutiny, which amplifies public expectations and organizational accountability, and is further conditioned by market competition, which incentivizes firms to adopt socially responsible strategies to maintain competitive advantage. Robustness checks confirm the validity of these findings across multiple model specifications. The study offers three key contributions: it advances theory by linking digitalization with internal CSR in a developing economy context; it highlights the critical moderating influence of media attention and competitive pressures; and it provides actionable insights for managers and policymakers seeking to leverage digitalization for ethical and sustainable employee practices. These results underscore the transformative potential of digital technologies in promoting socially responsible organizational behavior under dynamic institutional environments.
