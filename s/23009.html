Compassion and the Sustainability of Self-Leadership

SAP
Xueting Jiang (New York Institute of Technology)
Research on self-leadership has largely emphasized self-regulatory strategies rooted in control, such as standards, monitoring, and self-correction. These approaches often assume that individuals can continue leading themselves over time. This paper shifts attention to how self-leadership is sustained when control-based regulation becomes demanding. Drawing on compassion and sensemaking research, it develops a process model showing how self-attentiveness, care-oriented interpretation, and restorative action support continued self-directed influence under strain. By integrating self-compassion into self-leadership theory, the paper offers a process view of self-leadership as both agentic and sustainable.

Speaking the Future into Being: Rhetorical Work in Entrepreneurial Future-Making

SAP
Silvia Sanasi (University of Bergamo); Stefano Magistretti (Politecnico di Milano); Nico Florian Klenner (The Australian National University)
Entrepreneurs use language in the present to give form to envisioned futures and mobilize action to realize them. To shed light on how entrepreneurs bridge this present-future divide, we draw on the case of the Bjarke Ingels Group (BIG), a world-renowned architecture firm known for its use of verbal communication to support its provocative designs. Our analysis of public language used by BIG to communicate architectural projects reveals a process of future-making that leverages rhetorical work for legitimizing unforeseeable futures, articulating provocative futures, and enacting possible futures. Building on the interplay between three rhetorical devices—familiarity, contrariety, and absurdity—rhetorical work makes the future amenable to stakeholders in the present. The study contributes to the literature on language in entrepreneurship and future-making, and offers managerial insights for using language to build worlds with words.

Under-the-Radar Resource Converters: Reconfiguring Social Networks for Adaptation Mechanisms in SMEs

SAP
Su Hua Ou (Soochow University)
This paper examines how small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) adapt by leveraging social networks to reconfigure resources in dynamic environments. Adopting a social network reconfiguration perspective, we analyze how SMEs interpret industrial structures and mobilize relational ties to enhance adaptability. We introduce the concept of the converter role, adapted from the CDMO model, to explain how SMEs strategically reorganize networks to redeploy R&D and marketing resources. Our analysis identifies three adaptive mechanisms—disintermediation, midstream embeddedness, and cross-boundary brokering—that illustrate how SMEs achieve adaptation across resource, network, and industry-structure levels. In doing so, SMEs emerge as under-the-radar innovators operating beneath mainstream trends. This study contributes to the literature on social network reconfiguration and offers practical insights into pathways for SME adaptation and growth.

Leader Self-Sacrifice and the Architecture of Legacy: A Multi-Case Study on Family Firm Longevity

SAP
AIZA DE TORRES ASI (Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development); Michela Floris (University of Cagliari); Giuseppe Argiolas (Sophia University Institute)
Legacy in family firms connects past, present, and future through narratives and practices that transcend economics. This study adopts a mechanism-first view, positioning leader self-sacrifice (LSS)—the voluntary acceptance of personal costs for collective benefit—as the antecedent that activates legacy dynamics. Using an inductive multi-case design across continents, we show how LSS triggers three facets of legacy: unintentional building, shared intentional transmission, and intentional preservation and reframing. Through narrative, socioemotional, and spiritual channels, these facets convert sacrifice into sustainability and predict business longevity. We clarify boundary conditions distinguishing sacrifice from suffering, highlight governance choices such as professionalization, and examine reciprocity in predecessor–successor relationships. The Leadership Legacy Sustainability Roadmap formalizes the causal chain of LSS, legacy facets, sustainability and longevity. This work advances socioemotional-wealth theorizing, specifies sequencing, and offers practical guidance for succession and strategy beyond crisis-specific contexts.
