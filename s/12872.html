Does an IT Career Have a Shorter Career Lifespan? A Comparative Analysis

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
CTO

People

Authors: Yiqian Chen South China University of Technology; Chuan Hoo Tan National University of Singapore; Chunmian He South China University of Technology

Session overview

Information technology (IT) professionals are central to digital transformation, yet concerns persist about the IT career lifespan, linking high job mobility to perceived skill obsolescence and elevated exhaustion. Practitioner narratives reinforce the popular view that IT careers may be relatively short. Conversely, from the perspective of boundaryless career theory, IT careers may be sustained over long horizons with transferable skills and various cross-boundary opportunities, especially in the Artificial Intelligence (AI) era. This research uses the JobHop career-history dataset to conduct multiple comparative analyses and survival regressions of IT professions against benchmarking professions in Flanders, Belgium. We find that, compared with IT professionals, most other professional groups exhibit significantly higher exit hazards and shorter career lengths. IT professionals tend to remain in the labor market longer than others, providing empirical evidence challenging the perspective that the IT career lifespan is short. This study contributes to IS and career research by shifting attention from short-term mobility to long-run career lifespan, informing the design of sustainable IT career paths and workforce planning under rapid technological change.
