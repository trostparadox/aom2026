Visual Congruence of Digital Agents and Brand Positioning in Hospitality Services

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

Authors: Yao Hao Griffith University; Yixuan Yang; Haomin Yang

Session overview

As digital service agents become increasingly embedded in hotel service encounters, their visual representation has emerged as a consequential yet under-theorized element of brand communication. Existing research and industry practice largely assume that greater anthropomorphism uniformly enhances trust and consumer responses. Drawing on Congruence Theory, this study challenges this assumption by arguing that the effectiveness of avatar anthropomorphism is contingent on hotel brand positioning. We develop a conceptual framework proposing that guests evaluate digital agents not in isolation, but in relation to the symbolic meanings associated with the hotel context. Using a proposed 2 × 2 experimental design that varies avatar appearance (human-like vs. robotic) and hotel type (traditional luxury vs. smart technology), we theorize that human-like avatars enhance booking intentions for traditional luxury hotels by strengthening social presence and relational cues. In contrast, for smart technology hotels, robotic avatars are expected to generate higher engagement and preference by improving processing fluency and reinforcing a future-oriented brand schema. By demonstrating that anthropomorphism does not exert a universally positive effect, this research advances hospitality and service literature by identifying visual congruence as a key boundary condition in digital agent design. The findings offer strategic guidance for hotels seeking to align digital interfaces with brand positioning in technology-mediated service environments.
