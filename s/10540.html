Right-Hand or Left-Hand Partner: How Ingratiatory Followers Shape Leader Power into Self-Interest

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7
OB

People

Authors: Shangyun Chen Singapore Management University; Michael Bashshur Singapore Management University

Session overview

Power is not used nor interpreted in isolation. How a leader interprets power, whether as a license to advance personal goals or a responsibility to uphold collective welfare, is shaped by the people around them. Followers, in particular, can function as leaders’ “right-hand partners,” reinforcing the sense of responsibility inherent in their power, or as “left-hand partners,” subtly validating the independence and superiority also associated with power. We argue that ingratiatory followers—those who praise, flatter, and conform to leaders—often assume the latter role. By consistently affirming a leader’s preferences and insulating them from challenge, these followers shift the psychological meaning of power toward entitlement and away from solidarity, ultimately transforming power into a force that fuels leader self-interest. Across two studies, we show how this follower’s ingratiation alters leaders’ self-centric or prosocial tendency as their workplace power increases.
