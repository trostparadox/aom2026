Session overview

Research in developmental network studies has flourished over the last two decades, particularly after the redefinition of mentoring. Developmental Network (DN) draws its roots from the concept of “mentoring”, as proposed by Levinson et al. (1978). Mentoring, in simple terms, is a senior person helping a junior protégé in his/her career to gain higher positions, ranks, and experience (career help) and provides psychosocial help such as mutual trust, friendship, caring beyond work, social and emotional support (Thomas & Kram, 1988). While the effects of developmental networks have been vastly studied, the formation of how such relationships form is largely understudied. Through this study, we aim to understand the formation of developmental relationships, ie, focusing on the antecedents. Keywords: Developmental Network, Mentoring, Trust, Careers
