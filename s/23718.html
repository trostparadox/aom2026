Fostering Inclusive Workplaces for Employees With Functional Diversity

DEI
Maria Eduarda Soares (ISEG Lisbon School of Economics and Management, University of Lisbon); Telma Lourenço
This study examines key antecedent variables of work engagement and mental well-being of People with Functional Diversity (PFD), focusing on employees with physical and motor disabilities. Co-workers’ attitudes are considered a critical social contextual factor, while internal and external locus of control represent stable personal resources that may shape employees’ work-related experiences. The sample comprises 97 PFD (55 women and 42 men), aged between 18 and 65 years. Previously validated scales were used to assess work engagement, well-being, and locus of control. Perceived co-workers’ attitudes were measured using items adapted from prior research. Exploratory factor analysis revealed three distinct dimensions of these perceptions: perceived positive attitudes, perceived negative attitudes, and perceived feelings of obligation. Results indicate that mental well-being is positively associated with perceived positive attitudes and internal locus of control, and negatively associated with perceived negative attitudes and perceived feelings of obligation. Work engagement is positively associated with internal locus of control and negatively associated with perceived negative attitudes. Together, perceived co-worker attitudes and locus of control explain 59.5% of the variance in mental well-being and 39.4% of the variance in engagement. These findings underscore the importance of inclusive social contexts and individual psychological resources in fostering well-being and engagement among employees with functional diversity.
