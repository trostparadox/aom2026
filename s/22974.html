The Prestige Paradox: Uneven Distribution of Network Returns to Elite Education

OMT
Linhui Wu (Northwestern University)
This multi-method study identifies and theorizes a “Prestige Paradox” in elite labor markets: while women increasingly access prestigious educational institutions, they extract significantly lower network and career returns from these credentials than their male counterparts. Integrating cumulative advantage theory with the sociology of status, I propose that the conversion of educational prestige into social capital is not a gender-neutral mechanical process but is structurally stratified. Using a dataset of over 1.3 million digitized profiles and a complementary audit experiment, I demonstrate that the network benefits of attending an elite university accumulate at a steeper rate for men, creating a widening gender gap in professional reach and managerial attainment (e.g., managerial/C-suite). Crucially, I explore different supply-side and demand-side mechanisms driving inequality. I find no evidence that women under-invest in self-presentation or networking initiation. While self-presentational novelty partially mediates the effect, a large, unequal residual return remains. The vignette experiment confirms that elite women are disproportionately penalized in network formation with organizational gatekeeper. However, it also suggests that educational prestige is not always preferred for early-career professionals when no performance information is available. These findings challenge meritocratic assumptions by revealing that gender inequality in elite labor markets arises not only from differential access to prestige by professionals or preference by evaluators at early stage, but also from a systemic, self-reinforcing devaluation of the prestige itself when carried by women.

Walking on Two Legs: Antecedents of Network Capital Ambidexterity

OMT
Meijia Fang (The University of Hong Kong); Shuai Shi (The University of Hong Kong); Junwen LI (The Chinese University of Hong Kong)
Ambidexterity, conventionally conceptualized as an internal organizational or single network-based capability, largely overlooks the crucial external dimension afforded by a firm’s embeddedness in complex inter-firm networks. These networks, inherently possessing distinct characteristics, align with either exploratory or exploitative tendencies. Addressing this conceptual gap, we introduce and theorize network capital ambidexterity as the coupling coordination between external exploration manifested through innovation networks and external exploitation leveraged via established guanxi networks. To rigorously investigate its antecedents, we employ a panel fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) approach to identify configurational pathways leading to network capital ambidexterity among Chinese ChiNext-listed firms from 2015 to 2022. ChiNext firms, as high-growth entrepreneurial ventures, critically rely on network ambidexterity to simultaneously explore new innovation opportunities and efficiently exploit existing relationships for rapid market growth. First, network capital ambidexterity among ChiNext firms revealed a persistent stratification over time. Second, our fsQCA models illuminate three distinct and robust causal pathways: context-driven stable ambidexterity, leveraging environmental stability; human-centric flexible ambidexterity, emphasizing agile human capital; and capital-powered efficiency ambidexterity, driven by financial and resource endowments. Theoretically, this research offers a novel network-based perspective, advancing ambidexterity theory by incorporating external resources as primary drivers of ambidextrous outcomes. Practically, it provides actionable insights into multiple causal pathways for entrepreneurial ventures in relational culture economies to strategically leverage their external networks for opportunity capture.

Structural Basis of Gender Diversity Effect: Top Gun Flight Competition in the Korean Air Force

OMT
Minki Kim (Seoul National University); Sun Hyun Park (Seoul National University); Yonghoon Lee (Texas A&M University); Heejung Jung
This study explores structural accounts of the relationship between gender diversity and team performance in a unique empirical setting of the top-gun flight competition of the Korean Air Force. 10 years of archival data reveal that mixed-gender teams, including female pilots, outperform their all-male team competitors (Study 1). Drawing on open-ended interviews with pilots and data on knowledge-sharing networks, we examine how mixed-gender teams’ favorable positions in the broader knowledge-sharing network and constrained intergenerational knowledge transfer enable them to undertake greater innovation to achieve tactical perfection and safety (Study 2). Our study contributes to the burgeoning literature on workplace diversity, organizational learning and innovation, and performance.

Integrating Knowledge Through Collaboration: A Trajectory Approach

OMT
Shukrullah Fassehi (Curtin University - Perth); Christine Soo (The University of Western Australia); Amy Wei Tian (Curtin University - Perth)
This study explores how knowledge is effectively integrated within an organization’s daily routines. Using data from seven technology transfer cases within a multinational firm in Australia, we identify two distinct trajectories: one leading to effective integration and the other to less effective outcomes. The effective trajectory was characterized by collaborative agency between disseminating and recipient teams, involving a sequence of interrelated processes— knowledge co-creation, co-implementation, co-integration, and co-evolution. In contrast, the less effective trajectory was characterized by actions undertaken in silos, lacking meaningful collaboration. The study contributes to knowledge integration and organizational routine literatures by incorporating agency theory, process theory, and trajectory approach. Findings of the study also have important implications for organizations seeking to effectively embed knowledge within everyday operations.

Against Codification: Kinship, Discipleship, and the Preservation of Tacit Knowledge in the Gharana

OMT
Astha Mishra (Indian School of Business); Sougata Ray (Indian School of Business)
This paper addresses a core tension in the knowledge economy: while the most vital forms of knowledge are tacit, embodied, and relational, modern organizations are structured around explicit, codifiable, and scalable knowledge. Prevailing knowledge management models treat this tension as a problem of conversion, an assumed imperative, which seeks to transform the intuitive and the tacit into the procedural and the codified. Our paper proposes a different path, by asking: if certain knowledge systems resist codification, how might they be organized to endure across time? To answer this, the paper turns to an in-depth, qualitative, interpretive case study of the Benares Gharana, a blood and discipleship-based lineage system, through which uncodified musical knowledge of the north Indian subcontinent has continued to survive over centuries. Historically forged in response to the collapse of royal patronage in the 19th century, the gharana represents a purposefully built alternative organization structured around kinship and discipleship. It operates as a corpus corporatum of lineage that holds its stylistic knowledge (baaj) as collective, inalienable property, and the relational ‘guru-shishya parampara’, a master disciple tradition which transcends temporality, and embeds learning within the bonds of obligation and identity, rather than market transaction. By bridging critiques of knowledge conversion, with scholarship on alternative ways of organizing, and kinship, our paper shows how the gharana’s knowledge governance mechanism enables the intergenerational continuity of the tacit and the un-codifiable. The paper thus reframes the organizational imperative from one of extraction, conversion, and scaling, to one of sustaining the social relations that make embedded, intuitive knowledge possible. In doing so, it contributes a historically grounded, non-Western model to the literature on alternative organizing, one organized for the faithful reproduction of a tacit, living repertoire.
