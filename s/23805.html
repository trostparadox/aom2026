Why Followers Follow – Toward a Theory of Motivation to Follow

OB
Ioannis Tagos (University of Konstanz); Miriam Schwarzmaier (University of Konstanz)
Leadership cannot exist without followership, yet leadership scholarship still offers an incomplete explanation of a foundational question: why do individuals choose to follow in specific contexts. Despite extensive theorizing on followership, existing approaches largely infer followers’ motives from their behavior, implicitly assuming that similar acts of compliance reflect similar psychological commitments. This assumption obscures a central feature of followership: behaviorally similar follower actions may stem from qualitatively different motivations, with important implications for durability, ethics, and influence dynamics. We address this gap by theorizing Motivation to Follow (MTF) as a distinct motivational construct capturing the conscious psychological drivers that lead people to enact followership roles by deferring to others and sustaining asymmetrical authority relations, whether through internalized commitment or strategic compliance. Integrating insights from motivation theory, power–dependence frameworks, social identity perspectives, and followership role orientations, we develop a taxonomy of ten explicit motives to follow, organized into self-, relationship-, and ethical-directed orientations. For each motive, we delineate conceptual boundaries and advance falsifiable propositions specifying activation conditions and downstream risks, including fragile compliance, contingent loyalty, principled commitment, and covert resistance. By shifting attention from whether followers comply to why they do so, MTF explains why followership that appears identical in the moment can differ in stability, ethical risk, and responsiveness to leadership failure. In doing so, MTF reframes followership as motivated role enactment and establishes a focused agenda for measurement and future research on when following enables coordination, voice, and ethical resilience, or fuels silence, dependency, and destructive dynamics.
