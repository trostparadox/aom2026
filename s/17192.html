Session overview

The purpose of this PDW is to introduce, discuss, and delineate a research stream on men’s issues—crises facing men driven by systemic social and economic shifts (Reeves, 2022)—and entrepreneurship. Gender is an important topic in entrepreneurship research. However, as in the broader research environment, men have fallen to the wayside in the study of gender (Reeves, 2022). This situation is both problematic and relevant to entrepreneurship for three reasons. First, entrepreneurship is often a response to structural disadvantage (Lewis & Crabbe, 2024), including gendered structural disadvantage (Jennings et al., 2016). In many countries, particularly developed countries, we see patterns of gendered disadvantage now affecting men. In the United States, for example, men are 15% less likely to hold a bachelor’s degree than are women, account for three-fourths of deaths of despair (drug overdoses, suicide, alcoholism), and face shifting labor markets that devalue “men’s work” (Autor et al., 2019; Reeves, 2022). Consequently, entrepreneurship, as practiced and what is possible, may reflect and remediate these issues. Second, neglecting men and their struggles leaves research on entrepreneurship and gender vulnerable to backlash (i.e., Nkomo et al., 2019; Reeves. 2022), whereas attending to them opens up conversation and the possibility of collaboration (i.e., Cech, 2024). Third, although there are notable recent exceptions (e.g., Green & Wraight, 2025; Hytti et al., 2024), we know very little about the male experience of entrepreneurship, with gender-neutral research ignoring the relevance of gender to entrepreneurship while gendered research principally treats men as the comparison group for women. This has produced an implicit assumption that men’s experiences require less theorization, which limits our understanding of gendered entrepreneurial processes overall. This PDW introduces men’s issues as a subdomain of gender and entrepreneurship research. We introduce and delineate the topic, have a structured panel discussion with ETP and JBV editors (Aaron Anglin, Diana Hechavarria, and Johan Wiklund), offer a research agenda, and close with a Q&A.
