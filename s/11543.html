Rethinking Women’s Entrepreneurship: A Constitutive Review

Authors: Aybüke Temel Cakir Erasmus University Rotterdam; Laura Rosendahl Huber Rotterdam School of Management; Pursey Heugens Erasmus University Rotterdam

Session overview

Research on women’s entrepreneurship has long relied on individualistic and often implicitly essentialist explanations when explaining gender disparities in entrepreneurial outcomes. Despite the scholarly efforts on emphasizing gendered norms, power relations and institutional constraints, insights from different literatures remain fragmented and difficult to compare. This integrative review reframes women’s entrepreneurship through a transaction cost lens by drawing on a synthesis of 419 articles published between 2010 and 2025, across seven distinct academic fields. We argue that gendered entrepreneurial outcomes are not rooted in biology but emerge from socially constructed opportunity structures that generate systematically different costs for women-led ventures. We show how gendered information, negotiation, contracting, monitoring and enforcement costs shape organizational design and outcomes. By approaching gendered costs as a unifying analytical mechanism, this review integrates insights across sociology, feminist studies, economics, organizational research and development studies. We develop the theory of the women-led firm by reconceptualizing women-led ventures as unique organizational outcomes shaped by cumulative cost and opportunity structures rather than individual choice or deficiency. The findings further enlighten how to level the entrepreneurial playing field using stage-sensitive interventions targeting the sources of gendered costs.
