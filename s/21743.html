Managing Without Control: Steering Strategic Innovation In Healthcare Organizations

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 6
HCM

People

Author: Piengnapa Pakuthai Tilburg University

Session overview

Innovation-oriented healthcare organizations face a persistent challenge: while they seek to steer innovation in strategic directions, innovation work unfolds within professionalized settings characterized by strong professional autonomy. Prior research has examined how organizations balance demands from current operations and innovation, yet has paid limited attention to how organizations steer the direction of innovation once innovation is actively encouraged, particularly in healthcare contexts. In practice, healthcare professionals often remain highly engaged in innovation while devoting substantial effort to self-initiated initiatives rather than strategically prioritized ones. Drawing on literature on professional autonomy and organizational control, this study examines tensions that arise when healthcare organizations attempt to steer strategic innovation through administrative mechanisms. We identify three mechanisms: soft autonomy; voluntary self-limitation of autonomy; and peer-based layers of control, through which strategic innovation is steered under conditions of professional autonomy.
