Entrepreneurial Innovation as Costly Signal: Integrating Behavioral Ecology and Mental Health

Author: Georgios Afxentiou Cyprus University of Technology

Session overview

Entrepreneurs’ well-being and mental health are valuable internal resources contributing to the formulation of innovation during crises. This conceptual paper applies costly signaling theory to explore how entrepreneurs’ psychological and physiological conditions generate signals to reinforce innovative behavior in distressed environments. It addresses a literature gap in formulating a business innovation strategy while exploring stress responses, anxiety, depression symptoms, exhaustion, and the cost, quality, reliability, validity, and frequency of entrepreneurs’ signals. A theory synthesis is adopted to integrate the qualities of costly signaling, biological and economic signaling theories in developing the Innovative Signaling Theory (IST). By advancing a novel theoretical perspective, this study provides insights for both scholars and practitioners and establishes a foundation for developing innovation strategies that enhance resilience and innovation during crises. Keywords: Costly signaling theory, entrepreneurs, mental health, well-being, business innovation, consumer trust, crisis.
