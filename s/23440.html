From Grassroots Experimentation to Strategic Control: A Co-Evolutionary Model of GAI Implementation

TIM
Liat Burgin-Kotsel (Tel Aviv University); Abraham Carmeli (Tel Aviv University)
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) offers transformative potential, yet bridging the gap between its technical promise and organizational performance remains a persistent challenge. While traditional technology implementation is often conceptualized as a top-down strategic imperative, GenAI’s unique accessibility allows it to permeate organizations through individual employee experimentation, creating a complex integration dynamic. Drawing on an inductive analysis of healthcare SMEs, this study unravels the pathways through which organizations translate GenAI into improved processes and performance. We identify a dual-pathway model where employee-initiated (bottom-up) sensemaking collides and co-evolves with management-driven (top-down) governance. We demonstrate that the tension between local innovation and managerial control is not merely an obstacle, but a generative mechanism that drives organizational learning, reshapes HR practices, and refines work processes. By revealing these co-evolutionary dynamics, our findings advance socio-technical theory, suggesting that effective GenAI integration is not a linear adoption event, but a reciprocal process of aligning grassroots creativity with strategic intent.

How Artificial Intelligence Fosters Dynamic Ambidexterity Between Exploration and Exploitation

TIM
Chenyun Liu (Central South University); Chen Huang
While the management literature on Artificial Intelligence and its potential to drive exploration and exploitation has surged in recent years, how the adoption of AI affects the dynamic balance of exploration and exploitation over time remains vague. Grounded in the emerging resource-based view on AI, this study examines the impact of AI adoption on dynamic ambidexterity—defined as a firm’s capacity to dynamically balance exploration and exploitation over time. Furthermore, we conceptualize three core AI capabilities—autonomy, learning, and interaction—and investigate their moderating effects on this relationship. By analyzing longitudinal data from 922 Chinese listed firms with AI patents using text analysis, topic modeling approach, and fixed-effects regressions, the findings demonstrate that AI adoption enhances the scalability and flexibility of resources, enabling firms to allocate resources more fluidly between exploration and exploitation over time. Furthermore, AI interaction capability strengthens this positive relationship while AI learning capability weakens this relationship, underscoring the critical importance of developing specific AI capability to maximize AI’s potential in enabling dynamic ambidexterity. This study advances our understanding of leveraging AI to dynamically balancing exploration and exploitation and provides empirical foundation for resource-based view within the context of AI innovation.

Beyond the Hype: Distinguishing LLM Agents from Gen-AI Startups Through Model Context Protocol

TIM
Dequn Teng (University of Cambridge); Chen Ye (University of Cambridge); Veronica Martinez (University of Cambridge)
This study investigates whether Large Language Model Agents represent a genuinely novel technological paradigm or merely constitute incremental extensions of Generative Artificial Intelligence. Leveraging a natural experiment created by Anthropic's Model Context Protocol release on November 25, 2024, we employ difference-in-differences estimation with data from 4,532 startups in the artificial intelligence vertical from PitchBook, examining venture capital backing, funding amounts, patent activity, competitive positioning, and market diversification. Our findings reveal that the MCP release fundamentally reshaped the LLM Agent startup ecosystem through a democratization mechanism: agent startups experience a 6.4 percentage point increase in venture capital backing rates (p < 0.05), yet do not receive disproportionately larger funding amounts, suggesting MCP expanded access without creating premium valuations. Agent startups also exhibit 3.5 percentage points higher patent activity (p < 0.05) and face 0.64 additional competitors (p < 0.10), while vertical diversification remains unchanged. These patterns survive extensive robustness checks, including placebo tests and sensitivity analyses. We contribute to emerging theory on generative artificial intelligence by providing the first large-scale empirical evidence that infrastructural developments can democratize entrepreneurial participation while intensifying competitive dynamics, with implications for entrepreneurs, investors, and policymakers navigating the evolving AI landscape.

Faster or Better?AI Investment and Patent Time-to-Grant:Innovation Quality Upgrading as a Mechanism

TIM
Xiaoxuan Tian (Tsinghua University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is expected to accelerate innovation, yet its implications for the timing of value realization remain unclear. We theorize a quality– speed trade-off: AI investment enhances innovation quality by pushing firms toward more technologically sophisticated, frontier-oriented, and novel inventions, which increases examination complexity and slows patent granting. Using a panel of 5,273 Chinese listed firms (2012–2024), we measure firm AI investment from financial disclosures of AI-related intangible and fixed assets and estimate generalized estimating equation models. Results show that higher AI investment is associated with slower patent grant speed: a 10% increase in AI investment corresponds to a 3.39 percentage-point decrease in same-year grant probability and a 6.85 percentage-point decrease in grant probability within two years. Mechanism tests indicate that AI raises the share of invention patents, strengthens effects in high-tech and emerging domains, and increases both longitudinal and peer-relative text novelty. Regional data endowments amplify these effects, and the novelty gains are concentrated among the largest firms and are stronger for state-owned enterprises. Overall, the study reframes AI-enabled innovation as a quality–speed trade-off with regional data and firm heterogeneity as key boundary conditions.

Strategic AI under Opacity: How Architecture Level Patterns Shape Market Valuation

TIM
Rohit Nishant (Queen's University Belfast); Christian Keen (Laval University); Denis A. Gregoire (HEC Montréal)
Capital markets often assign sharply divergent valuations despite comparable artificial intelligence (AI) disclosures. This paper explains that divergence by theorizing AI renewal as an architecture-level design challenge evaluated under execution opacity. Using crisp-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (csQCA) of 428 firm–years from AI-intensive public technology firms (2021–2024), we identify four recurring configurations of disclosed AI architectures associated with high or low valuation credibility. High-valuation configurations exhibit coherence between value creation and control logic, either through internal anchoring and embedding or externally governed coordination. Low-valuation configurations arise when AI visibility lacks operational depth or credible governance signals. We show that valuation does not follow AI visibility alone but hinges on whether disclosed architectures make execution readiness legible. We contribute by reframing orchestration as an externally inferred mechanism and refining architectural alignment as structural coherence under opacity. Implications follow for how managerial and policy audiences interpret AI governance credibility through disclosure design.
