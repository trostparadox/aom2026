A Tale of Two Logics: Narrative Distinctiveness and the Realization of Social Impact

ENT
Mengqi Guo (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Xiaran Chu (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Yifei Ke
Narratives constitute a critical mechanism through which social enterprises construct legitimacy and sustain social impact. Yet existing work rarely examines narratives under institutional complexity, leaving limited explanation for how hybrid organizations use narratives to scale impact when facing tensions between multiple institutional logics. Drawing on Optimal Distinctiveness Theory (ODT), we analyze 216 Chinese social enterprises using topic modeling to identify narrative features. Our findings reveal two key insights. First, narrative distinctiveness exerts logic-contingent effects: market-oriented narrative distinctiveness significantly enhances social impact, whereas social-oriented narrative distinctiveness may undermine it. Second, organizational innovation capability and social organization rating positively moderate these relationships, amplifying the benefits of market-oriented distinctiveness and mitigating the legitimacy risks of social-oriented distinctiveness. This study advances theorizing on narrative distinctiveness in the social entrepreneurship context, extends the boundary conditions of ODT by incorporating institutional complexity and tensions among multiple institutional logics, and offers practical implications for how social enterprises can refine narrative strategies to achieve value creation and the diffusion of social impact.

Narrative Distinctiveness and Sustainability Orientation as Drivers of Legitimacy

ENT
Ester Christou (RWTH Aachen University)
Entrepreneurs depend on legitimacy to secure investor resources, with entrepreneurial narratives serving as a central mechanism of new venture legitimation. To attract resources effectively, these narratives must strike an appropriate balance of “optimal distinctiveness.” Analyzing 1,121 equity crowdfunding campaigns on Crowdcube in the period from 2017 until 2023, we examine how a sustainability orientation in entrepreneurial narratives shapes the effectiveness of strategic positioning through distinctiveness on equity crowdfunding platforms. Our study identifies a venture’s narrative distinctiveness and a sustainability orientation as key legitimacy drivers, with their combination significantly boosting campaign success. Our findings reveal that narrative distinctiveness enhances funding success in equity crowdfunding, and this effect is further strengthened for campaigns that incorporate a focus on ESG goals. Building on optimal distinctiveness theory, our study provides meaningful insights into how legitimacy mechanisms successfully enhance investor attraction, by addressing moral legitimacy as an additional facet of the construct of building legitimacy .Our study presents actionable insights, particularly for sustainable entrepreneurs, on how to develop effective narratives to in order to secure funding success in equity crowdfunding.

Shaping the Self: How Sustainable Entrepreneurs Leverage Institutional Logics for Legitimacy

ENT
Jiejie Lyu (Lund University); Iulia Stroila (Münster University of Applied Sciences)
Sustainable entrepreneurs (SEs) face the complex challenge of establishing and maintaining identity legitimacy while juggling commercial, social, and ecological objectives. This study unpacks how SEs strategically draw on and negotiate multiple institutional logics to build and legitimize their entrepreneurial identity. Using in-depth interviews with 34 SEs in Sweden, we find that legitimacy emerges as a dynamic, multifaceted process. Sustainable entrepreneurs anchor their identity in personal histories and moral commitments, engage with stakeholders and networks, reconcile conflicting logics, adapt in response to ongoing feedback, and communicate with authenticity. This iterative process yields four distinct legitimacy pathways: legitimacy endorsement, where identity aligns closely with dominant stakeholder logics; legitimacy brokerage, characterized by ongoing adjustments to accommodate competing demands; legitimacy defiance, when SEs challenge prevailing expectations to preserve authenticity; and pragmatic legitimacy, wherein SEs compromise or blend logics to secure resources and stakeholder support. Our study advances sustainable entrepreneurship research by theorizing identity legitimacy as a dynamic, logic-based process of alignment, strategic response, and continuous adaptation. It also offers practical insights for sustainable entrepreneurs on how to navigate identity construction and stakeholder relationships within complex and pluralistic institutional environments.

The Recipe for IPO Success: A Configurational Approach to Entrepreneurial Storytelling

ENT
YASUTAKA JIKUYA (Yokohama National University); Hikaru Kinouchi (Yokohama National University)
Despite the growing recognition of storytelling in resource acquisition, existing research remains dominated by a variable-oriented approach that isolates the net effects of narrative elements. Adopting a configurational perspective, this study examines how entrepreneurial narratives combine with organizational attributes and external endorsements to facilitate IPO success. Using fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) on a unique dataset of 95 Japanese ICT firms, we identify equifinal recipes that lead to high funding performance. We find that young ventures can overcome the "liability of newness" by effectively generating "hype" through "unfamiliar" narratives—but only when this novelty is "buffered" by the endorsement of prestigious lead underwriters. In contrast, mature firms achieve "optimal distinctiveness" by combining an established track record with novel storytelling to signal continuous innovation. By revealing these causal asymmetries, this study contributes to optimal distinctiveness and legitimacy theory, demonstrating that the strategic value of hype and novelty is not intrinsic but relationally determined by specific constellations of organizational signals.

Value Proposition Stability and Optimal Distinctiveness in Crowdfunding

ENT
Xiaojuan Hu (China University of Petroleum (East China)); Vincenzo Buttice (Politecnico di Milano)
We theorize that the effect of distinctiveness on resource acquisition depends on the perceived stability of the value proposition. In the early stage, when the value proposition appears prone to changes, distinctiveness predicts resource acquisition only when paired with stability anchors. In the later stage, timing itself functions as a stability anchor and distinctiveness operates on its own on resource acquisition. Analyzing 1,219 reward-based crowdfunding campaigns, we find that in the early-stage of the campaign, distinctiveness is associated with resource attraction only when transparent information or third-party endorsements are available; in the later stage, the association stands on its own, while transparency and third-party endorsements no longer moderate the relationship.
