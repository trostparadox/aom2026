Session overview

How can corporate purpose move systems from a state of adequacy to one of excellence? This symposium advances cross-divisional dialogue on the phenomenon and concept of purpose-driven system change in practice. While there is broad agreement across management scholarship that organizations play a critical role in addressing complex societal challenges, much of the existing literature has focused on why corporate purpose matters. Far less attention has been paid to how purpose-driven system change unfolds – specifically, how organizations sense, seize, and transform in ways that enable strategic renewal and reshape value creation for multitude stakeholders within dynamic and institutionally embedded systems. Bringing together leading scholars and practitioners from Strategic Management, Organization Development and Change, and Social Issues in Management, the symposium addresses this gap by integrating perspectives on dynamic capabilities, strategic renewal and organizational change, and system emergence and evolution. The discussion is anchored in two in-depth empirical cases: a U.S. hospital operating within a highly regulated, hybrid public-private system, and a Swiss-based chemical company operating in a dynamic and disruptive for-profit environment. These contrasting institutional contexts and value orchestration logics enable systematic comparison of how corporate purpose conditions strategic adaptation, capability reconfiguration, and system-level value creation. Building on and extending the symposium series presented at AOM 2023, 2024, and 2025, this session explores emerging theoretical, methodological, and empirical approaches to studying the drivers and mechanisms of purpose-oriented system change. By fostering interdisciplinary and practice-informed dialogue, the symposium defines new research directions that strengthen both scholarship and managerial practice.
