Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) supports scholars who aim to conduct and publish less commonly used and emerging qualitative research in supply chain management, responding to calls to engage grand societal challenges and to broaden the field’s methodological repertoire beyond its more dominant templates. The PDW is explicitly linked to the Journal of Supply Chain Management special issue “Toward novel ways of doing qualitative research in SCM: a developmental initiative,” which takes a developmental approach to questions of knowing, contextualizing, evaluating, and experimenting with qualitative methodologies across interpretive, historical, and critical traditions, among others. However, it might serve any scholar who is conducting qualitative research. The PDW has two integrated parts. Part 1 is an open, plenary “methods inspiration” session in which invited experts provide short, actionable talks on underutilized qualitative approaches for SCM (e.g., discourse/frame analysis, interpretivism and phenomenology, ethnography and participant observation, process research, and qualitative work attuned to complexity and systems). Part 2 is an invitation-based paper development session organized as mentor-led roundtables. Author teams submit extended abstracts prior to the conference; selected teams receive structured feedback from a dedicated mentor with relevant methodological expertise, plus peer feedback from the table. The PDW is designed to support authors in crafting publishable qualitative studies and to build a community of practice that strengthens shared – yet plural – understandings of how quality is argued for, demonstrated, and debated in qualitative research in SCM.
