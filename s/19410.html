Session overview

Organizations increasingly operate in settings marked by complexity (Armanios et al., 2025; Khapova et al., 2025), that is, systems where many interdependent elements interact to produce nonlinear dynamics and emergent patterns that cannot be predicted from studying any single component alone (Anderson, 1999). Recent examples include the COVID-19 pandemic, the rapid diffusion of generative AI, geopolitical shocks such as the war in Ukraine and resulting energy market disruptions, and escalating climate events that cascade through infrastructure, policy, and organizational systems. Yet much empirical research continues to isolate single variables and estimate average relationships under linear assumptions (Fiss, 2007; Steenbergen, 2016), approaches better suited to stable, decomposable environments. Addressing recent calls to better align methods, data, and theorizing (Bergh et al., 2025; Bliese et al., 2024), this PDW begins with an integrative introduction positioning emerging methods as tools connecting rich data with theoretical insight. This foundation is deepened through focused presentations on causal machine learning, multiverse analysis, large language models, and configurational approaches, each illustrating how data intensive techniques can generate, refine, and interrogate theory. Building on this perspective, a panel discussion brings together leading scholars to reflect on how these logics can be productively combined, addressing questions of inference, transparency, ethics, and theoretical contribution. The PDW concludes with a Live Method Genesis Lab, where participants bring a 1-page research idea focused on a phenomenon and theoretical puzzle (without specifying a method) and, guided by rotating faculty “method architects,” collaboratively generate and compare multiple viable analytic designs.
