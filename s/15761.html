Changeable or Unchangeable? Attribute Permeability and Perceived Work-Life Subgroups

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Chikae Naito Waseda University; Yoichiro Hayashi; Fumiko Kumada University of Tsukuba; Moka Kawase Keio University

Session overview

This study examines whether some attributes trigger perceptions of subgroups more strongly than others. Specifically, the current study focuses on the permeability of attributes, with the goal of determining whether attributes that are relatively difficult or easier to change are more likely to elicit subgroup perceptions. Building on research highlighting the importance of attribute configurations in shaping subgroup dynamics, we examine whether the perceived permeability of attributes serves as a psychological cue that strengthens the boundaries of subgroups. Data from 728 full-time employees indicate that attributes that are difficult to change (low-permeability) tend to evoke stronger subgroup perceptions than those that are easier to change (high-permeability). Although the hypothesis that this relationship would be stronger among individuals with a high “need for cognition” was not supported, the findings suggest a more complex underlying mechanism, potentially involving alternative cognitive processes. This study highlights the role of permeability and offers new perspectives on attribute classification and subgroup faultline research by refining the conceptual grouping of attributes.
