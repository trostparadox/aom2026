Determinants Of Battery Price Competitiveness In The EV Industry: Evidence From A Multi-Market Panel

OSCM
Kimo Bruns (The University of Sydney); Rico Merkert (The University of Sydney)
The rise of electric vehicles (EVs) and the rapid ascent of competitive Chinese manufacturers have reshaped the global automotive landscape, shifting the basis of competition from brand strength to cost efficiency and supply chain control. This study examines the determinants of automakers’ battery price competitiveness across the world. Drawing on battery electric vehicle (BEV) sales data from China, the U.S., and Europe spanning April 2009 to February 2025, we employ a panel regression approach grounded in the resource-based view (RBV) and design a global EV supply chain vertical integration index to evaluate how global commodity price dynamics, strategic choices such as vertical integration and specialization, and ecosystem positioning, such as headquarters location, influence battery pricing and, hence, competitiveness. Our results indicate that EV-demand-driven and scalable inputs (lithium, manganese, and graphite) exhibit significant positive pass-through price effects, while nickel and cobalt show negative associations, reflecting cyclical supply dynamics. Vertical integration reduces battery prices at the industry level, although this advantage weakens as integration becomes widespread and strengthens with BEV market tenure. BEV portfolio specialization does not directly lower battery prices but may indirectly facilitate integration and scale. Automakers headquartered in Asia, particularly China, achieve substantially lower battery prices across all three markets, underscoring the role of nationally embedded supply chain ecosystems. The study advances understanding of how automakers build competitiveness amid evolving supply chain dynamics and offers timely insights into price drivers and strategic levers for academia and practice.

Building Competence and Capability in Electric Vehicle Battery Recycling

OSCM
Jiaqi Luo (Newcastle University Business School); Yan Jiang (Newcastle University); Ying Yang (Newcastle University)
Electric vehicle (EV) battery recycling is essential to sustainable development in the EV industry, yet companies in this sector face persistent regulatory, technological, and market uncertainty. This study examines how companies build and configure competences and capabilities, and how these configurations shape strategic behavior in this emerging field. Drawing on embedded multiple case study with 26 companies in China, the analysis combines semi-structured interviews with documentary materials and applies an abductive approach. The study identifies four competence elements and four capability elements, and cross-case comparison across five company types shows systematic differences in how elements are combined. The competence-capability nexus is configurational: competence accumulation is necessary but not sufficient for strategic capability. Instead, capabilities depend on how competences are mobilized through organizational arrangements that shape commitments, decision rights, and exposure to uncertainty. Some companies achieve strong cost responsiveness despite limited technological depth, while technologically advanced companies prioritize stability and compliance, and business model reconfiguration remains rare. By revealing how configurations generate distinct adaptation pathways under uncertainty, this study contributes firm-level insights into sustainability transitions research beyond technology and policy, highlighting the organizational choices that translate competences and capabilities into action.

Scope 3 Emission Reduction: Configurational Pathways of Environmental Purchasing Practices

OSCM
Sina Behzadifard (Stockholm School of Economics); Matin Mohaghegh (Stockholm School of Economics); Pär Åhlström (Stockholm School of Economics)
Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions constitute 75-90% of firms' total carbon footprints yet present a governance paradox: firms are held accountable for emissions they do not directly control. Environmental supply chain management (ESCM) practices represent the primary instruments for extending environmental governance beyond organizational boundaries. Yet despite widespread adoption, aggregate Scope 3 emissions continue rising, suggesting that how practices combine matters more than whether firms adopt them. Prior research examines practices in isolation, neglecting configurational interdependencies. This study employs fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) on panel data comprising 239 firm-year observations from U.S. manufacturing firms (2018-2024). Eight ESCM practices serve as conditions; year-over-year change in Scope 3 emission intensity serves as the outcome. Results reveal five distinct upstream and three downstream configurational pathways to emission reduction, confirming equifinality. Monitoring appears in all successful configurations; commitment and management systems function as near-core elements. Upstream pathways exhibit higher enforcement intensity, while downstream pathways rely on collaborative mechanisms, consistent with power-based governance differentiation. Temporal analysis reveals consolidation from diverse experimentation (2019-2020) toward convergent configurations (2023-2024). This study contributes to sustainable supply chain management by demonstrating that ESCM effectiveness derives from configurational complementarity rather than additive accumulation.

Will Mandatory Climate Risk Disclosure Mitigate Global Warming and Promote Operational Performance?

OSCM
Shucheng MIAO (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Chris K. Y. Lo (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Andy C. L. Yeung (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University)
In response to climate change, the United Kingdom introduced a pioneering mandate in 2022 requiring large firms to publish climate-related disclosures aligned with the TCFD framework. While aimed at improving transparency and climate risk management, evidence of its dual environmental and operational impact has been sparse. Drawing on resource-based and dynamic capabilities perspectives, this study examines how such mandates influence firm performance and the distinct roles of transition risk versus physical risk disclosures. Using a Difference-in-Differences design with a matched European control group, we analyzed 1,098 listed manufacturing firms from 2020 to 2024. Findings show that the UK mandate significantly reduced greenhouse gas emissions and enhanced operational capabilities, particularly among firms with stronger complementary assets such as technological resources, operational slack, and ISO 14001 certification. Textual analysis further reveals that disclosures focused on forward-looking transition risks—emphasizing strategic adaptation—are associated with both emissions reduction and operational gains. In contrast, disclosures centered on physical risks reduce emissions without improving operations. The study demonstrates that well-designed disclosure regulations, coupled with strong internal capabilities, can simultaneously advance environmental and operational performance. Managers should cultivate technological, operational, and environmental competencies to turn compliance into a strategic advantage. Policymakers can amplify impact by promoting substantive, strategy-oriented disclosures that integrate climate planning into core operations. This research contributes to operations management by illustrating how external regulation and internal capabilities jointly foster sustainable competitiveness.
