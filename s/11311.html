The Impact of Leadership and Organizational Culture on Work Engagement in Tourism Industry

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

Author: Tomohiro Higuchi Ritsumeikan University

Session overview

This study investigates the mechanisms through which supervisory leadership and organizational culture influence individual performance, well-being, and job satisfaction, specifically examining the role of mediating work engagement. Utilizing secondary data from the Social Science Japan Data Archive (SSJDA), a quantitative analysis was conducted on a sample of 8,043 individuals of all industries and 379 individuals of tourism-specific across 20 countries in the Asia-Pacific and Western regions. The methodology employed exploratory factor analysis, structural equation modeling, and indirect effect regression analysis using Sobel test and bootstrap method. The findings reveal that, across all industrial sectors, both leadership and organizational culture exert a statistically significant influence on work engagement, performance, well-being, and job satisfaction. However, a distinctive divergence emerged within the tourism industry. While organizational culture significantly impacted work engagement, its influence on individual performance, well-being, and job satisfaction failed to reach statistical significance. This research offers a theoretical contribution by refining the engagement model specifically for the tourism context. Furthermore, the practical implications highlight the critical need for strategic organizational culture formation to bolster professional outcomes within the tourism sector.
