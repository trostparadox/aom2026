Attention and Invention Quality: A Topic Modeling Approach

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4

People

Author: Alex James Wilson University of North Carolina Wilmington

Session overview

This study investigates the consequences of the structural distribution of attention on invention quality. I posit that an increased number of attended-to problems in a business unit (BU) improves quality, although there is a point beyond which additional problems yield diminishing returns. The quality of inventions also depends on the similarity in the problems to which a firm’s business units attend – units with high overlap can collaborate effectively, units with low overlap can operate independently, but units with moderate overlap may focus on problems peripheral to both units. I test this theory using data on Motorola’s corporate structure and topic modeling of its patent data over a 23-year period. In short, I find a firm’s structural distribution of attention to technology problems affects the impact of its inventions. In identifying this linkage, this study contributes to both theories of organizational attention and design.
