From Classroom to Corporate: Evolving Entrepreneurial Intentions Among Entrepreneurship Graduates

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4

People

Authors: Evelyn Owie West Virginia University; Hyeonsuh Lee West Virginia University; Ryan Angus West Virginia University

Session overview

This study examines how entrepreneurship students’ intentions evolve as they transition from undergraduate education to paid employment. Although entrepreneurship education fosters entrepreneurial mindsets and capabilities, many students pursue traditional employment after graduation. Drawing on a preliminary investigation involving 24 in-depth interviews with entrepreneurship graduates from flagship U.S. universities (2020–2024), we explore the dynamic processes shaping their career choices and shifting entrepreneurial intentions. Using a grounded theory approach, we uncover how formal entrepreneurship education, personal motivations, and early career experiences influence the development, suppression, or transformation of entrepreneurial goals over time. By investigating how graduates adapt and apply their entrepreneurial competencies in paid employment, this study contributes to the literature on entrepreneurial career trajectories and highlights the career transition dynamics between entrepreneurship education and paid employment outcomes.
