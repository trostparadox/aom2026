How Discriminatory AI Advice Erodes Cooperation – An Experiment on AI-Induced Immoral Behavior

Authors: Artur Klingbeil Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg; Philipp Schreck; Nils C. Köbis

Session overview

While managerial decision-making once relied solely on human judgment, it is now frequently supported by artificial intelligence (AI) in both private and business settings, where AI adoption can yield more informed and efficient decisions. However, blind trust and over-reliance on AI recommendations can lead not only to factually incorrect decisions but also immoral ones such as unfair treatment due to algorithmic bias. Examples of ethical risks in management include the use of AI decision-support systems in recruiting, loan approval or insurance assessments, where biased AI may exacerbate illegitimate discrimination. By examining whether advice induces gender discrimination, our study responds to calls for experimental comparisons of the magnitude of AI-induced versus human-induced moral corruption. Our large-scale online experiment uses an incentivized social dilemma game to examine how advice influences immoral decision-making when consequences matter financially. We manipulate advice source (AI vs. human) and moral content (no discrimination vs. statistical discrimination vs. taste-based discrimination) so participants are randomly assigned to one of seven conditions in a 2x3+1 (no advice) between-subjects factorial design. Preliminary results show that participants are less likely to follow human advice the more blatant the discrimination. Remarkably, discriminatory content does not affect reliance on AI advice, although it is clearly identified by participants. This behavioral result is supported by self-reported data showing that participants perceive AI as more objective. Our study contributes to the understanding of how AI can reinforce social issues in management, inviting research on how to mitigate these risks in business applications of AI-assisted decision-making.
