Session overview

Field-based theories are widely used in organizational research to explain stability, power, and change. Yet many scholars across different career stages find it challenging to move from familiarity with an empirical setting to a theoretically grounded analysis of fields. In practice, concepts such as “field,” “boundary,” and “legitimacy” are often treated descriptively rather that analytically, leading to blurred field boundaries and under-specified explanations of power. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is designed to help participants strengthen how they use field theory in qualitative research. Rather than introducing field theory as a body of concepts, the workshop focuses on common analytical difficulties that arise when applying field-based approaches in empirical work. In particular, it addresses three interrelated challenges: (1) defining and justifying field boundaries beyond industry or sectoral descriptions; (2) maintaining theory-led reasoning to avoid descriptive drift during field analysis; and (3) analyzing legitimacy as a structuring mechanism that stabilizes power relations within fields. The workshop is highly interactive and organised around short conceptual prompts, small-group exercises, and collective reflection. Participants will work with their own research contexts to explore boundary-setting decisions, examine taken-for-granted assumptions, and diagnose how legitimacy operates within their fields of study. The session is intended for PhD students and early-career scholars using institutional or field-based theories who seek greater analytical clarity and rigour in their research. By the end of the workshop, participants will leave with practical strategies for seeing, bounding, and analyzing fields in ways that strengthen theory-driven explanations of organizational phenomena.
