Session overview

Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO) is widely associated with firm performance, yet limited research examines how EO operates as habitus for Black chief executive officers (CEOs) leading social enterprises within racially stratified entrepreneurial fields. Drawing on Bourdieu’s theory of habitus, capital, and field, this qualitative study explores how Black social enterprise leaders mobilize symbolic capital and how racialized habitus shapes the conversion of entrepreneurial dispositions into organizational performance outcomes. Using an interpretive design, the study analyzes in-depth semi-structured interviews with approximately 25–30 Black CEOs of legally formalized social enterprises in the northeastern United States, supplemented by organizational profiles and archival documents. Data are thematically analyzed using concept mapping to identify patterns in EO as habitus, symbolic capital deployment, and perceived constraints associated with racial bias. The study contributes to entrepreneurship and organization theory by reframing EO as a socially embedded, racialized construct and by advancing Bourdieusian applications in social enterprise research. Practically, the findings inform entrepreneurship education, capacity-building, and funding strategies aimed at strengthening equity and sustainability in social enterprise ecosystems.
