Session overview

Organisational scholars are interested in expertise and its implications for individual, organizational, and field-level phenomena. Drawing on a range of research traditions, scholars increasingly understand expertise as a situated and relational enactment, attribution, and achievement. However, developing such an understanding of expertise requires us to ask new, potentially revealing and critical questions about the performative and communicative nature of expertise, and its relation to traditional ways of understanding expertise as a cognitive attribute or an institutionalised understanding. Thus, our PDW is inspired by the question: ‘What does it mean to take the performing and communicating of expertise seriously, and what would such an approach entail empirically, methodologically, and theoretically?’ Taking a communicative and performative approach to expertise is fruitful for several reasons. First, how expertise is performed has important implications within organizations, for instance, in terms of whose expertise is recognised and applied across organizational boundaries. Second, professional experts are increasingly challenged and complemented by new claimants to expertise, including lay and counter-experts. How do such actors communicate their claims of expertise? Third, as professionals are performing expertise in new contexts and arenas, how is the communication of expertise changing, and what is its relation to underlying professional knowledge? Overall, our motivation is to raise urgent questions about the notion of expertise and lay the groundwork for novel theoretical contributions. As in the past two editions of the workshop, our primary goal is to build a community around the study of expertise within and around organizations.
