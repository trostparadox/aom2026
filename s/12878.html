Session overview

Interpersonal disagreements, divergent viewpoints, and political conflicts are inherent to personal and professional life. When addressed effectively, they can improve individual and organizational outcomes. Yet, people often avoid such interactions. Across political and interpersonal contexts, the symposium examines psychological barriers to effective engagement with opposition, and interventions that enable it. First, we show that people have exaggerated views of opposition, and believe the outgroup is more willing than their own group to let others suffer for gain. The papers document that people avoid engaging with disagreeing opinions and are highly concessionary, in contexts ranging from close relationships to interacting with AI (which should diminish many psychological barriers). Next, we identify that AI-assisted tools can support difficult exchanges by reframing negative feedback to reduce threat and enhance clarity. Moreover, embracing discomfort and resisting overly cooperative norms can improve conflict management and resolution. Together, the papers in this symposium illustrate how mindset shifts and technological interventions can encourage employees and managers to engage in disagreements and better deliver challenging messages.
