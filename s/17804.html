Session overview

As an integral part of today’s technological landscape, artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly becoming part of creative knowledge work, which brings innovation to organizations (Benanav, 2020). With the capacity “to learn, adapt, and act” independently of human instructions, AI can mimic human thought processes, behavior, and decision-making (De Cremer & Kasparov, 2021; Krakowski et al., 2023). This technological advancement transforms AI into “agentic IS artifacts” (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021), exemplified by recent generative AI tools like ChatGPT and Midjourney, which promote idea generation and knowledge work (De Cremer, 2023; Eloundou et al., 2024). Generative AI challenges the uniqueness of human creativity, as it becomes capable of performing tasks once thought exclusive to human cognition (Mateja & Heinzl, 2021); this includes the abilities to recognize patterns in data and to generate new data points, which allows generative AI to generate new text, imagery, and music based on natural languages prompts (De Cremer & Kasparov, 2021). Accordingly, literature on technology and innovation management has started to explore the impact of AI on various aspects of innovation, such as open innovation, digital transformation, disruptive innovation, innovation processes, and practices (Bailey et al., 2022; Faraj & Pachidi, 2021; Lee et al., 2024; Østerlund et al., 2021). Following these, there is a growing interest in understanding how generative technologies transform the nature and organizing of creative knowledge work. In light of the need to understand AI and its impact on creative processes (Zhou & Lee, 2024), the symposium seeks to advance scholarly understanding and foster discussions on recent perspectives and insights around the implications of AI. The integration of AI into creative knowledge work necessitates an evaluation of the legitimacy of human and machine knowledge work, the relationality of human and machine knowledge, as well as the differences between human and machine generated output. As such, the symposium provides a timely opportunity for scholars to engage in conversations about the present and future of work in the era of AI. The symposium features four paper presentations and an integrative discussion, which will explore how generative AI technologies shape organizational innovation processes, in both desirable and undesirable ways, and integrate different views that provide avenues for future research. In the first paper presentation, Jacqueline Lane reconceptualizes entrepreneurship by shifting the analytical lens from the solutions startups build to the underlying problems they seek to solve. The second paper, presented by Steven Randazzo and Moran Lazar, examines how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) reshapes idea evaluation, a critical yet understudied phase of the creativity and innovation process. The third paper presentation by Manav Raj examines how the digitization of book production and consumption reshapes the character of creative output, focusing in particular on the novelty of newly published works. The fourth presentation by Amanda Pratt examines how employees in organizations come to identify and construct meaningful uses for generative AI technologies. The fifth presentation by Rembrand Koning explores how generative AI tools can reduce innovation frictions and close gender gaps in the scientific discovery process. Tying the five presentations together, Professors Georg von Krogh and Michael Tushman will take the role of discussants in this symposium.
