Academic Identity in the Age of Generative AI: A Multistage Process Model

Authors: Rana Shehab Lebanese American University; Maria Kakarika Durham University Business School

Session overview

This conceptual paper examines how the rapid integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) into higher education reshapes academics’ professional identities and scholarly practices. It explores how faculty experience their identities in response to AI-mediated teaching and learning, what factors are central to these experiences, and what emotional and cognitive responses emerge as GenAI becomes embedded in academic life. Building on identity work research, we propose a multistage process model that conceptualizes academic identity as a cyclical process of meaning-making that is emotionally and cognitively charged, morally complex and institutionally mediated. The framework’s core claim theorizes GenAI as an external trigger that creates uncertainty, and results in psychological tension between historic and emergent professional selves, activating appraisals of challenge, threat or loss and algorithmic self-reflexivity, which together can produce identity dissonance. This dissonance shapes three identity outcomes (adaptation, protection, and erosion), which subsequently underpin behavioral strategies of resistance, hybridization or full GenAI integration. These processes are interdependently filtered by institutional support and disciplinary norms, demonstrating how contextual forces co-shape adaptation strategies. By framing academic identity as an iterative system rather than a static endpoint, this model advances identity theory in organizational behavior and offers a foundation for future research on how technological disruptions transform scholars’ work, selves, and institutions.
