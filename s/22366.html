Status as a Resource Position Barrier: Capital Access Disparities for Under-Resourced Entrepreneurs

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6

Author: Patrice Perry-Rivers Virginia Commonwealth University

Session overview

Utilizing the resource-based view and social stratification theory, this paper analyzes the impact of societally-imposed status on borrowed capital access for under-resourced entrepreneurs. We examine two critical dimensions of status that create systematic barriers: racial/ethnic minority status and rural/agricultural status. Using survey data from 1,306 small business owners in Virginia, we find significant differences in capital access between well-resourced entrepreneurs (non-minority, urban/non-agricultural) and under-resourced entrepreneurs (minority and/or rural/agricultural), even among similarly sized firms with comparable creditworthiness. Rural and minority entrepreneurs face similar patterns of disadvantage: they are less likely to seek borrowed capital, less likely to obtain it when they do seek it, and receive less favorable terms (smaller amounts, higher interest rates, more stringent collateral requirements). Moreover, the intersection of racial minority status and rural/agricultural status compounds these disadvantages, with rural minority entrepreneurs facing the most severe barriers. Education moderates some—but not all—of these relationships more effectively than wealth. These findings have significant implications for policies designed to support entrepreneurship in under-resourced communities, whether defined by race, geography, or economic status.
