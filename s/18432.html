Session overview

Social class has emerged as a central construct for understanding inequality at work, reflecting enduring disadvantages in lower social class employees’ access to economic, social, and cultural resources that shape job and career outcomes. Drawing from Kish-Gephart et al.’s integrative framework (2023) that organizes social class research around getting in (job access), getting along (intra- and interpersonal work experiences), and getting up and dropping down (upward and downward mobility), this symposium brings together four papers that illuminate contrasting dynamics of dropping down and getting up while also spanning structural, contextual, and interpersonal domains. Two papers foreground dropping down, showing how class-based network positioning and leader–employee dissimilarity give rise to greater vulnerability and constraint for lower social class employees. Two additional papers highlight conditions associated with getting up, examining how the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and leaders’ inspiration caused by upward transitioners enhance influence and opportunity for lower social class individuals. By juxtaposing papers that span both limiting and enabling career-relevant outcomes while examining important workplace intrapersonal (e.g., agency), interpersonal (e.g., leader-follower dynamics), contextual (e.g., artificial intelligence), and structural (e.g., networks) mechanisms, this symposium responds directly to calls for a more multiparadigmatic understanding of social class at work and highlights underexplored connections across domains. With Jennifer Kish-Gephart serving as discussant, this symposium collectively advances insight into how social class dynamics operate in contemporary organizational contexts and points toward future research directions aimed at creating more equitable workplaces.
