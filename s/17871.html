Session overview

As the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) continues to gain traction across a variety of organizations (Shrestha, Krishna, & von Krogh, 2021), the quality and inclusiveness of the data informing decision support systems are becoming increasingly important (Herath Pathirannehelage, Yash Raj, & and von Krogh, 2025). In complex, multi-stakeholder environments – an inherent characteristic of sustainable contexts – decision-making must also account for diverse and dynamic stakeholder perspectives (De Brucker, Macharis, & Verbeke, 2013). This supports the attainment of sustainable development goals, facilitates consensual decision-making, and affirms the ethical imperative of considering the perspectives of affected stakeholders as “the right thing to do” (de Gooyert, Rouwette, van Kranenburg, & Freeman, 2017). We introduce a novel approach to stakeholder analysis that generates participatory insights on stakeholder relationships, conceptualized as stakeholder balance. We also illustrate how stakeholder balance serves as a foundational input for AI-based advisory systems (Keding & Meissner, 2021), supporting organizations, policymakers and public decision-makers in comprehending multi-stakeholder complexities, thereby enabling more robust, informed and inclusive choices. In doing so, we contribute to scholarly discussions on the role of AI in augmenting managerial capabilities (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021), as well as in enabling global efforts to promote sustainable development (Goralski & Tan, 2020).
