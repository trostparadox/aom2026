Shaping Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Emergence: The Role of Government in Lin-gang, China

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Author: Chengyi Li The University of Sydney

Session overview

Entrepreneurial ecosystems are increasingly promoted by governments as tools for innovation and regional development, yet we know little about how governments shape ecosystem emergence as an ongoing process, particularly under strong and multi-level government involvement. Drawing on the Social Construction of Technology, this study conceptualises ecosystem emergence as a socially constructed process in which development directions are negotiated and gradually stabilised under uncertainty. It examines an ongoing qualitative case study of Lin-gang, China, where multi-level governments coordinate with state-owned enterprises and multinational enterprises. Preliminary findings suggest that ecosystem emergence is shaped by differentiated roles across governance levels and asymmetric contributions by dominant firms, which together contribute to the stabilisation of the emerging ecosystem. The study contributes to entrepreneurial ecosystem research by offering a process-oriented explanation of how ecosystem emergence unfolds through government intervention and multi-level coordination.
