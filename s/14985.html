Session overview

Economic theories have long provided powerful frameworks for understanding strategic decision-making, particularly in areas such as competition, resource allocation, and value creation. However, these frameworks often rely on simplifying assumptions of rationality and utility maximization that fail to fully capture the complexities of real-world decision-making. Incorporating behavioral perspectives allows scholars to relax these assumptions and more deeply examine the cognitive, relational, and organizational factors that shape strategic choices. Behavioral insights offer a richer lens for understanding how managerial cognition and bounded rationality influence resource allocation, how relational dynamics inform contract design and governance, and how perceptions of uncertainty, luck, and randomness affect strategic decision-making. These perspectives are especially valuable in contexts where traditional economic models struggle to explain outcomes shaped by cognition and relationships, such as complex governance mechanisms, contractual dynamics, and decision-making under uncertainty. By addressing these dimensions, researchers can develop a more nuanced understanding of how firms achieve competitive advantage, manage transactions, and adapt to dynamic environments. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to explore these intersections by providing a forum for scholars to integrate behavioral insights with economics-based theories of strategy. Building on last year’s PDW, which emphasized broad theoretical connections, this year’s workshop shifts toward deeper engagement with specific research projects through two focal papers and formal discussions. The PDW is designed to advance research that bridges behavioral and economic frameworks, encouraging both theoretical innovation and practical relevance. By fostering dialogue around concrete research examples, the workshop seeks to inspire impactful scholarship that reflects the multifaceted realities of firm behavior and equips scholars with guidance for studying strategic decision-making in complex and uncertain environments. The 2.5-hour session will follow a three-part format. In the first part, Richard Makadok will open the session by presenting overarching insights on integrating behavioral perspectives into economics-based theories of strategy. This will be followed by the presentation of two focal papers by Jessica Jeesoo Kim and Libby Weber, and Sandip Bisui, Anthony Gibbs, and Jeffrey Reuer, which serve as illustrative examples of how recent research is incorporating behavioral perspectives to address central questions in strategic management. These presentations will be accompanied by formal discussions from Lingling Pan and John Chen, who will not only engage with the specific contributions of the papers but also extend the conversation by situating them within a broader behavioral agenda and identifying promising directions for future scholarship. The session will conclude with Brian Silverman, who will synthesize the key insights from the presentations and discussions and reflect on how behavioral approaches can further advance strategy research. The second part will feature a semi-structured panel discussion, providing opportunities for interactive engagement. Finally, participants will engage in roundtable discussions to refine their research ideas with input from panelists and peers.
