Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is designed for working professionals and scholar-practitioners—particularly those in professional or non-traditional doctoral programs—who seek to translate doctoral research learning into viable and meaningful careers beyond the traditional PhD or tenure-track pathway. The workshop focuses on how research capability functions as portable career capital across teaching-focused faculty roles, applied and practice-based research settings, program leadership, and consulting. Drawing on the lived experiences of recent graduates of an ODC-grounded professional doctoral program now working across higher education, healthcare, and organizational settings, the PDW surfaces the often tacit ways research capability is enacted in practice. Rather than treating research as method alone, the workshop reframes research as a set of inquiry, sensemaking, and credibility-building capacities that support impact, legitimacy, and professional identity across roles. The session is highly interactive and experiential. Participants engage in brief narrative vignettes, structured peer dialogue, and small-group sensemaking activities (including Liberating Structures: Ecocycle Planning and 1-2-4-All) to examine career tensions, boundary-spanning challenges, and role-design strategies related to research use. Participants reflect on where research currently shows up in their work, where it is constrained, and how it might be intentionally repositioned. Participants will leave with clearer language for positioning themselves as scholar-practitioners, practical strategies for leveraging research capability in non-traditional careers, and connection to a peer community navigating similar professional terrain.STARTING POINT - DRAFT This PDW is designed for working professionals and scholar-practitioners—especially those in professional or non-traditional doctoral programs—who want to translate doctoral research learning into viable, meaningful careers across multiple domains: teaching in higher education (teaching faculty, professors of practice, adjuncts), applied research roles, program leadership, consulting and advisory work. Rather than assuming a single “academic career,” this workshop treats research capability as portable career capital that can be translated into multiple legitimate roles.
