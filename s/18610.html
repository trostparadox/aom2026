The She-cretary Effect: How Female Board Secretaries Enhance Stock Price Informativeness

Session overview

In an ideal capital market, firm fundamentals are efficiently reflected in stock prices. In practice, however, this process is neither automatic nor frictionless, as corporate information must be interpreted and communicated to external stakeholders. While prior research largely focuses on the content and timing of disclosures, far less is known about the individuals inside firms who manage this transcapital market. Since 2006, all Chinese listed firms are required to appoint a board secretary as an executive officer with legal responsibility for disclosure compliance and investor communication, allowing us to isolate a single, identifiable disclosure gatekeeper. Our central research question asks whether the gender of this gatekeeper affects the incorporation of firm-specific information into stock prices. Drawing on upper echelons theory and social role theories, we test this proposition using stock price informativeness (SPI) as a market-based measure of disclosure effectiveness. We find Firms with female disclosure gatekeepers exhibit significantly higher SPI relative to otherwise mission process. We address this gap by examining the board secretary, a uniquely powerful disclosure role in the Chinese similar firms with male board secretaries A difference-in-differences (DID) design based on secretary turnover further supports a causal interpretation.
