Session overview

One of Philadelphia’s most renowned symbols is the Liberty Bell: a bell that does not ring. Paradoxically, it persists as a civic symbol because of the absence of sound and the presence of a void. In a similar way, institutions can be conceived of as the Liberty Bell: what maintains them is the absence of explicit uttering of institutional values, and what holds them together are the cracks that allow actors to project into such institutions what they are not. These dynamics are particularly evident in today’s world, characterized by tensions, disorder, natural disasters, and geopolitical fragmentation. Institutional voids and gaps have become the norm rather than the exception. Some of these gaps cannot be repaired and may even resist sensemaking, as they have no sense at all. For example, AI increasingly produces what is presented as ‘true’ and legitimate through algorithms and technologies, while simultaneously creating alternative realities. Placing cracks, voids and non-sense at the center of institutional analysis to explain institutional persistence and (in)stability raises significant theoretical and methodological challenges for OMT, including how to study what cannot be seen, heard, or made visible, and how to engage with non-sense without imposing sense. This PDW provides a forum for scholars interested in the negative in institutions—silence, invisibility, illusion, mystery, and absence—to refine methods and theories that explore how cracks and voids simultaneously fragment institutions and hold them together, and to reflect on the implications of non-sense for institutional architectures.
