A Simple Structural Decomposition of Competitive Balance: Evidence from NBA Season Simulations

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3
OMT

People

Authors: Yifan Wang Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Johan Chu Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Session overview

Competitive balance is central to competitive systems in both sports and organizations, yet existing measures are largely descriptive and do not identify the mechanisms that generate observed outcomes. This poster introduces a simple simulation-based framework that decomposes competition into two components: a competition control parameter governing how deterministically capability differences translate into outcomes, and a season-specific dispersion parameter capturing inequality in underlying team capability. Using NBA data from 1980 to 2023, we estimate the model from season-end win distributions and show that competitive structure is stable over time, while variation in balance is driven primarily by changes in capability dispersion. The inferred parameters reproduce multiple empirical parity measures in generative validation tests. We present this framework as a basis for further refinement and invite feedback on its theoretical positioning, extensions, and applications beyond professional sports
