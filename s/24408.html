Unfair AI Makes Me Selfish: How Algorithmic vs. Human Management Affects Platform Workers’ Voice

OB
Qianyao Huang (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Jun Yin (Peking University); Xinrui Deng (UCL - University of London)
Although research on platform work shows that algorithmic management restricts workers’ opportunities to speak up, less is known about how different management modes shape the content and motivation of voice—particularly why it becomes self-interested and less likely to be heeded. Grounded in social information processing theory and the Computers Are Social Actors perspective, we conducted a three-study investigation of platform delivery work. Study 1 inductively analyzed riders’ naturally occurring open-ended suggestions and found that AI-only management was described as less respectful and less informative, and was accompanied by more self-interested voice focused on protecting personal outcomes. Study 2 tested these patterns in a field survey, showing that AI-only (vs. AI + human) management predicted lower perceived interactional justice, which in turn related to greater self-interested voice. Study 3 used a scenario-based experiment to establish causality and unpack mechanisms: AI-only management increased perceived intangibility and dehumanization, reduced interactional justice, and indirectly heightened self-interested voice. Together, the findings suggest that the platform voice predicament reflects not only constrained voice channels but also AI-dominated informational environments that erode interactional justice and shift voice from collective improvement toward self-protection, underscoring the value of human-on-the-loop designs for sustaining constructive worker voice.
