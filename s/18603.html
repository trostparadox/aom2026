Session overview

Over the past decade, organizational purpose has moved from the periphery to the core of management research and practice, reflecting growing societal expectations that businesses address social, environmental, and human concerns. In parallel, research on humanizing organizations has emphasized dignity, meaningful work, learning, and moral responsibility as essential elements of organizational life that contribute to societal well-being. Despite their shared normative aspirations, these two research streams—organizational purpose and humanizing organizational practices—have largely developed in parallel, with limited explicit integration. This symposium aims to bridge these streams by examining how organizational purpose can be meaningfully connected to humanizing forms of organizing. While purpose research has predominantly focused on strategic, governance, and macro-level perspectives, humanizing research foregrounds micro-level employee experiences and everyday organizational practices. Through a structured and interactive dialogue, the symposium brings together leading scholars in purpose and humanistic management to explore how purpose is enacted through leadership, culture, and governance, and under what conditions it fosters genuinely human-centered organizations rather than remaining symbolic. The panel will address four key themes: (1) the relationship between the purpose movement and the demands of humanistic organizing, (2) corporate purpose as a driver of humanizing organizations across levels of analysis, (3) the organizational and governance mechanisms linking purpose to humanizing practices, and (4) implications for future theory development and empirical research. Together, the symposium seeks to advance scholarship that more effectively serves humanity through organizational and systemic transformation.
