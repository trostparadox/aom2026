Is AI a Bad Apple? Exploring Employees’ Dual Appraisals of AI Collaboration on Quality of Work Life

OB
Wenyan Duan (Harbin Institute of Technology); Zhuojie Yang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Tung-Ju Wu (Harbin Institute of Technology)
The question of whether artificial intelligence (AI) will ultimately have positive or negative consequences is contingent largely upon employee perceptions of it. The increasing incorporation of AI in occupational settings underscores the necessity for prompt analysis of employees’ assessments of AI collaboration. Drawing from the cognitive appraisal theory (CAT), we develop a dual-pathway model to explain how employee-AI collaboration influences the quality of work life. We posit that employees cognitively appraise such collaboration as either a challenge or a hindrance, which in turn shapes their quality of work life through emotional exhaustion. Furthermore, we examine whether AI-related task complexity moderates these effects, positing that AI-related task complexity may attenuate the negative pathway while strengthening the positive one. The model was supported by a multi-method design comprising a field survey (N = 363) and a vignette experiment (N = 203). The findings indicated that challenge and hindrance appraisals serially mediate the relationship between AI collaboration and the quality of work life through emotional exhaustion. Moreover, it was observed that higher AI-task complexity weakens the hindrance pathway while reinforcing the challenge pathway. These findings elucidate the cognitive evaluation process underlying employee-AI collaboration and provide practical guidance for fostering constructive partnerships.
