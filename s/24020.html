5i of Learning: Ambidextrous Practices for Green Innovation in Resource-Constrained Agri-Food SMEs

ONE
Le Phuong Thao Quynh (Rennes School of Business); Laurent SCARINGELLA (Rennes School of Business); Morgane Scaringella (Excelia Business School)
This study uses an abductive multiple-case design with 14 agri-food SMEs, based on interviews, internal documents, and field observations. We identify five ambidextrous practices and organize them into a 5i learning cycle: intuiting, interpreting, integrating, institutionalizing, and influencing. Among these, institutionalizing is realized through infrastructuring, the establishment of an operational foundation, and minimal digital tools to help green practices be sustained over time. The results show that green learning is a multi-layered process where top management sets the pace and standards, and the employees are involved in trial-learning-feedback to refine practices in daily work. In some cases, once practices are established, this process can extend to influencing as a specific phase, where the business spreads practices to the local ecosystem and receives feedback. Besides the organizational learning theory, our research contributes a process lens on ambidexterity and the mechanisms by which small experiments are transformed into green initiatives within and outside the business.
