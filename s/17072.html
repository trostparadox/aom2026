Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) addresses the growing importance of the knowledge economy in shaping organizations, innovation systems, and societal outcomes in the 21st century. As knowledge creation, diffusion, and utilization increasingly drive economic performance and social transformation, scholars and policymakers face important conceptual, methodological, and practical challenges in understanding how organizations contribute to knowledge-based societies. This PDW aims to provide participants with integrative frameworks and analytical tools to study the knowledge economy from organizational, spatial, and policy perspectives, with particular attention to developing and emerging economies. The workshop is designed for policy-oriented researchers and early-career faculty interested in innovation, technology management, and the societal implications of knowledge production. Through a highly interactive format combining short presentations, guided discussions, and small-group exercises, participants will engage with key concepts such as knowledge spillovers, innovation ecosystems, institutional contexts, and the role of organizations in translating knowledge into societal value. Sustainability and green transition issues will be addressed as cross-cutting themes, highlighting how knowledge-driven innovation can contribute to inclusive and sustainable development without being the sole focus of the workshop. By fostering dialogue across disciplinary and geographic boundaries, this PDW contributes to the TIM Division’s mission of advancing research on technology and innovation management with real-world relevance. Participants will leave the workshop with clearer research positioning, concrete ideas for policy-relevant scholarship, and opportunities for collaboration within the AOM community. Participation is limited to 25 attendees and requires prior submission of a CV and a brief description of the intended contribution. Selected participants will be notified in advance. The session will be conducted in an open discussion format. For inquiries and submissions, please contact: zznagui@esca.ma
