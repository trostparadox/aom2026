Teaching Fundamental Alternatives Through an Ontological Framework

MED
Fabrice Cavarretta (ESSEC Business School); Pierre-Eloi GAY (ESSEC Business School)
Teaching fundamental alternatives in management education consistently fails despite various pedagogical attempts and growing recognition of their importance for addressing grand challenges. Students resist learning about cooperatives, employee ownership, and commons-based organizations—even when intellectually engaged with the material and exposed to functioning examples. Current explanations emphasizing ideological barriers, resource constraints, or accreditation pressures illuminate important contextual factors but fail to explain the mechanism of pedagogical failure. We reframe the question, asking not how to teach alternatives better but what teaching them makes necessary. This inquiry reveals two integrated necessities. First, teaching alternatives requires an alternative ontological framework students can comprehend. The mechanistic ontology dominating management education —emphasizing atomistic actors, transactional relationships, linear causation, and optimization logic—renders alternatives literally incomprehensible, creating ontological impossibility rather than mere ideological resistance. We present the holobionte framework, understanding organizations as ecosystems of interdependent organisms, as the necessary alternative ontology enabling comprehension. Second, alternative frameworks cannot be appropriated through conventional pedagogy that itself embodies mechanistic assumptions. We demonstrate how garden-based learning functions in integrated fashion as both metaphor (providing conceptual transfer from familiar gardens to unfamiliar organizational forms) and method (enabling experiential appropriation through embodied engagement with actual holobionte systems). These dual necessities explain persistent pedagogical failures and illuminate transformation pathways requiring not content addition but ontological transformation of management learning and education itself.
