Why Adoption Is Not Enough: Intensity, Individual Entrepreneurial Orientation, and Performance

TIM
Rohadatulaisy Nabilah Irwan (Loughborough University); Kun Fu; Shaista Wasiuzzaman
Although digital technology adoption is widely studied, prior research has tended to operationalise adoption at the organisational level, providing limited insight into the micro-level processes through which employees enact digital technologies in their work. Adopting a micro-foundations perspective, this study examines how non-managerial employees contribute to the performance implications of digital technology adoption through their intensity of use and entrepreneurial orientation. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) theory, digital technologies are conceptualised as job resources whose value depends on how employees mobilise them in everyday work, while individual entrepreneurial orientation (IEO) is framed as personal resource shaping such mobilisation. Using survey data from 209 non-managerial employees in micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs), the study distinguishes between digital technology adoption and intensity of use across foundational, operational, and intelligent technologies. The findings reveal systematic differences across foundational, operational, and intelligent technologies in how digital adoption and use relate to individual performance. While digital technology adoption is associated with greater intensity of use, the performance implications of adoption and use vary across technology tiers. Moreover, individual entrepreneurial orientation strengthens the relationship between digital technology intensity and individual performance.
