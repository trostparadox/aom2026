Session overview

This symposium advances hybrid work research by focusing on managers’ experiences, challenges, and practices in hybrid contexts. Across five papers, the symposium highlights how hybrid work transforms coordination, visibility, cognitive regulation, and HRM implementation, creating new and often less visible managerial tensions. The first two papers examine how hybrid arrangements affect employee engagement and boundary management, emphasizing the importance of employee voice, participatory leadership, and goal alignment in managing fluctuating patterns of co-presence. A third, conceptual paper introduces the notion of “visibility labor,” reframing visibility in hybrid work as a form of ongoing and unevenly distributed labor with implications for well-being, equity, and managerial sensemaking. The final two papers focus directly on managerial adaptation, showing how line managers actively reinterpret HR practices in hybrid settings and how hybrid leadership competencies can be developed through micro-interventions embedded in daily work.
