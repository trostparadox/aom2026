From Setbacks to Stepping Stones: How Does Managerial Coaching Behavior Facilitate Hospitality Emplo

OB
Doudou Liu (Guangdong University of Finance and Economics); Yiming Wang (Renmin University of China)
In the high-stakes hospitality industry, service failures are inevitable; yet, how leadership facilitates learning from these setbacks remains underexplored. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) theory, this study investigates how managerial coaching facilitates employees’ learning from failure through the mediating role of work meaningfulness. A three-wave lagged survey of 244 hospitality employees from China demonstrates that managerial coaching significantly enhances learning from failure through the mediating role of work meaningfulness. Work locus of control significantly moderates this process. Specifically, while managerial coaching facilitates learning from failure through work meaningfulness for employees with an internal locus of control, this indirect effect becomes non-significant for those with a more external orientation. This research extends JD-R theory by revealing a meaning-based motivational pathway in failure contexts and shifts the managerial coaching focus from success-based performance to constructive failure transformation. These insights help hospitality managers convert service failures into valuable developmental opportunities in high-stakes service environments.
