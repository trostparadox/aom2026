Has AI Reviewed This? A State-of-the-Art of Integratig GenAI in Review Process

CTO
Eileen Feind (University of Kassel); Jan Marco Leimeister (University of St. Gallen)
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) can significantly improve the scope and depth of reviews and editorial tasks. However, its use raises fundamental questions about the methodology, quality and ethical impact for reviewing. While prior research, especially in the Information Systems (IS) field, has extensively explored the capabilities and limitations of GenAI, there remains a gap in understanding its implications for authors, reviewers, and editors. In response, this paper focuses on analyzing what reviewing tasks should GenAI be able to do, regardless of its current technical capabilities. First, we analyze the state of the art of review processes and associated quality standards in Management and IS, as defined by leading journals. We then synthesized a unified process and a set of standards that not only supports a comprehensive understanding of existing review processes but also serves as a basic framework for potential GenAI integration. Based on ethical, methodological, and quality criteria we outline specific scenarios for incorporating GenAI into this framework and identify situations in which its use may be inappropriate, considering guidelines from publishers, journals and scientific bodies. Finally, we outline role-specific guidelines for reviewers, authors and editors including acceptable and unacceptable practices for using GenAI in reviewing processes.

Sanitized Patriarchy: Algorithmic Safety, Benevolent Sexism & the Construction of Gendered Reality

CTO
Yuanhao Liu
As Large Language Models (LLMs) evolve from passive tools into active AI co-workers, they fundamentally reshape the social fabric of the digital workplace. This study investigates the interpersonal reality of the human-AI dyad. Integrating Role Congruity Theory and Ambivalent Sexism Theory, I argue that bureaucratic safety alignment protocols function as a mechanism of algorithmic sanitization: they effectively purge overt hostility while inadvertently stabilizing benevolent sexism. Analyzing over 200,000 real-world interactions, I find that the AI operates not as a neutral arbiter, but as a sanitization engine. First, through dyadic entrainment, AI functions as a servile mirror, validating users’ paternalistic scripts to maintain conversational flow. Second, the AI distorts the cognitive structure of gender by collapsing the traditional trade-off between warmth and competence. For female subjects, it constructs a “Feminist Fairy Tale”—a representation of perfection (high warmth, high competence)—while permitting male subjects to remain nuanced and realistic. Finally, longitudinal analysis reveals that the cooling of sexist rhetoric is driven by the attrition of hostile users rather than pedagogical transformation. I term this phenomenon Sanitized Patriarchy, demonstrating how safety design can obscure structural inequality by masking it in the guise of protection and artificial perfection.

SEC Intervention and AI Washing: Widening the Disclosure Gap Between Transparent and Opaque Firms

CTO
Sedat Erdogan (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Dilek Coskun Erdogan (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Yanbing Li (The University of Texas at Arlington)
This study investigates the impact of the SEC’s targeted regulatory intervention on corporate disclosure strategies regarding “Artificial Intelligence (AI) washing”. Exploiting the SEC Chair Gensler’s December 2023 warnings and subsequent enforcement actions as an exogenous shock, we employ a difference-in-differences design that compares firms with high ex-ante exposure to AI-washing concerns to control firms without such exposure. We document a significant increase in the intensity of AI risk disclosures among high-exposure firms after the SEC Chair Gensler’s intervention, reflecting a structural shift from promotional narratives in Business (Item 1) to substantive AI-related risk disclosures in Risk Factors (Item 1A). Conversely, the increase in AI risk disclosures is significantly less pronounced for firms with high information asymmetry, as proxied by the bid–ask spread. Our study contributes to the corporate disclosure and enforcement literature by showing that soft-law interventions may paradoxically widen the transparency gap, prompting additional disclosure among firms with low information asymmetry while failing to induce comparable disclosure among more low-transparency firms, where information asymmetry is most severe.

The Penalty of AI-assisted Disclosure: Language Convergence, Innovation, and Market Reaction

CTO
Jujun Huang (State University of New York at Binghamton); Ziyi Xiong (Kennesaw State University)
The diffusion of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly large language models (LLMs), is transforming managerial disclosure practices, yet its implications for capital market communication remain unclear. Drawing on information adoption and influence theories, this study examines whether and how the usage of AI reshapes the language of managerial disclosures and affects market reactions. Using a large sample of quarterly earnings call transcripts, we exploit the paired structure of scripted presentation and spontaneous Q&A interactions to isolate AI-related effects. Employing a difference-in-differences-in-differences design, we find that language convergence, measured by BERTScore, increases significantly in prepared presentation scripts following AI diffusion, but not in the Q&A segment. Instrumental-variable estimates further show that greater language convergence is associated with lower market returns in the initial reaction period, consistent with reduced informational influence. Importantly, this negative association is attenuated for firms that project a strong innovation-oriented corporate impression. Together, our findings reveal a paradox of AI-assisted disclosure: while AI enhances efficiency and fluency, it can undermine informational value unless embedded within credible strategic communication. This study advances research on AI and corporate disclosure and highlights the strategic importance of managing AI-generated language in managerial communication.
