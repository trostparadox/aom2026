Session overview

Digital platforms have become a dominant organizational form for coordinating work, giving rise to new modes of gig and platform-based employment across industries and regions. By mediating how workers are matched to tasks, how effort and performance are observed, and how firms, workers, and consumers interact, platforms fundamentally reshape the organization, evaluation, and experience of work. At the same time, platform entry has altered competitive dynamics in many traditional industries, raising new questions about how incumbents and workers adapt to platform-mediated environments. This symposium brings together a set of empirical studies that examine how platform design, information structures, and governance mechanisms shape work outcomes in the gig economy. Collectively, the papers explore how platforms influence hiring and matching processes, how performance is assessed and managed in settings characterized by limited direct supervision, and how workers and firms respond to platform-enabled competition. The studies draw on diverse empirical settings, including online labor markets, on-demand service platforms, and platform-impacted traditional industries, and employ a range of causal, quasi-experimental, and qualitative research designs. By focusing on the mechanisms through which platforms organize, monitor, and evaluate work, the symposium advances understanding of how platform-mediated labor markets function and evolve.
