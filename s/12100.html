Demands–Ability Fit and Performance: A Moderated Mediation Model of Knowledge Sharing and Reflexivity

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

Authors: Jee Young Jeeny Seong Jeonbuk National University; Doo-Seung Hong Seoul National University; Jun Woo Lee Hanbat National University

Session overview

This study investigates the relationship between individual-level demands–ability fit and task performance, focusing on the mediating role of knowledge sharing and the moderating role of reflexivity. Based on data from 205 working professionals, we tested a moderated mediation model using regression analysis. Results indicated that demands–ability fit positively influenced knowledge sharing, which in turn predicted task performance. Moreover, reflexivity significantly moderated the effect of demands–ability fit on knowledge sharing. The indirect effect of demands–ability fit on performance via knowledge sharing was stronger when reflexivity was high. These findings highlight the importance of both social-cognitive and behavioral processes in optimizing individual performance.
