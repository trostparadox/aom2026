Authors: Zhiya (Alice) Guo Utah State University; Russell Eric Johnson Michigan State University; John Bush University of Central Florida

Session overview

We introduce the concept of the polarization mindset. Although polarization is often viewed as a political or societal issue, an individual-level proclivity for polarization can be triggered and maintained within the workplace. We begin by outlining the core features of the polarization mindset and identifying four key psychological states that contribute to its emergence. Building on this foundation, we then draw from motivation theory to propose a needs-based framework. In doing so, our work connects motivation theory with broader societal concerns.
