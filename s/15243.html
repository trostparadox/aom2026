Shifting Sands: Professional Identity Work in Evolving Human-AI Collaboration

Authors: Saskia Schmid OTH Amberg-Weiden University of Applied Sciences; Christian Schieder OTH Amberg-Weiden University of Applied Sciences; Andy Weeger Neu-Ulm University of Applied Sciences

Session overview

Human-AI collaboration (HAIC) is reshaping professional work by redistributing tasks, decision authority, and responsibility between humans and artificial intelligence (AI) systems. Existing research often treats identity change as a discrete transition following technology implementation. This assumption is difficult to sustain in the context of AI, which evolves continuously through increasing autonomy, learning, and scope of application. Drawing on identity theory and a weak sociomateriality perspective, we develop a conceptual process model in which profound HAIC triggers identity work that produces incremental micro-adjustments of professional role identity (PRI), which accumulate over time into a hybrid professional identity. Professionals’ perceptions of AI as augmenting versus substituting human expertise moderate these identity development trajectories. By theorizing identity change in HAIC as iterative, accumulative, and identity-mediated, this paper contributes to research on identity work, sociomateriality, and the dynamics of HAIC.
