Forward-Looking Mixed Gamble Perspective: Industry Dynamism and Family Firms’ CVC Targeting

ENT
Ying Ding (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Jin Chen (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Fei Zhu (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Yueyue Zhang (University of Nottingham Ningbo China)
Family firm research increasingly adopts a mixed gamble view, arguing that controlling families evaluate strategic choices as joint gambles over financial wealth and socioemotional wealth (SEW). Yet much mixed gamble theorizing privileges backward-looking frames by locating financial wealth and SEW trade-offs in ex-post, observable stakes. We extend this perspective by making its forward-looking foundations explicit and theorizing industry dynamism as a forward-looking external cue that reshapes family firms’ evaluations of SEW gains and losses under uncertainty. We test these arguments in corporate venture capital (CVC) contexts, where investments are made under substantial uncertainty and outcomes and socioemotional implications unfold over time. Using 7,423 CVC investment deals in China from 2008 to 2017, we find that, relative to non-family CVCs, family CVCs are more likely to target ventures in lower-dynamism industries, consistent with stronger weighting of SEW downside exposure. This tendency weakens as the parent firm’s core industry becomes more dynamic, extending the mixed gamble perspective by showing that when the core business’s future trajectory is ambiguous—implying both upside potential and heightened downside risk—family firms reweight financial wealth and SEW trade-offs so that the anticipated SEW costs of inaction become more salient, making a more risk-accepting posture comparatively more attractive. Our study contributes to mixed gamble research by shifting theorizing from primarily backward-looking triggers to forward-looking external cues, showing that family firms reconfigure financial wealth and SEW trade-offs based on expectations about uncertain future states rather than solely in response to realized shortfalls. It also contributes to CVC research by demonstrating how environmental dynamism and family control jointly shape CVC industry selection.
