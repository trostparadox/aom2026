Helpless but Ethical: The Impact of Corporate Social Irresponsibility on Employee Unethical Behavior

SIM
Puwen Shang (Sun Yat-Sen University); Juelin Yin (Sun Yat-Sen University)
While research on the consequences of corporate social responsibility (CSR) for employees is burgeoning, our understanding of whether and how corporate social irresponsibility (CSiR) may impact employees’ ethical behavior within the workplace remains limited. Drawing on the appraisal theory of emotion, we argue that employees may appraise CSiR as a harmful and threatening event, which elicits their prospective emotional responses and coping strategies. With a field survey with Chinese employees and two experiments, we find that perceived CSiR is negatively related to employee unethical behavior through helplessness and moral rationalization. Additionally, moral relativism significantly weakens the negative effect between perceived CSiR and employee unethical behavior via helplessness and moral rationalization. We contribute to the micro CSR literature by uncovering how emotional consequences of perceived CSiR influence employees’ ethical cognition and behavior in the workplace.
