Artifacts of Doubt: Externalizing Uncertainty to Advance Theorizing From Data

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13

People

Authors: Tien Dung (Jack) Le WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management; Jane Kirsten Le WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management

Session overview

Doubt is a pervasive feature of qualitative theorizing. Although doubt is widely acknowledged as central to abductive reasoning and theory development, methodological accounts continue to privilege data collection and coding while offering limited insight into how researchers actually work with doubt. This paper introduces artifacts of doubt as a conceptual lens for understanding how doubt is productively engaged in qualitative theorizing. We define artifacts of doubt as visual, material, or discursive byproducts of analytic sensemaking that externalize researchers’ not knowing and thereby enable abductive leaps. We theorize how engaging with such artifacts allows researchers to give form to doubt, examine and manipulate it, and incorporate it as a generative resource, rather than suppressing it in the pursuit of analytic closure. By conceptualizing theorizing as a practice that materially engages doubt, this paper advances an authentic view of qualitative analysis, offering a way to address enduring tensions between rigor and creativity, and validation and discovery, in qualitative research.
