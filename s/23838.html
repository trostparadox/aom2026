Human Leadership and Trust in Embedded AI: A Signaling Theory Perspective

OB
Yuan Xiang (Wuhan University); Jing Du (Wuhan University); Xueni Zheng (Wuhan University); Yuanjing Luo (Huazhong Agricultural University)
Insufficient employee trust in artificial intelligence (AI) has become a critical barrier to the effective implementation of AI in organizations. Drawing on signaling theory, this study investigates how human leadership fairness facilitates employee trust in AI by shaping the way employees process AI fairness signals. A preliminary study demonstrates that employees are able to distinguish between AI fairness signals and human leadership fairness signals. Study 1 shows that perceived AI fairness enhances trust in AI, which in turn influences employees’ AI-related coping behaviors, task performance, and counterproductive work behaviors. Study 2 further reveals that human leadership fairness significantly moderates the relationship between AI fairness and AI trust, such that this relationship is stronger when AI transparency is low. Overall, these findings advance understanding of the formation of AI trust and highlight the constructive role of human leadership in AI-managed work contexts.
