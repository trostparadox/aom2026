Unlocking the Black Box: How Servitization Reshapes Operational Capability to Enable Trade Credit

OSCM
Bowen Tong (Central South University); Weijiao Wang (Central South University)
We investigate the impact of servitization on supply chain finance in manufacturing firms, focusing on the mediating role of operational capability and the moderating role of financial slack. As an innovative business model, servitization fosters closer relationships with supply chain partners and increases the demand for external financial support, yet it also poses risks that may affect operational and financing capabilities. Using panel data from 911 publicly listed U.S. manufacturing firms from 1990 to 2022, our study reveals that operational capability mediates the U-shaped relationship between servitization and trade credit. Moreover, financial slack negatively moderates the relationship between servitization and trade credit. By uncovering the mediating role of operational capability, this study identifies a new mechanism through which servitization affects trade credit. Furthermore, our model highlights financial slack as a critical boundary condition that shapes the influence of servitization on both trade credit and operational capability. In doing so, this paper adds novel insights to the servitization literature from a supply chain perspective and suggests targeted strategies that help managers secure trade credit.
