Resourcing with Data Across Different Contexts: Unpacking the Process of Data Value Realization

CTO
Qicheng Pan (Zhejiang University)
While data is viewed as a vital strategic asset, organizations continue to face a 'data value paradox'—the inability to transform massive data resources into measurable organizational performance. Existing theoretical perspectives treat data as a universally valuable asset, assuming that mere possession automatically leads to performance. However, this static view obscures the reality that data value is not inherent but is enacted through specific utilization processes that vary across different contexts. Adopting a Resourcing Perspective, this study addresses this gap through a multi-case study of four departments in China Railway Construction Bridge Engineering Bureau Group. We demonstrates that the trajectory of data value creation varies according to the contexts. We reveal that an Enforcement Logic drives Data Experientialization and Instrumentalization in low cross-utilization contexts through repurposing and reconstructing, respectively. In contrast, an Innovation Logic leverages extensive cross-utilization to achieve Data Commodification or Intelligentization, depending on the depth of data processing. This research benefits both academics and practitioners by contributing to cumulative theoretical developments related to data value creation—shifting the scholarly focus from static resource possession to dynamic resourcing enactment— and by offering corresponding practical insights to open the 'black box' of transforming data into value through the strategic configuration of resourcing actions across heterogeneous contexts.
