Navigating Leadership: Early Executive Education Among Founding Generation Entrepreneurs 1950-1990

MH
Juha Kansikas (University of Jyväskylä); Tuomas Möttönen (University of Jyväskylä)
This study focuses on the early executive education of business founders. These founder-generation members have succeeded in their careers by creating a high-status business career and by achieving a high societal impact. Thus, based on their personal merits, they have been awarded the honorary title of Counsellor by the Government of Finland, signifying a position in the institutional business elite. Methodologically, a database was created based on prosopography, covering a review period from the 1950s to early 1990s. By focusing on the late 20th-century business elite, we show that early executive education after WWII was cohort-based and resource-dependent. According to the upper echelon theory, we contribute to the business elite concept by demonstrating that, in the case of founding entrepreneurs, executive education has been influenced by pre-event experiences that have occurred throughout their careers. Simultaneously, executive education has influenced the first post-event experiences of founding generation entrepreneurs.
