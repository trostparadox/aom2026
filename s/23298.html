Conceptualizing Endogenous Uncertainty: A Word-Embedding Analysis of Entrepreneurial Podcasts

ENT
Philipp Dahl (Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich)
Uncertainty is widely treated as the defining condition of entrepreneurship, but most research focuses on uncertainty “out there” in markets, technologies, and institutions. This paper shifts attention to endogenous uncertainty: the meaning structure through which entrepreneurs interpret not knowing, and through which exogenous uncertainty becomes relevant for judgment and action. Drawing on Wittgenstein’s idea that meaning is revealed in ordinary language use, I examine how founders talk about “uncertainty” and whether its meaning-in-use varies systematically by founder characteristics. I analyze a large corpus of long-form podcast interviews with entrepreneurs (6,046 hours). Using word embedding models trained separately for four founder groups (men vs. women; novice vs. experienced), I compare how “uncertainty” is positioned within each group’s conceptual space and which concepts form its closest semantic neighborhood. Across groups, “uncertainty” has a shared embedding position, but its surrounding associations differ. Men connect uncertainty more strongly to environmental and technology-related terms, while women link it more to markets and resources. Across all groups, uncertainty is anchored mainly in endogenous concepts (cognition, emotion, and behavior) rather than exogenous conditions; negative affect is a stable backdrop. Experience is associated with less language of inhibition and more language of adjustment, with women showing a stronger shift toward social and coordination-oriented responses. These findings suggest that entrepreneurship depends not only on how uncertain the environment is, but also on how founders make uncertainty meaningful and actionable in talk—implicating theory, measurement, mentoring, and evaluation.

De-Risked, Not Democratized: Who Gets to Surpass Entrepreneurial Uncertainty?

ENT
Nilesh Singhania (IE Business School)
Venture studios are a rapidly emerging organizational form that reconfigures how new ventures are initiated, resourced, and scaled. Whereas Entrepreneurial Action Theory (EAT) typically positions the entrepreneur as the primary bearer of early-stage uncertainty, this study shows how venture studios organizationally subsume much of that uncertainty through staged governance, pre-validation routines, and internalized resourcing. Drawing on an inductive qualitative analysis of interviews, field observations, and digital data, I uncover a distinctive venture-creation logic in which the studio designs the pathway while entrepreneurs execute within defined milestones and decision gates. I theorize the ‘shielded entrepreneur’, an entrepreneurial archetype characterized by reduced exposure to Knightian uncertainty and a role oriented toward disciplined execution under a structured cadence. These findings extend EAT by specifying a boundary condition under which entrepreneurial action is partially decoupled from the entrepreneur’s direct uncertainty-bearing as uncertainty is subsumed by the venture studio.

How Emerging Technology Ventures Manage Response Uncertainty: An Exploratory Case Study

ENT
Xiaoxuan Yu (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Nan Lin
How emerging technology ventures manage response uncertainty remains underexplored in entrepreneurship research. This study investigates this question through an in-depth analysis of two leading Chinese AI ventures. The findings reveal that ventures can proactively identify and manage customer-side uncertainty in the early stage of opportunity development—first by addressing customers’ cognitive uncertainty, and then by managing customers’ action uncertainty through either externalization or embedding strategies. These actions enable ventures to mitigate response uncertainty and gradually build dynamic capabilities for managing it. Distinct from prior studies that mainly focus on supply-side uncertainty, this study highlights the critical role of customer-side uncertainty in shaping entrepreneurial action, positions customers at the core of opportunity realization, and provides new insights for research on entrepreneurial experimentation and entrepreneurial empathy.

Role of Imagination in Shaping Entrepreneurial Pathways: From Possibility to Actualization

ENT
Charu Rastogi (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Srivardhini K. Jha (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Sanjay Jain (California State University-Northridge)
The opportunity actualization perspective highlights how entrepreneurs bring possibilities into being under real-world constraints, yet it gives limited attention to the cognitive and interpretive work that channels this process. In particular, imagination is often treated as a one-time spark rather than an ongoing practice. This paper addresses that gap through a comparative process study of eight deep-tech ventures in India, a context characterized by high uncertainty, long development cycles, and regulatory fluidity. Our analysis yields three main insights. First, we develop a typology linking kinds of entrepreneurs to archetypes of imagination and show how these archetypes function as epistemic heuristics: vision-oriented entrepreneurs employ exploratory imagination to probe uncertain futures, while problem-oriented entrepreneurs use constrained imagination to advance solutions within bounded problem spaces. Second, we open the black box of entrepreneurial work within opportunity actualization theory by identifying three recursive microprocesses (possibility emergence, problem framing, and solution refinement) that progressively transform imagination into actionable pathways. Third, we show that actualization is shaped not only by what entrepreneurs pursue but also by what they discard, illuminating engagement with the non-opportunity space. Our contributions reposition imagination as both the window into opportunity and the ongoing practice through which actualize opportunities.

Three Forms of Entrepreneurship in the Quest for Product-Market Fit in Entrepreneurship

ENT
Thomas Markus Zellweger (University of St. Gallen); Djordje Djokovic (University of St. Gallen)
Entrepreneurship unfolds under uncertainty, which complicates entrepreneurs’ efforts to align idea, actions and environment towards achieving product-market fit. To illuminate how entrepreneurs perform such alignment, we build on the active inference framework from cognitive neuroscience and discuss a two-dimensional model of uncertainty, composed of agentic and environmental uncertainty. Agentic uncertainty is rooted in the entrepreneur’s uncertainty about the interpretation of the environment perceived (perception uncertainty) and about the feasibility of turning an idea into a tangible product (execution uncertainty). Environmental uncertainty, in turn, stems from uncertainty about the environment’s state (state uncertainty) and from uncertainty about the effect of entrepreneur’s actions in the environment (effect uncertainty). To mitigate these uncertainties, entrepreneurs engage in idea-environment (entrepreneurs as visionaries), idea-action (entrepreneurs as engineers), and action-environment (entrepreneurs as experimenters) alignment. Each of these three alignment strategies is associated with benefits and constraints towards achieving product-market fit. Our paper thus generates new insights into how entrepreneurs effectively align idea, action and environment in the pursuit of product-market fit, a critical precursor of success in entrepreneurship.
