Celebrity Firms: Which Legitimacy to Break?

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4
OMT

People

Author: Hasibur Rahman Abir University of Kansas

Session overview

Celebrity firms tend to attract widespread public attention and evoke positive emotional responses when the media portray them as protagonists of industry-level change. Prior research has largely treated nonconformity as a generalized antecedent of firm celebrity, leaving open questions about which types of nonconforming actions are celebrated and under what conditions. Although nonconformity is typically associated with legitimacy loss, celebrity firms often appear to avoid this outcome, in part because media narratives may legitimate their deviations. In this sense, legitimization can be viewed as a byproduct of celebrity creation. In this paper, I argue that, in the process of creating celebrity, the media do not legitimate all forms of nonconformity indiscriminately. Building on Rindova et al. (2006), I propose that nonconforming actions vary in their likelihood of receiving media legitimization and contributing to celebrity creation. Nonconforming actions that primarily violate cognitive legitimacy or taken-for-granted assumptions about how things are normally done may be more easily reframed as innovative or necessary, whereas actions that undermine pragmatic or moral legitimacy tend to be less likely to be celebrated. Pragmatic and moral legitimacy thus appear to be important conditions for celebrity attainment; however, they may not always be sufficient. The likelihood that cognitively nonconforming actions are endorsed may further depend on other forms of social approval, as firms with stronger reputations and higher status may be more likely to receive favorable media portrayals.
