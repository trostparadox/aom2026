Leaving Behind Evolutionary Roots of Success: Leaders' Values and Organizational Outcomes

ODC
Rok Crešnar (University of Maribor, Faculty of Economics and Business); Zlatko Nedelko (University of Maribor)
This study examines how leaders’ personal values translate into organizational productivity within the context of digital transformation in organizations. Building on evolutionary perspectives on human values and insights from behavioral economics, we investigate whether leaders’ value orientations directly shape productivity or primarily influence it through the organizational structures and processes. Survey data were collected between 2019 and 2025 from 1,534 employees in manufacturing and service firms and analyzed using structural equation modeling techniques with bootstrapped indirect effects. Results show that leaders’ values are positively associated with productivity when examined in isolation; however, once organizational structures and processes are taken into account, the direct effect becomes nonsignificant. Instead, leaders’ values influence productivity fully through organizational mediators, such as culture, communication, openness, and strategic/business-model arrangements, which emerge as the strongest predictors of both production productivity and employee productivity. At the value-dimension level, achievement, openness to change, and self-transcendence-oriented values are most consistently linked to supportive structures and higher productivity, whereas power-oriented values show no significant or lower effects. These findings suggest that in digital era contexts, leadership values matter less as individual traits and more as inputs to organizational design: productivity gains depend on leaders’ capacity to institutionalize value-driven behaviors into systems that coordinate work, enable learning, and support rapid, high-quality decision making.
