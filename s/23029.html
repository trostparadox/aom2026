The Recipe of Workplace Mindfulness Intervention: A Systematic Review and Practice-Based Model

MSR
Nandani Agarwal (Indian Institute of Technology, Delhi); Vivek G. Nair (Indian Institute of Technology, Delhi)
Workplace mindfulness-based programmes (MBPs) are widely implemented to address occupational stress and enhance employee well-being, yet systematic understanding of what specific practices drive which outcomes remains underdeveloped. This systematic literature review of 181 workplace mindfulness interventions (190 distinct programmes, 2003–2024) addresses this gap by cataloguing 260 distinct mindfulness practices and analysing their distribution across theoretical mechanisms and occupational outcomes. We identify a critical practice-outcome asymmetry which reflects a fundamental disconnect: practitioners have advanced intervention contextualization (occupational framing) faster than intervention specification (aligning practices to mechanisms to outcomes). To address this gap, we present a model grounded in neuroscientific evidence and organizational behaviour scholarship. The model comprises four sequential levels (Intention ? Attention ? Awareness ? Regulation) and differentiates awareness and regulation across five domains (self, interoceptive, emotional, social, contextual), each with distinct neurobiological mechanisms and occupational relevance. This review represents the first systematic categorization of workplace MBPs beyond clinical nomenclature and the first exhaustive practice-level catalogue of the field, providing practitioners and researchers with a new theoretical grounding for moving beyond generic "mindfulness training" toward practice-based, mechanism-informed interventions.

Beyond Mindfulness: Introducing HEAL as a Second-Order Construct

MSR
Anmol Basant (Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad); Vishal Gupta
Workplace stress and well-being have received extensive scholarly attention; however, organizational research has largely treated distress as a surface-level response to external demands, leaving the deeper experience of employee suffering under-theorized. In parallel, mindfulness—one of the most prominent interventions in this domain—has become conceptually fragmented and increasingly reduced to attention regulation, limiting its capacity to engage with the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral roots of suffering. Addressing these gaps, this thesis introduces HEAL, a second-order construct that conceptualizes employees’ capacity to engage constructively with stress and suffering through the integration of Harmony (cognitive), Equanimity (emotional), and Authentic Living (behavioral). The scale development process began with a qualitative foundation of 23 interviews, which then informed a rigorous quantitative phase involving four rounds of both exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis. This multi-stage approach arrived at a validated 17-item measure, confirming that HEAL functions as a coherent, higher-order construct. By shifting the focus from simple stress management toward the existential roots of well-being, this research offers management scholarship a more integrative way to measure and promote human flourishing.

Can Meaningful Work and Mindfulness Help Remote Workers Thrive? An Empirical Investigation (WITHDRAWN)

MSR
Vineet Arora (Not Associated); Amit Shukla
The accelerated digitalization of work, intensified by the COVID-19 pandemic, has firmly established remote work as a central characteristic of contemporary organizations. At the same time, emerging insights from both scholarly and practitioner literature suggest that the distinctive challenges associated with remote work may limit employees’ ability to thrive. Addressing this issue, the present study investigates the roles of meaningful work and mindfulness in fostering thriving among remote employees. Data were collected through a time-lagged survey of remote workers in India, and the hypothesized relationships were tested using structural equation modelling. The findings point to meaningful associations between meaningful work, mindfulness, and thriving among remote workers, with these linkages operating through an underlying psychological mechanism. The study offers practical insights for organizations seeking to support thriving in remote work contexts, while contributing to ongoing theoretical discussions within the positive organizational scholarship and human resource management literature.

The Mediating Role of Mindfulness Between LMX and Work Behaviors: A Comparison of India and the USA

MSR
Zahidul Karim (Augustana University)
Scholars are interested in revealing the effects of employees’ psychological traits and relationship quality on employees’ work behaviors and job performance. Although there is abundant literature on leader-member exchange (LMX) and mindfulness (MND) and their effects on employee job satisfaction and performance, present literature shows inadequate empirical investigation to examine the mediating role of mindfulness between LMX and work behaviors, such as entrepreneurial orientation (EO), sustainable behavior (SB), organizational citizenship behavior (OCB, and well-being (WB) in a cross-country perspective. Thus, this study aims to reveal the direct effects of LMX on employees’ work behaviors and the indirect effects through mindfulness. Cross-country data analysis (India and USA) has been conducted, using PLS-SEM to explore the differential effects of LMX and MND on work behaviors and well-being. The study examines the significant direct effects of LMX and MND on work behaviors and the differences in relationships between the two countries. Moreover, mindfulness mediates the relationships between LMX and EO, as well as between LMX and SB, in the USA, but not in India. Further, mindfulness mediates the relationship between LMX and OCB but does not mediate the relationship between LMX and well-being for the countries studied. The study also provides important theoretical and managerial implications, as well as future research directions. Keywords: Mindfulness; LMX; Work Behaviors; Well-being; Comparativ Study; India; USA

Synchronizing Minds and Hearts: Leader–Member Sensemaking Fit and Resilience in R&D Teams

MSR
Mouxuan Sun (Dalian University of Technology); Meihan Liu (Dalian University of Technology); yuxi jia; Nick Zubanov (University of Konstanz)
R&D teams face high uncertainty and frequent setbacks, making team resilience critical. This study explores how leader–member sensemaking fit (alignment in interpreting and responding to adversity) shapes such resilience via a sequential mixed-methods design: survey data from 84 R&D teams and in-depth interviews with seven teams. Quantitative results show that higher sensemaking fit, especially the high-leader/high-member configuration, enhances team resilience; this effect is mediated by shared mental models and amplified by leaders’ effective emotional management. Qualitative findings identify an iterative emotion–cognition tension loop in resilient teams, where leaders and members co-regulate emotions and cognitions to enable collective adaptability. Conversely, misaligned sensemaking—particularly the low-leader/high-member configuration—causes fragmented interpretations and weakens resilience. Integrating cognitive and emotional perspectives, this study advances a holistic team resilience theory and provides practical insights for R&D leaders to foster collective sensemaking and emotional stability under pressure.
