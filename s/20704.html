Session overview

Assessment remains one of the most consequential yet least reimagined elements of higher education. While teaching has increasingly embraced student-centred and constructivist approaches, assessment practices continue to rely heavily on high-stakes, content-centric, and reductionist formats such as time-bound examinations and multiple-choice tests. The rapid diffusion of generative AI has further exposed the fragility of these models, raising a fundamental question: are we assessing answers, or understanding? This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) introduces a novel LLM-enabled, dialogue-based assessment system developed at Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology (TIET). Grounded in learning science rather than surveillance or proctoring logic, the system evaluates student understanding through structured small-group discussions. A subject-trained LLM analyses tagged student dialogue to assess conceptual accuracy, depth of reasoning, misconception repair, and partial understanding, producing continuous scores rather than binary outcomes. The approach enables lower-stress assessment, recognition of learning in progress, multiple attempts without compromising rigor, and scalable implementation that does not increase faculty workload. The PDW combines conceptual framing, system demonstration, hands-on redesign of traditional assessment questions, and critical discussion of ethical and pedagogical boundaries. Participants will leave with a practical, pilot-ready blueprint for rethinking assessment as a learning process, positioning AI not as an efficiency tool, but as an epistemic partner in interpreting student understanding.
