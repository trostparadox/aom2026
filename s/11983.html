A Process Study of Liability of Foreignness on Dynamic Capabilities: Japanese MNEs in Emerging Asia

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 2

Authors: Akiko Sato Kyushu University; Kunio Ikari Kyushu University

Session overview

Multinational enterprises (MNEs) are increasingly relying on their foreign subsidiaries to sense opportunities, seize them, and reconfigure resources in volatile environments. Yet we know little about how liability of foreignness (LOF) shapes the microfoundations of dynamic capabilities (DCs) within subsidiaries operating under institutional duality and cultural distance. Drawing on a qualitative, multi-case study of 14 Japanese manufacturing subsidiaries in Vietnam and Thailand, anchored in an in-depth process-tracing focal case, we develop a multilevel process model that links LOF to subsidiary DC microfoundations. We find that LOF manifests internally as (1) LOF-induced sensing distortion, where expatriate and headquarters (HQ) interpretive frames misread local institutional and market signals; (2) LOF-constrained seizing, where HQ-centric authority, inflated risk perceptions, and low psychological safety suppress local initiative and slow resource commitment; and (3) LOF-slowed reconfiguration, where structural inertia, hybrid HRM systems, and HQ– subsidiary trust deficits impede organizational redesign. At the same time, subsidiaries cultivate countervailing microfoundations such as bilingual boundary spanners, selective localization of leadership, and local-to-global knowledge flows that partially neutralize LOF constraints and enable DC development under institutional duality. Our study contributes to LOF research by presenting a novel process model of the mechanisms. We also suggest reframing LOF as an internal, microfoundational constraint on learning and adaptation; to DC theory by positioning LOF as a boundary condition on sensing–seizing– reconfiguring in MNEs; and to subsidiary capability research by explaining how DC emerges through negotiated interactions between expatriates and local actors in high-LOF environments.
