Session overview

Workplace dehumanization, defined as employees’ perceptions of being treated as less than fully human, has become a pressing concern as intensified efficiency imperatives and technology-enabled forms of control increasingly shape everyday work. Yet research remains uneven and fragmented, privileging mechanistic over animalistic dehumanization, lagging behind technology-mediated practice, and offering limited multilevel insight into how dehumanization emerges and how it can be buffered. This symposium brings together five complementary studies spanning gig platform work, AI-based HRM, human-chatbot interactions, ethical leadership under algorithmic monitoring, and person-centered profiles of dehumanization. Collectively, the papers extend the dual-model account of dehumanization across contemporary settings, illuminate sociotechnical mechanisms through which dehumanization is produced, and identify organizational and individual buffers that can preserve humanness in digitally organized workplaces.
