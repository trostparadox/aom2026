Circular Economy Tensions and Resolution Strategies in the Healthcare Sector

HCM
Shobod Deba Nath (University of Dhaka); Tama Chowdhury (Bangladesh University of Professionals); Md. Soleman Mollik (Bangladesh University of Professionals)
This paper examines the key challenges and resolution strategies associated with the adoption of circular economy (CE) practices in the healthcare sector of an emerging country, Bangladesh. To investigate this, we conducted in-depth semi-structured interviews with 33 key stakeholders across the healthcare sector, including healthcare managers, diagnostics, clinicians, suppliers, and policymakers from diverse healthcare institutions nationwide. Thematic analysis of the data identified several tensions that challenge CE adoption in healthcare, including financial–commercial dominance, temporal pressures between short-term service delivery and long-term sustainability, regulatory and policy rigidity, moral and ethical concerns related to patient safety and infection control, and limitations in technological capability. Simultaneously, the study highlights several resolution strategies to address these tensions, such as climate financing, regulatory flexibility, paradox capability building, technology subsidies, and cross-supplier partnerships. Beyond linear economic and operational logics, our findings support and extend paradox theory and contribute to the CE literature by highlighting distinctive normative forces, including moral and biosafety considerations. These findings offer important implications for healthcare managers and policymakers seeking to address adoption challenges and develop strategies to promote CE transitions in material-intensive, resource-constrained healthcare systems.
