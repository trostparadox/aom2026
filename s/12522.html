Power Dynamics, Resilience, and Outcome Satisfaction in Negotiations

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CM

People

Authors: Mehdi Rasouli Ghahroudi University of Wisconsin-Platteville; Sundar Balakrishnan University of Washington

Session overview

Power asymmetries are central to negotiation and conflict management, yet perceived power does not always translate into actual influence or satisfactory outcomes. Drawing on conflict management and negotiation theory, this study examines how sense of power shapes outcome satisfaction through a sequential process involving risk attitude, resilience, and actual power. Using a simulation-based buyer–seller negotiation, data were collected from 104 professionals across public and private sectors. Participants negotiated multiple contested issues and completed pre-negotiation measures of sense of power, risk attitude, and resilience, followed by post-negotiation measures of actual power and outcome satisfaction. Sequential mediation analyses reveal that sense of power increases both risk tolerance and resilience, but resilience plays a pivotal role in converting perceived power into realized influence. Risk attitude strengthens resilience but does not directly predict outcome satisfaction. Instead, actual power emerges as the strongest predictor of satisfaction, indicating that negotiators’ evaluations of outcomes depend primarily on realized leverage rather than subjective confidence alone. These findings contribute to the conflict management literature by clarifying how psychological resources enable effective conflict handling and translate internal power perceptions into tangible negotiation outcomes. Practically, the results suggest that conflict management and negotiation training should emphasize resilience-building alongside power and strategy development to improve negotiation effectiveness and satisfaction.
