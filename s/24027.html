Repairing the Circular Economy: Competing Transition Logics

ONE
Daniel Stabler; Henri Hakala; Anne-Laure Mention (RMIT University); Ahmad Alaassar (RMIT University)
Repair is positioned as a key practice within the Circular Economy (CE) transitions, yet its sustainability potential remains unevenly theorized and inconsistently mobilized. While repair is commonly framed as a higher-order circular strategy within hierarchical conceptualizations of CE contributions, this overlooks how different organizational and institutional configurations shape its role in sustainability. This paper addresses this gap through a integrative literature review of 82 peer-reviewed studies published between 2016 and 2025, synthesizing research across CE, business model innovation, sustainability transitions, and repair studies. The analysis reveals that repair is embedded within competing transition pathways rather than a singular circular logic. By integrating transition orientation (efficiency versus sufficiency) with organizational architecture (centralized versus localized), the paper develops a typology of four repair-oriented value creation architectures: Managed Circular Repair, Distributed Service Ecosystems, Institutional Sufficiency Systems, and Community Repair Commons. These architectures differ fundamentally in how repair is governed, how value is created and distributed, and how consumption, control, and responsibility are configured. By reframing repair as a site of contestation and boundary practice within CE transitions, the paper advances theoretical understanding of circular value creation, challenges hierarchical circular frameworks, and offers implications for circular business model innovation and repair governance.
