Too Painful to Bounce Back: The Influence of Physical Pain on Resilience Under Challenge

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

People

Authors: Xiurong Chen National University of Singapore; Jean SK Lee

Session overview

Drawing on the stress and coping approach to resilience, we propose that physical pain, as a physiological stressor, reduces individuals’ resilience under challenges. Reduced resilience, in turn, impairs functioning under challenge. We also explored the roles of perceived stress, pain sensitivity and pain tolerance in these processes. We tested these hypotheses using two multi-source correlational studies conducted in distinct contexts. Study 1 was a survey conducted in the Gobi Desert, a highly physically intensive setting, with students enrolled in a leadership course as focal participants. Study 2 was a field survey conducted in office settings, which are less physically intensive, with participants from diverse industries. In the Gobi Desert study, physical pain was negatively related to functioning via resilience. Pain tolerance was positively related to functioning via resilience, whereas pain sensitivity showed no significant effects. In the office contexts, physical pain was not directly related to resilience; however, the negative serial indirect pathway from physical pain to perceived stress to resilience to functioning was supported. Neither pain tolerance nor pain sensitivity showed significant effects in the office context. Together, these findings demonstrate the influence of physical pain on resilience under challenges and its downstream consequences for work functioning. This research contributes to the literature on physical pain and resilience by identifying physical pain as a physiological antecedent of resilience across both highly and less physically intensive contexts.
