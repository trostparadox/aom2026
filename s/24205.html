When Worse Is Better: A Typology of Strategic Downgrading Tactics in Product and Service Design

SAP
José Osvaldo De Sordi; Jeremias Bandiera; Carlos Jorge; Carlos Rigato
New patents with the purpose of intentionally downgrading companies’ products and services have attracted the attention of society and motivated the present study. This research aims to discuss the tactics employed by companies for downgrading, as well as their motivations, exploring their strategic potential. Using the Design Science Research approach, a construct-type artifact, structured in the form of a typology, was developed to describe the creative tactics associated with intentional downgrading. To this end, four focus group sessions were held with experienced managers, aiming to identify business experiences that led to a worsening of the design of their processes, products, and services. The developed typology consists of five tactics: frugalism, planned obsolescence, degradation, destruction, and inconclusion. All of these are justified in terms of their relevance to the business and characterized as strategic resources. The developed construct is innovative because it integrates and jointly discusses several tactics that were previously only addressed separately. It also describes tactics hitherto disregarded in the literature concerning the design of products and services. The construct presents a perspective that opposes the dominant mindset in the design area, which focuses on tactics aimed at continuously improving and perfecting products and services.
