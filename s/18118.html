Transition From Dichotomization to Continuum of Opportunity and Necessity Entrepreneurship

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Latrell Hudson University of Mississippi; Clay Dibrell University of Mississippi; Walter Davis University of Mississippi

Session overview

When measuring opportunity and necessity entrepreneurship, researchers generally dichotomize the two as distinct concepts with no overlap or relationship between each other. However, some scholars have highlighted lack of variation among individuals in each category as one of the key weaknesses of dichotomizing entrepreneurial motivation. To address this concern, we developed a preliminary scale that measures the two concepts as a continuum in order to determine the possibility of gauging individual variation of entrepreneurial motivation. In line with previous research, the findings of our proposed scale classify the motivation to enter entrepreneurship as opportunity and necessity. Our results also show that not all individuals are motivated entirely by opportunity or necessity. Rather, some individuals express varying degrees of both opportunity and necessity as their reason for becoming an entrepreneur. This scale shows that although individuals may have desires more strongly aligned with one form of entrepreneurial motivation, they are not restricted to only one type of motivation for opening a startup. As a result, our proposed scale contributes to the call by scholars to transition from the dichotomization of opportunity and necessity entrepreneurship in order to gain more accurate understanding of why individuals decide to become entrepreneurs. Thus, this scale promotes a more robust measurement of entrepreneurial motivation.
