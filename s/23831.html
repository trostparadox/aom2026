AI Misuse at Work: Conceptualization, Measurement, and Outcomes

OB
Na Lu (Shandong University); Weipeng Lin (Shandong University); Zhaoyue Sun (Shandong University)
With the rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, AI use at work has become a prominent research topic. However, existing studies primarily conceptualize and operationalize AI use in terms of its extent (e.g., frequency or intensity), implicitly assuming that AI use is inherently appropriate and compliant, while overlooking the widespread phenomenon of inappropriate AI use in practice. Thus, we introduce the construct of AI misuse at work, defined as employees’ use of AI in ways that deviate from organizational norms and carry potential harm. Across a series of studies, we develop and validate a 17-item, four-dimensional measure of AI misuse at work—AI slacking, AI misrepresentation, AI concealment, and AI privacy violation. We further examine the performance consequences of AI misuse and its underlying mechanisms. Using multi-source, time-lagged data, we found that AI misuse at work was positively associated with professional de-skilling and anxiety about AI use, which in turn predicted lower task performance. Although AI misuse was positively related to AI moral disengagement, this mechanism did not translate into immediate performance decrements. By shifting scholarly attention from how much employees use AI to how AI is used and whether such use violates norms, this study advanced understanding of employee–AI interaction and highlighted the importance of responsible AI use in organizations.
