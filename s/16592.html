From Tools to Work-Agents: Managerial Discretion in AI Integration

Authors: Melike Nur Findikoglu Ozyegin University; Ceyda Maden Eyiusta Özyegin University

Session overview

This study examines the emergence of managerial discretion in shaping intentions to integrate AI across different domains of organizational work. Through qualitative interviews with 30 managers from six firms in the financial services and retail sectors, we show that managerial discretion develops through an emergent judgment-crafting process. This process is driven by managers’ perceptions of AI’s identity—who or what AI is—filtered through their expertise and their assessment of AI’s value proposition and limitations. Managers characterize AI variously as a tool, assistant, thinking partner, or autonomous self-learning entity, shaping their initial orientations toward AI. These perceptions are then translated into attitudes—ranging from positive to skeptical or negative—through personal orientations such as self-efficacy, learning orientation, need for control or autonomy, and relational orientation. Finally, managers weigh motivational signals from senior leadership against material signals of the firm’s long-term commitment to AI, including investments in talent, infrastructure, and incentives, to arrive at discretionary judgments. Our findings underline that successful AI integration depends not only on technological investments and capabilities but also on managers’ discretion. Rather than treating AI integration as a uniform process, our study demonstrates that managers actively calibrate AI’s role across various aspects of organizational work.
