From Linguistic Diversity to Organisational Resilience:A Review of Mechanisms and Boundary Condition

DEI
Samrendra Kishore (Indian Institute of Management); Mamta Tripathi (Indian institute of management Jammu)
Purpose The purpose of this thematic review is to demonstrate that linguistic diversity is a critical yet under-theorised factor in shaping organisational resilience. By identifying key mechanisms and boundary conditions, the study advances a process-oriented framework that explains how linguistic diversity can either strengthen or undermine organisational adaptive capacity. Design/methodology/approach This study adopts a structured integrative literature review approach to synthesise research on linguistic diversity and organisational resilience. PRISMA flowchart has been used for the study selection process to systematically analyse the existing studies categorised according to the ABDC journal quality list and published between 2005 and 2025. Findings The insights derived from the thematic analysis were integrated to develop a process-oriented conceptual framework linking linguistic diversity to organisational resilience. Mechanisms were identified as mediating processes through which linguistic diversity influences resilience outcomes, while boundary conditions were theorised as contextual factors shaping the direction and strength of these relationships. Research Implications This review makes several important contributions to theory by repositioning linguistic diversity as a central, rather than peripheral, element of organisational resilience by integrating language-at-work research with organisational resilience scholarship, advances theory by moving beyond static, outcome-oriented perspectives on linguistic diversity and the articulation of boundary conditions, contributes to a more nuanced understanding of diversity and resilience. Originality/value This study is original in its systematic approach addressing a long-standing fragmentation in the existing literature on organisational resilience and linguistic diversity. Resilience research has traditionally privileged structural, cognitive and leadership-based explanations, often treating communication as a neutral conduit. By contrast, this review demonstrates that language and linguistic diversity in particular, actively shapes sensemaking, coordination and adaptation processes that are foundational to resilience. Keywords: linguistic diversity, multilingual workplace, organisational resilience, adaptive capacity. Type of Paper: Conceptual literature review
