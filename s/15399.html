Session overview

Entrepreneurship research has predominantly focused on new venture creation, leaving entrepreneurial entry through acquisition largely underexamined. This panel symposium brings together leading scholars to explore how Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition (ETA) and search funds—a distinctive organizational vehicle enabling aspiring entrepreneurs to acquire and operate existing small businesses—inform and extend current theoretical perspectives in entrepreneurship, strategy, and organization theory. The panel examines the diffusion of search funds as an organizational form across institutional contexts; entrepreneurial selection and human capital signaling across the search-acquisition-operation lifecycle; value creation through managerial practices and the MBA-to-ETA pathway; and empirical challenges in studying privately held, long-horizon entrepreneurial processes.
