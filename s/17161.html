Betting on the Jockey, Banking on the Horse: The Split Personality of Crowdfunding Success

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Anushka Iyengar Daunt Washington and Lee University; Bright Frimpong Washington and Lee University

Session overview

How do equity crowdfunding investors publicly justify their investment choices, and how do these justifications relate to funding outcomes? Prior research has largely focused on entrepreneur and venture attributes, but less is known about how investor evaluative discourse relates to capital allocation at different levels of analysis. Analyzing 9,234 investor comments from 656 campaigns on Republic, we examine whether publicly expressed evaluative emphasis predicts funding outcomes at the individual and campaign levels. We find that investors who engage in founder-focused evaluative discourse contribute significantly larger individual investment amounts, whereas venture-focused discursive resonance between investor commentary and campaign narratives predicts overall campaign funding success; founder resonance exhibits no campaign-level effect. Together, these findings demonstrate that individual commitment and collective funding outcomes are shaped by distinct evaluative mechanisms, reframing the jockey–horse perspective as a multi-level process rather than a binary choice.
