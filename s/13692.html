Session overview

Confidence is generally understood as "a certainty about handling something, which…entails what a person desires or needs to do" (Stajkovic, 2006). Both confidence and the closely linked concept of self-efficacy are connected to a variety of professional and leadership outcomes, such as empowerment and interpersonal influence (Mowday, 1979; Conger & Kunungo, 1988), effectiveness in salary negotiations (Stevens, Bavetta, & Gist, 1993), the progression into leadership roles (Tharenou, Lattimer & Conroy, 1994), and performance (Stajkovic & Luthans, 1998). Research also links confidence to objective and subjective career success (Spurk & Abele, 2014), as well as improved academic involvement and results (Schunk, 1991). By bolstering motivation, effort, and performance (Bandura, 1997; Lent et al., 1994), confidence fosters resilience against challenges and strengthens the resolve to reach specific objectives. Ultimately, it shapes the decisions individuals make, the nature of the goals they establish, and the level of energy they invest (Zeldin & Pajares, 2000), thereby influencing long-term career trajectories. Developing confidence is, therefore, a fundamental requirement for modern life and careers. In this Professional Development Workshop (PDW), we explore different ways to cultivate confidence in students. This PDW seeks to connect seasoned academic educators with workshop participants aiming to enhance their teaching portfolios related to helping undergraduate, graduate and executive-level students develop confidence. Building on established theory and practice, the PDW offers confidence development pedagogies, tools to enhance the confidence of students, including women students and students belonging to underrepresented and diverse groups, and relational and configurational approaches to develop confidence. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
