How Servant Leadership Protects Service Performance and Rule Commitment Under Customer Mistreatment

HR
PABLO RUIZ-PALOMINO; JORGE LINUESA-LANGREO (University of Castilla-La Mancha); Vicente Peñarroja (Universitat Oberta de Catalunya)
In customer-facing industries such as hospitality, frontline employees often experience customer mistreatment, which can undermine their commitment to service rules and service-oriented task performance. Drawing on Affective Events Theory, Conservation of Resources Theory, and the Job Demands– Resources model, this study examines the effect of customer mistreatment on service-oriented task performance, the mediating role of service rule commitment, and the moderating role of servant leadership. Using PLS-SEM on data from 242 hotel employees in the Canary Islands (Spain), results show that customer mistreatment negatively affects service-oriented task performance through reduced commitment to service rules. Servant leadership attenuates the negative effect of customer mistreatment on service rule commitment and neutralizes its indirect impact on service-oriented task performance. These findings contribute to the service management and leadership literature by clarifying the emotional and resource-based mechanisms underlying service performance and by offering actionable insights for hotel managers seeking to sustain employee engagement.
