Entrepreneurial Ecosystems and the Prevalence of Regional Zombie Firms: Evidence from China

ENT
Xikai Yu; Dongling Wang (Shanghai University)
Zombie firms are a significant impediment to sustainable regional economic growth. While previous research recognizes that effective regional de-zombification requires synergies among multi-stakeholders, the field lacks a comprehensive analytical framework capturing the complex interdependencies involved. Addressing this gap, we extend the linear inquiry predominant in extant work to a more comprehensive configurational analysis to explore the link between entrepreneurial ecosystems (EEs) and the low prevalence of regional zombie firms, which enhances insights into the systemic and complex process of regional de-zombification. The main findings are as follows: First, no single EE element is a necessary condition for achieving a low prevalence of regional zombie firms. Second, four distinct paths exist through which EEs contribute to a low prevalence of regional zombie firms, namely, the competition–incentive synergy, competition–signaling–spillover synergy, competition-dominated, and incentive-dominated paths. Our findings provide compelling insights for decision-makers seeking to drive regional governance through optimizing EEs.
