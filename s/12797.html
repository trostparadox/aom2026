Session overview

Longitudinal research designs are often viewed primarily as methodological refinements—tools that yield more robust estimates, reduce common method bias, and strengthen causal inference. While these advantages are well recognized, this view understates the deeper theoretical value of longitudinal inquiry. Many organizational phenomena are inherently temporal, involving dynamic patterns of change, mutually influential processes, and mechanisms that are best understood as unfolding over time rather than at a single point. These dynamics cannot be meaningfully theorized—let alone tested—using cross-sectional, time-lagged, or single-shot experimental designs. This symposium advances the argument that longitudinal designs are not merely methodologically superior, but theoretically indispensable. Although the five papers address different topics, they converge in showing how longitudinal approaches enable scholars to ask unique research questions—questions that remain inaccessible under traditional designs.
