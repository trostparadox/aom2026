Entrepreneurial Agility and Sustainable Competitive Advantage: Effectuation Theory Perspective

ENT
Tasneem Fatima (Brock University); SADAF MANZOOR (International Islamic University); Tazeem Ali Shah
ABSTRACT Entrepreneurial agility is widely recognized as a critical capability for ventures operating in dynamic and uncertain environments; however, scholarly understanding of the underlying processes through which it influences venture outcomes remains limited. Drawing on effectuation theory, this study examines the effectuation-based pathway linking entrepreneurial agility to venture success and sustainable competitive advantage through opportunity identification and opportunity exploitation. The moderating roles of environmental uncertainty and social networks are also investigated. A four-wave, time-lagged research design was employed, involving data collected from 431 entrepreneurs and top management members of multi-venture enterprises. The findings provide strong empirical support for the proposed relationships, indicating that entrepreneurial agility enhances venture outcomes primarily by fostering a systematic, opportunity-driven approach shaped by environmental uncertainty and social networking. These results contribute to the development of an effectuation process-oriented understanding of entrepreneurial agility and offer practical insights for entrepreneurship research and managerial practice.
