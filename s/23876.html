The Impact of the Dark Triad on Employees’ Pro-social Rule Breaking: A Moderated Mediation Model

OB
Tiansheng Xia (Guangdong University of Technology); Aopeng Qiu (Guangdong University of Technology)
Drawing on impression management theory, this study examines how employees’ dark triad personality traits influence pro-social rule breaking (PSRB) and explores the underlying motivational mechanisms and contextual boundary conditions. We propose a moderated mediation model in which assertive and defensive impression management motivations mediate the relationship between the dark triad and PSRB, while error management climate (EMC) moderates these motivational pathways. Survey data from 334 employees across multiple industries in China were analyzed using structural equation modeling and bootstrapping. Results show that the dark triad is positively related to PSRB, with this effect mediated by assertive impression management motivation but not by defensive impression management motivation. Moreover, EMC strengthens the positive effect of the dark triad on assertive impression management motivation, thereby enhancing its indirect effect on PSRB, whereas no moderating effect is found for the defensive pathway. This study contributes to the literature on dark personality traits and pro-social rule breaking by identifying impression management motivation as a key explanatory mechanism and highlighting error management climate as an important contextual boundary condition. Keywords: Dark Triad, Error Management Climate, Assertive Impression Management Motivation, Defensive Impression Management Motivation, Pro-social Rule Breaking.
