Governing Stakeholder Forms: Pathways of Hybrid Value Conversion in Social Enterprises

SIM
Li-Hsiang Yi (National Cheng Kung University); Akihiro INOUE (Keio University)
While prior research has examined social enterprises as hybrid organizations balancing social and commercial goals, less is known about how they govern heterogeneous stakeholders at the business–social interface to enable hybrid value conversion. Existing studies often focus on isolated governance mechanisms and distinctive stakeholder forms in the commercial field, offering limited insight into how governance is configured in the hybrid field. Drawing on qualitative data from sixteen social enterprises in Taiwan, this study investigates how social enterprises activate and combine governance mechanisms to manage heterogeneous stakeholders and achieve hybrid value conversion. Through comparative case analysis, we extended the discussion of three types of stakeholder governance—instrumental, institutional-related, and normative governance—each representing a distinct configuration of participation, authority, legitimacy-building, and value distribution. Our findings show that these governance forms shape different pathways through which social value is recognized, legitimized, and converted into economic support, extending social enterprise research by incorporating a stakeholder governance lens.
