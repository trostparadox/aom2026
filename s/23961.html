Back in Charge, Steering Towards Recovery: The Role of Boomerang CEOs in Declining Firms

STR
Zhaoxing Hao; Yang Ye (Southwestern University of Finance and Economics)
Facing organizational decline, firms often replace their CEOs, yet the optimal leadership source for recovery remains unclear. This study focuses on boomerang CEOs—rehired former leaders—who, having experienced external changes, possess updated cognitive frameworks while retaining firm-specific knowledge, potentially making them effective agents of turnaround. We propose that, because organizational decline is frequently driven by technological path dependence, boomerang CEOs can promote post-decline performance by implementing technological resource divestiture to break free from these lock-ins. Furthermore, we examine how independent directors’ human and social capital moderate this mechanism. Using data from declining Chinese listed firms from 2007 to 2020, we find that boomerang CEOs effectively enhance post-decline performance through technological resource divestiture. The effect of boomerang CEOs on technological resource divestiture is strengthened by the presence of industry-expert independent directors and weakened when independent directors are busy. Our findings highlight the critical role of boomerang CEOs in reconfiguring technological resources, and reveal the nuanced but important role of independent directors’ capital. This study offers valuable implications for declining firms regarding CEO selection, board composition, and strategic resource management.
