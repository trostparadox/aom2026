From Creation to Curation: AI in the Visual Arts

OB
Sem Hurkmans (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Hille C. Bruns (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Stefan Heusinkveld (Radboud University Nijmegen)
As generative AI tools become increasingly integrated into creative practice, they reshape how creative work is produced as well as how it is experienced. This study examines how creatives use AI in their work and what this means for creative work itself. Based on 33 semi-structured interviews with creatives from various disciplines, such as graphic design, illustration, photography and game design, this study explores the use of AI at different stages of the creative process. The findings reveal a broad spectrum of AI use, ranging from light, supportive use to heavy, end-to-end integration. Although AI enables faster experimentation and production, it also shifts the creative effort from making work to generating and selecting AI output. This shift is closely linked to a perceived transition from creator to curator and has implications for craftsmanship, authorship and creative fulfilment. Light users tend to remain focused on the practical process and physical engagement, while heavy users focus more on results and direction. Together, these findings show that AI is not experienced as a neutral tool, but as a dynamic presence that reconfigures creative work and its meaning.

From Play to Performance: Creating with AI in Personal Life as a Source of Work Creativity

OB
MANQING XIN (Tsinghua University); Jie Zhong (Beijing University of Chemical Technology)
Generative AI is rapidly becoming a creative companion—not only at work, but also after hours. We examine whether employees’ personal-life AI-assisted creating spills over into the workplace and, if so, how and when it boosts creativity. Integrating work–life enrichment theory with self-determination theory, we propose a motivational enrichment pathway: creating with AI outside work can generate intrinsic motivational resources that carry into work. We further distinguish domain-related versus domain-unrelated personal-life AI-assisted creating and theorize that they energize different targets of intrinsic motivation —intrinsic motivation for work and intrinsic motivation for creating—thereby shaping creativity at work. We test these ideas using a multi-wave field study across two companies and a complementary online experiment. Results converge on a clear insight: personal-life AI-assisted creating predicts higher workplace creativity through differentiated motivational routes, yet these benefits are contingent on task usefulness requirement. By shifting attention from task-level AI use to cross-role motivational spillover, this research expands the “AI in management” agenda, refines intrinsic motivation theorizing in AI-supported contexts, and reveals an indirect route through which generative AI can fuel workplace creativity.

When Coworkers Use Gen-AI: A Dual-Pathway Model of Employee Responses to Coworker Creativity

OB
Yaqi Huang (Nanjing University); Weihao Ye; Chunyan Jiang (Nanjing University)
Generative artificial intelligence (Gen-AI) is transforming how employees produce and evaluate creative work. However, little is known about how employees react when their coworkers use Gen-AI to demonstrate creativity. Drawing on appraisal theory of emotion and territoriality theory, this study provides a novel extension to the creativity and Gen-AI use literature by developing a dual-path model of employees’ emotional and behavioral reactions to their coworkers’ creativity. Using multi-wave survey data from 373 knowledge workers, our results reveal that coworker creativity does not elicit employee malicious envy under low coworker Gen-AI usage, but high Gen-AI usage consistently reduces knowledge hiding and promotes knowledge seeking, either via activating benign envy or attenuating malicious envy. By incorporating coworker Gen-AI usage into interpersonal behavior research and conceptualizing knowledge actions as territorial responses, this study advances theory on workplace envy and offers new insights into how digital technologies reshape knowledge interaction patterns in modern organizations.

From Technology Empowerment to Human-Centric Activation: How Generative AI Usage Drives Bootlegging

OB
Bo Li (Nanjing University Business School); shuai wen Chang (Central South University)
The widespread application of Generative AI (GenAI) provides a crucial opportunity to alleviate the resource constraints facing bootlegging, yet the mechanism converting external technological advantages into internal innovation momentum remains unclear. Drawing upon Social Cognitive Theory and Self-Determination Theory, this study explores the dual activation paths of GenAI usage on bootlegging. Through an empirical analysis of 570 three-wave questionnaires, the results indicate that: (1) GenAI usage significantly promotes employee bootlegging; (2) There exists a dual chain mediation mechanism of “technology empowerment ? human-centric activation”, specifically, GenAI enhances creative self-efficacy by improving information richness (the cognitive path), and stimulates intrinsic motivation by granting work autonomy (the motivational path), thereby driving bootlegging; (3) AI Identity significantly and positively moderates the transformation efficiency from technology empowerment to human-centric activation. This study elucidates the internal mechanism by which GenAI breaks through the resource constraint dilemma of bootlegging, providing a new theoretical explanation for understanding human-AI collaborative innovation in the digital intelligence era.

When Trust Becomes a Crutch: The Biased Effects of Ethical AI on Employee Creativity

OB
Linpei Song (Nanjing University); Zhuang Ma (University of Huddersfield); Young Eun Kim (Sungkyunkwan University); Cuizhi Yi (Hunan Institute of Science and Technology); Chenhong Hu (Nanjing University); Gang Chen (Nanjing University)
Organizations champion ethical AI (EAI) to build trust, assuming fairness fosters creativity. Challenging this consensus, we draw on fairness heuristic theory to propose a fairness trap: high fairness triggers a heuristic that induces cognitive withdrawal, a state of strategic offloading rather than active scrutiny. We predict that this creates a critical tension: while fairness fosters the cognitive flexibility necessary for radical creativity, the triggered withdrawal inhibits incremental creativity by reducing the active scrutiny required for refinement. Study 1’s latent change score analysis (481 employees) showed procedural fairness predicts distributive fairness changes when outcomes are ambiguous, supporting substitutability. Study 2’s experiment (200 participants) confirmed ethical AI boosts distributive fairness via procedural fairness, mediating between cognitive flexibility and withdrawal. Study 3’s multi-wave survey (381 employees and 48 supervisors) validated that distributive fairness and flexibility mediate ethical AI’s positive effect on radical creativity. We contribute to the AI-human collaboration literature by demonstrating that excessive trust in fair systems risks engineering a workforce that is efficient but intellectually passive.
