Session overview

This symposium advances theoretical and empirical understanding of deep technology commercialization pathways, from academic labs to market impact, as a critical mechanism for addressing humanity's grand challenges including climate change, sustainable mobility, healthcare, and energy transition. Five cutting-edge papers employ diverse methodological approaches (longitudinal studies, field experiments, machine learning, and advanced econometrics) to examine key dimensions of deep-tech commercialization across multiple levels of analysis. The research explores: how deep tech ventures actively shape their time to market through temporal agency and strategic pacing decisions; the trade-offs between parallel versus sequential exploration strategies in asset-intensive contexts and their impact on venture performance; the evolution of technology-market matching from individual scientists' opportunity beliefs to spinoff commercialization strategies; how acquisition timing in sequential innovation systems affects technology development outcomes and can lead to systematic misallocation of developmental responsibility; and the development of a rigorous typology and scalable classification system to identify deep tech ventures in large-scale empirical research. Together, these contributions bridge insights from innovation management, entrepreneurship, and technology strategy to illuminate how scientific discoveries transition from lab to market, revealing critical mechanisms that determine whether breakthrough technologies successfully create economic value while generating societal impact. The presenter symposium offers both theoretical advancement and practical implications for academics, industry leaders, and policymakers committed to accelerating innovation that addresses broad societal needs.
