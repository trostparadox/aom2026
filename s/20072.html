Institutional Theory Through the Lens of Conflict-Enabled Social Entrepreneurship

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5

Authors: John Aiyede University of Louisville; Sergey Anokhin University of Louisville

Session overview

Armed conflict fundamentally disrupts formal institutions, generating institutional voids that undermine market coordination and state governance. While such voids are conventionally treated as constraints on entrepreneurship, this study advances a dynamic institutional perspective that conceptualizes conflict-induced voids as generative opportunity spaces for social entrepreneurship. Integrating institutional theory with the External Enabler Framework, we argue that armed conflict operates as a powerful exogenous shock that simultaneously disables conventional, value-capturing entrepreneurship and enables social value creation through mechanisms of substitution and legitimation. In conflict-affected contexts characterized by institutional ambiguity, fragmented authority, and acute social needs, social entrepreneurship emerges as a critical mode of governance, substituting for weakened state and market institutions while contributing to institutional emergence and resilience. We empirically test our theoretical model using a novel panel dataset of 103 countries spanning 2010–2023. Armed conflict intensity is measured using data from the Uppsala Conflict Data Program and ACLED, institutional voids are proxied by indicators from the World Governance Indicators and the Heritage Foundation’s Index of Economic Freedom, and social value creation is captured using the World Giving Index. Employing fixed- and random-effects models with robustness checks, we demonstrate that armed conflict is associated with deeper institutional voids and higher levels of social value creation at the country level. Overall, this study reconceptualizes institutional voids as dynamic outcomes of conflict and positions social entrepreneurship as a key mechanism through which institutional order and social value are reconstructed under conditions of violence.
