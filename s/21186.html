Prestige Over Fairness? Fit-Based Rejection Framing and Reapplication Intentions

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Thomas P. DePatie Deloitte Consulting

Session overview

Organizations frequently justify hiring rejections using “fit” language, yet fit can be framed as a mismatch with job requirements (person–job; PJ) or a mismatch with organizational culture and values (person–organization; PO). Integrating applicant-reactions theory with signaling and identity perspectives, we examine how these fit frames combine with organizational prestige to shape a consequential post-rejection outcome: reapplication intentions. We argue that PJ-fit rejection is more likely to be interpreted as job-relevant and actionable (supporting a “path back”), whereas PO-fit rejection is more identity-relevant and potentially discouraging because it implies non-belonging. At the same time, prestige signals high instrumental and symbolic payoff and can confer perceived legitimacy on organizational judgments, sustaining persistence even when feedback is subjective. Using an experimental vignette design with working adults, we find that applicants are generally more willing to reapply following PJ- than PO-fit rejection and more willing to reapply to high- than low-prestige organizations. Critically, prestige sustains reapplication intentions across both fit frames, including within PO-fit rejection. This suggests that candidates may continue pursuing high-status employers even when rejection feedback is ambiguous or identity-threatening. The study contributes to applicant-reactions research by (a) centering reapplication intentions as a pipeline-retention outcome in rejection contexts, and (b) identifying organizational prestige as a boundary condition that helps explain why some applicants persist despite “fit”-based rejection. Implications for theory elaboration and rejection messaging aimed at retaining high-quality candidates are discussed.
