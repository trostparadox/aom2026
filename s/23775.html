The Resource Curse and Entrepreneurship: Evidence from an Oil Field Discovery in Brazil

ENT
Joao Fernando Rossi Mazzoni (Baylor University); Daniel L. Bennett (University of Louisville); Peter G. Klein (Baylor University)
This study examines how large natural resource discoveries function as external enablers of entrepreneurship, focusing on the discovery of the pre-salt oil fields in Brazil. Drawing on the External Enablers framework and the resource curse literature, it analyzes how such discoveries shape new firm creation and labor allocation over time. Using a difference-in-differences and event-study design with municipality-level panel data, the study shows that entrepreneurial entry responds with a lag, increases temporarily during the early phase of shock activation, and dissipates thereafter. Instead of generating sustained entrepreneurial growth, the discovery creates a narrow window of entry consistent with demand-driven activity and constrained access to sector-specific opportunities. These results highlight the temporally asymmetric effects of resource discoveries and underscore the role of institutional context in mediating entrepreneurial responses. The study contributes to entrepreneurship theory by integrating resource curse dynamics into the External Enablers framework and by conceptualizing external enablers as time-bound processes rather than persistent shifts in the opportunity set.
