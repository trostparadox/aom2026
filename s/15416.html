How Coordination Breakdowns Shape Capability Development in Temporary Organizing

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Author: Daniel Massie

Session overview

How do organizations develop coordination capacity when roles, relationships, and work processes cannot be clearly defined in advance? Through a 38- month ethnographic study of an urban waterfront development project, I examine how project actors collectively navigate coordination breakdowns in temporary, boundary-spanning organizing. The analysis reveals two coping dimensions (problem space definition and engagement modality) producing four distinct modes. Problem space expansion distinguishes capability-building from capability-eroding responses. This research contributes by (1) specifying micro-foundations of dynamic capability development through practice-level responses to disruption, and (2) revealing how emergent, person-centered responses complement designed coordination mechanisms in temporary organizing. The findings show coordination capability develops not only through role-based design but through accumulated patterns of navigating inevitable breakdowns.
