Session overview

Many academics enter university life with a strong sense of vocation—to teach, to develop students, to pursue truth, and to contribute to the common good. Yet the pressures of contemporary higher education, including performance metrics, rankings, managerialism, and publication expectations, can gradually distort intentions and form the character of professors in unintended directions. This Professional Development Workshop creates space for participants to reflect on who they are becoming through their work, and how institutional structures, daily choices, and internal motivations influence their calling and virtue formation. Grounded in virtue ethics and drawing inspiration from classical and contemporary ideas about the university as a community ordered toward truth, the workshop invites academics to examine the formation, erosion, and cultivation of professorial virtues. Through guided reflection, journaling, small-group dialogue, and shared learning, participants identify where their inner calling aligns or conflicts with their lived experience and choose a virtue-oriented practice to develop. The session is designed for faculty and doctoral students across career stages and aims to foster sustained reflection, peer support, and renewed intention toward flourishing as professors. Attendance is limited to 30 participants to maintain an interactive format. Participants are encouraged to arrive 5–10 minutes early.
