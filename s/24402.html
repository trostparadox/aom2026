Mental Health and Well-Being in the Digital Health Care Workplace: A Multilevel Systematic Review

HCM
Samuel Gyamerah (RMIT University); Leila Afshari (RMIT University); Dennis Asante (James Cook University); Jocob Leonard Ago (RMIT University)
Digital workplace transformation (DWT) is fundamentally reshaping healthcare work, yet its implications for employee mental health and well-being remain fragmented across disciplines. This systematic review synthesizes 35 empirical studies to examine how digitally mediated changes in work design, leadership, and individual capacities shape healthcare employees’ well-being and mental health. Adopting a multilevel perspective, the review identifies organizational-level antecedents (fragmented systems, participatory design, leadership support, digital autonomy) and individual-level antecedents (digital self-efficacy, resilience, emotional regulation) that jointly influence outcomes such as technostress, burnout, professional isolation, autonomy, work–life balance, and professional purpose. The findings reveal DWT as a double-edged process: digital technologies can enhance efficiency, flexibility, and patient care, yet also generate cognitive overload, emotional strain, and identity threats when poorly governed. The study advances understanding of how digitalization reorganizes the psychological conditions of healthcare work and outlines a future research agenda for sustainable digital workplaces.
