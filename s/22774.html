Employees’ Friend or Foe: Despotic Leaders, AI Usage, and Team Outcomes

SIM
Mariam Ayub (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Zhe Zhang; Raza Ali Tunio; Shehriyar Memon (Sichuan University)
Research on despotic leadership and its influence on employees’ creative behavior within service settings remains nascent. Drawing on the componential theory of creativity, we developed and tested a team-level model within service settings to examine the direct and indirect effects of despotic leadership on team creativity. We further hypothesized that AI usage and leader gender- coded as a dichotomous variable moderate the relationship between team creative self-efficacy and team creativity. Employing a multi-wave, multi-source quantitative research design, we collected data from banks, a key service sector in Pakistan. Team-level structural equation modeling was conducted using Smart PLS 4.0. Cumulatively, we find that despotic leadership depletes team creativity via team creative self-efficacy, and this indirect effect is attenuated when teams report high AI usage. Additionally, when the team despotic leader is female, the negative relationship between CSE and TC is stronger (the red line is steeper downwards). This suggests that for female in this role, higher CSE leads to a more pronounced decrease in creativity. Furthermore, when the team’s despotic leader is male, the negative relationship between CSE and TC is still present, but it is less steep (the green line is flatter than the red line). This indicates that the decrease in creativity as CSE increases is not as strong when the leader is male compared to when the leader is female. Our results offer a nuanced understanding of how despotic leadership shapes team creative behavior in service settings. We contribute to the service-creativity literature by integrating tyrannical leadership with the influence of AI usage and leader gender, and by outlining specific research and practical implications.

From Accuracy to Accountability: Identity Struggles in Responsible AI Design and Implementation

SIM
Sushil S. Chaurasia (University of New Brunswick); Dhirendra Shukla (University of New Brunswick); Jennifer Russell (University of New Brunswick)
Interacting with value chain partners around artificial intelligence (AI) offerings creates complex identity demands for employees engaged in boundary-spanning roles. Drawing on identity theory, this research examines how AI ethics–performance tension and AI ethics–performance synergy shape cooperative performance in interorganizational relationships. Study 1 uses survey data from 321 developers in technology companies who identified with both ethically oriented and performance-driven AI offerings they provided to channel partners. To strengthen causal inference, two experimental studies complement the field investigation. The results demonstrate that AI ethics–performance tension and synergy exert independent effects on cooperative performance. Further analyses reveal that self-efficacy, but not perspective-taking, mediates these relationships. High self-efficacy attenuates the negative impact of AI ethics–performance tension, whereas low self-efficacy diminishes the positive effect of AI ethics–performance synergy. By highlighting the role of employee identity integration and self-efficacy, this research advance identity theory and contributes to emerging work on responsible and trustworthy AI in interorganizational contexts.

Digitalization and Corporate Governance Effectiveness: A Generative AI–Based Approach

SIM
Yuanyang Song (East China University of Science and Technology); Hua Zhang (China Europe International Business School); Yu Zhang (China Europe International Business School)
Digitalization has emerged as a transformative force in the corporate world, profoundly influencing firm behavior and performance. While its impact on operational efficiency and organizational outcomes is well-documented, limited research examines its role in corporate governance effectiveness. This study explores how digitalization enhances corporate governance effectiveness by increasing transparency and efficiency in business processes, thereby enabling improved monitoring and checks by stakeholders. We hypothesize that digitalization has a stronger impact in firms with higher information asymmetry, such as those with complex or opaque operations. Using data from Chinese publicly listed companies and leveraging the latest generative AI (GenAI)–based measurement approach, we find that digitalization positively improves corporate governance effectiveness, with stronger effects observed for firms with higher information asymmetry, such as those characterized by greater business complexity or opacity. Leveraging an exogenous shock in China's IT infrastructure development, we also establish causal evidence supporting our findings.

The Ethical Challenge of Embedding Artificial Benevolence in Human–AI Interaction (WITHDRAWN)

SIM
Guillaume Mercier (IESEG School of Management)
AI systems are engaging in increasingly significant interactions with humans and exert a growing ethical impact on organizational life. Yet the possibility of embedding values and virtues within such systems remains underexplored, particularly benevolence, a foundational virtue of human relationships. This conceptual paper, supported by empirical vignettes, develops the notion of artificial benevolence, understood as an AI system’s active orientation toward the good and flourishing of humans, operating as a functional analog of human benevolence. The enactment of artificial benevolence is fundamentally human-centric: it is oriented toward the good of human users and realized through human involvement in the cultivation of virtue, the introduction of human intentionality, and the shaping of human–AI relationships. At the organizational level, artificial benevolence may serve as a lever for trust and ethical culture through both formal and informal modes of benevolent engagement. In practice, artificial benevolence exhibits functional overperformance in terms of constancy, stable attention, disinterestedness, and non-judgmentality. What appears as an enhancement may simultaneously operate as a subtle form of moral displacement, affecting how humans cope with vulnerability, exercise autonomy, and ultimately flourish. The article concludes with implications for the genuine calibration and development of artificial benevolence in organizations.

Leveraging Generative AI for Good: Experimental Evidence on CSR Initiative Design and Evaluation

SIM
Zepeng Sun (South China University of Technology); Lin Zhang; Honghui Chen (Sun Yat-Sen University)
Designing CSR initiatives that jointly create social impact and economic value remains challenging. Although generative AI (GenAI) holds promise for improving CSR design and evaluation, evidence on its effectiveness remains limited. We address this gap through an experiment comparing human-only, human–AI collaboration, and AI-only design modes. Results show that AI-augmented (Collaboration and AI-only) modes significantly outperform the human-only condition in overall quality. Disaggregated analyses reveal a novelty–feasibility trade-off: AI-only designs are most novel, whereas human-only designs are most feasible. Importantly, AI-augmented designs achieve higher economic and social value and exhibit a more balanced configuration of these objectives. Mechanism analyses indicate that cognitive load reduction, perceived task meaning, and innovation self-efficacy mediate the positive effects of human–AI collaboration. Moreover, aggregated GenAI evaluations closely align with expert assessments, suggesting a scalable approach to CSR initiative evaluation. Overall, this study advances CSR scholarship’s emerging design orientation and enriches understanding of human-AI collaboration in ethically and socially oriented decision-making.
