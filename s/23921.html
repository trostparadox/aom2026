From Clarity to Confusion: Balancing Algorithm Transparency and User Trust in Generative AI

TIM
Gabriel Bliss (TU Dortmund University)
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) systems are increasingly used as decision-support tools, yet their probabilistic and opaque outputs raise concerns about how users form trustworthiness evaluations and calibrate reliance on AI advice. Algorithm transparency is commonly proposed as a remedy. However, recent research suggests a transparency paradox, indicating that transparency may foster trust up to a point, but backfire when it becomes too detailed. Drawing on signaling theory and cognitive load theory, we conduct a vignette experiment with US citizens (N=531) to examine how different levels of algorithm transparency (low, moderate, high) and task criticality (low vs. high) shape trustworthiness evaluations and trust calibration in GenAI-supported decision-making, and whether these effects depend on task criticality and AI literacy. Contrary to assumptions underlying the transparency paradox, perceived algorithm transparency shows a consistently positive relationship with both trustworthiness and trust calibration. We find no evidence of diminishing returns or transparency overload, nor do task criticality or AI literacy moderate these relationships. These findings challenge the view of transparency as a double-edged sword and suggest that for GenAI systems, transparency may function as a robust trust signal rather than a cognitive burden. By disentangling evaluative trustworthiness from calibrated reliance, this study advances a more nuanced understanding of transparency in human-AI interaction. It offers guidance for designing GenAI systems that support appropriate and responsible use.
