Session overview

Organizations today face escalating change complexity driven by uncertainty, interdependence across systems, competing institutional demands, and rapid social, technological, and political shifts. In response, leaders are often presented with prescriptive change models and “best practices” that promise control and efficiency yet frequently fail to produce change that endures. This symposium advances an alternative approach by grounding best practices for leading change in enduring organizational change scholarship rather than decontextualized managerial prescriptions. The symposium is anchored in a systematic qualitative content analysis of the Palgrave Handbook of Organizational Change Thinkers, conducted by Drs. Szabla and Hills. Drawing on contributions spanning over a century of organizational change thought, the analysis identifies recurring, theory-informed practices that cut across change traditions and historical periods. Rather than offering a universal model, the findings surface enduring patterns of action that attend to complexity, context, human systems, and ethical leadership. Building on these findings, the symposium convenes a panel of distinguished scholars and practitioner-scholars featured in the Handbook to respond to, extend, and critically engage the identified best practices. Panelists draw on their own research and practice to highlight points of convergence and tension across change traditions and to explore how enduring ideas can be enacted in contemporary organizational environments. Designed for scholars, doctoral students, educators, and practitioners, this symposium contributes to the Academy of Management’s ongoing commitment to rigor, relevance, and impact by linking theoretical lineage to pressing leadership challenges. Participants will gain integrative insights into what it means to lead change that endures in conditions of complexity and uncertainty.
