An Analytical Survey of Production Lines in History—Effectiveness vs Efficiency

MH
James M. Wilson (University of Glasgow)
Swift, even flow has been hypothesized to be an important theory in Operations Management. This argument has been supported with assessments of several historic factories’ operations. We reconsider these factories, broadening the number of cases considered and deepening analyses of their operations. The theory of swift, even flow is tested against historical facts. Our findings show that every major development in line-based production was driven by organizational needs to be responsive to changing demands, while maximizing the efficiency of internal production operations was never the primary motive. Although smooth, even flow might be optimal for internal operations these factories’ overall operations were not smooth and varied significantly between periods. Our investigation of historic, archetypal production lines reveals that they enabled their organizations to respond effectively to seasonal and irregularly occurring changing demands, under various resource and materials constraints.
