How Do CSiR and Corporate Purpose Affect Employee Commitment?

STR
Pauline Asmar (HEC Paris)
This paper studies employees’ reactions to corporate social irresponsibility (CSiR) in purpose-vocal firms and in particular employees’ commitment in irresponsible firms. Relying on the Social Identity Theory, I hypothesize that employees are less committed to irresponsible firms and to less purpose-vocal firms. However, employee commitment is weaker in irresponsible firms when they are more vocal about their corporate purpose statements. When firms are more vocal about their purpose, the discrepancy between these statements and irresponsible behavior is starker, which reduces employee commitment in these firms compared to less or non-purpose-vocal firms post evidence of CSiR. Using data from Violation Tracker (US), the 10K annual reports, and Compustat, I measure CSiR using financial penalties, employee commitment using employee retention measured as options’ ratio, and purpose voice using the textual analysis of firms’ descriptions. The main findings support Hypothesis 1, and additional analyses offer some conditional validity to Hypothesis 3. This paper contributes to the literature on social identity theory, corporate social irresponsibility, and corporate purpose.
