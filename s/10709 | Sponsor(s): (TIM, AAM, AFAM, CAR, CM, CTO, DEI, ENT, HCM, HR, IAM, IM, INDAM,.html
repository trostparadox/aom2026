Social Media for Research Impact: Developing Purposeful Strategies for Academic Openness

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Regency B

People

Organizers: Marcel Bogers Eindhoven University of Technology; Attabik Awan Tilburg University

Session overview

In an era where researchers face mounting pressure to demonstrate societal impact while navigating an overwhelming and often negative social media landscape, this PDW offers a constructive and reflective approach to using social media strategically. We introduce the Researcher's Social Media Compass —a practical framework with five interconnected dimensions: Purpose (your motivations), Presence (your digital identity), Platform (your tool choices), Personality (your authentic voice), and Practice (your sustainable routines) (Bogers & Young, 2026). Drawing on concepts of academic openness and boundary-spanning technologies, we move beyond narrow citation metrics to embrace a broader perspective on impact that includes policy influence, public engagement, and cross-sector collaboration. This 90-minute workshop combines brief plenary input with structured individual and peer-based exercises at roundtables, supported by live polling and a hands-on compass-building exercise. It is open to academics across career stages, disciplines, and institutional contexts, regardless of their level of social media experience. Participants will also explore AI-assisted drafting and revision for clarity and audience fit, with explicit guidance on maintaining scholarly voice, avoiding over-automation, and ensuring accuracy. Participants leave with (1) a completed Social Media Compass, (2) a personalized action plan, and (3) a curated post-PDW resource pack (including selected readings, templates, exemplar posts, and optional AI tools). In the end, our aim is to transform social media from an overwhelming obligation into a purposeful tool for making a difference, so that attendees will leave with actionable plans aligned with their specific goals, resources, and values.
