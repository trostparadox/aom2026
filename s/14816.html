When Firms Engage in Crowdsourcing? The Role of Invention Maturity

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 6
TIM

People

Authors: Zhixia CHEN NEOMA Business School; Michael Zaggl NEOMA Business School; Mehdi Bagherzadeh NEOMA Business School

Session overview

Abstract: Crowdsourcing has become a strategic tool for firms seeking innovative solutions. Although prior research has examined the importance of crowdsourcing, little is known about how firms’ engagement in crowdsourcing varies across different stages of invention maturity. This paper investigates how the maturity stage of an invention influences the proclivity of companies to utilize crowdsourcing. Building on crowdsourcing literature and signaling theory, we argue that firms strategically use crowdsourcing to send different signals to external stakeholders as innovation projects evolve over time. Using quantitative data from one NGO, we found a non-linear relationship between invention maturity and crowdsourcing engagement. Specially, firms at early and late stages of invention maturity are more likely to engage in crowdsourcing activities compared to firms at middle invention stage. Drawing on complementary interview data, we further show that crowdsourcing serves different signals across stages. At early stage, firms primarily use crowdsourcing to signal learning intentions and to attract user feedback. At later stage, crowdsourcing mainly serves marketing-related signals aimed at promoting solutions. In contrast, at the middle stage, crowdsourcing is primarily functions as a legitimacy signal, emphasizing environmental and societal value to external stakeholders.
