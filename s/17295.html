Session overview

Research on entrepreneurial experimentation has made great strides in recent years. By employing rigorous methodologies and drawing inspiration (in part) from popular concepts like the “lean startup,” research suggests that extensive experimentation by startups enhances many desirable entrepreneurial outcomes, like survival, revenues, scaling and valuation. The accumulating evidence has fostered a view of entrepreneurial experimentation as something akin to the scientific method, one that is guided by well-formed theories of value, careful delineation of causal mechanisms, and articulation of hypotheses that are tested in real world experiments. Ongoing research advances provide further glimpses into the potential of such an experimentation-based view of entrepreneurship. At the same time, they also raise questions about when experimentation may be excessive and a focus on execution may be warranted instead. Put differently, what are the boundary conditions to the performance benefits from entrepreneurial experimentation? Supportive empirical evidence for the value of entrepreneurial experimentation is also more strongly aligned with the theoretical tradition in entrepreneurship that views it as a process of discovery. By experimenting, startups (and founders) may search over an unknown and complex, but prespecified, value landscape to find ideal positions to occupy. However, alternative traditions in entrepreneurship research have theorized about the entrepreneurial process as one of creation or effectuation, which are arguably less aligned with this search-based view of experimentation. The exciting emerging research on entrepreneurial experimentation and its juxtaposition against potential empirical and theoretical limits raise important questions: What are the prospects for research on entrepreneurial experimentation? What novel areas remain to be explored in the application of the scientific method to advance entrepreneurship? Is experimentation always beneficial for entrepreneurs and are there limits to its value? Under what conditions might an experimental approach constrain or damage, rather than enable, entrepreneurial success? What are the advantages of current empirical methods being used to assess the value of experimentation and what are their limitations? What role does experimentation play within creation and effectuation-based theories of entrepreneurship, as opposed to a discovery-based one? This panel symposium, which brings together leading scholars within the field, seeks to explore these themes, share emerging insights from ongoing research, engage in constructive dialogue, and highlight promising directions for future research in this exciting domain.
