Are Gender-Diverse Boards More Risk-Averse? Evidence from the Capitalization of Operating Leases

STR
Tianhua Cao (Indiana University Kokomo)
This study investigates the relationship between board gender diversity and firm-level risk aversion, measured by financial leverage, in the context of a quasi-natural experiment created by Accounting Standards Update No. 2016-02 (ASU 2016-02). This regulatory change mandates the capitalization of operating leases, predictably increasing firms’ reported financial leverage. Our findings indicate that firms with greater female board representation exhibit lower leverage before ASU 2016-02’s announcement. However, this trend reverses after the update takes effect. This shift in leverage dynamics among gender-diverse boards is primarily driven by the recognition of capitalized operating leases on the balance sheet and a greater reliance on long-term debt financing post ASU 2016-02. Prior to the regulation, these firms rely more on operating leases; however, following its adoption, they significantly reduce their operating lease commitments. Further analysis reveals that firms with higher female board representation adjust to ASU 2016-02 by increasing short-term operating leases, reducing long-term operating leases, and expanding capital expenditures to acquire assets. These findings suggest that while firms with more female directors initially use operating leases to manage reported leverage, they ultimately maintain comparable or even higher leverage levels post-capitalization. Our results challenge the conventional notion that female directors inherently drive firms toward more risk-averse corporate decision-making. Instead, the findings suggest that gender-diverse boards employ adaptive financial strategies, adjusting dynamically to regulatory changes and financial reporting requirements.
