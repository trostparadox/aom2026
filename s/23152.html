How Artificial Intelligence Affects Labor Investment Efficiency?

CTO
Yixuan Fan (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Kun Su (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Jiahao Yan (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
This study examines how artificial intelligence affects corporate labor investment efficiency and demonstrates a positive association between artificial intelligence and labor investment efficiency. Artificial intelligence helps to enhance the information environment of decision-making and optimize the labor skill structure, thus contributing to labor investment efficiency. Furthermore, our additional results suggest that artificial intelligence reduces overinvestment (over-hiring and under-firing) and underinvestment (under-hiring) in labor. The positive impact of artificial intelligence on labor investment efficiency is more pronounced in firms with higher agency costs and environmental uncertainty, large-scale and labor-intensive firms. Further analysis reveals that artificial intelligence can reduce labor cost stickiness. Our findings have important implications for corporates to accelerate the development of artificial intelligence and improve labor resource allocation.

How Does Artificial Intelligence Application Enhance Enterprise Resilience? Evidence from China

CTO
Yuxin Hu (Nanjing University)
Focusing on Chinese A-share listed enterprises and based on the panel data from 2010 to 2024, this study systematically examines the impact of AI application on enterprise resilience, investigates the underlying mechanisms through which AI application influences resilience, explores the moderating role of industry-university-research collaboration in the relationship between AI application and enterprise resilience, and analyzes the heterogeneity of this effect across firm-, industry-, and region-level contexts. The empirical results show that AI application significantly enhances enterprise resilience. This effect operates through three primary mechanisms: optimizing resource allocation, alleviating information asymmetry, and strengthening firms’ innovation capability. Moreover, industry-university-research collaboration positively moderates the relationship between AI application and enterprise resilience. Heterogeneity analyses further show that the resilience-enhancing effect of AI application is stronger for large and non-state-owned firms and those with more extensive digital application scenarios, and is more pronounced in technology-intensive and high-tech industries as well as in regions with higher openness and more developed information infrastructure.

AI’s Dual Paths to Profitability: Can AI Enhance Both Productivity and Customer Satisfaction?

CTO
Yanjing Ren (University of South Florida); Udit Sharma (University of South Florida); Sunil Mithas (University of South Florida); Forrest Morgeson (Michigan State University)
The economic contribution of technology has been a central topic in enterprise IT research and marketing. While artificial intelligence (AI) has been extensively examined in relation to firm performance, such as profitability, productivity, its implications for the customer side remains comparatively underexplored. Considering AI’s capability to improve both operations and customer engagement, this study develops a dual-pathway model to assess AI’s influence on organizational profitability through two mechanisms: productivity gains and customer satisfaction. Our empirical analysis shows that AI capability has a positive and significant direct effect on customer satisfaction, especially among service firms, its indirect effect on profitability through customer satisfaction is negative. We do not find any statistically significant effect of AI capability on productivity. These findings offer a more nuanced understanding of the interplay among AI capability, customer satisfaction, productivity, and profitability. We offer managerial insights for both AI users and service providers.

Making Uncertainty Governable: Scaling Probabilistic AI in Regulated Organizations

CTO
Nico Baum (Technical University of Darmstadt); Alexander Benlian (Technical University of Darmstadt)
Scaling probabilistic AI from pilots to production requires organizations to make uncertainty governable, particularly in regulated settings where auditability and accountability expectations often presume deterministic control despite the non-deterministic outputs of large language models (LLMs). We analyze how regulated organizations establish such governability in high-stakes knowledge work. Drawing on a longitudinal case study in a leading European bank’s wealth and asset management division, we use sociotechnical systems theory to identify eight recurring misalignments between probabilistic model behavior and established organizational routines and control infrastructures. We theorize three interdependent mechanisms of epistemic realignment that make uncertain outputs governable at scale: (1) boundary-spanning hybrid structures integrating innovation and control, (2) cognitive recalibration of professional identity and judgment in human–AI work, and (3) proxy-validation and audit routines that translate probabilistic outputs into accountable, auditable, and compliant processes. We contribute a mechanism-based organizational explanation of how regulated firms make probabilistic AI uncertainty governable to scale beyond pilots, and we develop propositions that specify boundary conditions for organizing control, accountability, and expertise around probabilistic outputs in high-stakes domains.
