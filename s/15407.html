How Decision-Focused Learning is Applied to Financial Research

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Hanyong Cho; Frank Fabozzi Johns Hopkins University; Jang Ho Kim Korea University

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming finance through rapid technological innovation. Early applications in financial institutions primarily focused on language-oriented tasks such as translation, document summarization, and conversational interfaces, improving operational efficiency and access to information. More recently, however, AI has begun to play a direct role in financial decision-making, influencing portfolio construction, trading, risk management, and execution. In this context, decision-focused learning has emerged as a powerful end-to-end paradigm that trains predictive models directly for downstream decision quality rather than intermediate prediction accuracy. Departing from the traditional predict-then-optimize framework, decision-focused learning integrates prediction and optimization by defining learning objectives in terms of realized economic or risk-based outcomes. This study surveys the growing use of decision-focused learning in financial research, including applications in portfolio construction and risk management. We find that recent developments suggest decision-focused learning offers a promising framework for aligning machine learning with financial decision-making, while raising open questions regarding robustness, transparency, and economic interpretability.
