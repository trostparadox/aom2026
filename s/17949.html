Language Convergence Between Target and Acquirer CEOs in Acquisition Outcomes

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Author: Zhirong Fan Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University

Session overview

This study proposes that acquirer CEO - target CEO language convergence, a form of unconscious verbal mimicry, can provide insights into social interaction and influence acquisition outcomes. We argue and empirically verify that high acquirer CEO – target CEO language convergence reflects the target CEO’s strong attempts to ingratiate themselves with the acquirer CEO and model themselves to organizations that they perceive to be more legitimate or successful due to the certainty of M&A. Because ingratiation with the acquirer CEO can lead to the acquirer CEO’s positive evaluation of the target CEO, target CEOs who exhibit higher language convergence with the acquirer CEO are more likely to complete the deal, and target CEO is more likely to receive a higher premium and to stay after the acquisition. This main effect has been influenced by board power, as the board can align the interests of the acquirer CEO with shareholders, thereby exerting supervisory control over the acquirer CEO. Analysis of a sample of over 2700 North American deals from 2000 to 2024 supports our arguments.
