Session overview

Recent research has increasingly highlighted the expanding forms of employee vulnerability in contemporary workplaces, as employees confront disruptions that extend beyond traditional organizational boundaries. In particular, relational, societal, and environmental challenges have intensified employees’ exposure to uncertainty, risk, and strain in ways that are often insufficiently explained by established organizational theories. In response, this symposium brings together management scholars to advance a more comprehensive understanding of employee vulnerability across and beyond organizational boundaries. The five presentations examine diverse and emerging sources of vulnerability, including interpersonal mistreatment, environmental stressors, and situational transitions at work. The symposium first addresses interpersonal sources of vulnerability through work-related stalking and unwanted sexual advances toward Black men. It then turns to environmental sources of vulnerability by examining how heat exposure and poor air quality spillover into employees’ workplace experiences. The symposium concludes with a focus on vulnerability arising from liminality, highlighting how situationally induced vulnerability can generate both harm and adaptive coping. Collectively, these papers advance our understanding of employee vulnerability through rigorous theoretical development and empirical research, while offering important implications for organizations navigating an increasingly disrupted work environment.
