What Drains Authentic Leaders? Joint Effects of Bottom Line Mentality and Leader Conscientiousness.

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Jessica ZHANG The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Katrina Lin The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Lin Tang (Patrick) Sze The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

While existing research assumes authentic leadership preserves leaders’ psychological resources, we argue that it depends on contextual and dispositional factors. We investigate a boundary condition under which authentic leadership becomes a source of depletion rather than conservation. Drawing on ego-depletion and trait activation theories, we propose that top management’s bottom-line mentality and leader conscientiousness interact to moderate the authentic leadership–depletion relationship. For highly conscientious leaders, we hypothesize a reversal: authentic leadership reduces depletion under low bottom-line mentality but increases it under high bottom-line mentality. Two-wave survey data from 339 supervisors supported our three-way moderated mediation model, showing that ego depletion mediated the interactive effects on work engagement. By demonstrating that authentic leadership can psychologically burden leaders when their personal values conflict with top management’s financial priorities, particularly for those high in conscientiousness, our findings offer a more balanced, contingent perspective on the consequences of authenticity.
