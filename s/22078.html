Citation as Norm-Constitution: Legitimizing Methodological Mixing Without Justification

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
RM

People

Author: Issei Yamaguchi The University of Tokyo

Session overview

When qualitative researchers cite methodological works from epistemologically incompatible traditions without justification, what happens? We argue this creates a legitimation cascade: visible unreflective mixing in top journals signals to subsequent researchers that such practice is acceptable, progressively normalizing epistemological silence across the field. We test this through two-stage analysis: (1) bibliometric examination of 2,221 papers in FT50 journals (1990-2025) citing Eisenhardt (1989), Gioia et al. (2013), and/or Langley (1999), and (2) Methods section analysis of all 41 empirical papers citing all three works —extreme cases representing maximum methodological mixing intensity. Our bibliometric findings reveal mixing has risen six-fold from 5.4% to 30.5% over two decades, with tri-citation papers nearly tripling in just five years post-Gioia (12 to 34 papers). More critically, our qualitative analysis shows that among these most intensive mixers, only 10% state epistemological stance, only 5% justify combining traditions, and 88% cite Gioia exclusively for data structure technique—divorcing it from interpretivist foundations. We document a concrete consequence: in technique-only papers, first-order concepts use researcher-imposed abstractions rather than informant language, eliminating the co-constructed meaning that defines interpretivist research. Our contribution is identifying citation as norm-constitution: methodological citations do not merely reference prior work but actively constitute field-level norms about legitimate practice. The accelerating cascade threatens to hollow the epistemological foundations of qualitative management research.
