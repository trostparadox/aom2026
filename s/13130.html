Session overview

Geopolitical tensions have become a key source of operational uncertainty, prompting firms to reshape how they compete, innovate, and engage with stakeholders. This caucus convenes strategy, organizations, and innovation scholars to take stock of what is known, identify key gaps, and outline a research agenda on strategy and performance under geopolitical uncertainty. Description: Geopolitical tensions create a type of systemic uncertainty that cannot be fully hedged or diversified away. Entrepreneurs, inventors, and managers must navigate ambiguous and unstable access to technology, supply networks, corporate legitimacy, and inputs, including capital and knowledge. These forces affect not only multinational enterprises, but also domestic firms, scale-ups, and platform businesses embedded in cross-border suppliers, customers, and digital ecosystems. Yet strategy, organization, and innovation research still lacks shared constructs and cumulative evidence on when geopolitical disruptions become binding strategic constraints, rather than manageable market noise, and on which organizational responses generate resilience and competitive advantage. This caucus aims to bring together scholars from across research communities to: (1) synthesize current insights on how geopolitical tensions shape organizational behavior and market structure; (2) identify the key mechanisms and boundary conditions through which international fragmentation influences strategic and innovative activity across firms, entrepreneurs, and inventors; and (3) chart a research agenda that links geopolitical uncertainty to business decision-making, knowledge flows, and performance variation. Core topics will include: • Geo-tech competition and technology controls (e.g., export controls, investment screening, dual-use technologies). • Data sovereignty, digital borders, and cybersecurity. • Supply-chain redesign and operational resilience. • Industrial policy, subsidies, and the return of the state. • Non-market strategy and international animosity. • Sanctions, trade volatility, and compliance as strategy. • Political polarization, neo-nationalism, and stakeholder support. • Organizational design for geopolitical uncertainty (e.g., ring-fencing, modularization, governance). • Innovation and firm performance under fragmentation. Keywords: Geopolitical Uncertainty, Strategic Adaptation. Delivery Plan. The session will follow a true caucus format with no papers and no formal presentations. Instead, we will foster an open, facilitated, whole-room conversation around the core themes. The discussion will proceed in three rounds: (A) Phenomena, what firms and entrepreneurs are facing now, and how it varies by industry and location; (B) Mechanisms and Conditions, how and when geopolitics reshapes capabilities, governance, innovation processes, and nonmarket strategy; and (C) Research Designs, what data, measures, and identification strategies can move the field forward.
