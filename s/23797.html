Burnout Contagion Dual Pathway Model with Emotion Regulation and Cognitive Reappraisal Moderators

OB
Bichen Guan (La Trobe University); Denise Mary Jepsen (Macquarie University)
Burnout is not only an individual experience but can also spread socially through workplace interactions. This study proposes a dual-pathway model of workplace burnout contagion that integrates both emotional and cognitive mechanisms. Drawing on emotional contagion theory, appraisal theory, and the job demands–resources model, we examine how perceived colleagues’ burnout leads to employee emotional exhaustion via colleagues’ negative venting (the emotional pathway) and heightened workload perceptions (the cognitive pathway). We further investigate the moderating role of interpersonal emotion regulation strategies in the emotional pathway and cognitive reappraisal in the cognitive pathway. Two-wave survey data from 206 aged care employees support both pathways and show that using direct action to improve others’ situations significantly weakens the impact of colleagues’ venting and the indirect burnout contagion effect via the emotional pathway. In contrast, receptive listening neither amplified nor buffered these effects, and cognitive reappraisal did not mitigate burnout contagion via the cognitive pathway. Overall, our findings identify new mechanisms underlying workplace burnout contagion and highlight the importance of proactive interpersonal emotion regulation strategies for mitigating burnout in high-stress work environments.
