Session overview

Causal inference sits at the heart of management research, yet the field has been slower to adopt recent methodological innovations. This symposium bridges the gap between traditional econometric approaches and the contemporary causal inference frontier. The session opens with an introduction surveying key developments of causal AI in management research and industry. Four presentations follow: (1) how machine learning methods such as double/debiased machine learning and causal forests overcome limitations of conventional matching and regression approaches; (2) a pipeline combining causal diagnostics with LLM-assisted hypothesis generation; (3) the mechanics of sample selection bias and guidance on Heckman-style corrections; (4) treatment effect heterogeneity and why instrumental variable estimates may fail to recover average effects under essential heterogeneity. A discussant with editorial experience offers commentary, followed by discussion addressing what management research can learn from industry applications of causal AI.
