Reimagining the Human in the Age of AI: Transhumanist Assumptions and the Future of Consulting

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a central feature of contemporary organizing and an increasingly prominent domain of management consulting practice. Consultants play a critical role in advising organizations on AI strategy, in designing AI-enabled decision systems, and in legitimizing AI adoption among senior leaders and clients. In this context, AI is frequently framed as a tool for enhancing human cognition, improving judgment, and overcoming the limitations of human performance. Consulting discourse routinely invokes notions such as augmented intelligence, cognitive offloading, and bias-free decision-making, positioning AI not only as a technical solution but also as a moral and epistemic improvement over fallible human judgment (Brynjolfsson & McAfee, 2014; Raisch & Krakowski, 2021). While these narratives are often presented as pragmatic and client-centered, they carry implicit assumptions about what it means to be human at work—assumptions that remain undertheorized in management consulting research. Much existing scholarship on AI focuses on adoption, performance, or governance outcomes, often treating AI as a neutral technology rather than as a carrier of normative visions of expertise, agency, and responsibility (Faraj et al., 2018; Kellogg, Valentine, & Christin, 2020). This theoretical paper interrogates the transhumanist assumptions embedded in AI-related consulting discourse and examines how consultants shape organizational understandings of the human–AI relationship. Transhumanism is a philosophical and cultural orientation that treats human limitations as problems to be solved through technological enhancement and frames progress as technologically driven (Bostrom, 2005; Vita-More, 2016). Although consultants rarely invoke transhumanism explicitly, its assumptions increasingly permeate AI-related consulting narratives. Humans are often portrayed as biased, slow, or inconsistent, while AI systems are framed as objective, rational, and superior decision-makers. These framings subtly recast human judgment not as a source of professional value but as a liability to be mitigated, augmented, or delegated to algorithms. The core theoretical puzzle animating this paper is a tension between the promise of AI-driven human enhancement and the potential erosion of human agency, meaning, and responsibility in consulting and client organizations. While consultants legitimate AI through narratives of optimization and augmentation, these narratives may simultaneously reconfigure professional expertise, displace moral responsibility, and reshape what it means to exercise judgment in organizational life. By naming and interrogating these transhumanist assumptions, this paper shifts the analytic focus from what AI does for organizations to how consulting discourse shapes what kinds of humans—and consultants—organizations come to value.
