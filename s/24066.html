Unpacking the Effectiveness of Supply Chain Due Diligence Act: A Buyer-Supplier Dyadic Perspective

OSCM
Meitong Dong (University of Science and Technology of China); Di Fan (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Pengcheng Ma (Renmin University of China); Chengyong Xiao (University of Groningen)
The buyer-driven paradigm of sustainable supply chain management has long been criticized for its limited effectiveness in fostering systematic sustainability improvement of emerging-country suppliers. As a response, Germany enforced in 2023 Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (SCDDA), the first comprehensive piece of legislation that clearly specifies the legal responsibility of buying firms for their supply chain sustainability, particularly tier-one suppliers. Against this backdrop, we investigate how the SCDDA affects the sustainability and financial performance of both buyers and suppliers. Using a regression discontinuity design with the 3,000-employee threshold of the act and the dyadic data of German buyers and foreign suppliers, we find that SCDDA exposure improves the sustainability performance for both parties, increases buyers’ ROA, but reduces suppliers’ ROA. Three factors, supplier’s operational flexibility, supplier’s market share, and buyer-initiated training, significantly moderate these influences, although to varying extents. Our study provides one of the first systematic empirical evidence on the effectiveness of SCDDA. Moreover, it provides several nuanced insights into the buyer-supplier dynamics triggered by this act, particularly on the issue of cost-sharing/bearing for supplier sustainability improvement.
