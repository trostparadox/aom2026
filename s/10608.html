From Routine to Capability: Retention Capability Emergence in SMEs

Author: Yemi Oluseun Grenoble Ecole de Management

Session overview

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) frequently experience growth instability due to customer churn, despite sustained investment in acquisition. While retention is widely recognised as vital to long-term performance, it is often treated as an outcome or metric rather than an enacted organisational practice. This qualitative study examines customer retention as a capability that emerges through everyday managerial and operational routines. Drawing on organisational routines, capability-based perspectives, and the exploration–exploitation literature, the research explores how SMEs balance short-term growth pressures with longer-term investments in retention practices. It surfaces recurring mechanisms through which retention is operationalised in practice and investigates how routines are formed, enacted, and adapted under conditions of constraint. Empirical material will be generated through in-depth interviews with SME founders and senior operators, complemented by analysis of customer-facing processes and decision routines. By examining retention at the level of organisational practice, the study aims to clarify how retention capabilities develop unevenly across firms and why many SMEs struggle to translate strategic intent into sustained customer outcomes. The findings will inform broader theorising on how organisational capabilities emerge, stabilise, and erode through everyday routines.
