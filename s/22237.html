Paradoxes of Meaningful Work in Franchise Networks: A Conceptual Framework

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
OMT

People

Author: Daniel saldanha Guedes Fundacao Dom Cabral

Session overview

Franchise networks represent a hybrid organizational form combining contractual governance, distributed ownership, and brand-based control. Although research on meaningful work has grown across psychology, HRM, and organization studies, little attention has been paid to the subjective experience of work in hybrid entrepreneurial contexts such as franchising. We argue that franchisees occupy a structurally and relationally paradoxical position: they are independent investors and local entrepreneurs, yet also operators bound by brand standards and monitoring systems. Drawing from meaningful work (Lysova et al., 2019; Bailey et al., 2024; Blustein et al., 2023), franchising (Bradach, 1997; Combs et al., 2004; Nijmeijer et al., 2014), and paradox theory (Smith & Lewis, 2011; Raisch et al., 2018; Vahle et al., 2024), we propose a conceptual framework in which franchisees navigate paradoxes across three interdependent dimensions: structural (autonomy–control, standardization–customization), relational (belonging–isolation, trust–surveillance), and identity-based (entrepreneur–operator–brand agent). These paradoxes shape sensemaking processes that enable or hinder the experience of meaningful work. The framework contributes by: (1) extending meaningful work theory to hybrid organizational forms; (2) enriching franchising scholarship by incorporating identity, agency, and dignity; and (3) positioning paradoxes as generative mechanisms linking governance, identity, and work meaningfulness. We outline research avenues for multi-level and longitudinal inquiry and discuss implications for designing healthier franchise systems.
