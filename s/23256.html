CEO Communication Through a Cultural Lens: East Asian CEOs’ Promotion Focus and Investor Valuation

STR
Shi Tang (City University of Hong Kong); Lingling Pan (University of Pittsburgh); Gerry M. McNamara (Michigan State University); Jackson G. Lu (Massachusetts Institute of Technology)
Although the strategic leadership literature emphasizes CEO communication as central to shaping investor perceptions, it has largely assumed an ethnically neutral view. We challenge this implicit assumption by theorizing how ethnic cultural expectations shape CEO communication and how such communication influences investor valuation of the firm. We focus on East Asian CEOs of U.S.-listed firms, who face a communication dilemma: Using promotion-focused language risks eliciting backlash for violating East Asian cultural expectations that emphasize prudence and stability, whereas conforming to those expectations risks being perceived as insufficiently ambitious in U.S. corporate contexts. Analyzing earnings call transcripts from S&P 1500 firms, we find that East Asian CEOs use more promotion-focused language than White CEOs and CEOs of other ethnicities, and that investors respond more favorably when East Asian CEOs do so. Our findings advance CEO communication research by moving beyond an ethnically neutral view and extend strategic leadership research by highlighting how East Asian CEOs communicate in light of the divergence between U.S. corporate and East Asian cultural expectations.

Strategy in Pictures: The Influence of Visual Representations on Strategic Perception

STR
Joao Cotter Salvado (Católica Lisbon School of Business & Economics); Freek Vermeulen (London Business School)
This study examines how the design of strategy communication shapes the comprehension and evaluation of strategic actions at the interface between top management and financial audiences. We focus on acquisition presentations and conceptualize them as moments of strategic sensemaking at this interface and theorize visualizations as design tools that shape these sensemaking events. Using a multi-method, multilevel design, we show that strategy visualizations can enhance audience comprehension of value creation (Study 1: experiment) and translate into more certain analyst assessments and more positive immediate market reactions (Study 2: field data), particularly when presented as directional frameworks. These findings link micro-level cognition to macro-level valuation outcomes and explain how, in particular, the use of frameworks can enhance strategy communication.

Not a United Front: The Adverse Effect of Revealing Strategic Divergence on Analyst Firm Assessment

STR
Ningzi Li (University of Chicago); Livia Markoczy (University of Illinois at Chicago)
Drawing on expectancy violation theory and strategic consensus research, we predict that in the context of earnings calls, revealing divergence in the strategic priorities of senior executives violates analysts' expectations for strategic consensus. This violation, in turn, negatively influences analyst assessments of a firm, even after controlling for firm performance. We further predict that salient contextual cues can amplify or mitigate these negative assessments by influencing analysts’ attributions of the reasons for strategic divergence. When female executives are on the call alongside men, analysts are more likely to attribute strategic divergence to interpersonal tensions, thereby amplifying the negative effect of revealing strategic divergence on analyst assessments. Conversely, when executives primarily express negative sentiment, analysts tend to attribute strategic divergence to the transparent expression of executives’ actual beliefs. Analysts can interpret this as an effort to be authentic, rather than a signal of dysfunction within the executive team, which mitigates the negative effect of the revealed divergence on analyst assessment. Using computational text analysis of earnings call transcripts and subsequent analyst recommendations from 2011 to 2019, we find support for our predictions. Our findings highlight analysts' assessments as an alternative path to internal processes, by which strategic divergence influences firm outcomes.

The Market Can Be Surprised but Never Fooled Empirical Investigation of CEO Emotion Market Reaction

STR
Yi Tang (The University of Hong Kong); Zixuan Wang (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); Yuanhao Ciaran Li (Indiana University Bloomington)
The Market Can Be Surprised but Never Fooled: An Empirical Investigation of CEO Emotion and Market Reaction Abstract While Upper Echelons Theory has successfully mapped how CEO cognition, values, and personality shape firm outcomes, the role of executive emotion remains a comparatively unexplored frontier. Existing research is fundamentally limited in its ability to represent emotion itself, often conflating it with stable personality traits or relying on sanitized corporate disclosures that mask authentic affect. We break through these inherent limitations to examine the market consequences of authentic CEO emotion across conflicting temporal horizons. Leveraging Large Language Models on a comprehensive dataset of 27,651 tweets from S&P 500 CEOs, we analyze this phenomenon in a high-discretion environment. Our empirical analysis yields a striking dichotomy. In the short term, higher CEO sentiment drives positive cumulative abnormal returns, suggesting that investors are temporarily susceptible to emotional contagion. However, this relationship inverts over the long horizon. We find that excessive positivity is negatively associated with long-term firm value, and high emotional volatility further exerts a negative impact on valuation. These results suggest that while the market is susceptible to the initial sugar rush of high sentiment, it eventually penalizes unfounded optimism and instability that are not supported by firm fundamentals. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the information environment serves to amplify these effects, as both analyst coverage and market uncertainty strengthen investor sensitivity to executive emotion.

Scripted Optimism and Unscripted Truth: How Executives' Tone Decoupling Shapes Market Reactions

STR
Xisha Jin (Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz); Endrit Ademi (Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz); Andranik Tumasjan (Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz)
We develop and test theory on executive impression management in interactive disclosure settings. Integrating impression management theory with expectancy violations theory, we examine how executives’ ability to sustain a consistent narrative across both scripted and unscripted communication shapes investor reactions. Focusing on earnings conference calls, we introduce the concept of executive tone decoupling, i.e., the extent to which the tone of prepared presentations is more positive than the tone of executives’ spontaneous responses to subsequent analyst questions. We argue that tone decoupling constitutes ineffective impression management and triggers negative market reactions. We further theorize disclosure timing as a boundary condition of the tone decoupling effect. Specifically, when earnings conference calls are delayed relative to earnings releases, investors have more time to process relevant information (e.g., analyst commentary) prior to the interaction. This additional processing time reduces the perceived severity of tonal inconsistencies, thereby attenuating the negative market impact of tone decoupling. Based on a comprehensive sample of earnings conference calls by S&P 500 firms from 2010 to 2025, our results provide support for our predictions across multiple robustness tests and alternative tone measures. Our findings contribute to impression management theory by shifting attention from one-way messaging to narrative consistency and identifying disclosure timing as a boundary condition of impression-management effectiveness. Finally, we offer a novel machine learning-based measure of executive tone decoupling that filters out expected tonal variation in interactive settings to capture executive impression management under scrutiny.
