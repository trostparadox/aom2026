Developing a Dynamic Process Model of Organizational Silence in Project-Based Environments

Authors: Leonora Nana Adwoa Amponsah-Asante University of Southampton; Ian Graham John Dawson University of Southampton Business School; Melanie Ashleigh University of Southampton

Session overview

Organizational silence is the deliberate withholding of ideas, opinions, or feedback by employees. It poses a critical threat to learning, innovation, and performance within project-based organizations (PBOs). Whilst the presence of organizational silence is widely acknowledged, the underlying dynamics that shape how silence is created, transmitted, and sustained across project environments remain inadequately understood. This study explored the multidimensional nature of silence in PBOs utilising a qualitative inductive research approach. Thirty-five semi-structured interviews were conducted with project professionals across seven infrastructure firms operating under project-delivery models. Thematic analysis identified the antecedents of three dimensions: (i) Silence Creation, rooted in authoritarian culture, oppressive behaviors, communication barriers, unethical organizational culture, and sociocultural contexts (ii) Silence Transmission, facilitated by institutional constraints, and assimilation behaviors (iii) Silence Sustenance, maintained via prevailing cultural climate, external influence, and institutional and structural barriers. The results of this study challenge traditional views that frame silence solely as a symptom of fear, revealing its active reproduction through organizational routines and adaptive behaviors. Silence was found to be the combined influence of organizational forces, societal norms, and national culture, and not purely a function of organizational behaviors. These results offer new insights for scholars of organizational behavior and project management who should consider silence as a dynamic social process, rather than a fixed outcome. A framework is proposed that advances theory on workplace silence and provides a diagnostic lens for examining communication patterns in project settings.
