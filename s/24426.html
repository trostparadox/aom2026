Engagement with Poverty in Organizational Studies: A Detached Scholarship

CMS
PAOLA RODRIGUES DE MATOS (Grenoble Ecole de Management); Anne Antoni
This article examines how research in Organizational Studies engages with the issue of poverty. Using a Systematic Literature Review, we analyzed the last ten years published articles to understand the discursive and analytical positioning of poverty within the field. Our findings reveal a body of research that appears as attached to poverty, giving voice to poor people to expose their living conditions and how they struggle with power relations due to limit access to material and immaterial important aspects. However, we also found a prevailing detachment from research, where poverty is often treated as a background for research, a profitable opportunity for organizations, or where poor people act as a supporting role, with their voices absent on the studies. We identified a distance from Organizational Studies to the topic, which raises concerns about how the field engages with the issue of poverty. We argue for a reorientation of organizational research that centers the experiences of those living in poverty, accounting for the aspects that hinders a decent social existence.
