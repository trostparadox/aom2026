Session overview

While occupational well-being and health are becoming central issues for organizations and HRM, more and more people experience anxiety, fatigue, stress, and burnout at work. Bringing alternative quality-of-working-life policies is necessary but not sufficient; it calls for the imagining and design of discussion spaces and relationships grounded in the lived, corporeal, cognitive, and affective experiences of well-being at work. This Professional Development Workshop explores the role of embodied conversations, about crucial needs, resources, and constraints through a playful card game, called SLAC The Game, designed by two French researchers, based on a theoretical framework SLAC: Sensemaking, Link, Activity, Comfort (Abord de Chatillon & Richard, 2015; Corbett-Etchevers et al., 2019). We engage participants in a playful discussion space about work well-being with 3 main objectives: First, discovering the SLAC model and its different dimensions; second, understanding the diversity of individual perspectives on occupational well-being; third, identifying the main constraints affecting the work team or organization, and the main resources to be mobilized. The expected outcomes for participants in this PDW are: 1. to discover and/or share active pedagogy involving the SLAC card game for an occupational well-being and health intervention in an organization or for a specific team. 2. to reflect on the potential of such a pedagogical game and the associated risks. 3. to reflect on implications of gamified methods for consultants, the relationship with leaders, and/or within teamwork, and potentialities, risks, and limits.
