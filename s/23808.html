Are You Selfish Enough to Share Your Knowledge? A Mediated-Moderated Experiment on Contagiousness

OB
Carla Curado; Mírian Oliveira; Tiago Rodrigues Gonçalves (ISEG Lisbon School of Economics and Management, University of Lisbon)
Knowledge sharing (KS) is an inherently prosocial organizational phenomenon, yet its diffusion within organizations remains a managerial “black box” at the individual level. Exploring individual conditions, this study examines KS as a socially contagious process, focusing on employees’ willingness to replicate knowledge-sharing behaviors in organizational contexts. Drawing from the Theory of Reasoned Action and the Theory of Interpersonal Behavior, we develop an experimental research design to investigate how interpersonal and contextual factors shape KS intentions. Using an experimental vignette methodology embedded in an online survey, we simulate KS scenarios under varying organizational contexts. Data was collected from two samples (experimental and control) comprising professionals employed in service-oriented SMEs in Portugal. The study manipulates two organizational conditions: the provision of rewards for KS and the attainment of promotion as a consequence of KS. Structural equation modelling is employed to test a mediated-moderated model. The results indicate that willingness to replicate KS mediates the effects of trust and reciprocity on KS intention, while both organizational rewards and promotion opportunities exert significant moderating effects. Our findings suggest that KS follows a contagion logic within organizations, driven by interpersonal dynamics and reinforced by contextual incentives, with practical implications providing blueprints to facilitate KS as a contagion effect via organizational leverages.
