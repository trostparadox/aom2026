Session overview

This professional development workshop addresses accent bias, which is a universal phenomenon where people make assumptions about others based on the way they speak, which are rooted in social stereotypes. In the workplace, these sentiments can have major implications for organizational outcomes, including hiring, where standard-accented applicants have historically received more positive employment interview evaluations than their non-standard-accented counterparts, even when formal selection procedures are in place. Consequently, this workshop was developed to help organizations proactively address this unique form of discrimination and to reframe the conversation around accents to a more positive one. Attendees will develop a deeper understanding of interdisciplinary research from both Social (language attitudes) and Industrial-Organizational Psychology (interview discrimination) on the prevalence of accent bias in employment interview evaluations, including the conditions that affect the extent to which that bias will occur. Through lecture, active exercises, and group discussions, participants will actively engage with research findings and identify evidence-based practices that their organizations can use to minimize accent bias during talent assessment and evaluations, and augment their talent pool. Furthermore, attendees will leave the session having had the opportunity to critically consider points in the selection process where accent bias might occur and recognize the value of accent diversity in their respective organizations. As such, this proposed accent bias workshop is a rare example of one that has strong empirical foundations paired with practical components that can improve talent assessment and evaluation processes. Prior iterations of this workshop have been presented to career development professionals at the City University of New York and to members of the International Personnel Assessment Council.
