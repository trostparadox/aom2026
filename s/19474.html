Logic of Interaction: Exploring the Microfoundations of Corporate Entrepreneurship in Family Firms

Author: Sumit Kumar Jaiswal Ahmedabad University

Session overview

Corporate entrepreneurship (CE) is a fundamental driver of firm survival, profitability, and strategic growth. Family firms provide a distinctive landscape for exploring these processes, as they operate at the intersection of two divergent institutional logics: the family logic of nurturance and stability, and the market logic of entrepreneurial growth and efficiency. These logics impose conflicting demands on the dominant coalition, fundamentally shaping organizational choices and strategic outcomes. We argue that capturing the essence of these processes requires a move toward the microfoundations of the firm, specifically the interactional dynamics between family and non-family members. Despite their criticality, the role of these individual-level interactions in driving CE remains under-theorized. Integrating institutional logics with a microfoundational lens, we propose a conceptual framework and a typology in the form of a 2x2 matrix. This model elucidates how the interplay of organizational interactions facilitates or constrains a family firm's entrepreneurial trajectory, offering a nuanced understanding of the path-dependent nature of strategic renewal.
