Spiritual Pedagogies For Wise Management Education: Lessons From Constellations And Leadership Workshops

MSR
Catherine Voynnet Fourboul
Management education is increasingly criticized for producing technically skilled but existentially unprepared graduates, unable to navigate the moral complexity and human depth of organizations. In response, this paper explores how spiritually inspired pedagogies can support wisdom and purpose development in a Master-level HR program. Drawing on ten years of teaching experience in a French business school, the study examines two experiential devices: (1) systemic constellations used to analyze complex organizational cases, and (2) a leadership workshop combining personality tools (MBTI-type questionnaire, Belbin roles, values work) with cases of derailment and leadership transitions. Conceptually, the paper builds on the literature on workplace spirituality and spiritual leadership, as well as on systemic approaches and self-knowledge as foundations for inner work in management education. Spirituality is understood here as a mode of relating to work that involves meaning, presence, and connection to something larger than the individual ego, rather than as adherence to a specific religious tradition. Methodologically, the research adopts a qualitative, interpretive and longitudinal design, mobilizing observations, student productions and recurrent patterns identified over a decade. The findings show that systemic constellations help students access “elevated states of awareness” regarding invisible dynamics of belonging, legitimacy and power, while maintaining psychological safety. The leadership workshop fosters self-knowledge, recognition of vulnerabilities and a more responsible understanding of one’s potential impact on others. Together, these devices function as spiritual pedagogies in practice: they invite inner reflection, embodied attention, and a renewed sense of purpose in relation to future managerial roles. The paper discusses implications for the MSR community in terms of designing spiritually informed pedagogies that are rigorous, non-confessional and aligned with inner development goals in higher education institutions.

Purpose, People, and Profit: A Review of the Impact of Spirituality in the Workplace on Well-Being

MSR
Shiwani Shiwani (Indian institute of management Jammu); Mamta Tripathi (Indian institute of management Jammu)
The pursuit of wealth creation frequently overshadows other critical dimensions of sustainability. Spirituality in the Workplace (SIWP) can address the fundamental issues organisations face regarding employees’ quality of life, which is often compromised in profit-oriented organisations. This literature review provides a comprehensive view of the role of SIWP in Employee Well-being in profit-oriented organisations. To cover the complexity of the concept, comprehend the phenomenon, and provide a future research agenda, the Theory, Context, Characteristics, Methodology (TCCM) and a Multi-level Outcomes framework have been developed. The study comprehensively analyses SIWP’s operational constructs, analyses employee well-being as an outcome of SIWP, and provides various future research directions. The study also proposes a conceptual model depicting SIWP’s impact on organisational sustainability and community well-being. Additionally, it offers significant insights for scholars, practitioners, and policymakers aiming to cultivate a spiritually enriched and purpose-driven workplace.

The Moderating Role of Workplace Spirituality in the CSR-UPB Relationship

MSR
siyao chen (Tongji University); Xiaojing Ni (Tongji University)
This study examines how employees’ perceptions of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) may paradoxically lead to Unethical Pro-Organizational Behavior (UPB), and how Workplace Spirituality (WPS) moderates this relationship across two cultural contexts: China and Indonesia. While prior research has focused on CSR’s positive outcomes, little is known about its unintended ethical effects in non-Western settings. Drawing on Social Exchange Theory and Hofstede’s cultural dimensions, this study explores how workplace spirituality shapes employees’ moral reasoning in response to CSR initiatives. Survey data from 756 employees (305 from China and 451 from Indonesia) reveal that perceived CSR positively influences UPB in both countries, suggesting that employees may justify unethical acts as benefiting their organizations. However, WPS moderates this relationship differently: in China, higher WPS strengthens the CSR–UPB link, while in Indonesia, it weakens it. These findings highlight that workplace spirituality can function as either a moral amplifier or a safeguard depending on cultural context. The study contributes to CSR theory by uncovering its ethical paradox and emphasizing the cultural contingency of moral reasoning in organizations.

Workplace Spirituality and Ethical Leadership: Evidence from the Service Industry

MSR
Joelcia Valentim (Estácio de Sá University); Marcus Brauer; Ettore de Carvalho Oriol (Fundacao Dom Cabral); Francisco José dos Santos Alves (University of the State of Rio de Janeiro); Wlamir Goncalves Xavier (Lipscomb University)
This research addresses a notable scarcity in studies examining the relationship between workplace spirituality and ethical leadership, despite the increasing relevance of both concepts within organizations. The primary objective of this study was to investigate the impact of workplace spirituality on ethical leadership specifically within the service sector, thereby contributing to the existing literature and narrowing a recognized research gap. To achieve this, a new scale was developed by integrating two established instruments: the Spirituality Scale (Liu & Robertson, 2011) and the Ethical Leadership at Work Questionnaire Adapted Scale (Almeida, Den Hartog & Porto, 2018). The validation of this scale's items was performed using Confirmatory Factor Analysis. The theoretical model developed was validated, and the proposed hypothesis was confirmed. The findings reveal a positive and significant influence of workplace spirituality on ethical leadership within the service sector. This study offers a pragmatic contribution by suggesting that organizations can leverage these findings to implement initiatives that foster spirituality in the workplace. Such initiatives hold the potential to positively enhance an organization's ethical culture. Furthermore, the research proposes that the connection between spirituality and servant and transformational leadership can be integrated into the mindset of leaders. In summary, this study validates a theoretical model demonstrating a positive link between workplace spirituality and ethical leadership in the service industry, offering actionable insights for organizations aiming to cultivate a stronger ethical culture and leadership mentality.

The Missing Pulse: Transformative Potential of Workplace Spirituality to Organizational Challenges

MSR
Muhammad Nadeem Dogar (University of Central Punjab); Ummara Yousaf (The University of Lahore)
Organizations across the Global South face persistent challenges, including corruption, unethical conduct, employee burnout, and workplace stress, often intensified by avaricious orientations and weak ethical governance. This qualitative study examines the practice and transformative potential of workplace spirituality (WS) as a holistic organizational intervention. Drawing on qualitative data collected in Pakistan, the study analyzes forty-six interviews and focus group discussions conducted across three social sector organizations (A, R, and J). Using thematic analysis, we identify thirteen interrelated dimensions of WS practice, including embedded spirituality, volunteerism, and a family-oriented work environment. These dimensions shape ethical norms, selfless service, meaningful work, and supportive organizational cultures. The findings show that WS practices mitigate key organizational challenges by reducing corruption through trust-based and value-driven systems, alleviating burnout via enhanced psychosocial and spiritual well-being. Collectively, these practices contribute to the development of resilient and ethically grounded organizational cultures. The study highlights the salience of workplace spirituality within Pakistan’s socio-religious context, while offering insights relevant to other institutionally constrained and corruption-prone environments in the Global South. We theorize workplace spirituality as an inimitable, context-embedded organizational resource that enables ethical governance in Muslim-majority contexts.
