Who Commits to the Nation? Female Executives and Rhetorical Nationalism

SIM
Ya Wang (Tongji University); Mengmeng Shan (Fudan University); Shouming Chen (Tongji University); Wenjia Du (Tongji University)
This study examines why some firms are more inclined than others to engage in rhetorical nationalism and explains such variation from a role commitment perspective. We conceptualize rhetorical nationalism as a binding organizational commitment toward the nation-state that can generate sociopolitical legitimacy while imposing long-term obligations, scrutiny, and constraints on strategic flexibility. We argue that firms with higher female executive representation are less likely to adopt such high-commitment rhetoric because female executives place greater weight on risk, ethical exposure, and reputational costs. We further show that this relationship is contingent on contextual factors that reshape the commitment calculus. Analyses of Chinese A-share-listed firms from 2010 to 2023 support our arguments, highlighting executive gender composition as a microfoundation of corporate nationalist expression.
