Fast Outputs, Fragile Expertise: the AI-Enabled Capability Paradox

TIM
Ritu Raj (Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad); Shreya Pandey (Queen's University Belfast)
The prevailing scholarly consensus celebrates knowledge work's augmentability as a strategic advantage for preserving humans’ role. This paper challenges that assumption by developing a conceptual process model to explain how the characteristics that render knowledge work augmentable simultaneously enable frictionless delegation to AI. Organizations are increasingly shifting from using AI as a support tool to relying on it to produce substantial parts of their output. At the same time, humans focus more on editing, assembling, and signing off. This change can seem successful because people usually evaluate performance based on precise results, such as how fast and how much work is done, how polished it looks, and whether there are any obvious mistakes, rather than examining the underlying process of execution. As a result, careful checking and deep reasoning are often rushed or reduced. Over time, people in this domain become less able to perform core tasks independently, especially under pressure, novelty, or scrutiny, even as measured performance improves. We theorize how this dynamic can become self-reinforcing when work pace is recalibrated to AI speed, making independent work increasingly difficult to complete within expected timelines. The model also identifies conditions under which this outcome can be avoided, specifically when organizations make reasoning visible and consequential through stronger accountability, faster feedback, and deliberate routines that protect opportunities to practice core skills without relying on AI.
