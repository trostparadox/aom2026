Rethinking Capstone Projects: Mentoring, Student-Client Relationships, and Emerging Practice

Presenters: Lilian Ngozi Schofield Queen Mary University of London; Joanne Jin Zhang Queen Mary University of London

Session overview

This caucus brings together management educators to explore emerging challenges in capstone projects, focusing on student-client relationships, mentoring, managing diverse capstone projects/clients (startups vs. corporate clients), and additional stakeholders such as Generative AI. Through open discussion, participants will share experiences, surface new ideas and solutions, and identify future directions for inclusive, future-facing capstone pedagogy. Capstone projects are widely used to connect students with practice, enhance employability, and integrate learning. Despite their ubiquity, capstones remain pedagogically under-theorised, particularly when they involve live engagement with start-ups, SMEs, or corporate organisations. This caucus creates an open, collaborative space for educators to critically examine the relational, inclusive, and emerging dimensions of capstone projects. The caucus focuses on student-client relationships as a shared challenge across capstone models. Participants are invited to reflect on unpacking different types of capstone projects (startups vs. corporate clients), the role of students in the context of Generative AI (GenAI and Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI), and the role of module organisers/programme directors in supporting student learning. These differences are often flattened in curriculum design, resulting in uneven student experiences and increased pressure on academic staff to mediate relationships informally. A central theme of the caucus is the role of mentoring as an enabling mechanism for capstone learning and managing different types of capstone projects. The caucus encourages participants to exchange ideas about how different forms of mentoring, external practitioner mentoring, internal hub-based support (e.g. entrepreneurship hub), and academic support can support students in navigating professional relationships. The caucus also addresses the emerging role of Generative AI as a possible additional stakeholder in some contexts, influencing how students partner with AI in their relationships with the clients. Participants are invited to share how students negotiate GenAI use, authorship, and accountability in live projects, and how educators are responding to this in practice. Equity, diversity, and inclusion are explored through the lens of student experience, recognising that students enter capstones with different levels of confidence, professional exposure, and access to informal support, which shape how they engage with organisations and problems. The caucus is designed to encourage exchange, collaboration, and idea development. The caucus will be facilitated as a structured, discussion-led session designed to encourage broad participation and the free exchange of ideas. The session will begin with a brief framing by the organisers (5 minutes) outlining the purpose of the caucus and introducing three guiding themes: (1) variation across capstone models (start-up, SME, corporate), (2) mentoring as a mechanism for supporting student-client relationships, and (3) emerging conditions shaping student learning, including AI as a possible additional stakeholder and equity in student experience. Following the framing, the caucus will move into a whole group, facilitated discussion structured around a small number of open-ended questions. Participants will be invited to share challenges, practices, and unanswered questions from their own contexts. In the final segment, the caucus will shift to collective sensemaking and agenda-setting, inviting participants to identify shared themes, promising practices, and areas requiring further research or pedagogical development. Where appropriate, the discussion will surface opportunities for cross-institutional collaboration, future PDWs, or joint research initiatives related to capstone pedagogy and mentoring. The caucus directly addresses the AoM conference theme by focusing on future-facing management education, relational pedagogy, and the evolving conditions under which students learn to work with organisations.
