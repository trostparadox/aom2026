Explaining Role Suspension of Hospice Social Work in China

PNP
Ting Gao (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen)
This study examines why hospice social work in China remains institutionally recognized yet practically absent. Drawing on 48 in-depth interviews with hospice practitioners and policy document analysis, I introduce the concept of “role suspension” to capture the gap between discursive presence and functional absence. Findings show that during the localized translation of hospice care, social work becomes marginalized through medicalized institutional design, symbolic organizational adoption, and occupational-level dynamics, including responsibility absorption by medical staff and defensive withdrawal by social workers. By theorizing role suspension, this study extends institutional translation and sociology of professions by explaining how professional roles can be formally embedded yet substantively suspended in public service localization.
