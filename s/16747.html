Session overview

Organizational misconduct has become a central concern in management over the past decade. Yet scholarship remains fragmented across disciplines and lifecycle stages, limiting understanding of how misconduct emerges, is surfaced, and reverberates into organizational and field structures. This Presenter Symposium advances an integrative perspective by bringing together four papers that examine linked facets of misconduct across antecedents, reporting, and consequences in a largely common conceptual and empirical context. The first presentation links accelerated paths to IPO to post-IPO misconduct, arguing that such association is driven by underdeveloped governance. The second presentation explains community variation in citizen reports of organizational misconduct, showing how community recruitment, resources, and psychological engagement shape the reporting intensity. The third study theorizes employees as identity-bearing stakeholders, showing that corporate social irresponsibility undermines commitment and increases exit—especially in purpose-vocal firms where word–deed misalignment is salient. Finally, the fourth presentation examines industry spillovers, arguing that scandalized misconduct reweights and reshapes the curvature of evaluative dimensions within the focal category, prompting peers to reposition. Together, the symposium offers an interactive, stakeholder-spanning view of misconduct in which it is enabled by governance and growth pressures, mediated by community structure and institutionalized disclosure channels, and consequential for talent flows and competitive reconfiguration, seeding a more cumulative research agenda.
