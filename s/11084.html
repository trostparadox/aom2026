Session overview

Customer retention is widely recognised as strategically important, yet it is most often understood and managed as a set of outcome metrics, such as churn or repeat purchase. This focus obscures the organisational work through which retention is actually enacted in practice—particularly in small service firms operating with limited resources, informal structures, and overlapping roles. This Professional Development Workshop invites participants to explore customer retention as an organisational capability rather than a customer-level outcome. Drawing on qualitative research-in-progress and practice-based perspectives, the session examines how retention becomes embedded (or fails to embed) in everyday organisational routines, role ownership, and cross-functional coordination. Through guided discussion and small-group exercises, participants will analyse empirical examples from small service businesses to surface recurring patterns in how senior leaders interpret, prioritise, and operationalise retention. Rather than introducing prescriptive tools or maturity models, the workshop focuses on developing a shared language to recognise retention-related capabilities, gaps, and tensions within organisations. The session is designed for scholars and practitioner–researchers interested in organisational capability development, service management, and strategy-in-practice. Participants will leave with a clearer understanding of how retention-related routines emerge, fragment, or stabilise over time, and how this perspective opens new avenues for research and reflective practice.
