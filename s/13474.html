Session overview

Decentralization is often associated with agility, innovation, and employee empowerment, empirical research also documents coordination failures, conflict, and employee overload. This symposium advances the argument that these tensions stem in part from how decentralization has been conceptualized—too often as a static, unidimensional structural attribute rather than as a dynamic, multilevel, and social process. Bringing together four complementary papers, the symposium reconceptualizes decentralization as an evolving pattern of authority distribution that varies by decision domain, mode of authority exercise, and social interaction over time.
