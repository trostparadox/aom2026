Contested Futures: Professional Visions and the Making of a Political Reform

Authors: Francesca Zoccarato Free University of Bozen-Bolzano; Mattia Vincenzo Olive Politecnico di Milano

Session overview

This paper examines how imagined desirable futures translate into implemented outcomes, and with what consequences, focusing on the role of professions in shaping implementation success. We study the Italian National Recovery and Resilience Plan (PNRR) in the post-COVID context, and in particular the reform of territorial healthcare through the creation of Houses of Community and Hospitals of Community. These initiatives articulate a future-oriented vision of proximity-based, integrated care intended to replace a fragmented and hospital-centered territorial system. Drawing on a longitudinal qualitative case study, we analyze how this desirable future was narrated, contested, and enacted over time. Our data include approximately 2,000 media articles published between 2020 and 2025, transcripts of four parliamentary hearings, and recordings of two negotiation meetings between physicians and their unions. We show that perception of the implemented outcomes diverge sharply depending on the standpoint adopted. While policymakers frame the reform as successful due to the physical completion of infrastructures, medical professionals and unions emphasize failure, pointing to the absence of physicians and misalignment with their professions. Our preliminary findings highlight competing narratives of success and failure, a temporal evolution of desirability claims, audience-specific constructions of the future, and a profound clash of visions between key actor groups. The study contributes to research on future making by showing how professions mediate the passage from imagined futures to enacted realities, and how implementation can stabilize materially while remaining socially and professionally unfulfilled.
