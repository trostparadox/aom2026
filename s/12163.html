Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, especially generative models, have become integral to scientific research, transforming scientific practice from theoretical modeling and data analysis to article review and the preparation of scholarly publications. Building on the experience of current and former editors of leading management journals, this PDW is designed to critically examine what it means to engage in research writing and publishing in an era where AI challenges traditional boundaries of originality, peer review, and confidence in the reliability and relevance of communicated knowledge. Notably, it is the hope that we could clarify the role of AI in management research, transitioning from awareness to actionable framework that ensure AI’s responsible and effective deployment in scholarly output. Grounded in research on technological mediation, authorship ethics, epistemic exclusion, and institutional accountability, this PDW invites participants into a reflective and collaborative space to explore three core questions: 1. How do we navigate human and machine co-authorship as acts of both innovation and ethical responsibility? 2. What are our scholarly obligations when the tools that generate knowledge can also obscure its provenance or amplify systemic biases? 3. What form does intellectual courage take for researchers who engage with generative AI while striving to preserve the authenticity and integrity of human scholarship?
