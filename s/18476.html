Session overview

The Ecosystems "Voice" Entrepreneurial ecosystems thrive on diversity, connectivity, and the bridge between theory and practice. However, early-career scholars often struggle to find "the hook" that resonates with both top-tier journals and the practitioners they study. The Ecosystems “Voice” is an innovative, high-engagement PDW designed to amplify the research voices of doctoral students and junior faculty. When we work with entrepreneurs, there are pitch competitions but the best models are far more developmental and collaborative. Why not the same for newer/younger ecosystems scholars? Drawing inspiration from The Voice, this session shifts the focus from adversarial critique to collaborative "coaching." Before the conference, selected participants are matched with a Mentor Duo: one world-class ecosystem scholar and one "Ecosystem Builder" or policy expert (e.g., from the ESHIP Alliance). This trio works together to refine the research’s "pitch" for both academic rigor and real-world relevance. During the 120-minute session, five finalists present their work to an "In-Person Chair" panel of senior scholars and practitioners. The audience acts as an extended ecosystem, providing live "peer support" through structured feedback and offers of tangible resources—such as access to datasets, field sites, or policy networks. Unlike traditional "Shark Tank" formats, the goal here is not to eliminate, but to "turn the chairs" by demonstrating project viability. Judges identify the most promising projects based on their potential for impact and theoretical novelty. Participants walk away with a refined research plan, a strengthened network across the academic-practitioner divide, and actionable takeaways for navigating the complex ecosystems research landscape.
