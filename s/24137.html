How and When Outcome Interdependence Fosters Employee Trust in Collaborative Service Robots

CM
Qiushi Wang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Shuwei Hao (Xi'an University of Science and Technology)
Drawing on the extended self theory, this study examines how and when outcome interdependence between frontline employees and service robots affects frontline employees' trust in these collaborative robots. Data were collected via a time-lagged field survey of frontline hospitality employees and analyzed using partial least squares structural equation modeling. The results indicate that outcome interdependence enhances frontline employees' trust in service robots through the serial mediation of frontline employees' investment of self into service robots and their perceived compatibility with the robots. Furthermore, this positive indirect effect is weaker for frontline employees with greater seniority experience. Overall, this study identifies outcome interdependence as a critical antecedent of frontline employees' trust in service robots and advances a novel extended-self mechanism that explains why this trust develops. Our findings also provide service managers with actionable guidance for improving employee-robot collaboration by reshaping motivational structures within cobotic service teams.
