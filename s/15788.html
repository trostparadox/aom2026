The Behavior–Cognition Paradox of Containing Language in Mobile Price Communication

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Authors: Hung Wen Chen National Yunlin University of Science and Technology; Chin-Yi Chen National Yunlin University of Science and Technology; JIA-HONG HUANG National Yunlin University of Science and Technology

Session overview

High price complexity in travel products often triggers defensive skepticism. While Mazor et al. (2024) proposed the "Containing Language Strategy" to reduce cognitive load, its efficacy within the high-context Chinese mobile commerce environment remains underexplored. We conducted an online experiment (N=199) to examine the cognitive and behavioral impacts of containing language. The results reveal a striking "Behavior-Cognition Paradox": objective containing language failed to directly reduce perceived complexity due to cultural skepticism; however, the subjective perception of such language significantly drove purchase intention. We identify a "Dual Mechanism" facilitating this paradox: (1) a Buffer Effect, where containing language mitigates the negative spillover from complexity to unfairness perception; and (2) a Shortcut Effect, functioning as a "Swift Guanxi" signal that creates a decision-making shortcut bypassing rational evaluation. By integrating the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) with Signaling Theory, this study elucidates how linguistic cues reshape decision pathways under cognitive constraints.
