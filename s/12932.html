Session overview

This symposium reframes corporate philanthropy as a complex organizational process involving multiple stakeholders and diverse outcomes. Across four empirical papers (one qualitative and three quantitative studies), we show (1) how attention and backstage intermediation shape which causes and nonprofits are presented to decision-makers, (2) how executive and board influence varies systematically across distinct discretion domains, (3) how employees limit the instrumental use of philanthropy for political access, and (4) why philanthropic returns depend on the targeted causes. Taken together, these studies move the field towards a more nuanced understanding of the strategic organizational decision-making processes that drive stakeholder engagement through corporate philanthropy. Leveraging robust and complementary theoretical traditions (attention-based view, upper echelon theory, corporate political activity, and prospect theory), the four papers illuminate overlooked sources of heterogeneity in philanthropic decisions and their outcomes for both firms and their primary stakeholders.
