Job Autonomy – A Benefit or a Burden? Employees’ Experiences in a Self-Managing Organization

CMS
Valentina Voimavuo (Aalto University School of Business)
More and more organizations aim to provide more flexibility and higher job autonomy to employees. Meanwhile, the existing literature provides somewhat inconsistent findings on how high job autonomy affects employees. To investigate this topic, I have conducted a case study of employees with experience working in a self-managing organization, where high job autonomy is an essential condition. The findings discuss how employees can experience job autonomy as a demand (i.e., requiring extensive efforts) depending on individual and organizational contexts, which problematizes the predominantly positive view of the effects of job autonomy on employees. This study demonstrates that to understand employee experience of job autonomy, it should be considered in interaction with contextual factors such as employee personal demands and resources, and the existence and accessibility of organizational support. Finally, it suggests the expansion of vertical fit of a practice to include individual-level context in addition to the organizational-level one.
