Session overview

Platforms have emerged as an increasingly attractive and popular context for strategy research, because platform research shift important strategy problems such value creation, appropriation, and competitive advantage, from primarily intra-firm organization to the facilitation of interdependence across platform actors (Ceccagnoli et al., 2012; Chen et al., 2022; Jacobides et al., 2018; Kapoor, 2018). Traditionally, strategy research has long examined performance differences in firms through multiple perspectives such as governance (Barney, 1991; Eisenhardt, 1989; Meckling & Jensen, 1976; Williamson, 1985), structure and networks (Dyer & Singh, 1998; Granovetter, 1985; Gulati, 1998), and dynamics (Chen, 1996; Gnyawali & Madhavan, 2001). Platforms and their ecosystems offer an inviting context for scholars to revisit traditional perspectives while providing an opportunity to extend strategy theory and perspectives for the distinct characteristics of platforms, such as multi-actor participation, cross-side interdependence, and value creation distributed across firms and communities. Collectively, the platform context creates numerous additional opportunities to (re)theorize traditional mechanisms and explore new boundary conditions.
