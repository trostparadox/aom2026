Session overview

Whilst the dark side of digital communication has long been recognized (Flyverbom, 2016; Vaast & Kaganer, 2013), it has recently intensified in both scale and impact. More sophisticated algorithms, the rise of generative AI, and the growing power of platform logics and influencers now amplify the reach, speed, and emotional intensity of communication in ways we are only beginning to understand (Chadwick, Hall, & Vaccari, 2025; Hajli, Saeed, Tajvidi, & Shirazi, 2022; Lazer et al., 2018). For example, deepfake videos of celebrities and AI- generated stakeholder engagement now shape strategic decision-making and organizational legitimacy at unprecedented scale, as algorithms fail to distinguish – or deliberately privilege – synthetic content. Through this PDW, we aim to foster an integrative scholarly conversation on how these dynamics reshape organizing, evaluation, and collective action. To accomplish these goals, the PDW will begin with a concise session that surfaces key theoretical tensions, offering perspectives on sociotechnical processes (Leonardi, 2013; Leonardi & Vaast, 2017; Vaast, Safadi, Lapointe, & Negoita, 2017), cultural dynamics (Garud, Snihur, Thomas, & Phillips, 2025; Gehman & Wry, 2022), psychological mechanisms (Bundy, Pfarrer, Short, & Coombs, 2017; Maitlis & Christianson, 2014; Petriglieri, Ashford, & Wrzesniewski, 2019), and societal processes (Greve, Rao, Vicinanza, & Zhou, 2022; Suddaby, Bitektine, & Haack, 2017; Tufekci, 2017). Subsequently, the PDW shifts to methodological approaches studying the dark side of digital communication. We then host a panel Q&A on publishing work in this area. The PDW concludes with a paper roundtable session.
