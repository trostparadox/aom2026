Who Benefits from the Convergence of Bio and AI Technologies?

Authors: Kyungnam Kim Korea Research Institute of Bioscience and Biotechnology; Taewon Kang Chungnam National University

Session overview

The convergence of biotechnology and artificial intelligence (BIO-AI) has been widely discussed as a potential new window of opportunity for latecomer countries seeking to narrow technological gaps in biotechnology. While AI-driven approaches are expected to accelerate research and development processes, it remains unclear whether such convergence fundamentally alters the structural conditions of technological catch-up or instead reinforces existing asymmetries between leading and follower countries. This study examines whether BIO-AI convergence constitutes a new technological window of opportunity and identifies which countries are structurally positioned to benefit from it. Drawing on the technological regime framework and theories of economic catch-up, BIO-AI is conceptualized as a distinct technological regime characterized by differences in technological cycle time, biotechnology intensity, appropriability, and dependence on leading-country knowledge. Using USPTO patent data combined with the Artificial Intelligence Patent Dataset (AIPD), we analyze BIO-AI patents over the period 1981–2020. Time-series analyses, mean comparison tests, and fixed-effects regressions with country-level interaction terms are employed to compare BIO-AI with conventional biotechnology and to examine cross-country heterogeneity in competitive outcomes. The results indicate that BIO-AI technologies exhibit shorter technological cycles and higher levels of technological convergence, suggesting potential entry opportunities for follower countries. However, BIO-AI also shows strong dependence on leading-country knowledge, particularly from the United States, and no systematic reduction in appropriability constraints. Regression results further reveal that relative technological advantage in BIO-AI is primarily explained by existing biotechnology capabilities rather than by AI convergence alone. Country-level analyses highlight divergent strategic responses across China, Germany, Japan, Taiwan, and Korea, underscoring that BIO-AI convergence creates asymmetric opportunities shaped by pre-existing national capabilities rather than uniform catch-up paths.
