A Token Win: Reversed Bonus Gap in Ex Post Bonus Adjustments

SIM
Susie Lee (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Melody M. Chao (The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology); Kin Fai Ellick Wong (The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology)
Organizations increasingly conduct pay equity analyses and implement compensation adjustments, yet it remains unclear which components of pay are corrected, through what decision processes, and with what consequences for gender inequality. Focusing on bonuses—a flexible pay component governed by managerial discretion—we integrate research on gender pay inequality with work on ex post bonus adjustments to develop a restitution perspective: when tasked with correcting inequity, decision makers may contrast away from known gender disadvantage, allocating higher adjusted bonuses to women than to otherwise comparable men. We test this account using a multi-method approach. In an archival panel of UK employers (2018–2025), we show that the prevalence of female-favoring bonus outcomes increase overtime. In four experiments (total N = 1,210, including two preregistered), participants and working managers, female employees receive higher adjusted bonuses than male employees holding performance and prior pay constant, and this effect is specific to pay adjustments rather than performance evaluation. A final study shows that laypeople generally expect male-favoring adjustments, indicating that this female-favoring bonus adjustment is not intuitive. Together, the findings identify ex post bonus adjustments as a key micro-level mechanism through which pay equity initiatives may reshape gender pay gaps across compensation components.
