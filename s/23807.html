How Do Auditors Respond to Firm-level AI Ethics Awareness?

OB
Xingying Wu
While the advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has increasingly raised ethical concerns, limited research has explored the extent to which firms’ management pays attention to AI ethics and its consequences. Using textual analysis and machine learning techniques, we develop and validate a novel firm-level, time-varying measure of AI ethics awareness (AIEA) and investigate its impact on audit pricing. Our results show that firms with higher AIEA are associated with lower audit fees, and this finding remains robust across various endogeneity and robustness tests. Mechanism analysis reveals that this effect operates through enhanced corporate governance and reduced information asymmetry, with the impact being more pronounced when firms have lower board gender diversity, weaker media governance, or higher stock price synchronicity and informed trading probability. Moreover, higher AIEA is linked to improved internal control and financial reporting quality. Our research is the first to provide large-sample evidence on how auditors respond to firm-level AIEA, we also offer valuable insights for firms seeking to improve their awareness and disclosure of AI ethics issues.
