Session overview

Extant research on academic careers highlights the important of PhD advising to doctoral students’ career trajectories. Advisors’ management styles impact doctoral students’ progression, their research program development, and their early academic placements. Yet, the potential impact of advising can extend far beyond establishing a research program. Framed from the lens of developmental relationships and career growth, these relationships have the potential to shape early career academic’s personal and professional growth and foster scholarly identity formation. While organizational scholarship on developmental relationships is well-established, these advances have rarely been applied to management academics’ career. In this interactive PDW, we seek to address this gap as we explore the role of developmental relationships in shaping early career academics’ personal and professional growth. By reframing doctoral advising not as mere research oversight but as a process of developmental support and broadening “advising” to consider formal and informal developmental relationships, this PDW examines how these relationships impact early career academics’ identity and career development. In learning from career scholars and seasoned doctoral and early career advisors as well as reflecting on their own experiences receiving and providing developmental support, we hope that participants will gain new, actionable insights for supporting early career academics as well as consider potential research collaborations to deepen theorizing on this topic. Our goal is to foster a community of scholars committed to cultivating developmental relationships with intentionality and compassion that enable management academics to build impactful, generative and sustainable academic careers.
