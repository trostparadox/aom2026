Session overview

Leaders’ openness and appropriate self-disclosure have long been emphasized as crucial predictors of employee trust. Yet, recent evidence suggests that when it comes to leaders’ use of AI, transparency may actually backfire. Given that AI-supported leadership is likely here to stay, leaders therefore face a fundamental dilemma: how to meet normative expectations for transparent AI disclosure without undermining employee trust, which is essential for effective leadership. Addressing this tension, we draw on leadership meta-talk theory to shift the focus from a binary question of whether leaders should disclose AI use to reconceptualizing AI disclosure as a communication framing choice that may shape how employees interpret leaders’ reliance on AI. Building on two core categories of leadership responsibility, we develop conceptual arguments distinguishing disclosure without additional explanation from two purpose-focused meta-talk framings: a task-focused explanation emphasizing productivity gains for the leader, and a relationship-focused explanation emphasizing fairness for employees. To test our theorizing, we utilize an experimental design in which employees are randomly distributed into one of three disclosure conditions and work on a proposal writing task. Relative to simple disclosure, task-focused meta-talk was associated with lower employee trust, whereas relation-focused meta-talk showed no significant difference. Taken together, these findings indicate that adding explanatory meta-talk does not inherently increase trust and that for some forms of justification, it may even be counterproductive.
