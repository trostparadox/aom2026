Beyond Market Commonality and Resource Similarity

STR
Lee Warren Brown (Texas Woman's University); John Anthony De Leon (Texas A&M University-Corpus Christi)
While competitive rivalry drives many firm outcomes, existing research has not systematically distinguished between competitors merely present in one's market versus those directly encountered in competition. Building on the Awareness-Motivation-Capability framework we theorize that near opponents generate stronger performance improvements than distant peers in the broader competitive environment and that top-tier opponents generate greater performance benefits. Using comprehensive data from 973,391 powerlifting competitions spanning 2000-2025 with 228,387 individual lifters, we find that near opponent quality demonstrates substantially larger effects than distant peer quality on performance improvements. Further top-tier opponents generate disproportionately stronger impacts compared to median opponents. These findings advance competitive dynamics research by establishing geographic proximity as a critical moderating condition in the AMC framework. The study findings also reveal hierarchical rather than additive competitive pressures, suggesting managers should prioritize direct encounters with elite competitors over general participation in high-quality competitive environments for the greatest performance enhancing effects.
