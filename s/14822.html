Impact of LMX on Turnover Intention of Nurses Through the Mediating Effect of Employee Engagement

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
HR

People

Authors: Antara Mahbub University of Southern Mississippi; H. Quincy Brown

Session overview

For any organization, the leader-follower relationship, employee engagement, and turnover intention are critical components. Understanding what motivates nurses to be more or less engaged and retained is crucial in hospitals, where they face demanding workloads and a staff shortage. This study aims to investigate how employee engagement mediates the relationship between Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) and employee turnover intention among registered nurses working in hospitals in Mississippi. The study will employ a non-experimental quantitative research approach and conduct a structured online survey to gather data from nurses at multiple hospitals. The LMX-7 scale (Graen & Uhl-Bien, 1995), Employee Engagement Scale (EES) developed by Shuck et al. (2017), and Intention to Turnover Scale (ITS) developed by Colarelli (Colarelli, 1984) will be used as the instruments of this study. Collected data will be analyzed using Pearson’s r, correlation analysis, and the PROCESS macro for mediation analysis. Theoretically, this study will contribute to the LMX theory by revealing the relationship between high/low LMX and high/low levels of employee engagement and turnover intention. In practical terms, this study will provide supervisors and hospital managers with valuable insights to enhance leadership practices, ultimately improving patient care outcomes in the healthcare sector. Keywords: LMX, employee engagement, turnover intention, nurses.
