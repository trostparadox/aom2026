Why Should I Take Charge? Nexus Of Supervisor Ostracism, Dehumanization, And Psychological Hardiness

OB
Ali Tariq (The University of Lahore); Tehreem Fatima; Muhammad Kashif Imran; Sobia Shabeer (Mukabbir University of Science & Technology, Gujrat, Pakistan); Saira Altaf (The University of Lahore)
In this paper, we used Conservation of Resources theory to explain how supervisor ostracism suppresses the taking charge behavior of employees. We theorize that supervisor ostracism burdens the socio-emotional resources of employees by creating an impression of organization dehumanization and thus hinders the ability to invest effort in proactive action. We further propose psychological hardiness as a personal resource that qualifies this process by weakening the relationship between supervisor ostracism and organizational dehumanization. A time-lagged field study (n=308) and an experimental vignette study (N=118) offer support for our model. The data were collected from and analyzed using. Overall, this study contributes to our understanding of how social exclusion limits employee proactivity and generalizes Conservation of Resources theory by making organizational dehumanization a key mechanism for loss of resources that connects supervisory mistreatment to reduced taking charge.
