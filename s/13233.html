Session overview

Professionalism is a key differentiator in hiring and career success, yet gaps persist between students’ self-perceptions and employer expectations. While 89.4% of students rate themselves as proficient, only 42.5% of employers agree—a misalignment that undermines graduate employability and institutional credibility (Powell, 2024). This 90-minute interactive Co-Design Studio Professional Development Workshop engages faculty, administrators, and doctoral students in defining professionalism, designing institutional implementation plans, developing curriculum strategies, and critiquing existing models using evidence-based frameworks and successful program implementations. Participants will explore professionalism as encompassing integrity, responsibility, effectiveness, collaboration, respectfulness, and appropriate appearance—moving beyond vague "soft skills" to measurable competencies (Clark, 2012). Through four interactive modules, participants will: (1) conceptualize professionalism in business education; (2) design institutional plans incorporating curricular and co-curricular elements; (3) develop progressive, multi-year curriculum frameworks; and (4) critically evaluate and enhance an existing professionalism program. The workshop combines small-group collaboration, digital tools, and facilitator-led synthesis for active engagement and practical application. Deliverables include a shared definition of professionalism, a draft implementation plan, a curriculum design template, and access to a post-workshop resource repository. This workshop equips participants to bridge employer expectations and enhance graduate career readiness by addressing the ethical and strategic dimensions of professionalism education.
