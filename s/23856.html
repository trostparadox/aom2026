When Leaders Share Positive Events: Leader Capitalization Fuels Team Positive Affect and Performance

OB
Waiseng Lou (Peking University); Junchao Huang (Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences)
The act of sharing positive events, known as interpersonal capitalization, has primarily been examined with regard to its effects on individuals, whether the discloser or the responder. In this research, we extend this line of inquiry to the team level by investigating how leaders’ sharing of their positive work-related events with team members influences collective outcomes. Drawing on affective events theory, we propose that leader workplace interpersonal capitalization toward team members enhances team positive affective tone, which consequently improves team effectiveness and promotes team organizational citizenship behaviors. We further hypothesize that team leader dependence moderates the relationship between leader capitalization and team positive affective tone, such that the positive effect is stronger when dependence is higher. Results from a multi-source, multi-wave study of 70 teams support our predictions. Our research advances theory on capitalization and offers valuable insights for fostering positive team dynamics through leader informal communication.
