The Role of Reciprocity and Abundance in Increasing Regenerative Capacity Through Supply Chains

OSCM
Kelsey Taylor (University of Guelph); Felicia-Adina Kemmner (University of Lausanne); Lydia Bals (Mainz University of Applied Sciences)
Going beyond more traditional scopes of sustainability like the triple bottom line, implementing social-ecological regeneration in the supply chain is not possible for any single company to achieve alone, but requires inter-organizational participation. The study uses an inductive, exploratory multiple case study approach to examine the supply chains of 12 award-winning B Corps to understand how purpose-driven organizations work to develop regenerative capacity at multiple scales. Based on insights from the data, we then deploy collective action theory as an interpretive lens to generate theoretical propositions situated in the empirical data. The findings suggest that increasing regenerative capacity is a stepwise process in which companies first leverage direct reciprocity to build regenerative capacity within the supply chain, before practicing generalized reciprocity when moving to regenerative capacity building beyond the supply chain. The findings advance research and practice on regenerative supply chains through insights on: 1) the sequence of regenerative capacity building, 2) the importance of inter-dependent decision making, and 3) consciously reconsidering the level of rivalry or excludability in the deployment of firm-held resources.
