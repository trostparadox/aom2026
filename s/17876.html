Session overview

Despite widespread organizational commitments to diversity, equity, and inclusion, disparities in hiring, promotion, and overall workplace experiences persist across sociodemographic categories, including race, gender, disability status, age, social class origin, and biologically rooted traits. This symposium explores the psychological foundations of these disparities, focusing on perceptions of interpersonal differences and biases in decision-making, conceptions of diversity and fairness judgments, and support for diversity initiatives. Specifically, it addresses: (1) how individuals perceive and respond to inherited racial advantages and disadvantages, (2) the impact of chronotype bias on leadership endorsement, (3) how organizational diversity language affects whether disabled individuals request accommodation, (4) how feminist identity influences perceptions of gender diversity, and (5) when and why class and age inequalities in hiring are perceived less unfair than gender and racial inequalities. As such, this symposium provides researchers studying organizational behavior, human resources, and diversity, equity, and inclusion with novel insights about how individuals’ perceptions and attitudes shape workplace inequities across social categories and organizational gateways, offering inspiration for future research and practical insights for managers seeking to enhance fairness and inclusion within organizations.
