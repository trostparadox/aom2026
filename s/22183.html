Following the Gaze: A Review on Eye Tracking’s Rise in Research on People Management

Session overview

Eye tracking has traveled a long path from early laboratory instruments in experimental psychology and human factors to today’s portable, analytics-ready sensors. Yet, its uptake in research related to the workplace and employees remains uneven and dispersed across adjacent fields. This scoping review maps how eye-tracking methods have been used to study employees and workplace phenomena, what findings have dominated the field, and how research questions and tools shifted over time. Following PRISMA-guided identification and screening of Web of Science and Scopus data, we find that eye tracking first supported managerial aims of safety, vigilance, and ergonomic design. Then, eye tracking entered management research as a process-tracing method for attention and decision-making assessment, and only recently began to target core employee phenomena shaped by digitalization. Thematic synthesis yields five recurring workplace-related clusters. Thus, we position eye tracking as the newest chapter in the management-history arc of measurement, and propose a future research agenda emphasizing key findings, theory integration, field contexts, and ethical guidelines for business leaders and managers.
