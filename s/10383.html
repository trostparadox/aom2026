Why Can't We Be Friends? Gendered Microinclusions and Microexclusions at Work

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Sonia Ghumman University of Hawaii at Mänoa; Tamar Admati Kreps University of Hawaii at Mänoa

Session overview

Is it a good idea to hang out with coworkers outside of work? For some employees, being included in social conversations and events outside of work can function as a powerful signal of belonging. Prior research on microinclusions (Muragishi et al., 2024), focusing on in-role interactions, has linked being included at work to workplace belonging, cohesion, and performance. In this paper, we conducted a study to illuminate how these subtle interactions work in the extra- role conversations more characteristic of multiplex workplace friendships. Following relevant prior work, we focused on how an employee’s gender and the gendered nature of extra-role socializing affect employees’ responses. We conducted a vignette experiment with 606 participants and found that, for women employees, experiencing microinclusion increased feelings of workplace social inclusion, team satisfaction, and belongingness, and lowered state anxiety, compared with experiencing microexclusion as either a woman or a man. These findings shed light on the potential for workplace friendships to signal inclusion toward employees from different backgrounds, and can inform managers about how to improve inclusion at work.
