Session overview

AI disruption is uneven: some organizations adopt AI and strengthen while others deploy similar tools yet fail to convert them into durable advantage. This panel symposium integrates six components to build a multi-level account of resilience to AI disruptions: (i) AI as a system-level capability (VRIN-like) rather than plug-and-play, (ii) incumbent renewal via AI-enabled business model innovation, (iii) AI innovations under geopolitical and regulatory disruptions, (iv) ambiguous capital market attitudes toward AI and AI washing, (v) how human and AI complement each other for innovation and science, and (vi) startup human-capital reconfiguration after the AlexNet discontinuity. Across these interrelated components, we emphasize (a) how organizations create advantage through search, learning, and reconfiguration (Katila & Ahuja, 2002), (b) how AI enables business model innovation and continuous reconfiguration of value creation (Aagaard & Tucci, 2024), (c) how firms develop and adopt AI innovation under geopolitical tension and uncertainty (Huang & He, 2026; Huang, Jia, & Ge, 2024), (d) when firms perceive investors’ AI preferences through inquiry scope, vigilance, and sentiment, they adjust their attention allocation toward AI, which can induce or constrain AI washing behavior (Niu, Zhang, & Hu, 2026), (e) relationship between human and AI to understand and collaborate in the science-based innovation process and scientific discovery (Motohashi et al., 2026), and (f) how human capital composition and mobility shape strategic outcomes and path dependence (Sakakibara & Balasubramanian, 2020).
