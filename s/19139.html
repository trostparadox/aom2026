From Shared Threat to Collective Resistance: How Exploitative Leadership Reduces Knowledge Hiding

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Tian Zehui Hebei GEO University; Qian Shanshan Guangdong University of Foreign Studies

Session overview

Exploitative leadership may prompt employees to seek collective coping strategies through informal peer networks. Within this context, subordinates’ knowledge strategies play a critical role. Grounded in social comparison theory, this study examines the mechanism through which exploitative leadership influences knowledge-hiding behavior. Using three-wave survey data collected from 30 leaders and 246 employees in two Chinese manufacturing enterprises, the results show that leader-member exchange differentiation moderates the positive relationship between exploitative leadership and team-member exchange, such that the relationship is stronger when LMX differentiation is low rather than high. Furthermore, LMX differentiation moderates the indirect effect of exploitative leadership on knowledge hiding through team-member exchange. Specifically, the indirect positive effect of exploitative leadership on knowledge hiding via team-member exchange is stronger when LMX differentiation is low rather than high. The theoretical and practical implications of these findings are discussed.
