From Growth to Waste: How Affluence, Technology, and Culture Shape Global E-Waste Generation

ONE
Karthik Krishnan (Indian Institute of Foreign Trade); Arunima Rana (Indian Institute of Foreign Trade)
This study investigates the socio-economic, technological, and cultural determinants of e-waste generation across countries, with a particular focus on the moderating and moderated moderation effects among these factors. Drawing upon the IPAT framework, Hofstede’s cultural dimensions, and the concept of affluence-driven consumption, the analysis employs a Mundlak random-effects estimation using panel data from multiple countries over time. The results confirm that population, affluence, and technology are significant drivers of e-waste generation. Affluence not only directly increases e-waste but also amplifies the effect of population on it, reflecting how rising income levels accelerate consumption and device replacement. Interestingly, technology demonstrates a dual role, while directly increasing e-waste, higher ICT maturity reduces the marginal effect of population through improved efficiency and longer product lifespans. Education further moderates this relationship, mitigating the adverse technological impact on waste generation. Cultural dimensions exert differential influences: Power Distance and Individualism intensify the population–affluence–e-waste linkage through conspicuous consumption, whereas Uncertainty Avoidance weakens it by promoting cautious purchasing behaviour. The study also highlights the importance of integrating cultural and behavioural insights into global e-waste management strategies, moving beyond purely economic explanations, and highlights policy implications for promoting sustainable consumption and circular economy practices worldwide.

Waste Management – Literature Review from a Network Perspective

ONE
Monika Okreglicka-Mazur (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
Abstract: Waste management (WM) has become a global challenge as material throughput, urbanization, and consumption intensify environmental and social pressures. While research on WM in business and management has expanded rapidly, it remains fragmented across waste types, sectors, and analytical lenses. This paper synthesizes WM scholarship through Actor–Network Theory perspective, treating waste as a socio-material actant embedded in networks of regulation, technology, organizations, infrastructures, and behavior. Drawing on a systematic review of 212 peer-reviewed articles (2015– 2025), the paper identifies recurring translation mechanisms through which networks shape waste outcomes and, conversely, how waste reshapes networks. Four dominant mechanisms structure positive and negative outcomes: rule-driven translation, tech/data-mediated coordination, behavioral enrollment and norm activation, and operational orchestration and supply-network design. Two emerging but underdeveloped mechanisms—governance/finance alignment and ecosystem capability reconfiguration—are discussed as future research avenues. The analysis demonstrates that waste outcomes emerge from patterned translations across heterogeneous actants rather than isolated interventions. The paper contributes by integrating WM and circular economy (CE) research into a unified socio-material framework and by foregrounding waste as an active agent in sustainability transitions.

The (Im)Possible Circular Economy? Barriers and Best Practices in Resource-Intensive Industries (WITHDRAWN)

ONE
Konstantin Remke (EM Normandie); Philipp Möhringer (ESCP-Europe Business School - Berlin)
The Circular Economy (CE) promises to replace the linear ‘take–make–waste’ model with closed material and energy loops, yet its implementation is constrained by thermodynamic limits, rebound effects, definitional ambiguities, and system-boundary challenges. Existing research tends to emphasize sector-specific or single-element solutions, leaving a gap in industry-agnostic and holistic best practices. Using a qualitative multiple-case study of 12 European firms across resource-intensive industries (RIIs), such as construction, plastics, electronics, chemicals, and packaging, we (1) identify principal barriers impeding CE adoption and (2) derive transferable, practice-based strategies that address these barriers. We synthesize findings into a best-practice framework organized by context, intervention, mechanism, and outcome (CIMO), offering actionable guidance for managers and policymakers pursuing systemic circular transitions. The study advances theory on CE barriers and provides scalable, cross-industry solutions to accelerate circular transformation in resource-intensive industries.

Circular Economy Implementation in the UK: Do Exogenous Shocks Matter?

ONE
Thi Hoa Nguyen (Heriot-Watt University); Bing Xu (Heriot-Watt University); Junyang Yin (Heriot-Watt University)
Although the circular economy (CE) offers significant benefits – such as waste minimization and resource efficiency maximization, it faces a range of barriers during the implementation. Furthermore, in a context of high uncertainty, exogenous shocks - such as Brexit, Covid-19 pandemic, and other disruptive institutional changes - may have intensified difficulties facing the CE journey. Yet, these impacts have underexplored. Our study, therefore, investigates the impact of exogenous shocks on the CE implementation in the UK context, aiming to identify how these external factors drive the CE engagement and how internal institutional environment enables firms to respond to these shocks differently. Using an empirical approach, we revealed a dual role of exogenous shocks: while the Circular Economy Policy Package (CEP) served as a positive shock fostering the CE engagement, both Brexit and Covid-19 pandemic had significant negative impact on this engagement. We also documented heterogenous impact of external shocks on CE investment across sub-samples. Based on these findings, we proposed relevant implications for policymakers, practitioners, and academics.

Circular Ecosystems: a Framework for Navigating Complexity and Digitalization

ONE
Lucrezia Sgambaro (Politecnico di Milano); Davide Chiaroni (Politecnico di Milano - School of Management); Fabian Takacs (University of St. Gallen); Andrea Urbinati (LIUC - Carlo Cattaneo University)
Circular ecosystems refer to complex systems, which involve heterogeneous actors that collaborate to achieve a circular system-level outcome. The concept of circular ecosystems emerged as a hot topic in managerial literature, and its relevance has been recognized. Scholars converge on identifying digital technologies as one of the main enablers of circular ecosystems. Still, there is a lack of research at the nexus between circular ecosystems and digital technologies. Our study aims to bridge this gap and shed light on the role of digital technologies in enabling circular ecosystems. To do so, we leverage the complex adaptive system lens to identify three complexity features of circular ecosystems. An exploratory empirical analysis is conducted to develop a novel research framework. Our findings unveil four typologies of circular ecosystems based on their level of complexity and digitalization. Our study has twofold implications for existing literature. First, we question the role of digital technologies as circular ecosystems enablers. We posit that digital technologies do not represent a panacea to enable circular ecosystems; rather, they are used when they are instrumental to achieve the circular system-level outcome. Second, we posit that a high level of digitalization does not necessarily lead to a high level of complexity.
