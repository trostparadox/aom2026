Gender and Leadership Effectiveness: A Physiological Perspective

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Sharmeen Merchant University of Cincinnati; Scott Dust University of Cincinnati; Joanna Tochman Campbell University of Cincinnati

Session overview

Although research has made great strides in improving our understanding of the role of gender in leadership effectiveness perceptions, much of this work takes a relatively narrow psychological approach that lacks integration with physiological responses that underlie social evaluations in a gendered context. To address this limitation, we integrate insights from human sexuality research to demonstrate how physiological responses characterized by attraction and disgust invoke excitatory and inhibitory mechanisms that interact with cognitive expectancies in response to leader gender role violations. Our review illustrates that leadership effectiveness evaluations emerge from the interplay of these embodied and cognitive processes. Importantly, we advance an integrative framework that explains when and why evaluations can be favorable or negative based on threshold effects, highlighting the non-linearity in the relationship between leader gender role violations and effectiveness evaluations. Our theoretical integration of the dual control model (DCM) of sexual response with existing research on leader effectiveness and gender highlights that investigating gender in a leadership context from a solely cognitive lens is incomplete and a synthesis with research on embodied experiences in the study of gender can lead to important new insights. Accordingly, we seek to integrate these literatures to generate implications for research and practice that can broaden our understanding and acceptance of both traditional and non-traditional social behaviors in a leadership context.
