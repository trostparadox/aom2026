Session overview

In recent years, corporate commitments to diversity, equity, and inclusion have surged and then, just as quickly, softened, narrowed, or disappeared. This Professional Development Workshop draws on the book, Disclosureland: How Corporate Words Constrain Racial Progress (Cambridge University Press 2026) to examine why corporate DEI rhetoric expands in moments of reputational and economic advantage and retreats when political and legal risks rise. Using original data from more than 2,000 companies, the workshop explores how organizational discourse shapes racial equity. The session focuses on three interrelated dynamics. First, it examines how corporate commitments are often untethered from historical patterns of inequality, making them especially fragile. Second, it shows how public statements and disclosures can function as tools for deflecting accountability while preserving organizational legitimacy. Third, it analyzes how shifting political contexts—particularly renewed hostility toward race-conscious initiatives—reshape corporate messaging in ways that affect employees, stakeholders, and broader social outcomes. Designed for scholars interested in organizations, ethics, governance, and social inequality, the workshop invites participants to critically assess when corporate talk about race promotes meaningful change and when it serves primarily reputational goals. By connecting empirical evidence with theory and practice, the session provides tools for analyzing corporate discourse, evaluating DEI claims, and advancing research that takes seriously the power of organizational language in shaping racial equity.
