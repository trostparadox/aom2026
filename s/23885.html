Double-Edged Sword Effect of AI-induced Status Threat on Leaders’ AI Symbolization and Resistance

OB
Yue Zhang (Shandong University); Zhenmin Lai (Shandong University); Wu Song (Xi'an University of Finance and Economics)
While artificial intelligence (AI) promises organizational efficiency, its encroachment on managerial domains presents a salient status threat to leaders. To investigate how leaders response to this professional challenge, we draw on cognitive-affective personality system (CAPS) theory to propose a double-edged model in which AI-induced status threat elicits divergent coping responses through distinct psychological pathways. Combining a scenario-based experiment with 142 participants and a multi-wave field survey of 143 leaders, we demonstrate that AI-induced status threat simultaneously triggers self-reflection and job anxiety. Specifically, self-reflection fosters AI symbolization, whereas job anxiety fuels AI resistance. Furthermore, we identify status strategy orientation as key boundary condition: prestige-oriented leaders are more inclined to engage in constructive self-reflection to facilitate AI symbolization. In contrast, the anxiety-driven resistance pathway remains robust across different leadership styles. This research contributes to the literature on leadership and technology by revealing how status preservation motives paradoxically drive both the symbolic adoption and the covert resistance of AI in the workplace.
