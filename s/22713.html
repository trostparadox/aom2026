Can AI Adoption by Focal Firms Curb Its Supply Chain Environmental Violation?

OSCM
Nan Zhang (Qingdao University); Wei Liu (Harbin Institute of Technology); Junjie Liu (University of Macau); Yumeng Luo (The University of Newcastle)
Under growing pressures from climate change and ecological constraints, environmental sustainability is critical for development. Although firms play a central role in sustainability, more than 90% of emissions originate in their supply chains, where environmental violations can seriously harm both reputation and governance. This study examines whether and how focal firms’ adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) reduces environmental violation risks across entire supply chain networks. Drawing on the knowledge spillover perspective, we employ a 2008 - 2019 panel dataset of Chinese listed firms and their supply chain networks to examine whether AI adoption by focal firms curbs environmental violations across their supply chains. Our results show that focal firms’ AI adoption significantly inhibits environmental violations along the supply chain, and that this effect is stronger when supply chains are more concentrated and geographically proximate. By extending knowledge spillover theory to environmental governance, this study demonstrates how focal firms’ AI adoption and its spillover effects shape environmental violations across supply chains. It also provides implications for managers and policymakers seeking to advance sustainable supply chain management.

Artificial Intelligence, Cooperation Stability, and Sustainability in Project-Based Supply Chains

OSCM
Xincheng Wang (Tongji University); Qiankun Zheng (Tongji University); Wan Cheng
Achieving sustainability in project-based supply chains (PBSCs) faces challenges from their transient and fragmented nature. While subcontractors’ artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities offer potential solutions, their utilization is limited by project time constraints and uncertain returns. Grounded in Social Exchange Theory, this study examines how subcontractors' AI capabilities enhance general contractors' sustainability performance, with a focus on the moderating role of cooperation stability (temporal and spatial). Empirical analysis using multi-source data from Chinese construction projects confirms that AI capabilities improve sustainability outcomes, and that cooperation stability strengthens this relationship. This research advances sustainability literature in PBSCs by conceptualizing AI as a shareable inter-organizational resource and extending SET through stability-based contextualization in transient environments.

Synergistic AI and Blockchain in Circular Agribusiness

OSCM
Simone Sehnem; Cristina Vaccari; Cristian Bau Dal Magro; Sady Mazzioni; Juliano Danilo Spuldaro
This study aimed to analyze the usefulness of AI(Artificial Inteligence) and blockchain in circularity within agribusiness. It was developed from a systematic literature review using the Scopus and WoS databases. A qualitative content analysis approach was adopted to group functions by semantic and functional similarity, identifying thematic overlaps. Subsequently, emerging patterns, discrepant aspects, and specific contributions to agribusiness were identified, with an emphasis on circularity, a concept that prioritizes waste reduction, resource reuse, waste traceability, and the closing of material and informational cycles. The findings were grouped into 50 functions and 7 central categories (revealing thematic redundancy), based on criteria of functional and technological similarity, such as the use of AI for analytical processing and Blockchain for data integrity, and combined for synergies. Each grouping receives a central code and associated details, including examples of functions, predominant technologies, and key authors. The conclusion indicates that there are technological synergies for cycle closing. The 7 core codes (OPT for optimization, DEC for decision support, TRA for traceability, SEC for security, INT for integration, MEA for measurement, and INC for incentives) reveal patterns such as the predominance of AI in analyses in 70% of the studies analyzed and Blockchain in integrity in 60% and synergies in 20%. The Digital Circular Codes framework enriches debates on sustainable management, while actionable examples such as smart packaging increase efficiency by 30 to 50% in chains such as grains and dairy products. However, success depends on collaborative governance to overcome barriers such as digital literacy.

Improving Access to Essential Medicines in Sierra Leone via Decision-Aware Machine Learning

OSCM
Angel Tsai-Hsuan Chung (University of Pennsylvania); Patrick Isaac Barnett-Bayoh; Jatu Abdulai; Lawrence Sandi; Francis Smart; Osbert Bastani (University of Pennsylvania)
A critical challenge in healthcare systems in Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMICs) is the efficient and equitable allocation of scarce resources, particularly essential medicines. This problem is complicated by limited high-quality data, which restricts the applicability of traditional data-driven techniques. We propose a novel machine learning framework for essential medicines allocation, which leverages a combination of multi-task learning and decision-aware learning to improve sample efficiency and ensure equitable allocation. In collaboration with the Sierra Leone national government, our framework has been deployed nationwide as a decision support tool to help reduce waste and improve essential medicines allocation. Our evaluation using synthetic difference-in-differences analysis demonstrates a 19% increase in medicine consumption, with no changes to the supply, improving access for approximately 3.7 million women and children under five. Through experimental validation, we demonstrate that our approach also significantly outperforms baseline approaches. Our work demonstrates the tangible impact of machine learning in optimizing high-stakes decisions in resource-constrained settings, improving efficiency while ensuring equity and cost-effectiveness.

Generative AI Framework for Predictive Optimisation in Manufacturing Using Synthetic Analytics

OSCM
Khalid Mehmood (Hubei Engineering University); Dr Qasim Ali Nisar (RMIT University); Amir Mehmood (Emaan Institute of Management and Sciences, Karachi, Pakistan); Imran Ali (Central Queensland University); Samina Qasim (Bahria University, Karachi Campus, Pakistan)
This study aims to assess the role of Generative AI and synthetic data augmentation in advancing the forecasting of demand and operational decision-making in manufacturing under data sparsity and volatility. The research has evaluated whether synthetic augmentation plays an important role in improving forecast accuracy across deep learning, statistical, and ensemble models. It also aims to assess how forecast accuracy translates into the efficiency of production schedules, inventory balance, and management of cost. Through the integration of digital-twin surrogates and regression models, results confirm that synthetic augmentation significantly enhances the accuracy of deep learning models such as LSTM. Results demonstrated that generative AI–driven synthetic augmentation significantly improved the accuracy of deep learning models (LSTM). However, the classical approaches (ARIMA, XGB) remained robust without augmentation. Improved forecast accuracy was directionally associated with higher production schedule efficiency, reduced inventory imbalances, and lower overstock and stockout costs. But these effects were moderate and varied across horizons. Moderation analysis also indicated that both data diversity and demand volatility shape the strength of these relationships, and the effect sizes were comparatively modest. These findings highlight the transformative potential of Generative AI in supply chain forecasting. It also underscores the contextual role of diversity and volatility in operational outcomes. The study demonstrates that Generative AI can mitigate the limitations in data. It can also enhance the accuracy of forecasting and support resilience in manufacturing operations. Firms can consider synthetic augmentation selectively and integrate deep learning with diverse data environments so that scheduling and inventory decisions can be optimized.
