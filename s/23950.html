When Capability Differences Undermine Innovation: Absorptive Capacity Configuration in Teams

TIM
Anjali Ashok Kumar Singh (K J Somaiya Institute of Management); Sumi Jha
Teams increasingly rely on members’ knowledge-related capabilities to innovate, yet teams often differ not only in the level but also in the distribution of such capabilities. This study examines how within-team variance in two dimensions of absorptive capacity, potential absorptive capacity (PACAP) and realized absorptive capacity (RACAP), shapes team innovation outcomes. We further investigate the role of team decision-making effectiveness as a process mechanism and team-member exchange (TMX) as a contextual boundary condition. Survey data were collected from 263 professionals nested within 65 teams operating in the pharmaceutical, IT, and manufacturing sectors in India. Established multi-item measures were used to assess absorptive capacity dimensions, team decision-making effectiveness, TMX, and team innovation. Hypotheses were tested using hierarchical linear modeling and moderated mediation analyses. Variance in both PACAP and RACAP was negatively associated with team innovation. However, only variance in RACAP exerted an indirect negative effect on innovation by undermining team decision-making effectiveness. This indirect relationship was contingent on relational dynamics within teams: high levels of TMX significantly weakened the negative effect of RACAP variance on decision-making effectiveness, thereby buffering its downstream impact on innovation. The findings highlight that capability diversity within teams is not uniformly beneficial. While uneven distribution of PACAP directly constrains innovation, variance in RACAP operates through team processes by disrupting decision quality. Importantly, strong interpersonal exchange among team members can mitigate these adverse effects. Together, the results underscore the critical role of relational context in enabling teams to manage internal differences in knowledge application capabilities and convert learning into innovation.
