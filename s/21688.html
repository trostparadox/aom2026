Session overview

Operations and supply chain management research increasingly addresses sustainability, ethical sourcing, and responsible procurement, yet many dominant approaches remain grounded in compliance, efficiency, and transactional control. In fragile and extractive sourcing environments, such as the Democratic Republic of Congo’s cobalt supply network, these approaches often struggle to explain or improve relational accountability, moral duty, legitimacy, and benefit-sharing outcomes. Building on emerging scholarship that positions Africa as a frontier for global supply networks, this PDW equips participants with conceptual and practical tools to theorize sustainable sourcing and multi-tier supply network governance using African indigenous philosophies. We focus on Ubuntu and ?m?lúàbí as complementary lenses that reframe supply networks as moral and relational systems, and translate those ethics into OSCM-relevant mechanisms, including buyer responsibility across tiers, supplier governance, participatory oversight, ethical traceability, and network configuration choices. The workshop combines brief framing talks with parallel roundtables and a plenary synthesis to help participants develop publishable research questions, positioning statements, and study designs for Africa-anchored OSCM research.
