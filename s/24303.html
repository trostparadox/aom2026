When Online Support Is Not Enough: Digital Sensemaking and the Limits of Collective Coping

DEI
Dennis Omondi Otieno (University of Liverpool); Lilian Otaye-Ebede (University of Liverpool)
Research has extensively documented the gendered racial microaggressions experienced by Black women in the workplace, yet far less is known about how these experiences are collectively interpreted when organisational contexts deny recognition and validation. Drawing on a longitudinal netnographic study of Black women’s workplace discussions on Reddit between 2008 and 2025, this study examines how Black women collectively make sense of harm, develop practical responses, and sustain psychological functioning in the absence of organisational support systems. The analysis identifies three constraint-responsive processes through which participants navigate sustained workplace discrimination: epistemic recovery, strategic action, and cognitive coping. These processes enable individuals to restore credibility to their experiences, exchange situational guidance, and manage the cumulative psychological toll of marginalisation through online communities. Building on these findings, we develop the Collective Sensemaking Infrastructure Model (CSIM) to theorise how these processes operate together as a compensatory form of collective sensemaking under conditions of organisational invalidation. Additionally, the study reveals a paradoxical dynamic in which workers who disengage most fully from workplace identity as a protective response simultaneously lose access to collective support, leaving them exposed to a dual condition of organisational exclusion and social isolation. We term this dynamic the Disinvestment Exit Paradox. The study reconceptualises organisations as environments that inhibit, rather than support, sensemaking for marginalised workers and positions digital platforms as infrastructures that expose, rather than resolve, gendered racial inequality in the workplace. Implications of the study are discussed.
