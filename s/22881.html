Conflict Development Model in Cross-Functional Teams: Dynamic Interactional Perspective

OB
Lianghui Zheng-Ye (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Yulan Han (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Tiantian Bao
Applying a grounded theory approach, we develop a model of dynamic conflict development in cross-functional teams. We purposively sampled members of cross-functional teams to study this phenomenon. Our findings illuminate how conflicts in cross-functional teams are triggered and unfold dynamically, what parties do during conflict episodes, and why such conflicts tend to recur. Specifically, conflicts are initiated by the emergence of multiple expectation incongruences. In response, team members engage in interactional rounds to reduce these gaps, during which cognitions, emotions, and behaviors are tightly intertwined. These interactional rounds then branch into different outcomes. In addition, our model demonstrates that multiple expectation incongruences are jointly shaped by multilevel factors at the organizational, functional-departmental, and cross-functional levels. Finally, we discuss the theoretical contribution and managerial implications of our model and outline directions for future research.

Managing Team Conflicts for Performance: The Role of Paradoxical Leadership and Psychological Safety

OB
Ying Zhou (Wuhan University of Technology); Yan Zhang (Peking University); Hanyu Gao (University of Macau)
Teams often struggle to manage conflicts arising from collaboration in ways that sustain high performance, yet we know limited about how to promote cooperative and curb competitive responses to conflict. Drawing on theory of cooperation and competition, we examine whether and how paradoxical leadership shapes team conflict management and under what conditions these effects emerge. Across two multi-source and time-lagged field studies, we find that paradoxical leadership promotes team cooperative conflict management, which in turn facilitates team coordination and performance. In contrast, its negative association with team competitive conflict management materializes only when team psychological safety is high. Together, these findings enrich research on conflict management and leadership by clarifying how paradoxical leadership shapes teams’ responses to conflict.

How Do Vertical Conflicts Between Team Leaders and Members Affect Team Performance?

OB
Yuntao Bai (Xiamen University); Jiali Shi (Xiamen University); Yu Chen (Xiamen University)
While prior research on team conflict has predominantly focused on horizontal conflict among team members, relatively little is known about how vertical conflict between leaders and members influences team performance. This study examines the effects of two types of vertical conflict—task conflict and relationship conflict—on team performance, and explores the mediating role of moqi, an implicit mutual understanding between leaders and members, as well as the moderating role of leader openness. Drawing on conflict theory, we develop a moderated mediation model and test it using multi-source longitudinal survey data. The results show that task conflict between leaders and members enhances moqi, which in turn improves team performance, whereas relationship conflict undermines moqi and subsequently reduces team performance. Moreover, leader openness strengthens the positive indirect effect of task conflict on team performance via moqi, while weakening the negative indirect effect of relationship conflict. By shifting the focus from horizontal to vertical conflict, this study integrates leader-member dynamics into team conflict research and offers a more nuanced understanding of how different forms of conflict shape team effectiveness through implicit coordination processes.

Making Discussion Content Visible: Language Models and a New Frontier in Teams Research

OB
Mingze Xu (University of Washington); Michael D. Johnson (University of Washington)
How can researchers systematically analyze what teams discuss? Teams research has emphasized behavioral processes (how teams interact) over discussion content (what teams talk about), leaving the predictive value of discussion content largely untested. We introduce embedding-based topic modeling as a method for identifying topics in team conversations. In Study 1, we compare computational approaches using transcribed team discussions and find that large language model embeddings outperform traditional methods. In Study 2, we demonstrate that topic patterns predict outcomes: teams whose discussions diverged early and converged late produced more creative solutions. These findings establish discussion content as a distinct dimension of team interaction with independent predictive validity.

The Impact Mechanism of Team Intelligent Dependence on Team Resilience

OB
Yiwen Shi (Beijing Institute of Technology); Yuanyuan Liu (Taiyuan University of Technology); Pingqing Liu (Beijing Institute of Technology)
As AI becomes more involved in team work, its impact on team resilience is clearer. But most research treats AI just as a tool. It misses how AI also acts as a relational entity. From a relational perspective, this study examines how team dependence on AI reshapes interpersonal and human-AI interactions, and thereby affects team resilience. Using social interdependence theory, we built a model to show the dual effects of team dependence. We analyzed data from 582 supervisor-employee pairs. The results reveal two main points. First, relying on AI can weaken emotional bonds among team members, like friendship. This can lower the overall resilience of the team. But at the same time, dependence on AI can create a sense of partnership. It makes team members more accepting of AI teammates. This, in turn, can boost resilience. The study further finds that team-building efforts significantly matter: they can mitigate the negative relational impacts of AI dependence while strengthening positive ones, such as AI acceptance. Overall, by revealing how dependence on AI reshapes team relationships through either distancing or integration, this research clarifies two pivotal pathways to resilience, offering leaders insights into building stronger, more adaptable teams in the digital age.
