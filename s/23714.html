Promoting Inclusion for Neurodivergent Employees via Strengths-Based Leadership and HRM

DEI
Shenyang Hai (Hainan University); Tianyi Long (Charles Darwin University); Zhaoyang Xin (Jilin University); Ran Xiao (Renmin University of China); Bo Yang (Hainan University); Tengfei Guo (Guangdong Polytechnic Normal University)
Workplace inclusion is critical for fostering equity and unlocking employee potential, yet neurodivergent employees often face exclusion due to persistent stereotypes, limiting their social integration and career advancement. By integrating the strengths-based approach with Shore et al.’ s (2011) inclusion framework, our study offers a novel framework explaining the interactive effect of strengths-based leadership and inclusive human resource management (HRM) practices on neurodivergent employees’ inclusion experiences and subsequent performance outcomes. We conducted two empirical studies among neurodivergent employees based on the weekly experience sampling method (Study 1: 187 participants from the United States with 860 weekly observations; Study 2: 252 participants from China with 1,177 weekly observations) to test our model. The findings reveal that strengths-based leadership is positively associated with inclusion experiences. These inclusion experiences, in turn, enhance contribution to group performance and diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) related voice. Notably, inclusive HRM practices strengthened the effectiveness of strengths-based leadership. When neurodivergent employees perceived that their organizations implemented inclusive HRM practices, the impact of strengths-based leadership on inclusion experiences was amplified, leading to greater contribution to group performance and DEI-related voice. These findings provide new insights for understanding and managing workplace inclusion, with leadership implications.
