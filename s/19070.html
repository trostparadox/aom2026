Geopolitical Uncertainty and Critical Mineral investments: A Real Option Approach

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3
IM

People

Author: Huong Lan Hoang University College Dublin

Session overview

Securing energy transition critical minerals like lithium, cobalt, and nickel is a strategic imperative amid rising geopolitical uncertainty from sanctions, export controls, and resource nationalism. This study draws on real options theory to examine how foreign investors dynamically adjust post-entry strategies when geopolitical risk intensifies. It examines how geopolitical uncertainty shapes the exercise of real options through capital staging and investment timing. Using firm- and project-level data, the study employs panel econometric analyses with firm and country fixed effects to assess how geopolitical uncertainty affects capital expenditures and delays between project announcement and final investment decisions. The analysis further considers strategic foreign ownership, institutional distance and prior experience as moderating factors influencing option recognition and execution. The study extends real options theory by shifting attention to post-entry option exercise and exploring contingent conditions. It provides empirical evidence on how firms restructure investments in geopolitically sensitive critical mineral value chains, offering guidance for managers and policymakers seeking to balance investment stability with energy security during the global energy transition.
