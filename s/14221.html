Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI)—especially foundation models and generative AI—is rapidly reshaping products, work, and competitive dynamics. Yet strategy and management research still has limited cumulative insight into AI commercialization: how firms translate AI advances into scalable offerings and durable performance gains, and why value capture varies widely across organizations and ecosystems. This presenter symposium addresses that gap by bringing together four complementary studies that conceptualize commercialization as a problem of coordinating key complements beyond algorithms. The presentations examine: (1) cross-ecosystem differences in commercialization capacity, (2) how data regulation reshapes differentiation strategies and shifts liability along the value chain, (3) frontier talent bottlenecks that condition productivity returns from AI, and (4) how AI interacts with startup organizational structure to shape innovation and scaling. Together, the papers offer an integrated, multi-level view of AI commercialization that links technological opportunities to institutional constraints, strategic choices, and organizational design.
