CSR Spillovers to Employees’ Socially Responsible Consumption: A Double-Edged Sword

OB
Yueru Ma; Ying Zhou (Central South University); Ya Luo
Corporate social responsibility increasingly shapes employees’ workplace attitudes and social behavior. As societal expectations for corporate and individual responsibility intensify, understanding how CSR influences employees’ socially responsible consumption becomes urgent and essential. Drawing on moral self-regulation theory, this study examines the double-edged sword effect of CSR on employees’ socially responsible consumption. Study 1, a three-wave survey of 393 employees over a two-month interval, shows that CSR fosters socially responsible consumption by enhancing organizational identification, but simultaneously undermines it by encouraging moral credit accumulation. Ethical leadership moderates this process by amplifying organizational identification and weakening moral licensing, promoting socially responsible consumption. Study 2, a scenario experiment with 101 employees, further confirms the effect of moral licensing on socially responsible consumption. Collectively, these findings advance understanding of the social consequences of CSR and underscore leadership’s role in leveraging CSR to foster authentic social responsibility, while outlining directions for future research.
