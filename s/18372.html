Sustainable Career Competencies of Pre-Retirement Older Workers in Japanese HRM: P–C Fit Mediation

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Authors: Kyoko Yamazaki Meiji University; Kunio Ikari Kyushu University

Session overview

Problem: Population aging is accelerating globally, yet sustainable career research may not fully capture late-career experiences in Japan, where traditional HRM (company-led job rotation and post-retirement reemployment) shapes older employees’ career trajectories. Theory: Integrating sustainable career theory with person–environment fit research, we propose that sustainable career competencies influence late-career outcomes through person–career fit, encompassing both needs–supplies fit and demands–abilities fit. Method: We tested this mediation model using a two-wave online survey conducted in June 2025 with late-50s employees working in traditional Japanese companies (final sample N = 482), measuring the Japanese Sustainable Career Competency Scale (SCC-jp: adaptive career agency, career social resources, passive career orientation), person–career fit, and three outcomes (subjective career success, meaning of work, and future work selves). Results: Structural equation modeling with bootstrapping indicated that person–career fit significantly mediates the relationships between sustainable career competencies and all three outcomes, with particularly strong indirect effects on future work selves; moreover, passive career orientation negatively predicts person–career fit. Contribution: This study extends sustainable career research by identifying person–career fit as a key mechanism for sustainable late-career development in Japan’s institutional context and by highlighting the importance of strengthening adaptive agency and social resources while reducing passivity to promote sustainable careers among older employees.
