Session overview

Management and organization theories have traditionally relied on empirical data as traces of real-world action. Recent advances in artificial intelligence challenge this foundation as synthetic data—generated through simulations, rule-based systems, and large language models—become increasingly central to both research and organizational practice. As synthetic data evolve from static datasets to interactive, agent-based simulacra that mimic organizational behavior, fundamental questions arise about what management theories explain and how they are built. This panel examines the implications of synthetic data for theory building from two interrelated perspectives. One focuses on scholars’ use of synthetic data as a research instrument, raising concerns about inference, validity, and representation when data are generated rather than observed. The other treats synthetic data as an organizational phenomenon, examining how firms’ reliance on generated data reshapes learning, strategy, and market dynamics. Together, the panel highlights a recursive relationship between research and practice and explores how theory building may be reconfigured in a world of generated data.
