Heroes With Depth: Role Models for Sustainable Careers Among Early Career Individuals

CAR
Catinca Roth (Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences); Veronika Kneip (Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences)
Contemporary careers are increasingly dynamic, uncertain, and non-linear, placing growing demands on individuals to proactively manage their development from an early career stage. While role models are known to shape career aspirations and motivation, their role within sustainable career development remains underexplored. Drawing on the sustainable career framework, this qualitative study examines how role models support early career individuals in navigating person–career fit, proactivity, and adaptability over time. We analyze 222 narrative reports from first-semester business administration students in Germany using abductive thematic analysis. The findings reveal three global themes capturing how students (1) identify with role models, (2) learn from them, and (3) value specific personal qualities, particularly in the context of challenge and crisis. Integrating these themes, we develop an integrated framework that conceptualizes role models as mirrors, catalysts, and simulators of sustainable career development. Role models function as mirrors by enabling reflection on values, identity, and person–career fit; as catalysts by fostering motivation, agency, and self-directed goal pursuit; and as simulators by preparing individuals for non-linear career trajectories and critical life events. By embedding role models within the sustainable career framework, this study advances theory on early career development and positions role models as a central social resource for building sustainable careers.
