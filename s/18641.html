The Effects of Newcomer Moral Identity Symbolization on Coworker Behavior

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Nan Wang Lingnan University

Session overview

To successfully adjust to a new work environment, a newcomer often needs to elicit a positive perception of himself or herself from coworkers to gain their support. Advances in person perception theory have suggested that morality is a key aspect of people’s perceptions of others when forming initial impressions. This study investigates coworkers’ perceptions of and reactions to newcomers’ moral identity symbolization, a process in which individuals publicly present their morality and ethical values to others at the workplace. This study builds and tests a model that delineates the mechanisms underlying the potentially mixed effects of newcomer moral identity symbolization and examines contingent factors that might enhance the positive effect or mitigate the negative effect of newcomer moral identity symbolization. The model was tested using dyadic data collected from newcomers and their coworkers, and the findings largely support the proposed model. This study offers a fine-grained understanding of the mechanisms and contextual factors through which newcomer moral identity symbolization influences coworker behavior.
