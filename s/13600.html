Session overview

Co-founder breakups are among the most consequential yet least understood events in the entrepreneurial journey. While most successful ventures are founded by teams, many ventures dissolve due to tensions between founders, even though their product or service would have great chances of success. The psychological factors leading to co-founder breakup are still unresolved. In this Professional Development Workshop (PDW), we pursue the following aims: First, we want to better understand the underlying psychology leading to co-founder breakups, by bringing together experienced scholars and their longstanding expertise on the psychological foundations on team interactions and dynamics in new venture teams. Second, this PDW seeks to generate a research agenda that focuses on the psychological reasons of co-founder breakup and the study of possible antidotes. Third, since co-founder breakup is a complex and multidisciplinary phenomenon, we want to foster networking across different management disciplines. The 90-minute session will include an overview of current research about different aspects of co-founder breakups and emerging perspectives on dynamics in new venture teams, a discussion on knowledge gaps, an interactive world café format for exchange, a concluding lightning round from the research discussions, a synthesis around findings from the PDW, and an agenda for future research. This PDW seeks to advance our understanding and future research on why founders fall apart, and how to predict and prevent it. It invites ENT, OB, MOC, and OMT scholars to collaboratively chart a research agenda and build a cross-disciplinary community focused on the psychology of co-founder breakups.
