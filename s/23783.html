Earning More Than Pay: How Performance-Based Rewards Reconfigure Employees’ Developmental Behaviors

HR
Han Zhang
Moving beyond the dominant focus on immediate effort and performance outcomes, this study examines how pay for performance practices contribute to employees’ long-term capability development. Drawing on conservation of resources theory, we argue that pay for performance practices influence employees’ resource investment behaviors, thereby fostering sustained developmental outcomes. Using survey data from 208 employees, we find that pay for performance practices foster employees’ personal growth initiative (i.e., self-growth engagement), which subsequently facilitate human capital development (e.g., learning and skill acquisition) and social capital development (e.g., network building). By identifying a developmental pathway through which pay for performance practices influence employees’ accumulation of human and social capital, this study extends the pay for performance literature from short-term performance enhancement to long-term capability development. Our findings highlight the role of incentive systems in shaping employees’ developmental trajectories and provide a resource-based explanation for the enduring effects of pay for performance practices.
