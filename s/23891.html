When Gratitude Comes Too Soon: Employees’ Dual Responses to Leader Anticipatory Gratitude Expression

OB
Yinxu Zhou (Renmin University of China); Xin Liu (Renmin University of China)
While extant research increasingly highlights the positive effects of leader gratitude expression on employees, these studies have predominantly focused on leader reactive gratitude expression—that is, acknowledging employees’ past contributions. In contrast, the impacts of leader anticipatory gratitude expression, a common phenomenon involving expressing appreciation for employees’ future work, remains largely unexplored. Drawing upon social exchange theory and self-determination theory, we argue that compared to the reciprocity nature of leader reactive gratitude expression, leader anticipatory gratitude expression may evoke either a sense of obligation to reciprocate leader or controlled motivation in employees, thereby exhibiting dual effects in facilitating or impairing subsequent task performance and proactive behavior. Furthermore, employees’ perceptions of their leaders’ level of narcissism (i.e., leader narcissism reputation) shape their divergent reactions to leader anticipatory gratitude expression. When leaders have a lower narcissism reputation, anticipatory gratitude expression tends to foster greater employee felt obligation to reciprocate leader, thereby improving employee task performance. Nevertheless, leader anticipatory gratitude expression is more likely to trigger employee controlled motivation when leader narcissism reputation is higher, which subsequently suppresses employee proactive behavior.
