Session overview

This study examines how Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) initiatives can promote financial inclusion among Base of the Pyramid (BOP) entrepreneurs in Bolivia, with particular attention to street vendors. It investigates the role of financial institutions in fostering inclusive practices, entrepreneurs’ perceptions of CSR programs, and the sustainability of these initiatives within the local socio-economic context. The research adopts a qualitative design, currently in progress, combining semi-structured interviews with financial institutions and surveys with BOP entrepreneurs to capture diverse perspectives. Guided by Shared Value Theory, the analysis positions CSR as a mechanism that aligns corporate objectives with societal well-being, emphasizing its potential to expand access to credit, savings, and financial literacy. By addressing the knowledge gap on financial literacy among vulnerable groups, the study seeks to generate practical insights for policymakers and practitioners. Ultimately, it highlights CSR’s capacity to reduce structural inequalities, empower entrepreneurs, and foster inclusive growth in emerging economies.
