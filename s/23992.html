Status Tournaments and Digital Transformation Following Rivals’ State-Sponsored Awards in China

STR
Yapu Zhao; Qian Xu (College of Science & Technology Ningbo University)
Studies of state-sponsored awards report mixed spillover effects on non-winners. Integrating the inter-organizational spillover literature with social-comparison and status-threat perspectives, we argue that relative status determines how rivals respond: when a lower-status competitor receives a prestigious award, higher-status firms experience a status threat that drives them to launch digital-transformation initiatives to restore their competitive standing. Using data from China’s Manufacturing Single Champion (MSC) program and a sample of listed firms (2016-2022), we find that MSC designations spur digital transformation in higher-status non-winners. This effect is strengthened when the status gap is larger, the focal firm is state-owned, or led by a founder-CEO—factors that amplify perceived threat. By identifying status threat as a key mechanism and clarifying its boundary conditions, we shift the focus from whether awards affect rivals to when and why they catalyze strategic action. We further find that such transformation correlates with diminished post-award digital innovation performance, suggesting that status reclamation may come at the expense of digital innovation efficiency.
