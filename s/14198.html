Organizing Diversity: A Typology of DEI Strategy in S&P 500 Firms

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Quentin T Baldwin; Ivan Hernandez; Amal Chekili; Joseph Simpson Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University; A.K. Ward Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University

Session overview

Organizations face institutional pressure to demonstrate commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI), yet firms vary widely in how this commitment is organized and articulated. Scholars often treat DEI as a unidimensional construct, measured through additive indices, disclosure scores, or isolated practices. Such measures obscure heterogeneity in how organizations structure DEI as a strategy. As a result, we lack an accurate understanding of the distinct organizing logics firms use to configure DEI in ways that may shape intended outcomes. We address this gap by developing an empirically grounded typology of DEI strategies among S&P 500 firms. Using DEI-related language extracted from Form 10-K filings between 2014 and 2024, we apply hierarchical topic modeling and firm-level clustering to identify recurring configurations of DEI discourse embedded in mandatory regulatory disclosures. This approach captures how firms formally organize DEI across governance, culture, metrics, programs, and external engagement, rather than focusing on individual practices or the intensity of commitment. Our analysis reveals a hierarchical structure of DEI topics, with limited differentiation at the highest level and meaningful heterogeneity emerging at an intermediate level. Based on firm-level clustering of topic profiles, we identify five distinct DEI strategy types: Culture-Centric, Governance-Embedded, Metrics-Driven Compliance, Programmatic / ERG-Centered, and Externally Oriented. These strategy types reflect qualitatively different organizing logics rather than differences in effectiveness or moral quality. By mapping how large firms structure DEI strategy in practice, this study provides a descriptive typology that offers theoretical infrastructure for DEI scholarship concerned with organizational systems, power, and inequality.
