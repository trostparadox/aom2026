Research in Action: Understanding the Barriers Hindering Action Research Adoption

RM
Miriana Ferrara (University of Salerno); Nicola Capolupo (San Raffaele Roma University); Maria Vincenza Ciasullo (University of Salerno)
The call for real impact research has established in the current academic landscape. In this context, Action Research (AR) emerges as a promising methodological approach able to combine the scientific approach with practical intervention. Nevertheless, it is still marginal in managerial and organizational studies. Therefore, the study aims to explore the barriers that neglect AR adoption among academic researchers. By employing a mixed-method approach, both quantitative and qualitative data were analyzed on 52 valid responses. Particularly, a correlation analysis was conducted to understand how diverse barriers in nature actually move together, and a thematic analysis was performed to investigate the factors underlying each barrier. The results reveal that, among relational, methodological, career and structural barriers, relational and structural barriers are correlated more than other barriers. Moreover, the analysis highlights the themes influencing the researchers’ perceptions for each barrier. This research contributes to the literature on AR by redefining the problem of methodological legitimacy and discussing implications for researchers and institutions oriented toward research of impact.

AI-Human Interaction for Abductive Qualitative Data Analysis at Scale: A Delphi Inspired Workflow

RM
Mehdi Montakhabi (University of Oxford); Benn Lawson (University of Oxford); Jens Roehrich (University of Bath); Brian Squire (University of Bristol); Michael John Gill (University of Oxford)
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) offers new opportunities for qualitative data analysis, particularly, when scholars work with large volumes of unstructured data. Prior work demonstrates how these tools can support discrete analytic tasks like coding and preliminary pattern detection, yet management scholars have limited methodological guidance on how to integrate GenAI into abductive, theory-developing analysis in ways that preserve interpretative rigor and epistemic accountability. We address this gap by advancing a “centaur workflow”, a structured protocol for AI-human interaction that supports abductive reasoning, while maintaining research oversight at critical interpretive junctures. Drawing on Delphi method principles of iterative expert judgement, reflexive evaluation, and progressive convergence of interpretations, we specify when and how GenAI outputs should be solicited, evaluated, challenged, and documented across phases of qualitative analysis. We illustrate the workflow using more than 135 hours of interviews with founders and executives in the space economy sector, showcasing both productive uses and failure modes associated with GenAI supported analysis. We then propose evaluative heuristics to support transparent reporting and assessment by authors, reviewers, and editors. Our contribution advances qualitative data analysis, with implications for how established standards of authenticity, plausibility, and criticality (Golden-Biddle and Locke, 1997) can be evidenced when analysis is mediated by structured AI and human interaction.

Fighting Theoretical Fragmentation: The Case for the Extended Case Method

RM
Erwin Danneels (University of South Florida)
Management research has become increasingly theoretically sophisticated yet simultaneously fragmented, marked by conceptual proliferation, siloed conversations, and limited cumulative development. This makes systematic efforts to refine, integrate, and reconstruct existing theory especially important. I argue that the Extended Case Method (ECM) offers a fundamentally different logic of inquiry that is particularly suited to this purpose. ECM uses empirical cases to interrogate, extend, integrate, and reconstruct existing theoretical frameworks. Its distinctive contribution lies in its recursive combination of deduction, induction, and abduction, with empirical anomalies serving as the primary drivers of theoretical advancement. I show how ECM differs from dominant qualitative and deductive approaches in its goals, role of prior theory, inferential logic, and epistemological commitments. I outline practical guidance for conducting ECM studies in management research. By offering a systematic methodology for theory elaboration rather than theory generation, ECM provides a powerful alternative for advancing coherence, integration, and cumulative theory development in mature and fragmented research domains.

“A Lifestyle of Building Relationships”: How Scholars Unlock the Potential of Engaged Research

RM
Lauren Walter (University of Victoria); Jialin Zhong (University of Victoria); Sara R. S. T. A. Elias (University of Victoria); Natalie Slawinski (University of Victoria)
Engaged research has been gaining prominence as scholars seek to address mounting sustainability crises by co-creating practically useful and academically important knowledge in partnership with non-academics. However, this type of scholarship involves many unique challenges, especially regarding the complex relational dynamics between the academic and non-academic partners. Prior work identifies these relational complexities but offers limited actionable guidance on how to navigate them. This gap in the engaged research literature may prevent scholars from undertaking engaged research, thus stymieing the potential of this approach to bridge the theory-practice gap and contribute to addressing global problems. We conducted an inductive qualitative study with 12 engaged researchers to learn from their experiences and identify the strategies that they have used to effectively navigate relational dynamics in engaged research. Our thematic analysis of the semi-structured interviews generated a refined definition of the engaged research concept and identified strategies that scholars use to engage in meaningful relationships with non-academics in engaged research. We define engaged research as a collaborative form of inquiry in which academics and non-academics co-create an advanced understanding of theory and/or phenomena in a reciprocal relationship. We also induced evidence-based, concrete strategies that scholars use across four overlapping stages of engaged research relationship development: finding partners, building relationships, maintaining relationships, and sustaining relationships after project completion. By clarifying engaged research’s boundary conditions and translating the relational principles of co-creation and reciprocity into practical strategies, this study makes engaged research more actionable, supporting more impactful collaborations between academics and non-academics.

Administrative Sciences in the Age of Advanced AI: Rethinking the Quantitative–Qualitative Frontier

RM
Adina Dabu (University of Bucharest); Paul Dragos Aligica (George Mason University)
This paper explores the shifts induced by the rise of advanced AI in Administrative Sciences research, with a focus on the epistemic assumptions and methodological perspectives associated to these changes. It argues that the evolving frontier between AI and human research capabilities is reshaping methodological hierarchies between quantitative and qualitative approaches, while relocating the comparative advantage of human intelligence toward interpretive and action-guiding inquiry. These changes, however, must be situated within the broader twentieth-century epistemic context in which they unfold and whose significance they revalorize. Specifically, the paper revisits: (1) Herbert Simon’s conception of Administrative Sciences as “sciences of the artificial,” grounded in design-oriented modes of theorizing distinct from the natural sciences; (2) the discovery–verification debate, with its implicit hierarchies of methods and research designs; and (3) the debate between theoretical and practical rationality and its influence on research approaches in the field. The advent of AI challenges and reconfigures the epistemic perspectives that emerged from this foundation, casting them in a new light. The paper advances a case for methodological pluralism through cross-methodological adjudication, while emphasizing the renewed prominence of qualitative inquiry. Case studies, grounded theory, and abductive reasoning illustrate the residual comparative advantage of human intelligence within an evolving division of labor with AI, pointing toward hybrid research designs and new methodological frontiers.
