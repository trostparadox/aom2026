Overqualification and Discretionary Behavior: A Moderated Mediation via Moral Disengagement

OB
Yaoyu Wang (Kyung Hee University)
Drawing on moral disengagement theory (Bandura, 1986), this study examines how perceived overqualification shapes employees' discretionary work behaviors through moral disengagement, and when this indirect process depends on moral identity. Using two-wave survey data from 353 full-time employees in China with a two-week lag, we assessed perceived overqualification, moral disengagement, and moral identity at Time 1, and measured organizational citizenship behavior and withdrawal behavior at Time 2. Results reveal that perceived overqualification increases moral disengagement, which in turn reduces organizational citizenship behavior and increases withdrawal behavior. Furthermore, moral identity moderates these indirect effects: employees with stronger moral identity are less susceptible to moral disengagement under perceived overqualification, thereby maintaining higher organizational citizenship behavior and lower withdrawal behavior. These findings highlight a moral-cognitive pathway through which overqualification translates into reduced prosocial contributions and heightened disengagement, and identify moral identity as a critical protective factor that buffers employees from the negative consequences of perceived overqualification.
