Digital Governance By Bits: Algorithmic Rigidity, Maladaptation Costs, and Firm Boundaries

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5
TIM

People

Author: Chun Chun Hsieh National Taiwan University

Session overview

Firm boundaries are central to strategy, yet classic transaction cost economics (TCE) typically takes enforcement technologies as given. Digital governance by bits (DGB), including blockchain-based smart contracts and distributed ledgers, endogenizes safeguards through algorithmic enforcement, enabling “virtual internalization” of governance without asset ownership. We argue that DGB reshapes the governance trade-off: algorithmic rigidity can curb opportunism but generates maladaptation and oracle costs when deterministic code confronts volatility and when performance is not digitally verifiable. Boundary decisions thus entail a three-way comparison among markets, hierarchies, and algorithmic governance. Integrating TCE with institutional theory, we develop a contingency model predicting that transaction uncertainty weakens DGB’s boundary benefits by amplifying maladaptation costs, whereas institutional weakness increases DGB’s value as a private substitute for public enforcement. We further theorize governance capability—the ability to design and reconfigure digital and organizational safeguards—as a key moderator shaping these boundary implications. The study foregrounds maladaptation and oracle frictions in algorithmic governance and positions governance capability and virtual internalization as central mechanisms in digitally mediated firm boundaries.
