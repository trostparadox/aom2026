Session overview

Humor is ubiquitous in organizational life, yet it remains under-researched in management studies despite evidence linking humor to outcomes such as leader–member exchange, engagement, job satisfaction, and organizational citizenship behavior. Key gaps stem from conceptual and methodological limitations. There is little consensus on how organizational humor should be conceptualized, defined, and measured. Prior work often imports intrapersonal humor conceptualizations into interpersonal and organizational contexts, and widely used humor style measures can blur humor behaviors with their assumed outcomes. Theory gaps remain as well. Conventional theories may miss humor’s dynamic nature, and effects depend on context and cultural norms that shape appropriateness. We organize this panel symposium to bring together experts to take stock of where humor research stands, identify what is missing, and map the next agenda. We aim to broaden exposure to humor research, advance methodological discussion in humor research, and spur theory development that captures humor’s dynamic and contextual role at work.
