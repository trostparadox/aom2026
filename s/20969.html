Reaching for the Steady Hand: Examining the Antecedents of New CEO Age

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Authors: Zheng Cheng University of Northern Iowa; Vincent L. Barker University of Kansas; Juan Carlos Morales The University of Akron

Session overview

When do boards of directors turn to older CEO candidates in the succession process? While strategic leadership research has traditionally found that older CEOs are more risk averse, an older CEO is also more likely to be steady and conscientious. Drawing on resource dependence theory and insights from the psychology literature, we examine new CEO age from a selection perspective investigating the conditions under which firms are more likely to appoint an older or younger executive as their new CEO. We argue that boards are more likely to select older new CEOs when (1) the previous CEO exited for non-routine reasons (removed for poor firm performance, removed for ethical or legal misconduct, or exited for CEO health-related issues) or (2) the firm exists in a hostile, difficult industry. We test these predictions using two large samples of CEO successions among S&P 1500 firms. The full sample includes 5,458 successions from 1992–2022, merged from multiple archival sources capturing succession circumstances, CEO characteristics, and firm financials. For analyses requiring board characteristics, we match these data to a board database, yielding a second sample of 3,814 successions from 1999–2022. Across both samples, non-routine predecessor exits are associated with significantly older successor CEOs, and the results are robust to alternative specifications. Overall, the findings position successor CEO age as an outcome of board adaptation under uncertainty and highlight the endogeneity of CEO characteristics in strategic leadership research.
