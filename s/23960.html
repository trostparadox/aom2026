CEO Regulatory Focus and Organizational Learning from Crises: A Configurational Perspective

STR
Frida Treichel (TU Dortmund University); Lorenz Graf-Vlachy (Friedrich-Alexander University of Erlangen-Nuremberg); Linda Schaedler
How and under which circumstances does a CEO’s regulatory focus shape organizational learning from crisis? We employ fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to examine how configurations of six conditions across three analytical levels—CEO regulatory focus, alignment conditions, and contextual conditions—enable organizational learning following crises. Drawing on a quasi-comprehensive sample of severe product-harm crises in U.S. pharmaceutical and medical-device firms between 2008 and 2018, we identify three distinct configurational recipes of success for organizational learning. Our findings show that organizational learning does not hinge on any single condition. Instead, learning emerges from alternative combinations of conditions spanning CEO regulatory focus, regulatory focus alignment among key crisis stakeholders, and the external context. Across these levels, both the presence and absence of specific conditions can facilitate learning, depending on how they are jointly combined. Neither CEO regulatory focus, alignment conditions, nor external conditions are inherently beneficial or detrimental in isolation; rather, their effects unfold configurationally. By foregrounding equifinality and the role of both presence and absence conditions, this study advances research on crisis management and strategic leadership and helps explain why otherwise similar crises can lead to fundamentally different learning outcomes.
