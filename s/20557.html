Session overview

Philanthropic organizations profoundly shape organizational fields from education to healthcare to climate policy, yet they operate under conditions that defy core assumptions of organization theory. They function outside traditional market mechansims, without democratic mandates, and often without clear performance outcomes. Despite, or perhaps because of, these distinctive features, philanthropic organizations increasingly determine which social issues receive attention, which solutions gain legitimacy, and which actors accumulate resources and authority. However, existing research has focused primarily on individual donor motivations, leaving the organizational dimensions of philanthropy fragmented across disciplines. That is, we lack systematic understanding of how philanthropic organizations actually make decisions, how field-level arrangements emerge, and how these actors position themselves relative to states and civil society. This symposium addresses this gap by integrating two perspectives that have developed in parallel but remain disconnected: philanthropy as activity (examining resource exchange between donors and beneficiaries) and philanthropy as organizing paradigm (examining philanthropic organizations as distinctive organizational types and field-structuring actors). Through five cutting-edge studies examining different levels of philanthropic organizing, from grantmaking decisions to temporal reproduction of elite power to field emergence, we demonstrate how philanthropic organizing offers a productive site for advancing fundamental questions in organization theory.
