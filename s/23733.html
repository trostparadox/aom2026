Gendered Entrepreneurial Resilience and Legal Pluralism: Evidence From Female Businesses in Nigeria

ENT
Tolu Olarewaju (Keele University); Jagannadha Pawan Tamvada (Kingston Business School at Kingston University London); Temitayo Olarewaju (The University of British Columbia)
This study investigates how female-led businesses in Nigeria experience and respond to economic, community, household, and price shocks. It explores how institutional context shapes gendered entrepreneurial resilience, specifically in the presence of patriarchal interpretations within plural legal systems, and examines whether capital stock and formalization through business registration mitigate the negative effects of internal and external shocks on profitability. Using nationally representative data from the 2018–2019 Nigeria Living Standards Survey (NLSS), this study analyses 6,359 non-farm enterprises (NFEs) employing hierarchical regression models regression and logistic techniques in Stata. Interaction effects are used to assess the moderating roles of registration and capital stock under different categories of shocks. Results show that female-led firms experience significantly lower profits during periods of economic and household crises, particularly in regions governed by plural legal systems containing patriarchal interpretations within plural legal systems. However, formal business registration and higher capital stock mitigate these negative impacts, with higher capital also reducing the adverse effects of community shocks. This indicates that institutional and resource-based resilience mechanisms are critical for female entrepreneurs. Overall, the findings highlight how formalisation and resource endowments act as gendered institutional insulation in restrictive environments, positioning religious institutions as active forces shaping entrepreneurial outcomes. The study contributes to entrepreneurship and institutional theory by highlighting how legal pluralism, gender norms, and resource endowments intersect to shape entrepreneurial resilience in developing economies. It underscores the need for contextually embedded policies that strengthen the resilience of businesses in legally plural environments and under conditions of internal and external shocks.
