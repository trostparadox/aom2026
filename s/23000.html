Globalization or Deglobalization? Leveraging Firm Data to Examine Global Strategic Reconfiguration

IM
Wei Lin (University of St. Gallen); Xiaoxu Zhang (University of St. Gallen); Winfried Ruigrok (University of St. Gallen)
The past few years have witnessed a plethora of scholars and practitioners embracing the concept of deglobalization. The prevailing deglobalization narrative, while compelling, suffers from three critical limitations: an over-reliance on macro-level trends for inferences, overlooking firm-level business realities, the use of single, partial metrics rather than a multi-dimensional evaluation, and a lack of longitudinal, cross-country comparative analysis. This study addresses these gaps by examining deglobalization through the lens of corporate globalization. We analyze the degree and geographic scope of market- and asset-internationalization for the largest 100 firms in 16 major economies from 2001 to 2022. By employing equal-weighted and value-weighed measures, we bridge firm- and macro-level perspectives. Our findings shed new light on the deglobalization thesis. We find a general upward trend in firms’ degrees of internationalization and a growing share of foreign revenues generated outside firms’ home regions. Concurrently, we identify that firms are generating higher foreign sales from a smaller base of foreign assets, indicating a shift towards leaner models of international operations. Instead of a straightforward pattern of deglobalization, our evidence points at a complex evolution in how firms orchestrate their global presence.

Tackling Different Geopolitical Risks:A Literature Review and Agenda for Future Research

IM
Jie Wang (Renmin University of China); Hai Guo (Renmin University of China); Pengcheng Ma (Renmin University of China); Lin Cui (The Australian National University)
In an era of escalating global instability, firms face growing challenges in addressing geopolitical risks (GPRs). Although the number of studies on GPRs has been increasing, the literature still lacks an integrated understanding of how firms respond to them. This literature review aims to systematically synthesize existing research, identify key research gaps, and provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of knowledge on firms’ responses to GPRs. In this review article, we systematically examine research on GPRs conducted between 2005 and 2025. We also develop a two-dimensional framework, which is based on directness and temporality to categorize GPRs into four distinct types, thereby providing a structured lens for analysis. Furthermore, drawing on existing literature, we propose targeted response strategies for each type of GPRs. By doing so, we expect to contribute to academic research in this field while offering actionable guidance for firms to navigate and balance geopolitical risks effectively.

Knowledge Flows Between U.S. and China in the Era of Rising Geopolitical Tension

IM
Lin Deng; Tailan Chi (University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee); Ting Yao (University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee)
Geopolitical tensions can reshape firms’ access to foreign knowledge even when underlying technical information remains codified and publicly visible. We examine how the July 2018 escalation in U.S.–China technology restrictions altered bilateral knowledge linkages and how firms’ pre-shock knowledge recombination patterns conditioned their responses. Using USPTO patents and applicant-added citations, we construct a firm–partner–month panel (2016– 2020) and implement a partner-country difference-in-differences design that compares China to seven innovation-intensive partners. We find that firms on both sides reduce citations to patents originating in the other country after July 2018, indicating a rapid reweighting of cross-border codified knowledge use. In contrast, U.S.–China co-invention does not decline in the short run, consistent with higher exit and switching costs in collaborative R&D. We further show that search configurations shape the adjustment of citation behavior. These findings extend the search-based view of innovation by showing how the structure of firms’ recombination strategies interacts with institutional shocks to reconfigure global knowledge linkages.

Deglobalisation or Reglobalisation? A Macroperspective on the Re-Emergence of the Global Economy

IM
Pratik Arte (Stockholm School of Economics)
Recent geopolitical and economic shocks have shaken the foundation of globalisation. The deglobalisation hypothesis is gaining traction in response to these shocks. This article attempts to debunk this hypothesis by proposing a counterbalancing reglobalisation hypothesis. Rather than representing a decline in globalisation, the global economy is transforming towards a more strategically realigned system. Through an analysis of trends in trade flows, foreign direct investment, and migration, this article argues that the global economy is undergoing a structural adjustment, marked by increasing regionalisation and technological advancement. The article identifies a shift in the global economic order and a transfer of political power from the West to the East driven by political realignment, relocation of trade and investment, restrategising, and remigration. It concludes that reglobalisation offers a more comprehensive framework for understanding regional transformations and their role in shaping the future of globalisation. Recommendations for future research are offered.
