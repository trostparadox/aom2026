When Jokes Bite: Defensive Silence as a Mechanism Linking Aggressive Leader Humor and Creativity

Authors: Darija Jablan Koljevic University of Montenegro; Stefan Jovetic University of Montenegro, Faculty of Economics; Edina Radoncic University of Montenegro; Aleksandra Jovanovic University of Montenegro; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro

Session overview

Humor is frequently positioned as a relational leadership tool, yet emerging evidence suggests that humor is a double-edged sword when it takes an aggressive form. Integrating social information processing theory with the employee silence literature, this study argues that aggressive leader humor encourages employees’ defensive silence which in turn undermines creativity. We further propose leaders’ performance pressure serves as a contextual boundary condition, such that higher pressure weakens the association between aggressive humor and defensive silence. These ideas were tested using a multi-source, time-separated dyadic field design in Montenegro. Employees reported aggressive humor displayed by their focal leader and defensive silence, while leaders reported performance pressure and rated employee creativity (N = 340 matched leader–employee dyads). Data were analyzed using OLS path models with bootstrapped indirect effects estimated using Hayes’ PROCESS macro (Model 7). Results show that aggressive leader humor is positively related to defensive silence, that this relationship is weaker under higher leader performance pressure, and that defensive silence is negatively related to creativity, partially mediating the humor–creativity link. The findings highlight a silence-based pathway through which aggressive leader humor can impair creativity and underscore the importance of contextual factors in shaping its effects.
