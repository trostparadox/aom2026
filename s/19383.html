When Doing Good Backfires: The Double Bind Of CSR Practice Positioning and Activist Targeting

Authors: Shuang Xie USI Lugano, Switzerland; Emanuele Luca Maria Bettinazzi Università della Svizzera Italiana

Session overview

Why do some firms become activist targets while others escape scrutiny? This study examines how activists interpret firms’ corporate social responsibility (CSR) positioning strategies—specifically, the coherence and distinctiveness of their sustainability practices—and how industry-level issue salience conditions these effects. Building on social evaluation and frame resonance theory, we conceptualize activists as analytical evaluators who seek vulnerable yet high-leverage targets. We argue that intra-firm coherence in CSR practices functions as a credibility shield, reducing targeting likelihood (H1), while inter-firm distinctiveness enhances precedent leverage, increasing selection risk (H2). Moreover, issue salience amplifies these interpretive effects (H3). We test these predictions using a global firm–SDG–year panel covering 7,163 publicly listed firms worldwide across 65 sectors (2015–2023), combining NLP-processed CSR reports from the GOLDEN dataset, SIGWATCH activist events, and Compustat fundamentals. OLS models with firm fixed effects support the first two hypotheses, while the interaction effects operate asymmetrically: coherence becomes more protective when an SDG is salient, but distinctiveness loses contrast and leverage under saturation. The study contributes to research on private politics and CSR strategy by unpacking how activists’ interpretive judgments link firms’ disclosure structures to field-level contention dynamics.
