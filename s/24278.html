Dynamic Fit of Strategic HRM Configuration Systems: A Tale of Two Firms

ODC
Pei Ma (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); Mingchao Zhang (Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
This study examines how founder-imprinted strategic HRM configuration systems evolve to achieve dynamic fit in shifting environments. Adopting a longitudinal comparative case study design, we draw on 29 interviews and archival data from two founder-controlled firms in China’s electric bicycle industry and trace the evolution of strategic HRM configurations across three industry phases through within-case and cross-case analysis. We develop an evolutionary model showing that dynamic fit under persistent imprinting emerges from the interaction between configurational openness and configurational tightness in strategic HRM configuration systems: founding-period imprinting encodes these structural properties, which subsequently condition the scope of managerial agency and the strength of configurational inertia as firms confront environmental shifts. The study advances strategic HRM configuration research by showing that configurational equifinality is bounded in dynamic environments because configuration content structures the feasibility of action over time, contributes to imprinting research by theorizing imprinting as a structural process through which founding interpretations become encoded into openness and tightness that preconfigure later possibilities for agency and inertia, and extends organizational change research by conceptualizing dynamic fit through a stability–change duality lens as tension-managed, evolutionary iteration in which firms preserve constructive tensions and engage in phased restructuring to enable gradual adaptation under shifting competitive demands.
