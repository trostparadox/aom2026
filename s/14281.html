Understanding the Influence of Public Concern on Technology Producers and Adopters

Discussant: Batia Mishan Wiesenfeld New York University

Session overview

Novel technologies hold promise for bringing about a brighter future, but as potential harms are articulated, the public may start to raise concerns. Scholars have begun characterizing the strategies through which technology producers attempt to morally legitimate their emerging technologies amidst this public concern. Much of this research delineates framing strategies in which rhetoric and discourse are used to direct attention to future benefits of the technology or to make the technology appear safer, but research has also pointed to the potential for technology producers, and adopters, to respond more tangibly, by adapting the technologies themselves, creating new technological offerings, or enacting regulatory standards to address public concern. Though scholars have identified these strategies (framing and tangible) for technology producers to chase moral legitimacy, there is opportunity to understand when and why technology producers choose a given response strategy or combine them. In this symposium, we chip away at this question with a specific focus on tangible strategies and explore how technology producers and users navigate evolving moral plurality, determine what constitutes morally legitimate technology and technology regulations, and build technology they consider moral.
