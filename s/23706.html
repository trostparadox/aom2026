DEI Perspectives on Institutional Voids and Network Deficits in SDVOSB Resource Acquisition

DEI
Yanli Chen (American Public University System)
Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Businesses (SDVOSBs) are important to our economy. These businesses make over $1 trillion in annual revenue. Yet they face real barriers to getting the money and support they need. This paper explains why these barriers exist. We look at three main causes: First, systems are missing that should help veteran businesses (banks don't understand military backgrounds); second, veteran networks get broken when people leave the military (they lose their professional contacts); and third, women and minority veterans face extra problems on top of these barriers. We studied 2,222 military entrepreneurs. We looked at bank lending data. We reviewed federal contracting records from 2024 to 2025. We talked to business owners about what works. Here is what we found. Women veterans get rejected for loans 51% of the time. Male veterans get rejected 36% of the time. Non-veterans only get rejected 18% of the time. Black veteran business owners make 78% less money than white veteran business owners. They also have much higher disability rates (92% vs. 78%). The federal government spends $32.8 billion with SDVOSBs each year. But the government does not track whether women, minorities, and disabled veterans actually get their fair share. This is a major gap. We propose three solutions. First, create programs that help veterans build networks (especially programs designed for women and minority veterans). Second, hire competent mentors who understand both military culture and the civilian business world. Third, change federal buying rules to track what happens with different groups of veterans. If we do these things right, we can reduce the problems experienced entrepreneurs face by 20–30%. This matters because it means more businesses will survive. Owners will make more money. The entire economy will be stronger. We also want to say something bigger: the research world needs to care about veterans. Veterans are a diverse group. They face real problems. But nobody is studying them. This paper starts to fix that.
