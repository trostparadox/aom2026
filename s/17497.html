Session overview

GenAI has surged in capability and popularity, promising to lower costs while increasing productivity in organizational processes. Integrating AI is increasingly viewed as a source of competitive advantage (Braesemann, 2025). Yet many companies struggle to move from pilots to measurable performance gains. Simply introducing AI in some processes rarely raises firm-level outcomes on its own, often due to organizational barriers (Vendraminelli et al., 2025) or limited know-how for integration (Kowalkiewicz, 2024). Research interest has risen accordingly. Studies show that employees and organizations can realize meaningful productivity and quality improvements. In particular, randomized and controlled roll-outs of generative AI report sizable productivity gains, especially for less-experienced workers, with quality maintained or improved (Brynjolfsson, Li, & Raymond, 2025). These effects are heterogeneous across tasks and users, underscoring the need for careful design and measurement (Brynjolfsson, Li, & Raymond, 2025). However, much of the current evidence still relies on surveys or small experiments; we lack reliable causal estimates that link AI adoption to organizational performance at scale. Field experiments can close this investment-to-impact gap by revealing what works and under which conditions. This PDW shares methodological best practices for designing such studies – covering research design, outcome, and variable selection choices – and exchanges practical insights on collaborating with firms, including data management and agreements. We also identify common pitfalls in research design and corporate implementation and how to overcome them. Finally, the PDW fosters connections among junior and senior scholars, method experts, and practitioners and surfaces the unique challenges of conducting AI field experiments.
