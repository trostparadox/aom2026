Born to Be Sustainable Entrepreneurs! But What Drives Their Entrepreneurial Intentions?

ENT
Amal Ben Cheikh (Higher institute of management, Tunis); Abir Mrabet
This study investigates how engineering students’ perceptions including AI performance and effort expectancy, perceived entrepreneurial desirability and feasibility, creativity, and risk shape their attitudes toward sustainability and, in turn, influence sustainable entrepreneurial intentions. Using survey data from 1364 engineering students and analysing relationships via PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4.1.1.0, the findings show that positive attitudes toward sustainability significantly drive sustainable entrepreneurial intentions. These attitudes are positively influenced by perceived desirability, feasibility, creativity, and AI performance expectancy, while perceived risk diminishes them and effort expectancy shows no significant effect. Attitudes significantly mediate the effects of these antecedents on intention, thus establishing their central role. This research extends UTAUT by integrating motivational, cognitive, and technological variables in the context of sustainable entrepreneurship among future engineers. Keywords: performance expectancy, perceived desirability, perceived feasibility, attitudes towards sustainability, perceived creativity, perceived risk, effort expectancy.
