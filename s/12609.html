Psychological Well-Being Across Succession Stages in Family Firms

Authors: Caroline Nicolas Université de Sherbrooke; Juergen Fritz University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Western Switzerland (HES-SO); Salma FATTOUM University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Western Switzerland (HES-SO)

Session overview

Family business succession represents a pivotal moment in the entrepreneurial life cycle, involving both leadership and ownership transfer (Nordqvist et al., 2013). Beyond organizational change, succession constitutes a critical entrepreneurial role transition for incumbent and next-generation leaders (Le Breton-Miller, 2011). While entrepreneurship research has extensively examined opportunity, governance, and strategic renewal in family firms, the psychological foundations of entrepreneurial continuity remain underexplored. This study examines how executives’ psychological well-being varies across succession planning stages in family SMEs. Drawing on survey data from 421 executives in Swiss family SMEs, we measure psychological well-being using the abbreviated 9-item version of Gilbert and Malo’s (2017) psychological well-being at work scale. Analysis of variance reveals significant differences in psychological well-being across four succession planning stages (F(3,417) = 3.65, p = .013, ?² = .026). Executives with an identified successor report higher well-being than those who are themselves successors. Additional analyses show that psychological well-being is positively associated with multiple entrepreneurial goals and challenges, particularly when succession is actively enacted. These findings position psychological well-being as a key micro-foundation of entrepreneurial persistence and intergenerational value creation in family SMEs.
