Shooting the Algorithm Instead of the Messenger: How GenAI Affects Upward Bad News Delivery

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 2
CTO

People

Authors: Yilan Li City University of Hong Kong; Yutong SUN Department of Management and Marketing, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Long Wang City University of Hong Kong

Session overview

The rapid rise of artificial intelligence calls into question whether Generative AI (GenAI) can reshape employees’ upward delivery of bad news and how managers react to the integration of GenAI into this process. Drawing on dual-process theory, we argue that when receiving bad news from GenAI instead of human employees, owing to GenAI’s capability of generating higher-quality content, neutral tone and solution-oriented framing, managers are more likely to engage in controlled processing rather than automatic processing, thus, they may feel less anger (affect), less evaluate deliverers negatively (cognition) and shift attention from event badness to higher-quality resolutions (attention) compared to human employees’ delivery of bad news. Additionally, we identify disclosure as a boundary condition when examining recipients’ reactions to the adoption of GenAI in delivering bad news. That is, disclosing GenAI usage to managers would reduce the positive effect of GenAI-assisted bad news delivery. Examining these multifaceted pathways is crucial for understanding the dual-process mechanisms underlying recipients’ responses to GenAI-assisted bad news delivery. One laboratory experiment will use eye-tracking equipment to investigate participants’ attention allocation when receiving bad news delivered by GenAI, and one online experiment will use working adults in the United States to replicate our findings with similar scenarios. Our proposal has potential theoretical contributions and practical implications to bad news delivery as well as artificial intelligence by enhancing our understanding of people’s responses to the infusion of GenAI and bad news delivery.
