Joint Effects of Career Shocks & Protean Career Orientation on Career Aspirations and Outcomes

CAR
Dave Bouckenooghe (Brock University); Saima Naseer (Brock University); Nazia Bibi (International Islamic University)
Employing the assumptions of career construction theory (Savickas, 2005), the current research analyzes how employees' positive career shocks promote career crafting behaviors and career goal reengagement through the underlying dynamics of career aspirations. In addition, we examined how these indirect relationships are contingent on the protean career orientation in a moderated mediation study. We utilized a time-lagged research design with data (n=326) from employees working in different industries at three-time intervals with a one-month lag between measurement points. The findings confirm the mediation effects of positive career shocks on employees’ career outcomes via the career aspirations. Moreover, these indirect effects are stronger with high levels of protean career orientation. Overall, our study sheds light on key mechanisms and boundary conditions through which positive career shocks positively affect an individual's career outcomes. Our study adds to the literature on career development and organizational behavior by emphasizing why and when positive career shocks bring benefits for employees.
