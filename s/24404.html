Managing Local Embeddedness Across Generations: Implications for Family Firm Internationalization

IM
Giulia Galizzi (University of Vienna); Francesco Debellis (University of Vienna); Emmanuella Plakoyiannaki (University of Vienna); Roberta Oppedisano (University of Rome Tor Vergata); Emanuela Rondi (Politecnico di Milano)
International business research increasingly emphasizes the role of local embeddedness in shaping firms’ internationalization strategies and trajectories. Family firms, in particular, are often assumed to possess strong local embeddedness due to their long-term orientation and enduring social ties, which provide access to localized knowledge and resources while reinforcing a shared organizational identity. However, existing research largely treats local embeddedness as a stable, inherited condition, offering limited insight into how family firms actively build, maintain, and transform embeddedness across generations, especially as they expand into international markets. This study examines the intergenerational dynamics of local embeddedness and their implications for family firm internationalization through a longitudinal single-case study of House of Silk, a fourth-generation Italian family firm with deep roots in Naples and a strong presence in the global luxury market. Our findings show that local embeddedness is not merely inherited but continuously enacted across generational transitions through distinct mechanisms. We develop a process explanation of intergenerational embeddedness that explains how family members manage persistent tensions between preserving local ties and pursuing international expansion. This study contributes to international business and family firm internationalization research by reconceptualizing local embeddedness as a dynamic, intergenerational process rather than a fixed legacy. By doing so, it highlights how locally rooted social ties can be strategically mobilized to support international growth and sustain competitiveness in global markets.
