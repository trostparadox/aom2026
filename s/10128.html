Session overview

This symposium sheds light on key developments in empowering leadership (EL) research, as reflected in the following questions: What role does EL play in adversely affecting employees’ home lives? When are leaders more (vs. less) likely to empower their subordinates? What are the predictors and consequences of EL? How do we best define and measure EL? Specifically, the presentations included in this symposium address: (i) how leader social support inadvertently strengthens the positive effect of EL on employee mental preoccupation and anxiety as well as decreased sleep quality (Bui & Dennerlein); (ii) whether EL and follower age qualify the positive effect of integration preferences on actual integration and downstream employee performance and exhaustion (Buss, Metzger, & Kearney); (iii) how centralization, span of control, boundary spanning, and job constraints affect structural EL (Johnson, Mathieu, Oh, & Schlake); and (iv) how structural and psychological EL affect unit learning climate and learning depending on organizational centralization (Schlake, Oh, Johnson, & Mathieu). The discussant, Dr. Bradley Kirkman, will summarize the presentation themes, highlight future research directions, and lead an interactive discussion among the presenters and the audience.
