Session overview

Organizational ethnography provides deep insights into everyday organizational life by immersing researchers in the field. Critical ethnography builds on this by amplifying marginalized voices and interrogating power structures, ideologies, and inequalities, emphasizing reflexivity, positionality, and context. This approach aims not just to describe but to challenge and transform dominant narratives. In this PDW, we are interested in bringing together ethnographers and those with an interest in ethnography to discuss and build our knowledge of critical ethnographies; ethnographies that explore alternative economies, Indigenous organizing, feminist and cultural reflections, and the hidden or uncomfortable aspects of organizational life. We are particularly interested in work that reveals how issues such as gender, power, and vulnerability manifest in fieldwork, and how everyday practices can resist or reshape organizational and societal norms. This PDW is structured into four parts: (1) Thematic discussions led by the workshop organizers, focusing on ethics, reflexivity, positionality, and publishing. (2) Small-group thematic tables, with organizers rotating among attendees to facilitate dialogue. (3) Ethnography ‘speed-dating’, encouraging rapid idea exchange and networking. (4) Plenary debrief, synthesizing insights and reflections. Our goal is to provide practical guidance for conducting critical ethnographic research and to foster a community of like-minded scholars committed to challenging dominant narratives through ethnographic research.
