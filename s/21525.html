Why We Do What We Do: An Autoethnographic Account of Complicity, Humor, and Homosociality

Author: Roger Freitas de Aquino UNIVALI

Session overview

This autoethnographic study examines the persistence of gender inequalities in organizations, focusing on how homosociality, complicity, and humor maintain exclusionary dynamics despite Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) initiatives. Drawing on the author's twenty-year experience in the Brazilian banking sector and academia, the research employs retrospective evocation and an emotional memory protocol to reconstruct vignettes of veiled sexism. Data analysis uses an abductive and reflexive process, framed by theoretical lenses of homosociality and hegemonic masculinity. Findings reveal two mechanisms: the rationalization of exclusion and the use of humor for tension relief and bonding among men. Discussion illustrates how these mechanisms manifest as a Masculinity Contest Culture (Berdahl et al., 2018), where ostensibly objective practices (Acker, 1990) and sexist humor (Bouckaert et al., 2025) function as loyalty tests and social defense mechanisms (Baker & Rumens, 2025). The study argues that male complicity is often a defensive act, driven by the fear of losing status and group belonging, and highlights a significant collaboration cost (Bouckaert et al., 2025) for those who challenge these norms. This work concludes that unconscious bias training is insufficient, advocating for systemic interventions that disrupt social reward structures and challenge the masculine social contract for genuine inclusion.
