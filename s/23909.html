Revisiting Stakeholder Management in Megaprojects: Managing With Stakeholders, Hope, and Resilience

TIM
BARIS Morkan (Independent Researcher)
Megaprojects operate in environments characterized by profound uncertainty and frequent unexpected events. While recent scholarship has examined how stakeholder behaviors contribute to project resilience, less attention has been paid to the psychological and relational antecedents that trigger these behaviors. This conceptual paper extends Morkan et al.'s (2023) theory of megaproject resilience by integrating stakeholder management strategies and stakeholder hope as critical precursors to Megaproject Citizenship Behaviors. We distinguish three stakeholder management approaches---managing of, for, and with stakeholders---and theorize how each differentially shapes stakeholder hope through mechanisms of agency, power distribution, and participation. We propose that hope serves as a mediating psychological resource that transforms management strategies into collaborative actions during crises. Our integrative model suggests that managing with stakeholders generates the strongest hope, fostering collective agency and adaptive capacity that enhance project resilience. This paper contributes to megaproject management literature by introducing psychological dynamics into resilience theory, elaborating the differential effects of stakeholder strategies, and providing practitioners with actionable guidance for building hope-centered governance systems capable of weathering uncertainty.
