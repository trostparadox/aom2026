Session overview

Frontline workers—those who directly and physically engage with the public’s practical needs—must navigate situational demands while operating within institutional frameworks shaped by laws, policies, and regulations. Although their work has been examined across public, nonprofit, and private organizations in various professional domains, a substantial gap remains in understanding how frontline workers implement institutional mandates while attending to the situated needs and interests of their stakeholders. Prior research has largely emphasized organizational level factors such as work norms and culture, or workers’ individual attributes, to explain frontline worker decision-making. Yet much of frontline work—including everyday practices and administrative routines—unfolds where workers have modest discretion, molded by the contingencies that arise from emergent social interactions. This symposium brings together current studies of frontline occupations and professions to deepen our understanding of frontline work at a grounded, practice-based level across diverse empirical settings. Collectively, these studies highlight the significant and often underappreciated roles of frontline workers— frequently viewed as actors at the bottom of the hierarchy—and illuminate the interactional processes through which frontline workers interpret, adapt, and ultimately enact institutional mandates in the field. Doing so lays theoretical groundwork for understanding how those lacking in power can still shape the way policies and procedures are enacted.
