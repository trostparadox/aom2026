Technological Crowding and Multinational Enterprises’ International Patenting

STR
Yulan Shen (Zhejiang University)
Although unremitting technological rivalry is critically affecting multinational enterprises (MNEs), its effects on MNEs’ international patenting remain surprisingly understudied. This study sheds light on this underexplored topic by examining how technological crowding influences MNEs’ international patenting. Building on insights from competitive dynamics theory, we argue that technological crowding plays a critical role in shaping competitive tension between MNEs and their global competitors. When MNEs operate in more crowded technological fields, they may increase their international patenting because this strategy helps mitigate potential competitive tension through strategic signaling and practical protection mechanisms. We further argue that stronger intellectual property rights (IPR) regime and higher patent value positively moderate this relationship, whereas a higher domestic patent proclivity ratio serves as a scope condition that diminishes this relationship. Empirical results from a comprehensive panel of Chinese-listed MNEs in high-tech industries and their international patents provide support for these arguments. Taken together, this paper extends the international patenting literature by offering a new and important determinant and emphasizing the importance of technological rivalry for MNEs’ international patenting, while also expanding the competitive dynamics literature by proposing international patenting as an expanded concept of competitive response.

Strategic Patenting in Energy Transition: Incumbent Control of Technological Trajectories

STR
Chien-Hsin Wu (Chien-Hsin Wu)
This study examines how incumbent oil and gas firms strategically deploy intellectual property to shape technological trajectories during the global energy transition. Drawing on theories of profiting from innovation and socio-technical transitions, we conduct a large-scale analysis of 8,737 green patents filed between 2012 and 2024 by the world’s top ten oil companies, using patent portfolio analysis and technology overlap networks to map technological focus and strategic intent. The findings reveal that patenting activity is highly concentrated among a small number of supermajors and focused on technologies closely aligned with existing complementary assets, particularly waste management solutions such as carbon capture and storage and alternative fuels, with a strong emphasis on hydrogen. Rather than signaling a disruptive shift away from the fossil fuel regime, incumbent patent strategies reflect proactive efforts to govern the pace and direction of transition by securing control over foundational technologies and future competitive bottlenecks. We identify distinct strategic patenting archetypes—ranging from offensive leaders that aggressively shape emerging technological domains to defensive incumbents that use patents to protect existing positions, mitigate competitive threats, and delay disruptive entry. Taken together, these patterns point to a pathway of “incumbent-led decarbonization,” extending prior research on incumbent adaptation and regime reconfiguration in socio-technical transitions (Geels, 2002; Berggren et al., 2015). This pathway suggests that oil majors may continue to exert structural influence over emerging low-carbon systems, potentially perpetuating centralized energy infrastructures and creating new technological dependencies. By demonstrating how intellectual property functions as a strategic mechanism for shaping industry transformation, this study contributes to strategic management research on incumbent adaptation and offers critical insights for policymakers and industry strategists seeking to foster a more diverse and competitive green innovation ecosystem.

Judicial Specialization and the Rebalancing of Firm Innovation

STR
Shixiang Wang; Yanran Bi
Governments are strengthening intellectual property (IP) enforcement through judicial specialization, yet we know little about how such reforms reshape the composition of firm innovation. Building on innovation incentive theory and the resource-based view, we develop a benefit-cost framework of exploitative versus exploratory innovation under stronger appropriability regimes and ask how specialized IP courts differentially affect these two innovation modes, and under what conditions. We argue that judicial specialization alters the expected returns and risk profiles of innovation in ways that privilege some innovation paths over others, depending on firms’ litigation capabilities and the dynamism of their technological environment. To examine these issues, we exploit the establishment of specialized IP courts in China as a quasi-natural experiment and implement a difference-in-differences design using panel data on 1,349 listed firms from 2010 to 2021. Distinguishing exploitative from exploratory patenting, we find that judicial specialization significantly increases exploitative innovation while dampening exploratory efforts. These heterogeneous effects are stronger for firms with richer litigation experience and in industries characterized by faster technological change. By showing that the same IP reform can simultaneously encourage and discourage different types of innovation, the study offers a more nuanced account of how legal institutions shape strategic innovation choices under evolving IP regimes.

From Trade War to Patent Litigations: Are Chinese Firms Becoming Legal Targets in the U.S.?

STR
Sooah Kang (Seoul National University); Yijie Min (CIEFR-China Institute for Educational Finance Research); Sun Hyun Park (Seoul National University); Yanlong Zhang (Peking University)
This study examines how the U.S.-China trade war affected patent litigation risk for 503 U.S.-listed Chinese firms from 2010 to 2022. Using Difference-in-Differences (DID) and event study designs, we find that firms in industries subject to Section 301 tariffs experienced a significant post-2018 surge in defendant patent litigation. While institutional factors, such as foreign director ratios and Hong Kong cross-listings, failed to mitigate this risk, competitive capabilities proved effective as deterrents. Specifically, high market concentration (HHI) and R&D intensity significantly attenuated the increase in litigation, indicating that mutual forbearance and the credible threat of technological retaliation deter opportunistic lawsuits. With no significant effects observed in plaintiff-side litigation, these findings suggest that substantive economic and technological deterrence is more critical than symbolic legitimacy in defending against geopolitical legal vulnerabilities.

To License or Sell? Exploring Patent Strategies Based on Knowledge Specificity and Generality

STR
Xue Yang (Sichuan University); Shenghao Wu (Sichuan University); Qiuming Zhang (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University)
The tactical employment of patents in technology market has emerged as an integral component of corporate patent strategies. Despite this, how knowledge characteristics embedded in patents shape firms’ patent transaction decisions, particularly whether firms engage in patent transactions and how such transactions are governed, remains underexplored. Drawing upon resource-based theory and transaction cost theory, this study examines invention patents granted in China between 1985 and 2015, using patent descriptive and citation data to investigate the effects of patent specificity and generality on patent transaction, as well as the moderating role of firm innovation influence. The results reveal a two-stage decision process. At the transaction entry stage, patents with higher specificity and generality are more likely to be internalized by firms, reducing their likelihood of entering the patent transaction market. Conditional on transaction occurrence, patents with higher specificity and generality are more likely to be licensed rather than sold. Moreover, firm innovation influence significantly moderates these relationships, such that firms with stronger innovation influence rely less on patent-level knowledge characteristics in transaction decisions. By distinguishing between transaction entry and governance mode choice, this study clarifies how patent knowledge characteristics shape patent transaction behavior and contributes to the literature on patent strategy and markets for technology.
