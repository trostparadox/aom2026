Corporate Turnaround: A Configurational Perspective

STR
Milad Aghazadeh (The University of Manchester); Reza Salehnejad (Manchester University)
Organizational decline is a common phase in the lifecycle of most firms. Over the past decade, approximately 7,000 companies in the United States have filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy each year. Of these, only a third manage to recover through a successful turnaround, while the rest either opt for liquidation or persist with low profitability. Turnaround failure is a major contributor to firm failure. Empirical research has yielded conflicting findings about which actions are most effective in addressing the root causes of decline. An explanation for this inconsistency is that much of the empirical work has focused on testing the effects of individual actions (e.g., cost-cutting) in isolation or, at best, examining interactions between pairs of actions, which fails to fully capture the intricate nature of turnaround efforts. We build on theoretical literature to advance a configurational perspective on corporate turnaround that considers the whole cluster of interdependent actions a firm takes to address decline, while accounting for their intensity and temporal ordering. We employ machine learning clustering techniques to identify turnaround strategies and propose an econometric framework to test their effectiveness. We identify distinct turnaround contexts, define general strategies tailored to each, and empirically evaluate their effectiveness, offering valuable contributions to both theory and practice.
