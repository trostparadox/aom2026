Framing Novelty in Crowdfunding: Which Words Win Support, Where, and at What Stakes

ENT
Agnieszka Kwapisz (Montana State University)
We examine how entrepreneurs’ linguistic framing of novelty (“hype”) relates to the success of reward-based crowdfunding campaigns. Drawing on the elaboration likelihood model, we argue that the effect of hype depends on (a) which facet of hype is used—diagnostic competence language, credence/superlative “quality” language, or affective/attitude language—and (b) the elaboration context created by industry norms and campaign stakes. Using logistic models on 635 U.S. Kickstarter campaigns across five industries, we find no uniform “more hype helps” effect. Competence-signaling rhetoric is positively associated with success in technology campaigns and at higher funding goals (turning point ˜ $18k), affective/attitude language is beneficial in entertainment campaigns, whereas generic superlatives in design and utility talk in consumer goods are negatively associated. At higher stakes, both competence and quality language become more persuasive, consistent with deeper processing at larger asks (quality turns positive around ˜ $30k). The study shows that the persuasiveness of novelty-forward language is facet-, industry-, and stakes-specific, helping reconcile mixed results on entrepreneurial rhetoric in crowdfunding.

How Hype Facilitates Emergence and Inhibits Diversification of Entrepreneurial Ecosystems

ENT
Stefan Korber (The University of Auckland)
This study examines the role that hype played in the emergence of New Zealand's New Space Economy (NSE), a country with virtually no history in aerospace engineering or space exploration. To provide nuanced insights into the actors, mechanisms, and outcomes of hype-related narratives, we draw upon 25 interviews and substantial amounts of secondary data. Our study shows that hype related to space-related opportunities played a crucial role in the emergence of the NSE, but somehow paradoxically, evokes dynamics that could hinder future development and diversification. Most surprisingly, the celebration of individual founders and an over-focus on high-profile activities create exclusionary dynamics that undermine broader participation in the NSE. Furthermore, we link the hype with instances of disillusionment, quixotic entrepreneurial pursuits and ill-informed government investments. Our study not only sheds light on the micro-level dynamics that underpin NSE emergence and shape formal and informal institutions. In addition, we problematise how hype and compelling narratives impact the broader entrepreneurial ecosystems (EEs). Based on our findings, we show that 'attention channelling' is an important, but overlooked, aspect for better understanding how EEs emerge and mature. We suggest that an attention-based view on EEs might hold immense potential for future EE research.

Hype and the Entrepreneurial Dynamics of Nascent Prosocial Market Categories

ENT
Deepti Mishra (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Anirvan Pant (Indian Institute of Management Calcutta)
The proliferation of novel prosocial market categories draws our attention to the overlooked gap of entrepreneurial dynamics between category emergence and category viability. Not all novel market categories attain viability and, of those that do, some may attain viability long after emergence. The persistence of nascent, but no longer novel, market categories may be particularly acute for prosocial market contexts as they combine commercial and moral logics in complex ways. Adopting a generative conception of hype, we ask, How does hype shape the accomplishment of viability in a nascent prosocial market category? In responding to this question, we remain attentive to the practitioner construct of a ‘unicorn’, i.e., a privately held venture valued at or above USD one billion. Arguing that category-level hype can generate momentum, draw positive attention, and garner resources for the nascent market category, we conduct a process study of the emergence of Indian edtech from 2013 to 2022. Using topic modelling with qualitative coding procedures, we analyze media coverage of more than 3.3 million words on Indian edtech, and develop a process model that highlights three overlapping, but analytically sequential, modes of entrepreneurial dynamics - mission, commensuration, and scale – on the road to category viability. Through this process model, we draw attention to how ‘unicorns’, as category exemplars, may propel nascent categories through commensuration mode and scale mode, and thus enable the accomplishment of category viability.

Selling the Future Under Uncertainty: Technological Hype and Signal Portfolios in Crowdfunding

ENT
jing dong (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); yitong gong (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); zheng hua (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics)
Entrepreneurial framing plays a critical role in mobilizing resources, particularly in technology ventures where future potential is often emphasized. Such framing frequently takes the form of technological hype, narratives that stress unrealized or not-yet-validated technological potential to elicit forward-looking evaluations. Despite its growing prevalence, it remains unclear when and how technological hype is effective in mobilizing resources. Drawing on signaling theory, this study conceptualizes entrepreneurial framing as a signal portfolio in which multiple narrative signals jointly shape audience evaluations rather than operating in isolation. We focus on crowdfunding, a high-noise and low–due-diligence financing context in which backers rely heavily on project narratives and intuitive judgment. Using text analysis of 36,473 technology-related projects on Kickstarter, we examine the effects of technological hype and its interactions with temporal focus, framing distinctiveness, and framing redundancy. Overall, the findings demonstrate that the impact of entrepreneurial framing depends not on isolated narrative elements but on how multiple signals are strategically configured.

What’s Behind the Buzz? An Integrative Review of Hype in the Entrepreneurship Market

ENT
Eduardo Boada (Universidad de los Andes); Anastasia Koptsyukh
This literature review synthesizes research on hype in the entrepreneurship market, focusing on its antecedents, dynamics, and consequences at the venture, industry, and market levels. Drawing on scholarship from entrepreneurship, finance, and Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI) studies, we identify how hype emerges, influences investment behavior, and shapes new venture development. We propose an integrative framework that maps the interactions between different levels of hype, revealing feedback loops that drive cycles of exuberance and correction. This study advances understanding of entrepreneurial hype by bridging insights from fragmented research streams and identifying gaps in knowledge, particularly regarding market-level hype. We conclude by outlining avenues for future research.
