Session overview

Firms are regularly confronted with the need to reallocate their resources to different uses in the face of changing environmental conditions. Despite the importance of resource reallocation in shaping a variety of strategic outcomes (Bower, 1970; Burgelman, 1983; Cattani, 2006; Cattani et al., 2024; Levinthal and Wu, 2025; Helfat and Maritan, 2024; Maritan and Lee, 2017), existing research has often focused on the real options embodied in resources and on the financial implications of reallocation (Sakhartov and Folta, 2014; Sakhartov and Folta, 2015). More recently, research has begun to examine the boundary conditions of resource reallocation, unpacking how different types of organizational factors shape resource reallocation efforts (Levinthal and Wu, 2025; Helfat and Maritan, 2024), on the premise that firms are heterogeneous in their ability to reallocate resources. However, a critical yet largely unexplored factor is the technological dimension of resource reallocation—that is, how the nature of a firm’s technology shapes its ability to reallocate resources to different uses (Amore and Mastrogiorgio, 2022; Cattani et al., 2024). For instance, a technology characterized by high modularity may facilitate the decoupling and recombination of technological modules and components, thereby easing the reallocation of resources (e.g., technical and human resources) across the firm’s units.
