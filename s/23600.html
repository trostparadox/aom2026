Innovating with Nature: Poietic Learning Through Multisited and Pluritemporal Experimentation

TIM
Hannah Fults (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Hans Berends (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Fleur Deken (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
Sustainability problems are increasingly addressed through innovating with nature. Such innovations leverage biophysical systems, adding to the relational and temporal complexity of innovation processes due to their situatedness, unpredictability, and the varying temporalities they bring. These complexities challenge innovators’ ambitions to develop scalable solutions that are both robust and situated to local conditions. Drawing on a field study of four sustainable innovations in the built environment, we examine how innovators encounter and work with the biophysical through their experimentation activities. We find that innovators engage in multisited experimentation—switching between coupled and decoupled forms across different locales—to expose their innovations to diverse, situated biophysical conditions. They must engage with multiple natural rhythms and must either temporally align with them or selectively decouple from them to progress. We show how multisited and pluritemporal experimentation enables innovators to learn from biophysical resistances and pursue solutions that are simultaneously robust and situated.

Diffusion of Free Innovation and Knowledge in Social Movements: A Study of Veganism

TIM
Lars Bo Jeppesen (Copenhagen Business School); Tom Grad (Copenhagen Business School); Sandro Kaulartz
This paper investigates the diffusion of innovation and knowledge from social movements within the context of free innovation in households. Prior research has predominantly examined self-rewards as motivations driving household free innovations, but this paper contends that motivations differ significantly for social movement innovators. These innovators are primarily motivated by a shared cause, such as a quest for societal change, rather than individual self-rewards. Consequently, we argue that this difference in motivations has significant implications for the diffusion patterns of innovation and knowledge. To test this proposition, we present evidence from two studies: 1) data were collected via a survey conducted in 2020 among a representative population of vegans (n = 410) from the U.K. adult population, and 2) we analyze a dataset of 62 million social media posts about veganism from popular platforms (Twitter, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, and Reddit). The findings of both studies support the main argument, revealing that free innovators driven by common-cause motivations typical of social movements were more likely to diffuse innovation and knowledge. These results shed light on the role of motivations in driving diffusion patterns within the realm of free innovations, particularly in household settings. The study's insights offer valuable implications for future research and policy interventions seeking to foster innovation and knowledge dissemination.

Deficiency Play: How Corporate Buyers Use Purchasing to Build Nascent Markets

TIM
Mia Blatancic (Technical University of Denmark); Marin Jovanovic (Copenhagen Business School); Thomas J. Howard (Technical University of Denmark); Euiju (Eugene) Jeon (Technical University of Denmark)
How can buyers in nascent markets use purchases as investments without acquiring ownership? We explore this question through an abductive study of the emerging market for engineered carbon dioxide removal (ECDR), where corporate buyers pre-purchase unproven removal technologies at very high prices. Drawing on six years of purchase announcements, offtake contracts, and related communications, we discover that incumbents in a nascent market sense opportunities by addressing systemic market deficiencies rather than pursuing technological or commercial edge. We conceptualize this evolving pattern as deficiency play, a form of opportunity actualization in which firms use demand-side financing mechanisms, rather than ownership, to construct the very conditions under which future value can be captured. We delineate how deficiency play operates across two temporal orientations of capability deployment: that is, how current enabling assets spur future complementary services. Our study extends dynamic-capabilities and entrepreneurial-finance research by theorizing purchasing in a nascent market as a hybrid investment mechanism and contributes to market-formation research by explaining how incumbents catalyze and steer nascent markets under extreme uncertainty.
