Session overview

Most management scholars in the United States (U.S.) receive their doctoral degree from very high research (i.e., R1) primarily white institutions (PWIs). Consequently, this perspective (i.e., PWI & R1) shapes their understanding of the three pillars of academic careers (i.e., research, teaching, and service), career paths, and definitions of success. Globally, however, few business schools (B-schools) are PWIs and even fewer are R1s, meaning that most B-school scholars will begin and build their career in environments that differ from the R1-PWI model. Accordingly, our panel symposium seeks to educate management scholars about how they can build meaningful and impactful academic careers outside of this archetype. We do so by illustrating the varied and very rewarding career paths available to scholars at high research- (i.e., R2) and research- (i.e., R3) oriented minority serving institutions (MSIs), which we contend better approximates the research, teaching, and service expectations at most B-schools. Our attendees will hear about these issues from panelists with varied research interests and expertise, career backgrounds, and at different career stages. Attendees will gain a richer understanding of this oft-overlooked scholarly path, understand key considerations (e.g., selection of new faculty, promotion & tenure) at such institutions, and learn how they can build careers aligned with their values without having to compromise research goals. Our symposium is therefore valuable not only for future management scholars but also junior faculty and perhaps even seasoned scholars in the U.S. and worldwide who wish to develop their careers outside the dominant research-intensive (i.e., R1) paradigm promulgated by PWIs.
