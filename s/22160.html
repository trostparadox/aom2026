Authentic Leadership and Innovative Work Behavior: The Mediating Role of Autonomy

Session overview

Innovative work behavior (IWB) — employees’ intentional generation, promotion, and implementation of novel ideas — is essential for organizational adaptability and long-term competitiveness. Although prior research suggests that authentic leadership fosters employee innovation, the motivational mechanisms underlying this relationship remain theoretically underdeveloped. Drawing on Self-Determination Theory (SDT), this study proposes employee autonomy as a key mediating mechanism linking authentic leadership to IWB. SDT posits autonomy as a fundamental psychological need that energizes intrinsic motivation and proactive behavior, rendering it particularly relevant for understanding innovation at work. Using time-separated, multi-source data from 802 matched supervisor–employee dyads across diverse industries in Montenegro, we test a mediation model in which authentic leadership predicts IWB through employee autonomy. Employees assessed authentic leadership and perceived autonomy, while supervisors evaluated employees’ innovative work behavior. Mediation analyses indicate that authentic leadership is positively related to autonomy and IWB. Importantly, autonomy partially mediates the relationship between authentic leadership and IWB, while the direct effect remains significant (consistent with partial mediation). These findings sharpen the authentic leadership–innovation link by specifying an SDT-consistent motivational pathway and extending evidence to a non-WEIRD context.
