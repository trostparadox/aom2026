Session overview

Experimental methods are increasingly important for advancing social network research, as they enable scholars to establish causal claims and address emerging theoretical frontiers. Yet experimental approaches remain unevenly adopted across this interdisciplinary field, and organizational scholars often lack clear guidance on designing and publishing network experiments that meet high standards of theoretical and methodological rigor. This workshop brings together scholars with extensive editorial and publication experience to address this need through two complementary sessions. Part I features a moderated discussion and Q&A with senior scholars that provides a field-level perspective on where network research is heading, how experiments can advance network theory, and common conceptual pitfalls identified in the review process. Part II focuses on the practical craft of executing and publishing experimental network research, with leading scholars from multiple disciplines showcasing experimental studies ranging from online experiments to field interventions. The session concludes with small-group roundtables in which participants receive targeted feedback on design, methodological, and publication challenges in their work-in-progress projects. By integrating theoretical, methodological, and editorial guidance, the workshop equips participants to conduct experimental network research that is both theoretically meaningful and methodologically rigorous.
