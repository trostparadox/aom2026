Why Negative Workplace Gossip Makes Us Smile While Others Make Us Sigh: The Role of Relative Status

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7
OB

People

Authors: Yangyang Yu University of Macau; Fangjian Wu University of Macau

Session overview

Workplace gossip is a pervasive form of informal communication that conveys evaluative information about absent coworkers. While prior research has focused primarily on gossipers and targets (e.g., Sun et al., 2023), far less is known about how recipients respond to negative workplace gossip toward the targets. Drawing on social comparison theory (Boecker et al., 2022), we propose that recipients’ relative status vis-à-vis the target shapes their emotional and behavioral responses. Specifically, gossip about lower-status targets elicits sympathy, which motivates knowledge sharing, whereas gossip about higher-status targets evokes schadenfreude, which promotes knowledge hiding. These responses reflect recipients’ efforts to navigate status asymmetries and restore perceived social balance in the workplace. We test this model using a two-phase experience sampling study of full-time hospital employees, combining daily self-reports of gossip exposure and emotions with peer-rated knowledge behaviors. We plan to test our model using a two-phase experience sampling method among over 100 full-time employees in hospital settings.
