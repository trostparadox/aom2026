Helping Those Who (can) Help Themselves- Charismatic Donation Appeals Improve Donors’ Responsiveness

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 7

Authors: Roi Shir Dishon WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management; Yuval Kalish WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management; Danit Ein-Gar Tel Aviv University

Session overview

Personal donation-raising campaigns place donors in an environment of uncertainty, where credibility and potential impact are difficult to verify. We argue that charismatic leadership tactics (CLT)—codable rhetorical devices such as metaphors, contrasts, and moral conviction—help requesters reduce this uncertainty by signaling efficacy and agency. Across a pilot and four preregistered experiments (total N = 1,223), we manipulated only the density of CLT in a matched personal crowdfunding appeal while holding constant arguments, length, and informational content. In the joint evaluation, participants compared two appeals and consistently preferred the high-CLT requester, indicating a greater likelihood of donating. In a separate evaluation, high CLT did not reliably increase responsiveness, but it robustly increased perceived requester efficacy. Mediation analyses across separate evaluation studies revealed a consistent indirect effect of CLT on donation outcomes through perceived efficacy, even when controlling for empathy, authenticity, closeness, and relatability. In contrast, emotional and relational appraisals were strongly associated with giving but were not systematically elevated by CLT and did not uniquely explain CLT effects on donors’ responsiveness. These findings extend charismatic leadership theory to anonymous, one-shot prosocial decisions and identify a practical, text-based lever for improving personal fundraising effectiveness. The results also highlight evaluative context as a boundary condition: comparative settings amplify the behavioral advantage of charismatic rhetoric, whereas single-campaign settings primarily reveal its efficacy-based psychological pathway. For platforms, presenting multiple campaigns side by side may increase donors’ sensitivity to rhetorical cues, suggesting interface choices can shape who receives support in practice.
