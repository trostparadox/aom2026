Session overview

Family formation and caregiving are central to many employees’ lives, yet organizational research and practice continue to primarily focus on a narrow set of family and caregiving experiences. Demographic shifts, medical advances, and evolving social expectations have expanded how families are formed and how caregiving is enacted, leading to areas in current literature that are underexplored. This symposium seeks to advance understanding of the hidden and underexplored processes through which family-building and caregiving intersect with work across diverse life stages and arrangements. Bringing together four scholarly papers, the symposium examines infertility treatment, non-prototypical caregiving, men’s concealed caregiving roles, and the transition to first-time motherhood. Methodologically diverse, the papers draw on qualitative interviews, survey and experimental data, and longitudinal designs to reveal how these diverse caregiving and family-building demands are experienced, interpreted, and strategically managed across employees’ work and lives. By examining varied family trajectories and caregiving arrangements, especially those that fall outside prototypical narratives, the symposium offers insights into how individuals can better manage relevant demands and roles, and how organizations can design workplaces that better support their employees.
