The invisible Barrier: How Military Identity Constrains Access to Civilian Business Networks

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Author: Yanli Chen American Public University System

Session overview

Veteran entrepreneurs create 1.6 million businesses and generate $1 trillion in revenue annually in the United States. However, they report significantly lower access to civilian business networks compared to non-veteran entrepreneurs. This qualitative case study investigates why military identity creates barriers to civilian network access. We conducted in-depth interviews with 12 veteran entrepreneurs and 8 non-veteran entrepreneurs. We also analyzed national survey data from the IVMF and PenFed Foundation. We found three mechanisms that constrain veteran social capital: (1) cultural clash between military and civilian business norms, (2) network insularity that blocks access to venture capital and angel investors, and (3) identity discomfort that leads veterans to hide their military background. The military teaches fast decision-making and hierarchy. Civilian business networks value debate and horizontal relationships. This difference creates friction. Veterans also have military friends but lack civilian investor connections. Ninety-five percent of veteran entrepreneurs report no access to venture capital networks. Finally, veterans often hide their military experience at networking events, which reduces their participation. We conclude that the problem is not the veterans themselves. The business ecosystem was not built to include military-experienced populations. This study advances entrepreneurship theory by showing how organizational identity shapes network access and resource acquisition during entrepreneurial transitions.
