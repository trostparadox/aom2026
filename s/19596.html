A Spectrum of Reparatory Responses to Abusive Supervision

Author: Neha Adwani Indian Institute of Management Lucknow

Session overview

This research examines subordinates’ emotional and behavioral reactions to abusive supervision. Reactions were theorized to depend on subordinates’ attributions about the cause of mistreatment and, specifically whether self-blame was perceived as controllable or uncontrollable. We predicted that subordinates would experience shame when self-blame was uncontrollable, conversely, we predicted that subordinates would experience guilt when self-blame was controllable. However, the behavioral reactions of these emotions will depend on the individual difference in the construal level. Subordinates’ cognitive construal level shapes whether subsequent control- restoration efforts following abuse achieve balance. Achieving balanced control restoration, avoiding both passivity and obsession, requires alignment between controllability appraisals and construal level. Thus, whether self- conscious emotional responses to abusive supervision are adaptive or maladaptive is contingent on construal-controllability alignment as a boundary condition. Keywords: shame, guilt, construal level theory, compulsive reparation, positive reparation.
