The (In)Congruence Effect of Leader-Employee Perfectionism on SSG and UPB

OB
Huan Yang (Shihezi University); Xinyuan(Roy) Zhao
Leader perfectionism pursue everything perfect at workplace, which unfortunately triggers interpersonal conflicts and negative attitudes among subordinates. However, the potential impacts of (in)congruence in perfectionism between leaders and employees has been underestimated. This study, grounded in the supervisor-subordinate fit theory, differentiated self-oriented and other-oriented (in)congruence in perfectionism, and investigates how (in)congruence in perfectionism affects supervisor-subordinate relationships and employees’ unethical pro-organizational behaviors. We conducted a self-report study from employees (N1 = 329) and a multi-source, time-lagged study from leader-employee dyads two scenario-based experiments (N2 = 215). The data were analyzed by polynomial regression and response surface analysis. The findings reveal that alignment in leader-employee perfectionism could improve their relationship quality promote subordinates’ unethical pro-organizational behavior. These insights enhance our understanding of the combined effects of leader and employee perfectionism.
