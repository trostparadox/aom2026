Implicit Algorithmic Management Theories: Conceptualization and Scale Development

OB
Yuan-Xin Tang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Hai-Jiang Wang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Wenxing Liu (Zhongnan University of Economics and Law); Sarah Xu (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Algorithmic management is increasingly embedded in organizational work practices, with workers routinely interacting with algorithmic systems that perform managerial and control functions. Prior research has largely focused on individuals’ interpretations, attitudes, and behavioral responses to algorithmic management, treating these reactions as post hoc responses to algorithmic encounters. However, little is known about workers’ pre-existing beliefs regarding what algorithmic management should be. Such preconceptions matter because they shape how individuals interpret, evaluate, and respond to algorithmic systems, especially as algorithmic management becomes more pervasive and normalized. Drawing on implicit theory, we introduce and develop Implicit Algorithmic Management Theories (IAMTs), which we define as individuals’ shared cognitive schemas or prototypes of algorithmic management. By examining the content and factor structure of IAMTs, we demonstrate that workers hold a multidimensional cognitive representation of algorithmic management. By uncovering these foundational belief structures, our study advances theorizing on algorithmic management and contributes to the broader literature on human–algorithm interaction by shifting attention from reactive responses to underlying cognitive preconceptions.

Compulsory Citizenship Behavior of Food Delivery Riders Under Algorithmic Management

OB
Mei Yang (Renmin University of China); Yuri S. Scharp (Utrecht University); Jianmin Sun (Renmin University of China); Jing Zhao (Renmin University of China)
This study aims to examine how perceived algorithmic control (PAC) influences compulsory citizenship behavior (CCB) among gig workers, with a focus on the mediating role of organizational dehumanization and the moderating role of prosocial motivation. Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, this study conducted a two-wave time-lagged survey of 214 food delivery couriers in China. Structural equation modeling was used to test the hypothesized mediation and moderation effects. Results showed that PAC was positively associated with CCB through the mediating effect of organizational dehumanization. Furthermore, this indirect relationship was stronger for workers with lower levels of prosocial motivation, indicating a significant moderated mediation effect. Limitations of this study and future directions are discussed.

From Crossing Boundaries to Drawing Lines: Algorithmic Management and Gig Workers’ Compliance

OB
Xin Li (Tianjin University); Jiazhen Shi (Tianjin University); Yingyue Zhou (Tianjin University)
Although algorithmic compliance behavior is of significant importance to both organizations and individuals, empirical research on the relationship between algorithmic management and algorithmic compliance behavior remains scarce. Drawing on cognitive appraisal theory, this study examines whether and how gig workers’ perceived algorithmic management influences their algorithmic compliance behavior. Specifically, this research investigates the mediating role of job autonomy and the moderating role of coworker support. Based on a three-wave survey of 308 gig workers, the findings reveal a U-shaped relationship between perceived algorithmic management and both job autonomy and algorithmic compliance behavior. Furthermore, job autonomy mediates the U-shaped relationship between perceived algorithmic management and algorithmic compliance behavior. In addition, coworker support moderates the U-shaped relationship between perceived algorithmic management and job autonomy. These findings offer significant implications for understanding employees’ responses to algorithmic management.

Exiting Without Leaving: Shifting the Locus of Professional Identity Under Algorithmic Mediation

OB
Ayaj Rana (Cornell University)
Research on professional identity holds that when identity threats persist, adaptive identity work can become insufficient, precipitating identity exit, typically theorized as leaving roles, organizations, or occupations to eliminate the threat. This study examines a context that complicates this account of identity exit: digital freelance platforms that algorithmically mediate exchange between independent professionals and clients. Drawing on a qualitative study of freelancers on two global platforms, I analyze how workers interpret and respond to chronic identity threats produced by algorithmic mediation. The findings show that freelancers frequently engage in disintermediation—moving established client relationships off-platform in violation of platform rules— as a distinctive form of identity exit by shifting the locus of identity enactment rather than abandoning their occupations, platform participation, or client ties. This locus shift enables four interrelated identity processes: sustaining coherence between professional values and work practices; cultivating recognition as particular professionals within client relationships; reclaiming agentic authorship over routines and responsibilities; and experimenting with future professional selves without premature identity foreclosure. By reconceptualizing identity exit as a locus-based response in contemporary work arrangements, this study extends identity theory, advances scholarship on algorithmic governance, and reframes disintermediation as, in part, an identity-motivated rather than purely economic behavior.

Gain or Threat? How Algorithmic Management Drives Leader Algorithm-Oriented Change Behavior

OB
Longdong Wang (Nanjing University); Hanqiu Zhu (Wuhan University of Technology); Tongqing Zhu (Nanjing University); Weihang Cao (Nanjing Audit University)
As algorithmic systems increasingly permeate managerial work, scholars have begun to document how algorithmic management shapes employee behavior. However, far less is known about how algorithmic management influences leaders themselves-particularly their willingness to initiate algorithm-oriented change. Drawing on conservation of resources theory, we develop a dual-path framework that theorizes algorithmic management as a source of both resource enrichment and resource threat for leaders. Using multi-wave, multi-source data collected from 301 leader–subordinate dyads, we show that algorithmic management exerts a double-edged effect on leader algorithm-oriented change behavior. On the one hand, algorithmic management enhances leaders’ psychological availability, thereby encouraging greater engagement in algorithm-oriented change. On the other hand, it simultaneously heightens leaders’ perceived threat to hierarchy, which suppresses such change behavior. Moreover, leaders’ algorithmic understanding shapes how these competing resource processes unfold by strengthening the resource gain pathway while attenuating the resource loss pathway. By shifting attention from employees to leaders, this study advances research on algorithmic management and leadership. More broadly, it contributes to ongoing debates on digital technologies and managerial agency by revealing how algorithmic management simultaneously enables and constrains leaders’ capacity to drive organizational change.
