Reconfiguring Governance in Global Value Chains: Complexity, Codifiability and Capability

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
OSCM

People

Authors: Nour M Albuloushi; Faisal AlReshaid American University of Kuwait; Kathleen Park Boston University

Session overview

Global value chain (GVC) theory explains how value is created and distributed through international production networks governed by firms, institutions, and organizational actors. This study re-examines the GVC governance framework by empirically testing the three foundational attributes—complexity, codifiability, and capability (the “three Cs”)—alongside explicit coordination and power asymmetry, to identify data-driven governance forms in global supply ecosystems. Using a sequential mixed-method design involving scale development, validation, and latent profile analysis across 399 buyer-supplier dyads in Australasia, we uncover three empirically distinct governance profiles: modular, market, and an unpredicted extreme market form. Results indicate that higher complexity and codifiability increase coordination demands, while supplier capability moderates power asymmetry. Contrary to Gereffi et al. (2005), supplier power proved highest in extreme market profiles, where high switching costs coexist with minimal coordination. These findings challenge traditional assumptions of progressive power asymmetry and provide new evidence for the evolving dynamics of governance in global value chains. The study contributes scalable measurement instruments for GVC attributes and emphasizes the critical role of resource dependence and explicit coordination in shaping governance outcomes. Implications extend to firms seeking governance alignment for efficiency, resilience, and power balance in globally distributed production networks.
