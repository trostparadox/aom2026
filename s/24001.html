Guardians, Catalysts, Navigators or Pawns: The Board's Transformative Role in a Crisis's Context

STR
Verena Lubos; Nicole Alexandra Hinrichs (EHL Hospitality Business School); Achim Schmitt; Thomas Straub (University of Geneva)
Boards of directors play a pivotal role in corporate governance, yet research has largely examined their functions under conditions of stability and growth. This review shifts the focus to crisis contexts—periods marked by unexpected, disruptive events that threaten organizational survival. Synthesizing 103 articles, we reveal that crises transform board roles from static monitoring and advising to dynamic, evolving functions. Specifically, boards oscillate between guardian or catalyst roles during early crisis stages and navigator or pawn roles as crises progress. Building on these insights, we introduce four archetypes—Coal Mine Canary, Phoenix, Ostrich, and Griffin—that capture distinct trajectories of board engagement across the crisis lifecycle. These archetypes highlight the importance of adaptability, composition, and governance processes in shaping organizational outcomes during turbulence. Our review advances corporate governance theory by challenging assumptions of board stability and proposing a framework that contextualizes board behavior in crises. We conclude with research avenues emphasizing deeper exploration of underdeveloped dimensions, theoretical innovation beyond traditional constructs, and consistent crisis definitions. By reframing boards as transformative actors in times of crisis, this study contributes to both governance scholarship and practical understanding of strategic oversight under uncertainty.
