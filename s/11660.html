Session overview

The growing influence of geopolitics on international business (IB) strategy has prompted scholars to revisit political science frameworks, particularly those from International Relations (IR). While IB research frequently borrows from traditional IR theories—realism, liberalism, and constructivism—these paradigms often fail to capture contemporary international relations theory. This paper reviews the integration of IR theory into IB scholarship, highlighting a critical gap: reliance on outdated grand theories rather than modern, empirically testable approaches. We trace IR’s evolution from realism, constructivism and liberalism toward rationalist models emphasizing strategic interaction, bargaining, and institutional design. These newer frameworks offer clearer causal mechanisms and compatibility with IB theories such as the resource-based view. Through a systematic review of IB literature, we demonstrate how current borrowing practices misrepresent IR’s theoretical advancements and limit explanatory power in areas like market entry, global supply chains, and multinational enterprise (MNE)–state relations. We propose incorporating rationalist approaches to enhance theoretical rigor and policy relevance, enabling IB research to address pressing challenges such as geopolitical tensions, sustainability, and global governance. Our review outlines pathways for future research that bridge IR and IB, fostering interdisciplinary insights for scholars and practitioners navigating an increasingly politicized global economy.
