Hispanic Business Students’ Perceptions of Workplace Stereotyping and Inequality

People

Author: Azucena Herrera

Session overview

This study examines inequalities derived from stereotyping and discrimination as perceived by Hispanic college students enrolled in a University of South Texas, preparing to enter the business workforce. As Hispanic individuals represent a growing segment of the United States labor force, understanding how racial and cultural identity intersect with workplace stereotyping is critical. Grounded in social identity theory and organizational inequality research, this qualitative study explores how business students perceive stereotypes related to race, ethnicity, gender, nationality, and culture, and how these stereotypes may translate into discriminatory outcomes in professional environments. The findings contribute to research on race, ethnicity, and inequality in organizations by highlighting how future business professionals perceive and internalize stereotypes prior to workforce entry. Keywords: Stereotyping; Discrimination; Inequalities; Hispanic
