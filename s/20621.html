Employer Branding and Employee Engagement: Psychological Contract Across Employee Groups

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Miao-Ju Wang Feng Chia University; CHEN-LING LAI National Sun Yat-Sen University; Chi-Cheng Wu National Sun Yat-Sen University

Session overview

Employer branding has been widely recognized as a strategic tool for attracting external talent and retaining internal employees. However, prior research has largely focused on organizational-level outcomes or recruitment-stage effects, leaving a limited understanding of how employer branding operates at the individual level, particularly within non-Western contexts. Drawing on psychological contract theory, this study examines how employees’ perceptions of employer branding influence work engagement through psychological contract fulfillment, and whether these effects vary across employee groups. Using survey data collected from 3,135 employees of a large technology firm in Taiwan, the results show that perceived employer branding is positively associated with employee engagement. Moreover, psychological contract mediates this relationship, indicating that employer branding enhances engagement by shaping employees’ beliefs about reciprocal obligations with the organization. The positive effect of employer branding on engagement is stronger for indirect employees than for direct employees, and stronger for domestic employees than for international employees. By shifting the focus from organizational reputation to individual-level perceptions, this study extends employer branding research into the domain of employee psychology and behavior. The findings highlight the importance of the psychological contract as a key mechanism linking employer branding to employee engagement, underscoring the need to consider employee heterogeneity when evaluating the effectiveness of employer branding. Practically, the results suggest that organizations should adopt differentiated employer branding strategies tailored to distinct employee groups rather than relying on a one-size-fits-all approach.
