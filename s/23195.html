Career Value of Experience: Networks, Self-Efficacy, and Rural Students’ Entrepreneurial Intentions

ENT
Suhail Mohammad Ghouse (Dhofar University); Dennis Barber (East Carolina University); Cody Logan Chullen (East Carolina University)
Purpose: This study examines how rural-origin university students translate work experience into entrepreneurial intentions, focusing on the mediating roles of social networks and entrepreneurial self-efficacy. Design/methodology/approach: Using a two-stage geographic and tribal verification process, data were collected from 359 rural students in Dhofar, Oman. Validated scales were used to assess work experience, networks, self-efficacy, and entrepreneurial intentions. Confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modelling tested a simple mediation model and a serial mediation model. Findings: Entrepreneurial self-efficacy fully mediated the relationship between work experience and entrepreneurial intentions, demonstrating that confidence, not work experience alone, drives intention formation. The serial mediation model revealed that work experience builds networks that subsequently enhance self-efficacy, strengthening entrepreneurial intentions. A negative direct effect of networks on intentions when self-efficacy was controlled suggests that network exposure may discourage individuals with low confidence. Research limitations/implications: The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, and findings may not generalize beyond rural university students. Future research should employ longitudinal approaches and consider contextual moderators. Practical implications: Educational programs should prioritize structured work experiences, mentorship, and confidence-building activities to support entrepreneurial career development among rural youth. Originality/value: This study provides the first empirical evidence from rural Oman showing how work experience shapes entrepreneurial intentions through sequential social and psychological mechanisms, extending Social Cognitive Theory to an under-researched context.

Passion for Place: Neo Rural Entrepreneurship in Small Rural Towns

ENT
Vince James Sherry (Griffith University); Anya Phelan (Griffith University); Sara Ekberg; Luke Houghton (Griffith University)
This study examines how passion for place shapes the entrepreneurial journeys and embedding processes of ex-urban neo-rural entrepreneurs who relocate from metropolitan areas to small Australian rural towns. Drawing on narrative inquiry with 21 participants across three diverse rural contexts, the research explores how affective imaginaries, lifestyle aspirations, and emotional connections to place motivate relocation, and influence subsequent adaptation. The findings show that passion for place operates both as anticipatory energy that propels relocation and as a sustaining emotional anchor during periods of uncertainty. However, this same passion can intensify vulnerability when newcomers’ identities, expectations, or innovations intersect with entrenched rural norms, symbolic boundaries, and local power dynamics. Embedding emerges as an ongoing, relational negotiation in which entrepreneurs continually recalibrate their habitus, aspirations, and practices in response to place receptivity and recognition. While some towns harness newcomer passion as a catalyst for renewal, others resist external ideas, producing partial embeddedness, emotional fatigue, and constrained agency. The study extends entrepreneurial passion theory by demonstrating its spatial and relational dimensions and contributes to rural entrepreneurship scholarship by demonstrating how the heterogeneity of rural places ifluence the trajectories of adaptation. These findings underscore the necessity of place-sensitive strategies that establish legitimate pathways for newcomer contribution while also mitigating the emotional toll of entrepreneurial passion.

The Linguistic Styles of YouTube Entrepreneurial Instructors: Balancing Passion and Information

ENT
Fredrick Rice (Morgan State University); Kate Kidwell (Morgan State University)
This study investigates how linguistic styles used by entrepreneurial instructors on YouTube influence audience engagement. Analyzing 2,940 videos, we identify eight repeated styles characterized by the balance of passionate expression, analytical language, and financial discourse. Results from Linear Mixed Models and OLS regressions reveal a strategic trade-off: passion-driven styles (high enthusiasm, low technical density) elicit significantly higher emotional engagement through positive comment sentiment. Conversely, information-rich styles (high analytical and financial discourse) attract substantially larger, more committed followings, averaging 91,000 more subscribers than other styles. These findings suggest that instructors must align their linguistic choices with specific engagement goals rather than attempting to maximize all engagement forms simultaneously.
