The All-Seeing Eye of AI: Rethinking Electronic Surveillance and Workplace Control

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
STR

People

Author: Martijn Overman Tilburg University

Session overview

When is the use of such intrusive surveillance justified? What additional costs must be considered when designing an AI control structure? And does the ability to monitor an agent’s effort enhance or hinder innovative tasks? At first glance, AI monitoring appears to significantly reduce agency costs associated with behavioral control. It reduces the costs of monitoring systems, increases the capability of measuring effort through physical and digital performance measures, and reduces the bureaucratic costs associated with traditional monitoring. However, in many cases, the costs of taking away a workers’ autonomy might not be worth the tradeoff that comes with heightened surveillance. To incorporate the new reality of mass electronic surveillance into AT, we propose an update of the theory’s control logic. Rather than solely balancing the cost of information systems with the cost of measuring outcome and transferring risk to the agent, we argue that task characteristics¬ – such as task significance and task complexity – influence the optimal monitoring solution for an organization. The result is a broader framework for control strategies that incorporates agency theory, AI technology, and psychology research on intrinsic motivation and creativity.
