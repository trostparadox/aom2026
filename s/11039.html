Innovation Intermediaries in Context: Micro-Practices That Build Collective Action

Authors: Andrea Kerstens Delft University of Technology; Angela Greco Delft University of Technology; Hans Wamelink Delft University of Technology

Session overview

Cross sector partnerships (CSPs) are widely promoted as collaborative vehicles for addressing complex societal challenges, yet many struggle to move beyond surface level coordination. While innovation intermediaries are increasingly recognized as key actors who help bridge sectoral, cognitive, and governance divides, we still lack a detailed understanding of how their work supports collective action—and how this varies across contexts. This study investigates the question: How do innovation intermediaries support collective action across different contextual conditions? Drawing on a retrospective, multiple case study of six CSPs facilitated by a Dutch applied research institution, we combine interviews and extensive documentary data to identify eight components of collective action that intermediaries help construct: mission framing, stakeholder structuring, governance design, knowledge translation, capacity building, resource coordination, trust building, and strategic scaling. We then show that these components are not enacted uniformly. Instead, intermediaries adapt their micro practices depending on eleven contextual conditions, including issue characteristics, stakeholder constellation, institutional environment, cognitive distance, urgency, resource stability, and collaboration history. The findings reveal that intermediary roles are best understood not as fixed categories, such as broker or orchestrator, but as configurations of situated practices shaped by contextual demands. This perspective contributes to SIM scholarship by offering micro foundations of responsible, context sensitive collaboration and by connecting structural and behavioral dimensions of collective action.
