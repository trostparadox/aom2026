Session overview

Crisis cells have become central governance mechanisms for organizations facing unexpected, high-stakes events. Yet while the literature has extensively documented sensemaking in extreme contexts, the concrete influence of cognitive biases in situ, as they surface through real-time verbal interactions, remains under-theorized and under-observed empirically. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) offers an original, experiential exploration of the cognitive biases shaping collective sensemaking in crisis cells, combining recent theoretical insights, an immersive simulation, and interaction-focused analysis of participants’ exchanges. Through the enactment of a simulated crisis cell grounded in a realistic fictional case, participants will be immersed in an evolving crisis characterized by uncertainty, time pressure, and information asymmetry. The simulation will be facilitated in real time with explicit attention to turn-taking, silences, alignment, disagreement, and emerging narratives. The discussions produced during the simulation will become shared analytical material to identify the expression and influence of specific cognitive biases, including: (a) time-perception biases (primacy, recency, hindsight bias); (b) group-related reasoning biases (conformity bias, champion bias, anchoring, survivor bias); and (c) event- and experience-related biases (availability, representativeness, escalation of commitment, confirmation bias). By the end of the workshop, participants will have strengthened their ability to identify, analyze, and discuss cognitive biases in crisis-cell sensemaking—both during action and retrospectively—and to mobilize these insights in their research, teaching, and/or professional practice.
