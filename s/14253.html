Emotional Displays in Negotiations: A Comparison of High vs. Low Machiavellians

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CM

People

Authors: Manal Chakra Telfer School of Management, University of Ottawa; Jane O'Reilly University of Ottawa; A.J. Corner University of Ottawa

Session overview

Machiavellianism is characterized by strategic, goal-oriented behavior and a willingness to justify means by desired ends (Christie & Geis, 1970). Negotiations provide a suitable context for examining Machiavellians, given the clear goals, incentives, and time constraints (e.g., Birkás et al., 2015). While prior research indicates that Machiavellians are likely to employ manipulative tactics, less is known about how Machiavellianism shapes emotional expressions in interpersonal contexts, such as negotiations. In this study, we examine differences in emotional expression between negotiators who are high versus low in Machiavellianism. Set in a borrower-lender context, negotiations were video/audio recorded. We analyzed emotional expressions using automated facial-expression recognition software (FaceReader™) and observational coding. Preliminary findings indicate that similar emotional displays between low and high Machiavellians differ in their underlying meaning, timing, and behavioral accompaniment.
