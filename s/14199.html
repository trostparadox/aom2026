Session overview

We believe that studying games and their own analogous game mechanisms can provide a new source of insights into our study of the entrepreneurial process. Games have multiple mechanisms their designers employ to create engaging and memorable experiences. Games let us apply our intelligence and skills in roles we do not normally play in real life under conditions of known objectives and outcomes. Games set new, sometimes unexpected boundaries, and, importantly, have rules of play combined with elements of uncertainty and luck. Like entrepreneurial processes, games are often fraught with uncertainty and the need to pivot in response to, for instance, competitors’ actions or changes in resource availability. In this workshop panelists and participants will explore how entrepreneurship scholars can use game mechanisms to study and teach entrepreneurship. In particular, we strive to show how mechanisms that are used in popular games can be used to evoke deeper understandings of the mechanisms of the entrepreneurial process. Recent AI research has revealed the profound connection between games, intelligence, and education. AI tools will reshape how personalized, experiential, and practice-oriented students can experience the entrepreneurial process. Demis Hassabis, co-founder of DeepMind and 2024 Nobel laureate, trained AI systems to achieve superhuman performance by learning through games—from Atari classics like Pong and Breakout to the ancient game of Go. As Hassabis (2024) has articulated, games provide clear objective functions, bounded problem spaces with defined rules and artifacts, and opportunities for rapid iteration that make them ideal training grounds for developing sophisticated decision-making capabilities.
