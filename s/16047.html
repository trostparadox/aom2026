Author: Yunyun Emily Yuan The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

he rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has raised growing concerns about its implications for human identity. While AI enhances efficiency and intelligence, it may also threaten how individuals define their social value, professional roles, and personal authenticity. Drawing on identity theory and social comparison theory, this study examines whether human uniqueness—unique cognition and emotion—weakens AI identity threat, and explores algorithm aversion as a boundary condition. Using a mixed-method design combining a Wizard-of-Oz experiment and a multi-wave field study, we analyze data from 707 participants. Results show that human uniqueness significantly reduces AI social, role, and person identity threats. Algorithm aversion moderates the relationship between human uniqueness and AI person identity threat, but not social or role identity threats. By highlighting human uniqueness as a psychological resource, this study advances a multidimensional understanding of AI identity threat and clarifies when human values matter most in human–AI comparison contexts.
