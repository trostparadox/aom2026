Implicit Leadership/ Followership Theories and LMX: A Dyadic and Dynamic Perspective

OB
Huanxin Liu (Truman State University); Tiffany Keller Hansbrough (State University of New York at Binghamton); Guiyao Tang (Shandong University); Shelley D Dionne (State University of New York at Binghamton)
This study examines how leaders’ implicit followership theories (IFTs) and followers’ implicit leadership theories (ILTs) and the recognition of these implicit theories in their target partner evolve over time to shape leader-member exchange (LMX). First, we explored whether these implicit theories remain stable or change with repeated interpersonal interactions. The results indicated that while leaders’ IFT anti-prototypes and followers’ ILT anti-prototypes exhibit notable stability, leaders’ IFT prototypes and followers’ ILT prototypes increase over time, suggesting that positive schemas may be more malleable due to accumulating affirmative experiences. Second, we investigated how shifts in IFT and ILT recognition may correspond to changes in LMX quality. Leaders who increasingly recognize followers aligning with IFT prototypes reported higher LMX, reflecting enhanced cognitive and affective evaluations. However, followers with higher ILT prototypical recognition in their leaders did not experience LMX gains. Furthermore, neither leader- nor follower-rated LMX was significantly affected by changes in anti-prototypical recognition, which suggests that early negative impressions are “sticky.” Finally, dyads with high prototypical recognition (PP) reported the highest, most stable LMX, whereas asymmetrical prototype recognition inhibited the growth of LMX. Notably, dyads with high anti-prototypical recognition (AA) began with lower LMX but gradually improved, indicating room for recalibration.

When LMX Takes a Cue from Romantic Relationships:The Darkside of LMX and Follower Complacency

OB
Shu-tsen Kuo (Eastern Connecticut State University); Phillip S. Thompson (Michigan State University)
Leader-member exchange (LMX) is a relational approach to leadership that focuses on a positively synced dyadic relationship between a leader and their follower that virtually always results in improved follower job performance at one point in time. However, according to social entrainment theory, positively synced relationships may also lead to complacency which is most likely to arise around the mid-term point (i.e., 3-5 years) of dyadic relationships. This leaves questions if LMX leads to reduced objective job performance over time (3-5 years) due to the development of follower complacency. Using social entrainment and dyadic romantic relationship theories, we test our theoretical model employing field and experimental methodologies. Utilizing four samples of multi-wave data from leader-follower dyads collected over the course of four years, we find that (1) LMX after Year 1 leads to a negative 3-year change in objective follower job performance, (2) LMX after Year 1 is positively related to follower complacency, and (3) follower complacency mediates the relationship between LMX and the 3-year change in follower objective performance. Implications and future research directions are discussed.

Empower Me! I Will Take Charge of My Own Job: Evidence from FMCG Sales Context

OB
Rana Muhammad Naeem (King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals)
Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) sales jobs are inherently dynamic and fast-paced, often characterized by intense market competition and rapidly changing consumer demands. In this high-pressure work context, the importance of job crafting is accentuated as it enables sales employees to enhance their person-job fit. Limited research investigates the factors that may facilitate job crafting among those working in FMCG sales contexts. Drawing on uncertainty reduction theory, this study examines the relationship between empowering leadership and job crafting through job-based psychological ownership (job-based PO) and leader-member exchange (LMX) as parallel mediators while considering the moderating role of proactive personality and affective organizational commitment. We tested our hypothesized model using three-wave data with a two-week interval between each wave from 286 FMCG sales employees. Our findings from multi-wave data suggest that job-based PO and LMX mediate the association between empowering leadership and job crafting. Furthermore, a proactive personality and affective organizational commitment moderate the association between LMX and job crafting. However, a proactive personality and affective organizational commitment do not moderate the effects of job-based PO and job crafting. Our study suggests significant theoretical and practical implications.

Synthesizing Leadership-Outcome Relationships: A Second-Order Meta-Analysis

OB
Xiaoyu Li (China University of Mining and Technology (Xuzhou)); Zhaoli Song (National University of Singapore); Piers Steel (University of Calgary); Jiafang Lu
The exponential growth of leadership theories has led to construct proliferation and a corresponding surge in meta-analyses, often yielding discordant validity estimates for similar relationships. To resolve these inconsistencies and establish a more parsimonious understanding of leadership effectiveness, this study employs Second-Order Meta-Analysis (SOMA) to synthesize the accumulated evidence from existing first-order meta-analyses. We categorize the vast array of leadership constructs into five theoretical clusters: (1) Moral and Value-Based; (2) Change and Visionary; (3) Relational and Empowering; (4) Task and Transactional; and (5) Control and Destructive Leadership. By aggregating meta-analytic estimates, we establish precise grand mean correlations between these leadership constructs and key organizational outcomes, assessing whether prior variability stems from second-order sampling error or true conceptual differences. Results reveal robust, positive associations for the Moral, Change, and Relational clusters. Conversely, Control and Destructive leadership demonstrate consistent detrimental effects. Furthermore, residual heterogeneity across several relationships highlights the critical influence of contextual moderators. These findings advocate for a moratorium on redundant constructs and a return to functional, behavioral taxonomies to advance the science of leadership.

Fueled by the Leader's Wins:How and When Leader Capitalization Motivates Approach-Oriented Behaviors

OB
Xiurong Chen (National University of Singapore); Remus Ilies (Bocconi University); Trevor Watkins (University of Oklahoma)
When experiencing positive events, individuals often share them with others —a concept known as interpersonal capitalization. As an interpersonal behavior, the extant literature on capitalization has examined its implications in relational contexts where the capitalizer and the recipient are status equals. Yet sharing events and experiences is also relevant for leaders, whose responsibilities involve communicating with and motivating their followers. Drawing on theory from the approach motivation system, we propose that leader work-related capitalization fosters followers’ approach motivation, which in turn promotes proactive behavior and relationship building with the leader. Importantly, we examine followers’ power distance beliefs as a key moderator in this motivational process. Specifically, we hypothesize that the positive effect of leader capitalization on follower approach motivation is stronger when followers hold lower (vs. higher) power distance beliefs. Results from a multi-source, multi-wave field study provide support for the proposed moderated mediation effects. This research extends the scope of interpersonal capitalization to hierarchical relational contexts by examining its motivational function and developing and testing theory on the role of follower power distance beliefs as an essential moderating factor in the motivational process.
