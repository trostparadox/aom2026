How Daily Problem-Solving Rumination Relates to Employee Innovative Behavior via Perspective-Taking

HR
Quang Le Nguyen (National Sun Yat-Sen University); Shin-I Shih (National Sun Yat-Sen University)
This study examines how daily problem-solving rumination relates to employee innovative behavior via perspective-taking, and how this process is contingent on core self-evaluation and perceived organizational support. Using a daily diary design, 108 employees in Taiwan completed three surveys per day over ten consecutive workdays. Multilevel lagged analyses captured within-person fluctuations while accounting for between-person differences. Results indicate that perspective-taking mediates the effect of problem-solving rumination on employee innovative behavior, suggesting that constructive, problem-focused cognition facilitates innovation by enhancing employees’ consideration of others’ viewpoints. Core self-evaluation negatively moderated the first stage of this indirect effect, such that the positive relationship between problem-solving rumination and perspective-taking was weaker among employees with higher core self-evaluation. Perceived organizational support moderated the second stage, strengthening the link between perspective-taking and employee innovative behavior. Theoretically, the study extends innovation research by identifying how daily cognitive processes interact with stable self-evaluations and contextual resources to shape creative behavior. Furthermore, employing a daily diary approach provides a fine-grained understanding of within-person mechanisms underlying innovation in everyday work contexts. Keywords: problem-solving rumination, perspective-taking, employee innovative behavior, core self-evaluation, perceived organizational support.
