Session overview

Prevailing approaches to AI ethics emphasize principles such as fairness, transparency, and accountability, yet they largely remain grounded in instrumental and functional conceptions of the human self. As AI systems increasingly mediate how individuals perceive, evaluate, and optimize themselves, ethical challenges extend beyond governance and compliance toward deeper questions of human experience and flourishing. Drawing on Vedanta as a form of transcendental phenomenology, this poster argues that AI-enabled practices associated with the quantified self risk disrupting inner coherence, autonomy, and self-realisation, dimensions that remain largely absent from contemporary human-centric AI frameworks. Building on an integrative review of AI ethics and innovation scholarship, we introduce the notion of spirituality to the body of AI Ethics pre-trustworthy AI, highlighting the importance of examining ontological assumptions about the human self prior to and during the AI design, development, and deployment. We propose a conceptual framework that positions ethical reflection as a foundational design concern rather than a post hoc regulatory response. The poster contributes to management and spirituality research by offering spirituality as a lens to the field of AI Ethics. In doing so, it offers a non-Western epistemological lens that enriches technological ethics and opens new avenues for interdisciplinary research on human-centric AI.
