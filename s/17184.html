HPWS and Employee Disengagement: Roles of Work Intensification and Leadership

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Jennifer D. Milczewski The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley; Pinki Maria Sarker

Session overview

Although high performing work systems (HPWS) are widely glorified for enhancing organizational performance, there is also growing evidence showing that the same system may have unintended negative implications for employees in certain contexts. Drawing from conservation of resources (COR) theory, we offer theoretical explanations for how work intensity mediates the relationship between employees’ perceptions of HPWS and disengagement. Authoritarian leadership as a contextual moderator raises the performance pressure and reduces the recovery possibilities. By framing disengagement as a self-protective coping strategy for resource depletion, we aim to extend HPWS literature on explaining why performance-oriented HR systems can undermine employee well-being. Our propositions offer directions for future research and guidance to organizations that seek to manage performance pressure with sustainable work practices.
