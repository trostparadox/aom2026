When Does Family Ownership Enhance ESG Performance? Evidence from Chinese Family Firms

STR
Linxia Fan (Chongqing University); Aihua Chen (Chongqing University); Yilin Zang (Chongqing University)
ESG performance has become a central indicator of firms’ sustainable development, yet how family ownership shapes ESG outcomes remains insufficiently understood. Drawing on socioemotional wealth and agency perspectives, this study examines the effect of family ownership on ESG performance and its boundary conditions. Using a panel of Chinese A-share listed family firms from 2009 to 2024, we find that family ownership is positively associated with ESG performance, reflecting family owners’ long-term orientation and socioemotional wealth preservation motives. However, this effect is weakened when family board membership is high, as concentrated family control intensifies agency conflicts and decision-making rigidity. Firm internationalization also negatively moderates the relationship, as institutional adaptation pressures, financial risks, and resource dispersion reduce ESG investments. By identifying governance- and strategy-based contingencies, this study advances ESG research by clarifying when family ownership enhances sustainability outcomes and extends family firm theory to international and ESG contexts.
