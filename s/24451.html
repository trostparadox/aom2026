Digital Embedding and R&D Partner Choice between State-Owned and Foreign Firms in China

TIM
Haorong Liu (Shanghai International Studies University); Haifeng Wang (Shanghai International Studies University)
Digital transformation is reshaping how firms in emerging economies organize innovation and manage interfirm collaboration. Yet we know little about how digitalization alters partner-choice logics where both state and foreign actors are central. This study examines how Chinese firms strategically choose between state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs) as innovation partners under mixed-ownership reforms and high-level openness. Building on a stage-based view of digital embedding, we distinguish R&D-stage and application-stage digital embedding and theorize their differential effects on cross-institutional collaboration. Using large-scale panel data on Chinese listed firms, we find that R&D-stage digital embedding promotes collaboration with both SOEs and FIEs, whereas application-stage digital embedding reduces collaboration with SOEs but increases collaboration with FIEs. The study shows that digitalization reconfigures rather than uniformly strengthens collaboration portfolios, enriching international business theory on digital transformation, cross-institutional governance, and host-country firm agency.
