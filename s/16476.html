Lives at the Edge: Unveiling the Dimensions of Migrant Workers' Resilience

Author: Arunima Dixit Indian Institute of Technology, Roorkee

Session overview

Migrant workers constitute one of the most vulnerable populations, employed in highly insecure and hazardous industries. Their migrant status further leads to prolonged and multifaceted adversities. While existing resilience literature has largely focused on short-term, event-based adversities such as natural disasters, our study shifts the attention to resilience processes of prolonged adversities. Drawing on a qualitative approach, this study delves into 1) types of adversities experienced by migrant workers, 2) what dimensions of resilience emerge among migrant workers, and 2) what mechanisms enable these forms of resilience. Primary data were collected through semi-structured interviews from 22 migrant workers employed in India’s hospitality sector. Our findings reveal three distinct dimensions of resilience: (1) sustaining resilience, where workers strive to preserve continuity of self, (2) transforming resilience, where adversity fosters community-oriented change, and (3) liminal resilience, where the individual remains in an unresolved state of struggle. Based on our findings, we propose a grounded theoretical model. This study contributes to the literature on persistent adversity and employee resilience. Moreover, offers practical implications for governments, employers, and managers to address the challenges of migrant workers.
