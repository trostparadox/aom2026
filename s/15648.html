Emergence of New Organizational Forms: Collective Analogizing in the Rise of Platform-Based FinTech

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4

Author: Kamila Nigmatulina University of Oxford

Session overview

How do novel organizational forms emerge in highly regulated environments where institutional constraints appear to foreclose innovation? Prior research shows that analogies help entrepreneurs legitimate new practices, yet we know little about how analogical work unfolds collectively among entrepreneurs, regulators —especially where legality is a precondition for emergence. Drawing on a longitudinal qualitative study of the first generation of loan-based crowdfunding platforms in the United Kingdom (2004–2018), this paper develops a process model of collective analogizing. The analysis shows how actors construct, contest, blend, and converge analogies to generate both a novel organizational form and the legal architecture governing it. Three phases— divergence, alignment, and stabilization—structure the process. Early on, entrepreneurs used cross-domain and contrasting analogies to create space for experimentation (“eBay for money,” “not a bank”). Subsequently, regulators and lawyers blended and filtered analogies to align interpretations and codify a new regulatory category (Article 36H). The study shows that analogies enable innovation not only through similarity but also through contrast, ambiguity, and gradual convergence. It contributes to research on organizational emergence, institutional infrastructure, and innovation under regulation by revealing how analogical work shapes both new organizational forms and their governing institutions.
