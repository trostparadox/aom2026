Forced or Chosen: Why Autistic Individuals May Pursue Entrepreneurship

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6
ENT

People

Authors: Wyn Ma University of Central Florida; Maira Elize Ezerins University of Central Florida

Session overview

Recent research has established that entrepreneurship may be a viable career for neurodivergent people (e.g., people with ADHD). While neurotypical individuals are usually motivated by family security, recognition, and community to begin ventures, it is unknown whether or not the same motivations are applicable to neurodivergent people, such as those with autism. As such, in this research, we first explore whether there are differences or similarities in entrepreneurial motivation and find that autistic individuals indeed have different reasons for starting a venture. Second, because the motivations theorized in the literature have thus far focused on neurotypical people, we explore additional context-specific variables that may influence entrepreneurial intent. Specifically, we argue that because autistic individuals often cannot gain income through traditional employment, financial constraints ‘push’ autistic individuals toward self-employment, whereas some innate characteristics of autism may ‘pull’ them toward entrepreneurial engagement. In our second analysis, we test the effects of these autism-specific motivations on intent to pursue entrepreneurship and compare with a neurotypical control group. This study advances our understanding of the motivations that may lead autistic individuals to pursue entrepreneurship and provides a foundation for future research exploring entrepreneurship as a viable career path for autistic people.
