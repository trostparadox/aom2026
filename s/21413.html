Session overview

With globalization, the significance of tax sustainability is growing, particularly in light of increasing budget deficits. The generation of tax revenues, which is the primary responsibility of tax authorities, is becoming increasingly crucial. To enhance their effectiveness in this area, tax authorities have implemented tax management systems. In many developed countries, tax management focuses on controlling and managing tax risks in specific domains, relying less on punitive measures. Various factors influence taxpayer compliance, and tax authorities need to establish strategies to mitigate non-compliance. To achieve this goal, tax administrations have developed tax management practices based on the best methods from various countries, ensuring efficiency both in cost and effectiveness. Tax management encompasses the strategic, operational, and tactical activities of tax authorities aimed at maximizing compliance while fostering a positive tax culture (Huang et al., 2017; Huseynov & Klamm, 2012; Shaidurova & Homokyová, 2020). As a result, corporate sustainability management has emerged as a pivotal issue for companies around the globe. This management approach emphasizes achieving corporate sustainability by addressing economic, environmental, and social responsibilities within corporate practices. It is founded on the understanding that businesses cannot thrive in the long run if they solely chase short-term profits without addressing their environmental and social obligations. Consequently, these companies tend to adopt sustainable tax strategies that are viable over the long term instead of focusing on short-term tax reduction (Ma & Park, 2021).
