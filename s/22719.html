User Vigilance and Collaborative Human-Algorithm Demand Forecasting

OSCM
Rebekah Inez Brau (Brigham Young University); John Aloysius (University of Arkansas); Nada R. Sanders (Northeastern University)
Human–algorithm collaborations are transforming demand forecasting as algorithms are increasingly automating computational estimation with humans providing oversight. As automation reduces cognitive demands, maintaining user vigilance, a behavioral state in which users intervene only when it is appropriate, has become a challenge. Integrating Adaptive Control of Thought and Dual Process Theory, we propose two interventions that promote vigilance: declarative knowledge, which enhances vigilance through structured understanding, and cognitive reflection, which activates deliberate reasoning that supports the effective use of that knowledge. We test the efficacy of our proposed interventions using a laboratory forecasting experiment featuring two processes with forms of human participation: detection-and-calibration and detection-only. Declarative knowledge consistently improves user vigilance for both forecasting processes and sustains collaborative forecasting accuracy over time in the detection-only process. Cognitive reflection alone offers no benefits and sometimes impairs low-reflective users. However, when combined with declarative knowledge, cognitive reflection stabilizes vigilance across cognitive profiles and further improves accuracy in the detection-only process. As AI increasingly assumes more of the computational burden and humans focus on detecting special events, these findings underscore the need for interventions that integrate knowledge-based instruction with reflective engagement to sustain vigilance and accuracy in human–algorithm collaborative forecasting.

Orchestrating Supply Chain Formation Under Technological and Regulatory Uncertainty

OSCM
Onyeka John Chukwuka (University of Bristol); Jianhong (Sandy) Sun (University of Bath); Mehdi Montakhabi (University of Oxford); Brian Squire (University of Bristol)
Supply chain management research typically assumes the existence of supply chains that can be coordinated or optimised. This assumption is problematic in nascent industries characterised by technological novelty, regulatory ambiguity, and uncertain demand, where supply chains must first be brought into existence. Hence, this study examines how supply chains are formed under such conditions through the lens of orchestration. Drawing on a multi-case study of the UK cultivated meat industry, the analysis integrates semi structured interviews with founders, senior managers, and technical leads across the emerging supply network, alongside systematically assembled secondary data. Data were analysed through an abductive process involving open coding, axial coding, and aggregation into higher level dimensions. The findings show that supply chain formation unfolds through analytically distinct but non-linear modes of orchestration characterised by internalisation, provisional partner engagement, regulatory anticipation, and legitimacy-oriented coordination. These modes reflect deliberate responses to persistent uncertainty rather than transitional stages toward stability. The study reconceptualises orchestration as existence-enabling network construction, theorises supply chain formation as temporally constrained and reversible, and demonstrates how institutional conditions actively shape network feasibility and durability.

Executive Operational Bifurcation: How Top Management Structure Shapes Workplace Safety

OSCM
Yao chen (Bucknell University); Shuang Liu (Georgia State University)
How executive structures shape operational outcomes remains a critical but understudied question in organization theory. This paper investigates how the architecture of top executive roles influences workplace safety, a domain where clarity, accountability, and consistent attention are paramount. We introduce the concept of executive operational bifurcation, a structural arrangement in which strategic and financial authority is concentrated in one executive (either the CEO or CFO), while an independent Chief Operating Officer (COO) retains command over daily operations. Drawing on the attention-based view of the firm, we argue that such bifurcation fosters more stable executive attention, reduces ambiguity in safety priorities, and enhances the translation of strategic intent into operational execution. Using a longitudinal dataset of S&P 500 firms from 2016 to 2024, we find that the presence of an independent COO is positively associated with workplace safety outcomes. Further, greater power asymmetry between the CEO and CFO improves safety performance, and this positive effect is amplified in firms with an independent COO. These findings challenge prevailing assumptions favoring power balance in top management teams and offer new insights into how structural design at the apex of the firm can mitigate operational risk.

From Adoption to Performance: A Necessary and Configurational Perspective on Smart Manufacturing

OSCM
Matteo Podrecca (University of Padua); Francesco Arcidiacono; Michela Bearzi (University of Udine); Florian Schupp
Smart Manufacturing (SM) represents the transformation of traditional production systems into autonomous, interconnected, and data-driven networks. By leveraging advanced technologies, SM holds the promise of substantial improvements in firms’ operational efficiency. Despite such potential, many companies struggle to translate SM adoption into tangible performance gains, with initiatives often stalling before reaching scale. To address this gap, this study employs the Technology–Organization–Environment (TOE) framework to examine how technological, organizational, and environmental factors should be deployed to successfully reap the performance benefits tied to SM, focusing both on their sequencing and on the specific configurations leading to the highest outcomes. Using survey data from 234 firms in the automotive components industry, we apply an integrated methodological approach that combines Necessary Condition Analysis (NCA) and fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA). The NCA results show that organizational factors constitute necessary conditions for achieving even minimal performance improvements from SM adoption, whereas technological factors and environmental support are not necessary on their own. Complementary fsQCA findings reveal the existence of multiple, equally effective configurations of TOE conditions that lead to high performance outcomes. The study is the first to move beyond static classifications of TOE factors by examining both the sequencing required to secure performance benefits and the alternative configurations that enable firms to achieve superior outcomes. In doing this, it provides a fine-grained view of how TOE dimensions jointly shape socio-technical transformations such as SM.
