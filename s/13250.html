Session overview

Digital payment systems mediate billions of transactions daily, yet their legitimacy remains fragile. When biometric authentication, algorithmic fraud detection, and cross-border settlement converge, ecosystems involve actors with divergent interests: technology vendors seeking scale, regulators managing systemic risk, merchants demanding reliability, and users navigating surveillance anxiety. These actors must coordinate under asymmetric information and contested accountability. The result is a legitimacy problem: how do ecosystems maintain trust when authority is distributed, failures are opaque, and redress mechanisms remain unclear? This workshop reframes trust from a dyadic firm-user relationship to a multi-actor coordination problem embedded in institutional structures. Drawing on empirical materials from palm-vein biometric implementations across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and the United States, participants engage in a four-stage Diagnostic Cycle: mapping ecosystem actors, simulating breakdowns, designing repair mechanisms, and generating research questions. The workshop produces reusable artefacts applicable to platform governance and public-private infrastructure.
