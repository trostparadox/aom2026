From Constraints to Value: A Configurational Theory of Frugal Innovation

TIM
Dennys Eduardo Rossetto (SKEMA Business School)
This study develops a configurational theory of frugal innovation that explains why firms innovating under resource constraints achieve heterogeneous outcomes. Building on a systematic review of the frugal innovation literature, we synthesize evidence from 39 empirical articles comprising 72 firm-level cases across industries and geographic contexts. Using a mechanism-centered configurational approach, we identify five recurring causal mechanisms—cost and complexity reduction, resource repurposing, modularization, user co-creation, and institutional substitution—and show how these mechanisms combine into three distinct frugal innovation archetypes: reverse-scaling bricolage, platform-levered frugality, and institution-bridging partnerships. Our findings demonstrate that frugal innovation success does not arise from isolated practices but from specific configurations of mechanisms aligned with constraint profiles and contextual conditions. These configurations generate systematic trade-offs between speed of scaling, inclusiveness, and long-term resilience. By moving beyond descriptive typologies, this study advances theory on frugal innovation and innovation under constraints by offering a mechanism-based explanation of equifinal pathways to value creation, with implications for research, management, and policy.

Harnessing AI for Impact-Oriented Frugality of Innovation: The Situated Capability Perspective

TIM
Kazuhiro Asakawa (Keio University); Yi Li (The University of Sydney); Zhexiong Tao (University of International Business and Economics); Naihao Li (University of International Business and Economics)
This study introduces the concept of impact-oriented frugality—the firm’s outcome of achieving high innovation impact with minimal resource inputs—by examining how artificial intelligence (AI) capability can facilitate this dual goal under institutional constraints. Adapted from the situated capability perspective, we conceptualize AI not as a generic tool, but as a firm-specific capability developed through the process of recasting, whereby AI are reconfigured to align with strategic objectives. Using a panel dataset of 4,691 Chinese publicly listed firms from 2012 through 2022, we find that AI capability with recasting enhances impact-oriented frugality of innovation. However, this positive effect is weakened by institutional grounding (i.e., government subsidies) and institutional bounding (i.e., state ownership), which constrain strategic flexibility and data autonomy. Our findings extend the innovation and AI literature by theorizing how firms leverage AI capabilities to simultaneously attain resource efficiency and innovation impact under institutional constraints.

How Does Founders’ ICT Knowledge Enable Frugal Innovation? Evidence from Emerging-Market Startups

TIM
Rosana Dangui (UNIVALI); André Moraes dos Santos (Universidade do Vale do Itajaí - Univali)
Startups in emerging markets operate under extreme resource scarcity and high failure rates, yet research offers limited micro-level explanation of how founder technical expertise translates into frugal innovation and performance. Building on the Knowledge-Based View and bricolage theory, we examine whether founders’ Information and Communication Technology (ICT) knowledge enables frugal innovation by expanding entrepreneurial bricolage— defined as “making do” through creative recombination of resources at hand—and thereby improves venture performance. We conducted a multiple-contrast case study of four emerging market technology-based startups selected on two dimensions: founders’ ICT knowledge (high vs. low) and ICT development strategy (in-house vs. outsourced). Data include semi-structured interviews, secondary materials, and post-interview survey measures of ICT knowledge, bricolage, frugal innovation, and perceived performance, triangulated through theory-driven content analysis and cross-case comparison. Findings show that ICT knowledge is a critical enabler of frugal innovation, but not a sufficient condition for bricolage. Two ventures combining ICT expertise with a bricolage posture produced frugal solutions through rapid prototyping, recombination of low-cost components, and “basic-first” design, reporting superior performance. In contrast, a high-ICT venture pursuing a “buy, not build” strategy achieved frugality via governance and cost discipline but exhibited limited bricolage and only moderate performance; low-ICT outsourcing generated coordination frictions and weak frugality. We contribute by theorizing founder ICT knowledge as a micro-foundation of frugal innovation and identifying strategic orientation toward outsourcing as a boundary condition that decouples technical expertise from bricolage, with performance consequences.

Sanctions and Adaptation-as-Novelty: Identity-Infused Innovation in Kazakhstan’s Energy Economy

TIM
Olena Khlystova (University of Reading); Yelena Kalyuzhnova (Henley Business School)
We examine how firms craft and mobilise identity-infused innovation to sustain competitiveness and legitimacy in Kazakhstan under international sanctions. Grounded in organisational identity theory and the literature on technonationalism and sanctions, we ask how identity narratives shape firms’ choices of innovative responses beyond R&D and patents. We use a mixed-method design combining a survey (n=109) with semi-structured interviews (n=28). Our abductive analysis identifies a portfolio of ‘workarounds’ spanning governance, process, market and compliance innovations. Identity frames – national, sectoral and organisational – act as boundary conditions that legitimise certain novelty while constraining others; they channel resource reconfiguration toward permissible partners, logistics, finance and workforce solutions. Quantitative patterns corroborate interview insights, linking identity alignment to perceived effectiveness of alternative innovation channels and to downstream outcomes (delivery reliability, sales continuity, workforce stability). The paper contributes a process model of identity-infused innovation under geopolitical constraints and a measurement palette for non-technological innovation. Managerially, we outline how leaders can purposefully craft identity frames to legitimate adaptive innovation without breaching compliance. Policy-wise, our results highlight migration, standards and compliance infrastructures as levers that shape firms’ innovation choices under sanctions.

Towards Inclusive Base-of-Pyramid Technology Development: Evidence from Non-WEIRD economies

TIM
Sanyka Banerjee (Indian Institute of Management Sambalpur); SHIKHA BHARDWAJ (Indian Institute of Management Sambalpur)
Base of the Pyramid (BoP) technologies assist marginalized groups in non-WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic) economies like India with affordable, accessible, and locally relevant solutions. Despite the growing focus on inclusive innovation, the current literature needs a comprehensive and generalizable framework for BoP technology creation that incorporates traditional engineering methodologies and participatory practices. In doing so, this study investigates three technology centers to explore the whole BoP technology development lifecycle, from problem discovery to solution deployment. The study proposes a framework by integrating the PDCA (Plan-Do-Check- Act) cycle embedded with co-design and inclusive innovation. This hybrid approach involves end-users in development, improving relevance, ownership, and long-term impact. This paper proposes a fresh, empirically grounded approach based on two theoretical lenses—agency theory to the founder’s readiness and the social exchange theory as fundamentals of co-design in BoP technology development. Further, evidence from Indian academic institutions, social organizations, and incubators facilitate sustainable technology development by closing a major theoretical and practical gap. This study suggests an actionable framework for practitioners to implement BoP technologies at the grassroots level for clarity and efficiency, and it can be replicated in other developing regions.
