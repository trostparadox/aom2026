Coopetition Capability and SMEs’ Digital Innovation: Asymmetric Moderation by Decision Logics

TIM
Feng Zhang (South China University of Technology); Yingting Jiang (South China University of Technology); Lei Zhu
Digital innovation has become essential for manufacturing small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), yet firms engaging in similar coopetitive activities often achieve markedly different innovation outcomes. Drawing on paradox theory, we conceptualize coopetition capability as a higher-order paradox management capability and examine how it translates into digital product and digital process innovation under different decision logics, namely causation and effectuation. Using survey data from 366 manufacturing SMEs, we find that coopetition capability is positively associated with both digital product and digital process innovation. We also uncover an asymmetric moderating role of decision logics: causation strengthens the relationship between coopetition capability and digital process innovation but weakens its relationship with digital product innovation, whereas effectuation strengthens the link to digital product innovation while attenuating the link to digital process innovation. These findings advance a more fine-grained understanding of product–process heterogeneity in SMEs’ digital innovation and elucidate how decision logics reconfigure the boundary conditions under which coopetition capability yields distinct innovation outcomes.

Energizing Digital Transformation of Manufacturing SMEs For Cross-Boundary Value Creation-Case Study

TIM
Xuechen Ding (Beijing Technology and Business University); Xielin Liu; Zhinan Wang (Harbin Engineering University)
Drawing on the innovation ecosystem perspective, this paper examines how a manufacturing-based platform enables cross-boundary value creation by empowering small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) across three distinct sectors: recreational vehicles, ceramics, and agriculture. Using Haier’s COSMOPlat industrial internet ecosystem as a single case, the study develops a five-dimensional analytical framework encompassing digital technology empowerment, value co-creation, value chain reconstruction, ecosystem coordination, and value capture. The findings show that a manufacturing enterprise can evolve into a platform leader, building and orchestrating an ecosystem that engages SMEs across various industries in collaborative value creation. These five dimensions are not isolated enablers but mutually reinforcing mechanisms that form a dynamic and iterative transformation cycle. While COSMOPlat operates under a unified platform logic, its empowerment strategies are tailored to the sector-specific needs and transformation paths of each industry. This study not only enriches the digitalization and ecosystem governance literature by developing a framework that explains how platform leaders attract SMEs, manage cross-sector ecosystems, and realize distributed value creation, but also provides viable pathways for SME digital transformation and industrial upgrading in emerging and transition economies.

Guarding the Old, Facing the New: The Influence of Confucian Culture on AI Adoption in Family Firms

TIM
Yiyun Luo (Zhejiang University); Aiqi Wu; Jingyi Lei (Zhejiang University); Xinyao Wu (Zhejiang University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) represents a pivotal technology for competitive advantages, yet its adoption varies significantly across firms. Family firms are deeply embedded in cultural contexts, which serve as a double-edged sword that may constrain their behavior and choices. Drawing on institutional theory and socioemotional wealth perspective (SEW), our study investigates how the cultural informal institution of Confucian culture influences AI adoption in family firms. Analyzing a panel data of 3726 Chinese listed firms from 2011-2023 (25821 observations), we find that Confucian culture significantly inhibit AI adoption in family firms. Furthermore, we identify critical boundary conditions: internal governance structure (family involvement) amplified the effect by strengthening the guardians of the traditional logic, while external institutional support (city-level digital policy) attenuates it by legitimizing technological logic. Our study contributes to family firm’s AI adoption by unpacking the cultural barriers, illuminating the “dark side” of familiness, and advancing the application of institutional logic in family firm research.

Interplay of Social Media, Learning, Innovation, and Resilience: A Framework for SME Success.

TIM
Rodrigo Martin-Rojas (University of Granada); Víctor García-Morales (University of Granada); Raquel Garde-Sánchez (University of Granada); M. Ángeles Baeza (University of Granada)
The increasing volatility and complexity of business environments have challenged traditional management models into question. In response to these challenges, turbulent contexts have revealed the need for firms to develop adaptive dynamic capabilities in the strategic management literature, such as organizational learning, innovation, and resilience to maintain competitiveness and continuity. Due to their limited resources and structural vulnerabilities, Small and Medium-Sized enterprises (SMEs) are often more exposed to external shocks such as economic crises, technological disruptions, and global pandemics. They are therefore forced to adapt to rapid technological advances and the dual transition to a digital and sustainable economy. Despite growing academic attention to the digital transformation of businesses, few empirical studies have examined the mechanisms through which social media technologies influence organizational learning, innovation, and resilience, especially in SMEs to remain competitive. In this study, we address these gaps by developing and testing a comprehensive Structural Equation Model in 259 SMEs from Spain. The results confirm that strategic role of social media technologies can shape organizational learning and innovativeness in times of disruption by providing useful knowledge to enable SMEs to find new opportunities. They also position learning as a central dynamic capability in explaining both innovation and resilience outcomes and shed light on the understanding of resilience as a result of learning and innovation, and as a determinant of SME performance. Finally, conclusions, theoretical implications, practical contributions and future research directions are presented to discuss the effects of these social media technologies on SME performance.
