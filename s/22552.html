Session overview

The growing spread of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in organizational processes is significantly transforming the way decisions are made and supported in public and nonprofit organizations. Hence, the concept of agency in the loop highlights the need to maintain and rethink the role of human action within decision-making systems that are increasingly mediated by algorithmic tools. The adoption of AI-based technologies not only entails increased data analysis and process automation capabilities but also raises fundamental questions about the relationship between human autonomy, institutional accountability, transparency, and the legitimacy of decisions. Accordingly, this session aims to explore how artificial intelligence is redefining decision-making dynamics in public organizations and the nonprofit sector, highlighting opportunities and risks, as well as emerging organizational transformations. In particular, the session aims to discuss how the interaction between algorithmic systems and human actors is changing governance practices, policy-making processes, and the delivery of public and social services and value. The theme of “agency” is central to understanding how to design decision-making systems in which artificial intelligence supports, rather than replaces, the judgment, responsibility, and discretion of organizational actors. The emerging dialogue between leading scholars and policymakers will illuminate the organizational and institutional transformations generated by the integration of AI into decision-making processes, highlighting their implications for governance, public ethics, and the legitimacy of decisions in public contexts.
