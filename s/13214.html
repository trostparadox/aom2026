Session overview

Latin America represents one of the world’s most dynamic and rapidly evolving regions, encompassing more than 650 million people and a growing global diaspora. As a critical economic, cultural, and entrepreneurial force, the region offers significant opportunities for advancing management and entrepreneurship scholarship. Yet, despite its scale and relevance, research in and about Latin America remains fragmented and underrepresented in mainstream academic conversations. This Professional Development Workshop responds to calls for greater theorizing from the Global South by promoting collaboration between scholars based within and outside Latin America and emphasizing the importance of unique data, contextual richness, and methodological innovation, particularly in an increasingly AI-enabled research landscape. Many scholars outside the region are interested in conducting research on Latin America but lack contextual knowledge or collaborative pathways, while scholars based at Latin American institutions often face barriers related to mentorship, visibility, and access to global research networks. These asymmetries can contribute to misinterpretations of contextual meanings and the publication of work that may insufficiently reflect local realities. Beyond creating space for connection and idea exchange, the PDW emphasizes methodological techniques that can be intentionally integrated into research design, alongside positionality awareness as a reflexive approach to strengthening interpretation. Through panel discussions and interactive roundtables, participants will gain practical guidance on designing, conducting, and publishing research in and about Latin America. Overall, the PDW aims to strengthen the pipeline of rigorous, collaborative management and entrepreneurship research centered on Latin America and cultivate a sustained scholarly community This workshop consists of two parts. The first part, a panel discussion with invited speakers, is open to all attendees. The second part features interactive roundtable sessions designed for participants who submit abstracts in advance via email to the organizers and will receive developmental feedback on their research ideas.
