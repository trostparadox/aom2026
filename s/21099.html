The Role of Socioeconomic Status and Gender on Enactment of Forbearance Leadership

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Authors: Lucy Jeong Queens University; Matthias Spitzmuller

Session overview

Despite increasing organizational diversity, systematic inequalities based on socioeconomic status (SES) and gender persist, often privileging high-status groups in access to developmental opportunities. Drawing on Expectation States Theory (EST), this research examines how implicit status beliefs shape leaders’ perceptions of followers’ competence, thereby influencing the enactment of forbearance leadership, an autonomy-supportive leadership behavior in which leaders deliberately refrain from intervening to foster followers’ independent skill development. We hypothesize that leaders are more likely to enact forbearance leadership toward high-status followers (high-SES and men) than toward low-status followers (low-SES and women) because of perceived competence. Based on Intersectionality Theory, high-SES men are expected to be perceived as the most competent and thus receive the greatest forbearance leadership, while low-SES female followers will receive the least. To test these predictions, approximately 500 participants will complete a 2 (follower SES: high vs. low) × 2 (follower gender: man vs. woman) between-subjects experimental vignette study. Participants will assume a leadership role, respond to a follower’s work-related error, and report both their willingness to enact forbearance leadership and their perceptions of the follower’s competence. Expected results include main effects of SES and gender, an additive interaction favoring high-SES men, and mediation via perceived competence. Findings will extend EST to leadership, highlight SES as an understudied moderator of leadership, and clarify how everyday leadership behaviors may reinforce inequality in organizations.
