HRM and the Different Stages of Innovative Work Behavior

HR
Wali Ur Rehman (University of Essex); Matthew Allen (Manchester Metropolitan University)
Innovative work behavior (IWB) is widely recognized as a critical micro-foundation of organizational competitiveness. Prior research has predominantly treated IWB as a unitary construct, limiting theoretical insight into how human resource management (HRM) practices shape employee innovation across distinct stages of the innovation process. Building on the ability-motivation-opportunity (AMO) framework, we develop a stage-based model of IWB that theorizes differential effects of HRM practices on idea generation, idea promotion, and idea realization, and identifies employee autonomy as a key, yet contingent, mediating mechanism. We hypothesize that HRM practices designed to enhance employees’ abilities, motivation, and opportunities will be positively associated with each stage of IWB, and that perceived autonomy will mediate these relationships by enabling discretionary, agentic behavior. We test these hypotheses using time-lagged, multi-source data, allowing for stronger causal inference. Our findings reveal important asymmetries that challenge dominant assumptions in strategic HRM. Ability-enhancing HRM practices are negatively associated with all three stages of IWB, suggesting that investments in skills and training, which are often central to high-performance work systems, may inadvertently suppress innovative behavior when they reinforce standardization, efficiency, or narrow role prescriptions. In contrast, motivation-enhancing practices exhibit strong and consistent positive relationships with idea generation, promotion, and realization, underscoring the strategic importance of incentive alignment and commitment-based HR architectures for sustained innovation. Opportunity-enhancing practices are positively related to idea generation and realization, but not idea promotion, indicating that opportunity structures may differentially support ideation and implementation while leaving intra-organizational influence processes underdeveloped. Mediation analyses further advance strategic HR theory by demonstrating that autonomy functions as a selective conduit through which HR systems shape innovative behavior. Autonomy mediates the relationships between ability-enhancing practices and early-stage IWB, as well as between opportunity-enhancing practices and all three stages. However, autonomy does not mediate the effects of motivation-enhancing practices, suggesting that motivation-oriented HR systems may directly elicit innovative effort without necessarily expanding employee discretion. By integrating stage-specific innovation processes with strategic HR system design, this study contributes to strategic HRM theory by (1) revealing unintended consequences of ability-focused HR investments, (2) refining AMO-based explanations of HR system effectiveness through a contingency-oriented, mechanism-based lens, and (3) clarifying how autonomy operates as a strategic design feature rather than a universal mediator. Overall, the findings offer a more granular understanding of how HR systems create, and sometimes constrain, the micro-foundations of organizational innovation.

Broadening vs. Limiting: How Generative AI Training Shapes Dual Pathways to Employee Innovation

HR
Wenjia Zhang (Beijing Jiaotong University); Ying Lu (Macquarie University); Yue Qiao (Shandong University); Xin Qi; Yuhuan Xia (Shandong University)
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is rapidly reshaping organizational work and innovation, yet its consequences for employees’ innovation performance remain ambiguous. While GenAI can broaden knowledge and stimulate creativity, it also risks inducing inertia and constraining originality. Despite these competing possibilities, little is known about how human resource management (HRM) practices shape the innovation outcomes of GenAI use. Building on knowledge-based theory, we conceptualize GenAI as part of a socio-technical system shaped by HRM practices—most critically, GenAI training. Using a multi-wave, multi-source survey of 114 leaders and 700 employees in a large Chinese enterprise, we find that GenAI training channels employees’ use of GenAI toward knowledge breadth and enhanced innovation performance, whereas its absence fosters knowledge inertia and undermines innovation. By revealing how HRM practices condition the effects of GenAI, our study advances theory at the intersection of HRM and AI-in-management and offers practical insights for organizations seeking to unlock the innovative value of GenAI.

What Makes Agile Teams High Performing? Evidence From Two Decades of Empirical Research

HR
Kritika Sahu (Banaras Hindu University); Ram Shankar Uraon (Banaras Hindu University)
In a global economy that continues to evaluate organizational responsiveness, agile teams have emerged in a variety of practice and research areas. However empirical understanding of what makes an agile team high performing remains fragmented. This systematic literature review integrates findings from 44 studies published between January 2001 to July 2025 and tries to identify agile work practices that impact sustained team productivity and satisfaction. Guided by the Input-Mediator-Output-Input (IMOI) model, the review reveals that iterative development, sprints, daily stand-up meetings, and retrospective meetings strengthen team communication, coordination and reflexive learning. This ultimately enhances productivity, adaptability, and satisfaction. The findings indicate that agile teams attain their peak levels of performance when agile work practices operate within an ecosystem characterized by psychological safety and shared & adaptive leadership. By integrating Job Demands-Resources and Social Learning theories, the study proposes the conceptual model of high-performance agile practices and clarifies mediating mechanisms that link agile practices and outcomes at the team levels. The review contributes to cohesive theoretical development by unifying previously siloed research and provides practitioners with a clearer framework for building agile teams that are resilient, adaptive, and consistently high performing. Using DEMATEL (Decision-Making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory), the study strengthens the SLR by confirming the causal relationships embedded in the proposed high-performance agile team model.
