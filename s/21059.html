Governing the Void in Hinterland Access: Rail (Dis)Connectivity and Stakeholder Tensions

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 6

Authors: Nejat Köymen; Murat Atalay Akdeniz University

Session overview

Ports increasingly compete not only on quay-side performance but on how well they connect inland markets. This study examines the Port of Antalya (Türkiye), a strategically located regional gateway that operates without a direct railway connection and therefore relies heavily on road freight for hinterland access. The research investigates how rail (dis)connectivity shapes port competitiveness through economic, governance, and sustainability consequences, and how stakeholders interpret and respond to these outcomes. Adopting an interpretive qualitative case design, data were derived from 14 semi-structured interviews with port managers, public-sector representatives, exporters, logistics firms, and academics. Data were transcribed, anonymized, and analyzed via thematic analysis. Findings show five linked themes: rail disconnection as a competitive bottleneck; hinterland constraints that lock in road dependence; sustainability and urban externalities as a social license tension; multi-actor planning frictions and fragmented infrastructure governance; and future pathways for responsible transition (including interim inland logistics solutions and longer-term reconfiguration options). The study advances understanding by framing infrastructure gaps not merely as technical deficits but as governance problems with clear distributive and environmental consequences. Practically, results highlight the need for coordination mechanisms that translate multimodal ambitions into implementable, locally credible infrastructure pathways.
