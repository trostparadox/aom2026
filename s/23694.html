Impact of Negative Career Shocks on Employee Knowledge Hiding: A Moderated Mediation Model

CAR
Aqsa Mehreen (Zhejiang University)
Existing research on career shocks has paid limited attention to their workplace consequences, particularly how negative career shocks shape employee behavior. Drawing on the Stressor–Strain–Outcome (SSO) framework, we examine how negative career shocks lead to quitting intentions and knowledge-hiding behavior. We also propose that moral identity serves as a buffer, weakening these effects. Using a time-lagged survey of 335 hospitality employees and testing our model in Mplus, we found that negative career shocks significantly heightened quitting intentions and knowledge hiding, while moral identity moderated both relationships. Employees with stronger moral identities were less inclined to consider quitting, even when confronted with stressful career disruptions. We conclude by offering practical guidance for fostering healthy HR practices and supporting the retention of top talent.
