Attentional Hijacking or Task Anchoring? How Leader Error Feedback Influences Subordinates’ Attentio

OB
Bolin Luo (Zhejiang University)
Despite the growing emphasis on error management in organizations, a “feedback paradox” persists: why do leader interventions, often rooted in constructive motives, yield divergent outcomes? Drawing on Feedback Intervention Theory (FIT), we resolve this paradox by distinguishing between two behavioral enactments: Self-Oriented Error Feedback (SOEF), focusing on personal attributes, and Task-Oriented Error Feedback (TOEF), focusing on diagnostic analysis. We propose a dual-path model where SOEF captures attention at the meta-task level, triggering affective rumination and subsequent psychological withdrawal, while TOEF anchors attention at the task level, stimulating problem-solving pondering and learning behavior. Furthermore, we identify self-esteem as a buffer for SOEF’s maladaptive effects and task routineness as a boundary that renders TOEF’s informational value redundant. Three interconnected studies—including scale development (Study 1), a time-lagged critical incident study (Study 2), and a field study among newly graduated nurses (Study 3)—support our model. Ultimately, we advance error management and FIT literature by shifting focus from distal leader orientations to proximal behavioral enactments, illustrating how error feedback is framed determine organizational learning outcomes.
