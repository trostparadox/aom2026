AI Content Creation Agents:A Comparative Study of Fully Automated and Human–AI Collaborative Modes

Authors: Shujun Chen Communication University of China; Yue Guan Communication University of China; yining wang Hong Kong Baptist University

Session overview

On algorithmically governed content platforms, content production and dissemination are increasingly shaped by platform recommendation logics. At the same time, advances in large language models have enabled artificial intelligence (AI) content creation agents to evolve from assistive tools into autonomous or semi-autonomous actors capable of continuously generating content, interacting with audiences, and adjusting strategies over time. However, existing research has largely focused on how human creators adapt to algorithmic systems or has treated AI primarily as a creative support tool. As a result, limited attention has been paid to systematically comparing content creation modes characterized by different levels of AI autonomy and examining how they perform within algorithmic environments.This study situates itself within algorithmic recommendation platforms and compares fully automated AI content creation agents with human–AI collaborative modes in terms of performance dynamics, learning mechanisms, and strategy evolution during sustained content production. Drawing on sociotechnical systems theory and research on human–AI collaboration, the study conceptualizes AI agents as decision-making actors embedded within platform algorithmic governance structures. Using a controlled field experiment conducted in a real environment, and holding account initialization and posting rules constant, the study systematically examines differences between the two modes in content exposure, user engagement, and adaptive strategy adjustment.The study aims to reveal how varying degrees of AI autonomy shape agents’ interpretation of and responses to algorithmic feedback. By doing so, it extends theorization of creative actors and learning processes in algorithmic content platforms and offers empirical insights for the design of human–AI collaborative content production systems.
