Champions, Allies, and Change Agents: Men as Inclusive Leaders

DEI
Julia C. Nentwich (University of St. Gallen); Elisabeth K. Kelan (King's College London); Anja Kirsch
While inclusive leadership has garnered scholarly attention, the social identity of inclusive leaders remains largely unexplored. This article problematises this by reviewing 66 studies on the role of men’s leadership in advancing gender equality in work organisations. The integrative literature review identifies three key themes: First, men are constructed as complicit champions, whose support for gender equality at work is influenced by the power and privilege conferred by the gender system. Second, men employ various practices of allyship, and the effectiveness of those practices is related to allies’ social identity as men. Finally, becoming a change agent for gender equality at work is constructed as a process that men as leaders navigate over time. Based on this review, a research agenda is proposed that emphasises intersectionality, moves beyond an individualistic lens on inclusive leadership, and considers the vulnerabilities involved in becoming a change agent. Overall, this article highlights how leaders’ social identity matters for the change agency they can unfold. Furthermore, it contributes to advancing the understanding of men as inclusive leaders for gender equality.
