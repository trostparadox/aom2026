Orchestrating Consensus: How Social Bots Strategically Steer Public Opinion in Online Social Network

CTO
Linzi Zhang (Communication University of China); Zhensong Chen (Capital University of Economics and Business); Manman Du (Communication University of China)
Despite the growing influence of social bots in shaping public opinion online, little is known about how their strategic interventions affect the formation and evolution of collective consensus. Addressing this gap, we develop a multi-view hypergraph model that captures the triadic interactions among users, texts, and communities, overcoming the limitations of traditional binary network representations. Using data from two trending events on Weibo, we empirically examine how bot placement strategies—including timing, quantity, connectivity, and engagement patterns—influence consensus dynamics. Our findings reveal several key mechanisms: (1) bot influence follows an inverted U-shaped curve with respect to intervention intensity; (2) an optimal “golden window” exists for bot deployment; (3) network topology moderates effectiveness, with scale-free networks favoring centralized targeting and small-world networks responding better to distributed strategies; and (4) excessive bot presence can trigger audience backlash, such as spiral of silence. This study contributes to the literature on human–bot interaction and digital opinion ecosystems, offering evidence-based insights for platform governance and consensus management.
