Session overview

Globally, merger and acquisition (M&A) deal value in 2025 reached approximately USD 4.5 trillion, marking the second-highest annual volume on record (Bain & Company, 2025). Yet, despite this unprecedented scale, many transactions fail to generate anticipated value (King, Wang, Samimi, & Cortes, 2021). While prior research has examined multiple drivers of distinct acquisition choices and outcomes including deal characteristics, bidder attributes, and market reactions (Haleblian, Devers, McNamara, Carpenter, & Davison, 2009), insights remain inconclusive (King et al., 2021). A growing body of research considers the role of a new explanatory set of factors: ownership structure (e.g., Goranova, Dharwadkar, & Brandes, 2010; Feldman, Amit, & Villalonga, 2019; Nary & Kaul, 2021), yet insights still remain fragmented. Owners, including institutional investors, blockholders, founders, and other concentrated stakeholders, play a central role in shaping strategic (Boyd & Solarino, 2016), monitoring managerial actions, and influencing decision-making before, during, and after acquisitions. Importantly, different ownership forms may also shape the degree of interpretation of information asymmetry surrounding acquisitions, influencing how deal-related information is produced, disclosed, and evaluated by key stakeholders. While existing studies have begun to shed light on how ownership affects acquisition choices and performance, our understanding is still limited, especially regarding how different ownership forms interact with governance mechanisms, managerial incentives, and human-capital considerations. This symposium aims to bring together research that illuminates the mechanisms through which ownership acts as a fundamental driver of acquisition strategies and outcomes.
