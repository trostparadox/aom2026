A Dual-Path Model of Coworkers’ Positive and Negative Responses to Ethical Voice Failure

OB
Bo Lv (Renmin University of China); Jie Xiao (Renmin University of China); Shijie Zhao (Renmin University of China)
Despite ethical voice has received considerable attention in both research and practice, it is often rejected by leaders due to its inherent risks. Yet, ethical voice failure—such a prevalent and important phenomenon—remains underexplored. To extend this line of inquiry, we draw on social comparison theory to investigate when and how coworkers react positively or negatively toward ethical voice failure. Specifically, we argue that when coworkers possess higher ethical voice self-efficacy, ethical voice failure can elicit coworker empathy, which subsequently promotes organizational citizenship behavior toward the ethical voicers. In contrast, for coworkers with lower ethical voice self-efficacy, ethical voice failure can evoke coworker schadenfreude, resulting in negative gossip toward the ethical voicers. Using a two-wave, multi-source field study, we find the support for all of our hypotheses. We contribute to the literature on ethical voice, social comparison, and the failure of proactivity.
