Basic Research, Endogenous Growth, and Firm Growth

TIM
Haican Diao (Institutes of Science and Development, Chinese Academy of Sciences); Yanqun Zhang; Chen Xu
Fostering original innovation is crucial for maintaining a nation's sustainable competitive advantage. Using machine-crawling and manual data collection, this study is the first to construct a measure of basic research for Chinese listed companies from 2010 to 2020 using scientific publication data. From the perspective of endogenous growth theory, we investigate the impact and underlying mechanisms of basic research on firm growth. The empirical results demonstrate that basic research significantly enhances firm growth. Mechanism analysis reveals that basic research promotes firm growth by increasing productivity, improving product quality, and accumulating innovation elements, thereby facilitating the conversion of knowledge into technology. This effect is particularly pronounced in state-owned enterprises (SOEs), firms in central and western regions, technology-intensive and labor-intensive industries, and the manufacturing sector. Furthermore, external innovation networks and the academic experience of senior executives positively moderate the relationship between basic research and firm growth. The analysis of lagged effects indicates that long-term accumulation of basic research can catalyze major technological innovations. This study not only enriches the literature on basic research but also provides robust evidence for firms to engage in such activities. It further offers policy implications for promoting technological self-reliance and achieving high-quality development.
