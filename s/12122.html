Session overview

Platforms are reshaping innovation, competition, and entrepreneurship, yet identifying the mechanisms that drive these complex, multi-level systems remains challenging. This panel symposium addresses that gap by bringing together scholars who use formal models alongside empirical and qualitative approaches to illuminate how platform design choices (from technologies to organizations to market strategies) shape innovation trajectories and ecosystem outcomes. The session is timely: management research has adopted foundational concepts originating in formal economic modeling (e.g., network effects, compatibility, modularity), but there is renewed demand for mechanism clarity, testable counterfactuals, and cumulative theory-building relevant to managerial practice. Our aims are threefold: (1) Showcase compact use cases where formal models clarify mechanisms that empirical work struggles to isolate; (2) Surface frontier questions from non-modellers acting as “critical friends,” ensuring relevance to managers, strategists, and entrepreneurs; and (3) Co-produce integration strategies that strengthen publication positioning across TIM, STR, and ENT. The panel features a methodologically eclectic group (Carmelo Cennamo, David Clough, Carolyn Fu, Martin Ganco, and Aija Leiponen) who will offer propositions and provocations grounded in active research programs spanning technology strategy, ecosystem governance, and standards-setting.
