Metaphors of Leading: Deep Figurative Frames and Leadership Legitimacy Across Cultural Contexts

MOC
Makoto Nagaishi (Chukyo University)
This article examines how leaders construct legitimacy through metaphorical narratives across local and global contexts. Drawing on metaphor theory (Lakoff and Johnson), organizational metaphors (Morgan), and cultural–institutional perspectives on discourse and meaning-making, the study analyzes public interviews with Mukesh Ambani, Chairman of Reliance Industries, and Satya Nadella, CEO of Microsoft. Through discourse and metaphor analysis, the article identifies deep figurative frames—patterned configurations of metaphors recurring across strategic, technological, cultural, and value domains. Ambani articulates leadership legitimacy through metaphors of survival, illumination, protection, and continuity, aligning his discourse with national developmental imaginaries and the institutional logic of family capitalism. In contrast, Nadella mobilizes metaphors of navigation, technological breakthroughs, learning, and facilitation, framing legitimacy around adaptability, innovation, and distributed empowerment within globally circulating cultural repertoires of technology leadership. The comparative analysis shows that metaphorical narratives function as culturally embedded symbolic resources that orient sensemaking and legitimacy evaluations, rather than as universal cognitive structures. The study contributes by conceptualizing deep figurative frames as institutionally situated discursive resources, and by advancing culture-sensitive research on leadership and legitimacy.
