Session overview

‘Freshwater change’, in contrast to climate change, is an understudied planetary boundary in the organizational literature and whose limits are exceeded. Irrigation and the rise of data centers are examples of current and important sources of stress for freshwater systems. It is clear that ‘business as usual’ is insufficient, requiring new and deeper insights on how organizations depend on, impact, and can support freshwater stewardship. To this end, organization scholars can benefit from understanding freshwater stewardship from a systems lens, or the understanding of complex and dynamic relationships among organizations and the natural environment. This PDW aims to inspire organization scholars to begin exploring research on freshwater stewardship through the lenses of systems, interdisciplinary engagement, and other meaningful approaches. The PDW begins with an introduction to the topic and ‘state of the literature’, setting the stage for short presentations. Presenters will offer diverse viewpoints to inspire further research, including: insights from freshwater systems science and practice, promising theories for understanding relationships among organizations and freshwater stewardship, qualitative and quantitative methods for identifying and assessing these relationships, and topics relevant to practice. A discussant will reflect on themes and questions that can inspire organizational research on freshwater stewardship. The final part features roundtable discussions, encouraging participants to critically engage with the topics presented. Our PDW provides a platform for scholars from ONE and other AOM divisions (i.e., OMT, PNP, SIM, STR) to exchange ideas and address research gaps at the intersection of sustainability, organizations, and freshwater systems.
