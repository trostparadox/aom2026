Intentionality in GenAI-Assisted Work: An Integrated Process Model of ACTIF

Author: Maggie Minghui Cheng Concordia University

Session overview

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly embedded in knowledge work, yet individuals using similar tools often exhibit significantly divergent trajectories, ranging from growing mastery to increasing dependence. Existing theories of regulation, including self-regulation, sociomateriality, and algorithmic management, offer partial insights but struggle to explain how intentional judgment is enacted when work unfolds through ongoing interaction with generative systems. In this paper, I develop ACTIF, a process model that conceptualizes intentionality as a form of regulation enacted through five interrelated dimensions: Agency, Calibration, Trace, Iteration, and Feedback. Rather than treating GenAI as either a work aid or an external constraint, ACTIF theorizes regulation as an ongoing, iterative process within which individual standards, evaluations, and adjustments are continuously negotiated through an integrative process. By specifying how intentionality is enacted and sustained over time, ACTIF provides a foundation for explaining why similar technologies produce divergent developmental trajectories and offers a platform for future theory on regulation and learning in GenAI-assisted work
