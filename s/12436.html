Influencing the Willingness to Continue Working of Older Employees Through Job Design & Job Crafting

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Hiske den Boer Open Universiteit

Session overview

Due to the aging of the workforce and the increasing structural labor shortage in Western economies, it is important to design suitable jobs for older workers that support their sustainable employability in the tight labor market. Research indicates that effective job design, specifically tailored for older workers, can significantly enhance their willingness to remain in their current roles. This can be accomplished through two approaches: employer-driven job design (top-down) and job crafting (bottom-up), where older workers actively adjust their roles to better align with their skills and needs. This poster summarizes findings of various job design studies that organizations can implement to encourage older workers to stay in the workforce. We propose that organizations should: (1) ensure that job design for older workers prominently includes elements such as autonomy, learning opportunities, and social support; (2) differentiate job redesign efforts based on the specific job categories of older workers; and (3) offer a job crafting intervention, allowing older employees to proactively improve their working conditions, which can enhance their long-term employability. Keywords: older workers; job design; job crafting; working longer; job satisfaction.
