Action Research on Building an OKR Implementation Climate in a Large HR Function.

STR
Natalia Startseva (Universität Innsbruck)
Strategic intent in organisations often fails to shape daily work: quarterly priorities exist, yet ad-hoc requests and siloed debates dominate team backlogs. This paper theorises and examines how an OKR-tool-cadence governance bundle can translate organisational strategy into day-to-day tasks by forming an OKR implementation climate - shared perceptions that OKR use is expected, supported, and recognised. Building on HRM system strength (distinctiveness, consistency, consensus) and implementation-climate theory, two targeted interventions are analysed: establishing an ambassador network and standardising cadence. The study follows a full-scale OKR rollout in HR department (approximately 250 FTE) within an automotive software organisation. Using an action-research design, dated artefacts (planning decks, calendars, templates, Jira issues, meeting minutes) and brief retrospective interviews are assembled into temporally bracketed episodes and process-traced to identify mechanism activation. Evidence indicates that stronger signals consolidated a climate that normalised OKR-adjacent routines and activated a reproducible mechanism chain: transparency and goal clarity first, followed by commitment/focus, cadence discipline, and learning loops. Boundary conditions included uneven retro uptake and clashes with finance/bonus rhythms. The paper contributes a mechanism-rich account of HR strategy implementation and a portable, auditable governance and evaluation template for staff functions adopting OKR governance under conditions of high work interdependence.
