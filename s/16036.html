Session overview

Emotions are an indispensable part of organizational life. Beyond reflecting individuals’ internal states, the expression of emotions also serves as socially functional cues that shape others' judgments and behaviors (Van Kleef, 2009). Although a substantial body of research has studied the interpersonal effects of emotions, several unresolved yet prevalent trade-offs have emerged in the prevailing literature on expressions of positive or negative emotions. This symposium explores and addresses these trade-offs between assertiveness and trust, urgency and minimizing distress, idea fluency and novelty, clarifying moral standards and maintaining relationships, and conveying empathy and authority. Employing diverse methodologies—theory building, experiments, field studies, and qualitative inquiry – our five papers introduce and examine a set of novel yet theoretically and practically meaningful boundary conditions that help individuals actively navigate the trade-offs inherent in expressing and responding to emotions. These include typologies of shaming (calling in, reintegrative shaming, stigmatizing shaming, and canceling), anger triggers (e.g., compassionate anger), the explanation (vs. no explanation) of incidental and integral negative emotions, creativity dimensions (idea fluency vs. novelty), and receivers’ response strategies (work-focused vs. relationship-focused). Our discussant, Dr. Gerben A. Van Kleef, who developed the foundational theory on the interpersonal effects of emotions, will close the symposium by offering insights on how these papers collectively shape new directions in this area of research.
