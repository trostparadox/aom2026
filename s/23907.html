Organizing Immersion: A Sociomaterial Study of AI-Driven Non-Wearable Interactive Environments

TIM
Chaolu Wang (Royal College of Art); Zhuojun Li (Tongji University); Yunsheng Su (Tongji University)
Sociomaterial research has fundamentally reshaped how scholars understand the constitutive entanglement of technology and organizing. Yet, despite its ontological commitments, existing empirical studies have largely examined technologies that remain experientially distinguishable as bounded artifacts— such as tools, systems, or platforms. This paper examines what happens when this implicit artifact-centered anchoring no longer holds. Even studies that emphasize fluidity, performativity, or becoming typically rely on empirical settings in which technologies retain identifiable material forms, interfaces, or systems that can be analytically foregrounded. Drawing on a qualitative, multi-site study of AI-driven, non-wearable immersive environments in experiential service settings, this study advances a design-oriented framework in which technology operates as a responsive environment rather than a discrete artifact. Centered on immersive narrative dining, the framework articulates an end-to-end automated pipeline that integrates multimodal generative AI, spatial computing, and real-time sensor-driven interaction, offering a replicable model for the design of next-generation experiential services. Our findings identify three shifts that challenge artifact-centered applications of sociomaterial theory: (1) organizational action emerges through environmental participation rather than artifact interaction; (2) managerial control is enacted through continuous calibration of environmental responsiveness rather than governance of stable systems; and (3) organizational routines stabilize as adaptive, improvisational patterns co-evolving with responsive environments. Theoretically, we argue that AI-driven immersive environments represent a boundary condition for artifact-centered sociomaterial theorizing and call for an extension that accounts for responsive sociotechnical environments. We contribute to Technology and Innovation Management research by showing how innovation increasingly involves designing and governing generative environments rather than implementing discrete technological artifacts, thereby reframing core assumptions about control, value creation, and organizational capability in AI-enabled contexts.
