Session overview

As generative AI becomes integrated into classrooms, workplaces, and daily life, a critical question emerges: when does AI augment human potential, and when does it undermine it? This symposium brings together researchers at the intersection of artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and organizational behavior who examine this question through rigorous behavioral experiments and theoretical analysis. Together, the five presentations offer converging evidence that AI's impact on human capabilities depends critically on system design, user characteristics, and the nature of human-AI interaction. Through an organizational cognitive science lens, the symposium pursues three interconnected goals. First, we investigate how people form mental models of themselves and their AI partners. A key concern with AI adoption is that poorly designed systems may diminish people’s ability to judge whether they are genuinely benefiting, making it harder to assess whether these tools are truly helping them. Second, we examine how this risk can be mitigated by designing interactions that scaffold human potential. Off-the-shelf AI often fails to support reflection, and learning, but careful interaction design can boost skills rather than replace them. Third, we reflect on the implications for organizations seeking to deploy AI in ways that enhance rather than diminish human capabilities. We present concrete cases where well-designed AI tools successfully augment human potential.
