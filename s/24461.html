Engaging with Career Chance Events: Adaptability, Work Values, and Social Origin

CAR
Maike Andresen (Otto-Friedrich University Bamberg); Victoria Charlotte Hammes (Otto-Friedrich University Bamberg)
Career trajectories are shaped not only by planning and effort but also by chance events, yet individuals differ markedly in their perception and utilization of such opportunities. Drawing on qualitative interviews (N=46) and a grounded theory approach, this study investigates how career adaptability resources and inherited work values jointly influence the recognition and use of career-related chance events, and how these patterns relate to social origin. Findings reveal four distinct types – Expander, Preserver, Striver, and Rooted – capturing variation in engagement with career chance events. Expanding on planned happenstance, career construction, and chaos theory of careers, the study shows that high adaptability alone is insufficient: alignment with development-oriented work values is crucial for actively leveraging serendipitous career opportunities. Social origin shapes the distribution of these combined resources, but structural disadvantages are not immutable; showing high adaptability resources and development-oriented values allowed to enhance career opportunity utilization for individuals from lower social backgrounds. The study advances theory by integrating psychological and sociocultural factors in the understanding of career chance events and offers practical guidance for human resource development, mentoring, and career counseling.
