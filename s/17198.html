A Head Start Without a Handshake: Team Context Introductions in Asynchronous Home Care Networks

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Andrew Kuznetsov Carnegie Mellon University; Christopher Dishop; Allen Samuel Brown Carnegie Mellon University; Ping-Ya Chao Carnegie Mellon University; Anita Williams Woolley Carnegie Mellon University

Session overview

Home-care networks coordinate asynchronously with informal roles and ad hoc channels, making “who to contact about what” difficult for newcomers (e.g., respite caregivers). Drawing on transactive memory systems (TMS), we test whether how team context is introduced shapes early teammate–task mapping and coordination. In a fast-paced online respite-care simulation (N=179), participants received onboarding artifacts with matched role coverage, packaged as either short narratives or a structured list summary. Narratives increased retrieval-oriented mapping and yielded higher completion of the highest-load interdependent episode (the multi-step “oval pill” chain), while summaries improved extrapolation to plausible responsibilities. Post-task self-report TMS and psychological safety were similar across formats. Results motivate behavioral operationalizations of TMS (e.g., first-contact routing) for short-duration, informal teams.
