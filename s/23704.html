His Career, Her Well-Being? Exploring Cross-Spousal Effects in Dual-Earner Couples

CAR
Akhila Veldandi (Institute of Management Technology Hyderabad); Sushanta Kumar Mishra
This study examines the cross-spousal effects of career satisfaction on subjective well-being among dual-earner couples, with particular attention to the mediating roles of work–family positive spillover and happiness transmission. Drawing on data from 452 working professionals comprising 256 dual-career couples, we test a crossover model in which one partner’s career satisfaction influences the other partner’s life satisfaction. Results provide evidence of significant crossover effects, demonstrating that career satisfaction extends beyond the individual to shape spousal well-being. These effects are mediated by positive spillover between work and family domains, which facilitates the transmission of affective experiences across partners. Further, we examine gender differences in these crossover relationships. Findings reveal a stronger effect of male spouses’ career satisfaction on female spouses’ life satisfaction than vice versa, indicating gendered asymmetries in career-related crossover processes. Overall, the study highlights the interdependent nature of career outcomes and underscores the importance of conceptualizing career satisfaction as a relational phenomenon with meaningful implications for employee well-being and family systems.
