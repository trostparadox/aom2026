Risk-Taking in Family Firms: The Interplay of Socioemotional Wealth and Sibling Involvement

ENT
Kaiyang Sun (Adelaide University); Henry Shi (Adelaide University)
Family firms differ in their strategic risk-taking, yet prior research often treats family involvement as homogeneous, overlooking intra-family heterogeneity. Drawing on the socioemotional wealth (SEW) perspective and agency theory, this study examines how three SEW dimensions, family control, family identification, and renewal of family bonds, influence risk-taking, and how sibling involvement in management conditions these effects. Using data from 512 Chinese listed family firms (2014-2023) and fixed-effects models, we find that family control and family identification reduce risk-taking, whereas renewal of family bonds through dynastic succession increases it. Sibling involvement in the top management team moderates these relationships by weakening both the risk-averse effects of family control and identification and the risk-enhancing effect of renewal. The findings highlight the multidimensional nature of SEW and identify sibling involvement as a key source of intra-family heterogeneity shaping strategic risk-taking of family firms.
