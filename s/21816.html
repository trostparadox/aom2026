Session overview

Multiple jobholding (MJH), or working more than one job at a time, has gained increasing scholarly attention in recent years. Despite the expansion of empirical research in this area, the field remains conceptually fragmented due to inconsistent terminology and definitions. Researchers use various labels, such as moonlighting, side-hustles, portfolio careers, hybrid entrepreneurship and gig work, often without clarity about which phenomena these terms describe. This creates a jingle-jangle fallacy, where the same label describes different phenomena, and different labels describe the same phenomenon, hindering literature synthesis and theoretical development in the long term. The planned Professional Development Workshop will address the jingle-jangle fallacy in the MJH literature. The session begins with an expert panel presenting their ideas on MJH conceptualizations, followed by an interactive Q&A to explore diverse international perspectives on the phenomenon. Structured roundtable discussions will then tackle the core challenge of any MJH research project: How to conceptualize, define and label the phenomenon, particularly when communicating with an international audience. Addressing the conceptual gap is promoting new initiatives that approach MJH in a more integrative and synthesized manner. In this regard, the session aligns with the Academy of Management's mission to foster interdisciplinary connections and inspire impactful scholarship on this growing phenomenon in many labor markets. Participants will gain actionable insights to advance their research and contribute to a more cohesive understanding of this critical workplace phenomenon, ultimately enhancing our understanding, how MJH impacts employee behavior and important workplace outcomes.
