More Resources, More Vulnerable? Performance Pressure and the Wealth Paradox

OB
QI CUI (Renmin University of China); Qiqi Wang; Jun Liu; Lifeng Zhong
Performance pressure has become pervasive in modern organizations, yet our understanding of when and why it triggers unethical conduct remains incomplete. Drawing on conservation of resources theory and the wealth paradox perspective, we propose that performance pressure leads to expediency through workplace psychological scarcity, employees’ subjective perception that available workplace resources are insufficient to meet their demands. We further propose that workplace psychological scarcity operates through two sequential pathways involving bottom-line mentality and emotional exhaustion. Additionally, we challenge conventional predictions by proposing that employees with higher current financial standing experience paradoxically intensified workplace psychological scarcity under performance pressure. Across three studies, we find support for our predictions. Study 1 develops and validates the workplace psychological scarcity scale. Study 2 provides experimental evidence for the dual pathways and the moderating role of financial standing. Study 3 demonstrates these relationships in a four-wave field study. These findings advance understanding of resource-based psychological mechanisms linking performance pressure to unethical conduct and reveal when financial abundance paradoxically increases vulnerability to organizational demands.
