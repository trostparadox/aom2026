A Norm-Based Perspective on the Nonlinear Consequences of Leader Leniency for Follower Deviance

OB
Jiang Xu (Beijing Sport University)
Departing from past research on leader leniency, this study proposes and examines a nonlinear relationship between leader leniency and follower workplace deviance. By integrating the “the too-much-of-a-good-thing” perspective with norm research, we theorize that leader leniency exerts an increasing curvilinear effect on followers’ perceived norm violation, such that the positive relationship gradually intensifies as leader leniency increases, which may eventually lead to greater follower workplace deviance. We further propose that the curvilinear effects of leader leniency hinge on followers’ moral identity. Using data from a time-lagged field survey, we find support for our hypotheses. The findings contribute to our understanding of the complex dynamics between leadership behavior and employee misconduct, highlighting the role of leader leniency in shaping organizational norms and employee behavior. Implications for theory and practice are discussed.
