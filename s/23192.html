Divided Attention and Venture Performance: An Attention-Based View of Hybrid Entrepreneurship

ENT
Baris Istipliler (University of Mannheim); SAMUEL ADOMAKO; Mujtaba Ahsan (San Diego State University)
This study examines how hybrid entrepreneurship affects venture performance. Using the attention-based view (ABV), we argue that hybrid entrepreneurs’ divided attention leads to capacity underutilization, impairing venture performance. Further, we posit that this negative meditation of capacity utilization is mitigated for ventures with larger top management teams, more employees and higher levels of innovativeness. Using 20,362 venture-year observations from a panel of German start-ups from 2013 to 2020, we find support for our hypotheses. Our findings contribute to hybrid entrepreneurship research by revealing the mechanisms and conditions influencing venture outcomes.

Dividing Attention: How AI Usage Reshapes Attention Allocation in Founding Teams

ENT
Ruidong (Dong) Zhu (Universität Innsbruck); Luise Kaufmann (Saïd Business School, University of Oxford); Yanan Li (Copenhagen Business School)
Although attention is central to entrepreneurship research, prior work rarely conceptualizes founding teams as systems of attention allocation and typically treats attention as a human cognitive constraint that shifts gradually through interaction and contextual change. We extend the attention-based view by examining how artificial intelligence (AI) reshapes attention allocation within founding teams and how these shifts influence opportunity recognition, evaluation, and execution. Drawing on the attention-based view, we apply the topic modeling approach to examine how attention is conceptualized in founding teams and then conduct an agent-based model to analyze the dynamic effects of these attention patterns. We find that a higher share of AI-using cofounders is associated with lower opportunity execution outcomes over time, while individualized accountability for AI-generated outputs improves performance.

How Climate Attention Emerges in an Entrepreneurial Ecosystem: A Situated Process Perspective

ENT
Kadígia Faccin (Fundação Dom Cabral); Luciana Maines Da Silva; Michele Kremer Sott (Fundação Dom Cabral)
This study examines how attention to the climate crisis is constructed and institutionalized in an entrepreneurial ecosystem (EE). Although environmental disasters temporarily expose territorial vulnerabilities, prior research does not explain how these shocks produce long-term sustainability outcomes. Drawing on a situated Attention-Based View, we investigate a flood-triggered realignment process in a Brazilian EE and show how collective attention evolves through five stages: blindness, shock, regress, struggle, and fixation. The findings reveal that disasters generate compulsory awareness but do not, by themselves, transform the ecosystem. The post-shock trajectory is shaped by whether actors regress to pre-crisis priorities or engage in attentional struggle, a process in which material evidence, temporal horizons, authority relations, and discursive framings are mobilized to define the meaning of reconstruction. Sustainability emerges only when governance mechanisms stabilize this struggle and protect climate attention from short-term economic pressures, leading to attention fixation for sustainability. This fixation anchors adaptation and climate risk as persistent criteria for entrepreneurial action, enabling the formation of a sustainable entrepreneurial ecosystem. The study advances research on SEEs by shifting the locus of explanation from crisis-driven change to the institutional protection of collective attention.

Hybrid Immigrant Entrepreneurs in High-Tech: Agent-Based Modelling for Decision-Making

ENT
Aliaksei Kazlou (Linköping University)
This study advances Entrepreneurial Learning Theory (ELT) by examining the unique behavioral strengths and limitations of foreign-born entrepreneurs in decision-making under uncertainty. Traditionally, ELT has largely overlooked the liability of foreignness, constraining its accuracy and comprehensiveness. However, the prominence of high-profile immigrant entrepreneurs and a substantial body of empirical research have prompted a critical reassessment of the factors shaping entrepreneurial decision-making, particularly in the context of hybrid entrepreneurship. We propose and articulate a novel theoretical framework that captures the nuanced trajectories of immigrant entrepreneurs, who often exhibit more deliberate and analytical approaches to strategic choices. Through agent-based simulations involving 2.8 million virtual entrepreneurs, this study identifies and clarifies the specific conditions under which foreignness either facilitates or hinders risk-laden decision-making. In doing so, we contribute a more expansive and inclusive formulation of ELT, advancing its integration of immigrant entrepreneurs and addressing the complex effects of liability of foreignness in this domain’s evolving understanding of venture creation within hybrid entrepreneurship.

Pension Reforms Reshape Late-Career Women’s Transitions from Hybrid Entrepreneurship (WITHDRAWN)

ENT
Christina Koutouroushi (Durham University Business School); Francesca Melillo (SKEMA Business School); Ahmed Maged Nofal (emlyon business school)
This study investigates how raising the pension age affects late-career women’s hybrid entrepreneurship dynamics. We argue that such reforms can unintentionally reduce natural transitions to full-time entrepreneurship while increasing returns to paid employment. Drawing on real options theory, we propose that raising the pension age diminishes both the value of exercising entrepreneurial real options and the benefits of holding them, prompting women to “close their real option portfolio” by exiting hybrid entrepreneurship. Using a 2011 UK reform that raised women’s pension age from 60 to 65, we find support for these predictions, with less pronounced effects among high-income women.
