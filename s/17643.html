From Abstract Values to Workplace Behaviors: An AI-Based Approach to Cross-Cultural Value Analysis

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9

People

Authors: Alina Starovolsky-Shitrit Tel Aviv University; Sharon Arieli; Johannes Kiesel GESIS – Leibniz Institute for the Social Sciences; Ella Daniel Tel Aviv University

Session overview

Understanding how values shape workplace behavior is central to international business research, yet most studies rely on abstract, survey-based measures that often fail to predict concrete actions. We address this gap by applying advanced AI language models to employee work goals, capturing values as they are instantiated in everyday behavior rather than merely stated ideals. Building on Maio’s framework of mental value representations, distinguishing between system-level, abstract, and concrete-instantiated values, we show that values expressed through behavioral instantiations at work are significantly more predictive of workplace outcomes (e.g., seniority, status, income) than traditional abstract measures. A cross-cultural comparison between Asian-American (N = 180) and Indian (N = 782) employees reveals that value alignment is significantly more substantial at the behavioral instantiation level than at the abstract level—highlighting how globalization is shaping shared expressions of workplace values. Methodologically, we introduce a state-of-the-art, scalable computational tool that extracts values from organic employee texts, overcoming the limitations of self-reports and the burden of manual annotation. This approach provides managers with a powerful lens to understand what truly drives employee behavior across borders, unlocking new possibilities for leadership, talent development, and global HR strategy.
