Problematizing Diversity and Inclusion Through Critical Pedagogy : An Ethic of the Isthmus

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1

Author: Youcef Bousalham

Session overview

Management education is increasingly saturated with discourses on diversity and inclusion. Yet recurrent incidents—such as the publication of xenophobic or misogynistic student writings in elite business schools—reveal a persistent gap between proclaimed values and lived practices. Rather than interpreting such cases as individual misconduct or moral failure, this paper interrogates the ordinary conditions that make these utterances possible, acceptable, and even humorous from within institutions that publicly celebrate diversity and inclusion. Drawing on a detailed analysis of a student-produced guide, the paper introduces the concept of peninsula thinking (pensée de la presqu’île) to describe a regime of ordinary perception characterized by strong in-group familiarity, naturalized boundaries between “us” and “them,” and the absorption of diversity and inclusion as ritualized and ultimately inoffensive values, what Rabelais called “frozen words.” In this regime, values circulate symbolically without ever functioning as ethical trials capable of unsettling group evidence. Against moralizing responses and disciplinary catharsis, the paper proposes an ethics of the isthmus as a critical and humanist pedagogical alternative. The isthmus designates a narrow and fragile space of passage, exposure, and displacement, through which individuals may confront the limits of their own group’s evidence. Rather than prescribing new values, the ethics of the isthmus seeks to reactivate diversity and inclusion as lived, risky experiences. The contribution lies in reconnecting critique, pedagogy, and lived experience, and in offering a way to form managers capable of sustaining dissensus, reflexivity, and ethical discomfort.
