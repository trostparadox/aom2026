Session overview

Generative AI is rapidly permeating scholarly workflows, from literature discovery and synthesis to writing and editorial processes. Yet, norms for disclosure, verification, and accountability remain fragmented. This panel symposium examines two tightly coupled governance challenges emerging in academic publishing: (1) the disclosure dilemma, in which transparent reporting of AI use may function as either a trust signal or a stigma cue, and (2) the policing burden, concerning how detection, verification, and responsibility should be distributed across the publication ecosystem. Bringing together perspectives from a researcher, reviewer, journal editor, university leadership, and technology provider, the session explores what constitutes "material" AI use, how verification can remain feasible without converting peer review into "AI detective work," and how accountability should be allocated when AI-enabled errors or fabrications enter the published record. The discussion culminates in a synthesis that translates stakeholder tensions into practical guidance for responsible integration of AI into scholarly work. Attendees will leave with three portable outputs: (i) an AI Disclosure Framework differentiating AI assistance across research phases (discovery, screening, analysis, writing), (ii) an Accountability Allocation Map specifying feasible duties of care across authors, editors/journals, reviewers, institutions, and tool providers, and (iii) a Verification Checklist outlining minimum due diligence expectations that strengthen auditability while protecting the sustainability of peer review.
