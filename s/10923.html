Session overview

Employee voice behavior (i.e., the expression of constructive opinions, concerns, or ideas about work-related issues; Van Dyne, Ang, & Botero, 2003) is important for organizational success and employee personal career advancement. At the same time, hierarchy is ubiquitous in social and organizational life and broadly shapes social dynamics (Durkheim, 1960; Magee & Galinsky, 2008). Power, individuals’ asymmetric control over valuable resources, is one of two fundamental bases of social hierarchy (Blau, 1964; Greer et al., 2017; Magee & Galinsky, 2008). Despite the fact that employee voice often occurs within hierarchical social relationships (e.g., unequal power), prior research has devoted limited attention to integrating hierarchy and voice (Pfrombeck, Levin, Rucker, & Galinsky, 2022). Leaders typically hold higher levels of power than their followers. Accordingly, employee voice inherently involves hierarchical dynamics, as lower-ranked employees speak up to higher-ranked individuals. Low-ranked employees may generate voice ideas informed by unique frontline insights that can yield substantial organizational benefits and help prevent unforeseen costs and risks. However, the voice of low-ranked employees is often less recognized and valued than that of higher-ranked employees (Howell, Harrison, Burris, & Detert, 2015; Pfrombeck et al., 2022). This symposium aims to advance our understanding of the interplay between social hierarchy and voice. The voice and hierarchy literatures are fundamentally intertwined, as voice often occurs within hierarchical social relationships. However, aside from a few extant works (Howell et al., 2015; MacMillan, Hurst, Kelley, Howell & Jung 2020; Pfrombeck et al., 2022), limited research in the past has integrated hierarchy and voice together. Hierarchy and voice scholars have yet to establish a comprehensive understanding of how the basis (e.g., power and status) of social hierarchy of followers and leaders may influence follower voice behavior (e.g., promotive, prohibitive, and supportive), quality of the voice episode (e.g., perceived value), and subsequent leader responses (e.g., endorsement and enactment). This symposium brings novel perspectives on understudied yet important voice-related phenomena (such as voice strategization, the disadvantage double bind, confirmatory solicitation, and voice ingratiation). In particular, we conceptualize the unique challenges faced by lower-ranked employees (i.e., the disadvantage double bind), highlight what employees can do to overcome this challenge (e.g., voice strategization), and explore what leaders may not do when they solicit voice from lower-ranked employees (e.g., confirmatory solicitation). We also explore how followers’ behavior (voice ingratiation) may be interpreted differently based on the power of the leader. We believe that this symposium will stimulate new ideas, perspectives, and theories that connect the hierarchy and voice literatures, facilitating sophisticated theorizing and rigorous empirical research in this line of research.
