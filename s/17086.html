Session overview

Collective behavior often draws ordinary individuals into actions that later feel difficult to explain, morally unsettling, or misaligned with their prior values. Leadership plays a central role in these moments by shaping how situations are framed, which emotions are amplified, and which actions come to feel legitimate or urgent. This symposium examines leadership as a form of social influence that operates through embodied processes, drawing on neuroscientific and physiological approaches to clarify how leadership influence emerges, is sustained, and shapes collective behavior across organizational contexts.
