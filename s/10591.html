Uncovering Invisible Expectations: Hidden Curriculum and Student Preparedness in Business Education

Authors: Isabella Sorenson Minnesota State University Mankato; Katheryn Zielinski Minnesota State University Mankato; Jennifer Lynn Schultz Minnesota State University Mankato

Session overview

Business school students are expected to meet a range of academic and professional expectations that extend beyond formal curricula. These expectations are embedded within the hidden curriculum and include unwritten behaviors, norms, and values that contribute to student success but remain largely implicit. As student demographics diversify and educational pathways expand, unequal exposure to the hidden curriculum contributes to disparities in preparedness and outcomes. Despite the potential impact, the hidden curriculum remains underexamined in business curricula. Grounded in Jackson’s (1968) formative work on the hidden curriculum and supported by contemporary scholarship, this study examines how implicit institutional expectations shape students’ experiences and success in business education. Drawing on research highlighting structural inequities (Koutsouris et al., 2021), unexamined norms related to equality, diversity, and inclusion (Schwabenland & Kofinas, 2023), and the need for curricular integration (Ottewill et al., 2005), this study suggests that hidden curriculum is a critical factor in student success. Using a mixed-methods design, data were collected at a midwestern (US) public AACSB business school. The findings are expected to inform instructional and institutional practices by increasing transparency, fostering inclusivity, improving retention, and aligning the formal and hidden curricula. Ultimately, this research seeks to contribute to more equitable learning environments and increased student preparedness in business schools.
