Session overview

Research on virtual teams highlights that collaboration across geographical distance introduces persistent challenges related to time-zone differences, coordination, communication delays, and trust-building (Morrison-Smith & Ruiz, 2020). Research collaboration is a natural part of any academic career (Aïssaoui et al., 2020; Jonsen et al., 2013). An increasing share of published papers are written by teams rather than by a single author, and more teams include scholars from different countries. However, how these challenges are experienced and managed in academic research collaborations remains under-examined. Global research collaboration is growing rapidly as academic teams increasingly span countries and continents and has become a key feature of contemporary academic careers. More specifically, while this creates opportunities for knowledge exchange, around-the-clock productivity, accelerate research progress, and create new forms of intellectual synergy (Manchin, 2025), it also introduces significant challenges such as scheduling (Dusdal & Powell, 2021), cross cultural communication, language differences, and coordination, and varied academic norms technology (Richardson & Zikic, 2007). This PDW aims to provide a highly interactive forum for scholars at all career stages to explore the challenges, advantages of, and strategies for effective cross-time-zone collaboration. The time zone mix can be an advantage as much as a disadvantage if a research community is to be built. Drawing on insights from established international research teams spanning multiple continents, the PDW combines expert reflections with structured roundtable discussions to identify practical strategies, personal insights, routines, and lessons learned. By the end of the session, participants will leave with concrete, actionable practices for coordinating global research projects, maintaining trust and momentum across distance, and integrating international collaboration into sustainable academic career trajectories.
