Authors: Julian Jonathan (JJ) Markus Vienna University of Economics and Business; Rebecca (Bex) Hewett Erasmus University Rotterdam; Moritz Appels Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University

Session overview

Self-managing organizations (SMOs) radically decentralize authority by eliminating hierarchical reporting relationships and distributing decision-making among workers. By granting employees greater control over their work and organizational processes, SMOs are often portrayed as responses to pressing organizational and societal challenges. Yet leaders remain central to these transformations. Moves toward self-management are typically initiated by organizational leaders, who must relinquish formal authority and transfer strategic and financial responsibility to workers. This work-in-progress draws on a multi-case study of 37 SMOs and analyzes leaders’ narratives using interviews with leaders and workers, blogs, and archival sources. We identify systematic differences between leaders who articulate more evangelical ideological commitments to self-management and those who adopt a more pragmatist orientation. Organizations led by evangelical proponents are more likely to adopt governance arrangements that separate control from financial returns, such as foundation ownership models, whereas organizations led by pragmatists exhibit greater tendencies toward re-centralization and shareholder-oriented growth. Furthermore, the study shows how leaders’ narratives evolve through interactions with other leaders and external skeptics, shaping both organizational change and broader institutional dynamics. Overall, this study contributes to organization and management theory by explaining how and why leader ideologies shape organizational decentralization. This leader-centric perspective challenges accounts that attribute decentralization solely to efficiency concerns or notions that organizational leaders strive for power retention and instead highlights ideology as a key driver of leader decision-making and organizational design.
