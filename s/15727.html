An Organizational Behavior Perspective on Generative AI, Cognition, and Ethical Self-Regulation

Authors: Antigoni Papadimitriou State University of New York, Farmingdale State College; Zilong Pan Lehigh University

Session overview

The rapid diffusion of generative artificial intelligence (GAI) is reshaping how individuals learn, think, and make decisions, raising important questions for organizational behavior scholars about cognition, self-regulation, and ethical sensemaking. While students increasingly rely on AI tools, little is known about how instructional design can shape how individuals interact with AI rather than simply whether they use it. This poster examines how an active learning– based pedagogical framework—Active Learning–Informed Generative AI Integration (ALGAI)—supports the development of AI-interaction competence from an OB perspective. Using an explanatory sequential mixed-methods design, data were collected from 123 undergraduate students enrolled in an Organizational Behavior course. Quantitative results indicate significant gains in AI-related self-regulation, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and confidence in ethical AI use over the semester. Qualitative analyses of reflective assignments and project artifacts reveal a shift in students’ cognitive framing of AI, from an answer-generating authority to a bounded decision aid requiring judgment, contextual awareness, and accountability. By foregrounding cognition, metacognition, and ethical agency, this study contributes to OB scholarship on learning, self-regulation, and technology-enabled work. The poster offers a scalable instructional model and assessment approach relevant to OB educators and scholars interested in how individuals develop competence and responsibility when interacting with intelligent systems. Implications extend to future organizational contexts in which AI increasingly functions as a collaborator in decision processes.
