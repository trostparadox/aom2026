Session overview

Positive emotions have drawn scholarly attention for decades, often cast as a basis for organizational flourishing and employee well-being. Yet, some argue that this cursory glorification of positive emotions has engendered a “cult of positivity” that frequently bypasses a sobering tension: while “feeling good” is celebrated for broadening our cognitive horizons and building our personal resources to thrive, it can simultaneously lure us down a path of shallow heuristics and biased decision-making. This puzzle may be particularly paramount to consider in demanding environments, where critical thinking is imperative. However, existing organizational scholarship is limited in reconciling how and when these positive emotions function in the face of challenges, uncertainty, and crises. To address the complexity of positive emotions in adverse contexts, we present a diverse body of research that explores how specific positive discrete emotions—including gratitude, hope, nostalgia, relief, and optimism—serve as contingent psychological resources for sensemaking, collective persistence, relationship building, and more. Together, the papers in this symposium employ a multitude of methodological approaches (e.g., ethnographic study, critical incident technique, archival analysis, interviews) to advance our understanding of positive emotions as pathways to persisting through difficulty. In doing so, we offer a more sophisticated roadmap for employees and managers to intentionally cultivate positive emotional resources that foster authentic resilience in an increasingly volatile modern work landscape.
