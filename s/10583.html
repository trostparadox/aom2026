Session overview

Academic careers are being fundamentally reshaped by AI, altering how research is produced, teaching is delivered, and professional reputations are built. While business schools increasingly equip MBA and executive students with tools to navigate AI-driven workplaces, early-career academics—particularly PhD students nearing graduation and scholars in the early post-PhD years—often receive limited guidance on how these same forces affect their own careers. Expectations around productivity, visibility, and strategic positioning continue to rise, yet formal training in managing an academic career in the age of AI remains scarce. This PDW addresses this gap by offering actionable strategies for early-career academics to design and manage their careers strategically in an AI-enabled academic environment. Drawing on insights from senior scholars and interactive peer-to-peer activities, the workshop focuses on five interconnected areas: strategic academic positioning, portfolio-based research management, AI-enabled productivity, professional visibility and networks, and leadership as a career signal. Participants will explore how AI can be leveraged responsibly to enhance research efficiency, teaching preparation, and professional communication, while maintaining academic integrity. Through structured exercises and facilitated discussion, participants will develop a personalised action plan to navigate career decisions more intentionally, build sustainable momentum, and increase their impact within academia. The PDW aligns closely with the Careers Division’s mission by equipping early-career scholars with practical, forward-looking tools to thrive in an increasingly competitive and technologically disrupted academic landscape.
