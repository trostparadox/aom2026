Family Functioning as a Micro-Foundation of HR in Family Firms

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Ekin Gulsun Turan Koç University; Eda Aksoy Koç University

Session overview

Family firms exhibit persistent heterogeneity in how they manage nonfamily employees, yet dominant explanations often treat “the family” as a unified actor and under-specify how within-family relational dynamics translate into distinct HRM systems and HR practices. Building on family systems theory and the Circumplex Model (Olson, 2000), we theorize owner-family cohesion and flexibility/adaptability as micro-foundations shaping the HRM logics that guide how family firms design and implement HR practices. We investigate this argument through an exploratory, constructivist multiple-case study of 10 fully family-owned apparel and textiles firms in Türkiye, drawing on 21 semi-structured interviews. Cross-case analysis reveals a coherent typology of owner-family approaches to HRM that varies along two central HRM objectives: building employee attachment versus managing rule adherence. Firms characterized by higher owner-family cohesion tend to implement a growth-oriented, commitment-based HRM system, emphasizing belonging, loyalty, and relational investment, and they address employee missteps through a resolution-oriented set of practices that prioritizes explanation, learning, and reintegration. In contrast, firms characterized by low flexibility/adaptability tend to implement a more output-oriented, performance-focused HRM system, emphasizing standards, role clarity, monitoring, and execution, and they rely on enforcement-oriented practices—stricter discipline and escalating sanctions—to manage repeated or serious violations. The study contributes by linking family functioning to HRM system heterogeneity in family firms, clarifying when relational dynamics foster commitment-based practices versus when rigidity produces stronger control- and enforcement-oriented practices, and offering implications for HRM system design without undermining the relational foundations of family firms.
