When Does Voluntary Overqualification Pay Off? Proactivity and Need Satisfaction

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Authors: Hsi-Fang Lai National Chung Cheng University; Min-shi Liu National Chung Cheng University

Session overview

Drawing on self-determination theory, this study examines when and how voluntary overqualification (VOQ) is linked to employee performance. Using three-wave data from 406 supervisor–subordinate dyads, we found that VOQ was positively associated with basic psychological need satisfaction, which was, in turn, positively associated with task performance and organizational citizenship behaviors. Although the overall indirect effects were not statistically significant, proactive personality strengthened the VOQ–need satisfaction association, yielding significant conditional indirect effects on both performance outcomes at higher levels of proactivity. These findings suggest that need satisfaction may represent one motivational pathway through which VOQ is translated into better work performance, particularly among proactive employees.
