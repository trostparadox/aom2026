Session overview

Organizational networks are not merely conduits for information or resources but constitutive elements of organizing, shaping outcomes from innovation to job performance. As such, networks are central to understanding and inducing organizational change. Although the idea of “network interventions” has gained prominence in network research, a coherent theoretical framework and a comprehensive understanding of the methodological challenges remain underdeveloped. A potential reason is the dual role networks occupy within organizations. From an instrumentalist perspective, networks enable the diffusion of ideas, practices, and behaviors through local interventions on individuals or organizations. Through social influence or contagion, attitudes and behaviors travel along network ties, facilitating spread of innovations, uptake of new practices, and institutionalization of emerging norms. From an ontological perspective, networks are integral to organizational design, functioning and governance. As the informal architecture through which work is performed, knowledge circulates, and collaboration occurs, networks themselves become objects of organizational interventions, aimed at enhancing inclusion, coordination, performance, or systemic resilience. These two perspectives are heavily intertwined, highlighting a fundamental tension in network theory: Networks both enable change and serve as design elements to be transformed. This intersection raises crucial questions about network emergence, tension between agency and structure, legitimacy and power, and effectiveness, implications and ethical concerns of (re)shaping relational systems. This panel symposium advances a conceptual conversation about network interventions, discusses the promises and pitfalls they generate within network theory, and explores how insights from network research can be translated into impactful advice for practitioners and policy-makers.
