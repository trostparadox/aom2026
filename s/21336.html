Governance in Complex Supply Chains: Evaluating the CSDDD Through a Field Experiment

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
OSCM

People

Authors: Christopher Schwarz University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria; Markus Gerschberger University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria; Christoph Bode University of Mannheim

Session overview

Global supply chains are complex, multi-tier networks in which environmental and human-rights risks often arise upstream yet remain difficult for firms to identify. The European Union’s Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) seeks to address these challenges by mandating structured due-diligence processes, but little causal evidence exists on how such regulation affects managerial risk identification in practice. Integrating supply-network theory, complexity science, and regulatory governance, this study conceptualizes the CSDDD as an intervention that reshapes managerial attention and supply-chain governance under conditions of limited visibility. We test these arguments using a field experiment embedded in the procurement functions of firms in the German and Austrian automotive industry. Managers are randomly assigned to a CSDDD-guided or business-as-usual condition and conduct a structured risk-identification task based on real supplier portfolios. The results provide causal evidence on how mandatory sustainability regulation influences risk identification in complex supply networks, contributing to research on supply-chain governance, regulation, and decision-making under complexity.
