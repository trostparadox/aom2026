Ecological Mechanisms of Organizational Risk and Failure

Authors: Chaeyeong (Margo) Lee Rutgers University; Matthew S. Weber Rutgers University

Session overview

Decentralized finance (DeFi) is one of the most volatile and failure-prone segments of the contemporary U.S. financial sector. Despite rapid growth, many DeFi projects exhibit short life spans and fragile organizational structures. These patterns raise questions about the ecological mechanisms shaping survival outcomes and whether they differ from those in more established sectors such as fintech. Population ecology theory conceptualizes organizational failure as the outcome of environmental selection processes, emphasizing liability of newness, liability of smallness, and density dependence. Using monthly event history analysis of longitudinal data on DeFi projects and fintech firms, the analysis examines how organizational age, size, and population density condition survival over time, extending population ecology to emerging financial ecosystems characterized by extreme novelty and institutional instability.
