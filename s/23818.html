Bouncing Back from Voice Non-Endorsement: Mindsets Condition Leader Sensitivity on Voice Resilience

OB
Si Qian; YI XIN JIA (Beijing Technology and Business University); Pengzhou Kuai
Drawing on Conservation of Resources theory, we investigate why some employees sustain their proactivity while others withdraw after their ideas are rejected. Using three-wave data from 321 employee–supervisor dyads, we examine a moderated mediation model centered on voice resilience—the persistence of voice behavior following non-endorsement. Results indicate that leader explanation sensitivity protects an employee’s state self-esteem, which promotes voice resilience. Importantly, the necessity of this external support is contingent on the employee’s internal cognitive framework. For individuals with a fixed mindset, who view rejection as a personal inadequacy, leader sensitivity acts as an essential compensatory resource that shields their self-worth. In contrast, for growth-mindset employees, leader sensitivity had no significant effect. These individuals possess an internal ability to handle setbacks, making them less dependent on the leader’s interpersonal style to stay resilient. These findings show that leader support is most critical for keeping employees proactive when they lack internal resilience.
