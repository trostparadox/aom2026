Session overview

In recent years, the continued rise of antisemitism around the globe has directly impacted higher education and management scholars. Antisemitism is not only reshaping campus life but also raising profound questions about academic freedom, voice, and academic rigor. While awareness of antisemitism is growing, many management and organizational scholars, especially those who identify as Jewish or engage in research that attends to antisemitism or Jewish identity in the workplace, must navigate complex trade-offs between speaking, staying silent, and safeguarding their scholarly identity and institutional standing. Building on our award-winning AoM 2025 PDW, which brought together a diverse community of scholars to respectfully and emotionally discuss antisemitism in the workplace and in higher education, this PDW shifts the focus from awareness to ethical academic action. This PDW is designed to critically examine and respond to the dilemma scholars face of maintaining an authentic voice as Jewish academics while experiencing institutional pressures that result in marginalization, silence, and epistemic gatekeeping – particularly for those who want to align their research agenda with the Jewish experience at work. Grounded in organizational scholarship on epistemic exclusion, institutional courage, minority tax, and voice in organizations, we hope this PDW will enable participants, both as allies and as individuals who identify as Jewish, to identify actionable insights from their authentic scholarly voice in the pursuit of rigorous scholarship on antisemitism and the Jewish experience at work.
