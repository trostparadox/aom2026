Session overview

Organizations often suffer from a “consciousness gap”: a widening distance in perception, empathy, and mutual regard that makes the relational space between people inaccessible—especially under pressure, identity threat, or competing interests (Askun, 2023). This PDW offers a practice-based pathway for identifying, understanding, and closing consciousness gaps using Appreciative Inquiry technique (Krahnke & Cooperrider, 2008). Drawing on the oneness hypothesis which frames well-being and virtue through patterns of connection that benefit both individuals and the larger wholes they inhabit (Ivanhoe, 2017; Ivanhoe et al., 2018), participants learn to recognize when the self is expanded versus contracted in workplace interactions (Askun, 2023; Aron & Aron, 1986). Through brief framing, appreciative dyadic interviews, small-group dialogue, and a design sprint, participants will (a) map their gap triggers (e.g., self-centered threat responses, urgency, fairness dilemmas), (b) surface stories of “I-to-We” moments at work, and (c) co-design a micro-experiment to cultivate consciousness-between (e.g., appreciative check-ins, inclusive listening routines, stakeholder reflection). The session is designed for a mixed audience of scholars, doctoral students, and practitioners seeking actionable tools for spirituality at work, conscious leadership and communication, inclusion, and flourishing.
