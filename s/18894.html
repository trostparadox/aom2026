Neurodivergence at Work: A Systematic Review of Employee, Manager, and Recruiter Experiences

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Abhinanda Patra; Deepa R. Loyola Institute of Business Administration; Poornima Viswanathan

Session overview

Research on neurodiversity at work is growing, yet few studies triangulate the experiences of neurodivergent workers, managers, and recruiters in the workplace. Most studies in this area focus solely on the experiences of the neurodivergence itself; the perspectives of the other important parties who shape the experience of the employees are rarely explored at depth. Employers or supervisors and recruiters most often have jurisdiction on whether a regular employee feels included and supported at work. This is far more noticeable when it comes to neurodivergence when the normative rules of the workplace are set by these stakeholders. Moreover, emotional and social perceptions of neurodivergence by neurotypical entities at work often is missed out on in literature. Addressing this gap, a systematic literature review (SLR) was conducted to synthesize findings relating to the employment cycle of neurodivergent individuals, from recruitment and selection to day-to-day work, accommodation provision, and career development. Further, Content analysis was used to extract important themes identified in literature. Using Employment Mode Theory and Critical Disability Theory, findings show that neurodivergent employees often carry the burden to adapt, emphasizing an urgent need for workplaces to change. Neurodivergent employees are required to expend greater effort to participate on equal terms: from masking traits in interviews to navigating sensory and social demands at work informally. They often face stalled advancement due to limited formal support. Meanwhile, managers and recruiters express willingness to be inclusive but operate within systems that prefers neurotypical communication, profit over morality, and rigid performance structures.
