Mitigating Escalation of Commitment through Resource Convertibility

OB
Kai-Yu Hsieh (Chang Gung University)
While research on escalation of commitment has expanded beyond individual psychology to include broader contextual factors, the extant literature remains fragmented, lacking an integrative logic that links organizational, partnership, and market dimensions. To address this gap, the current research identifies “convertibility” of invested resources as a unifying concept. By transforming what appears to be irreversible commitment into recoverable assets, convertibility alleviates the dilemma between persistence and admitting failure. The empirical analysis examines the divestment decisions of Taiwanese manufacturing firms regarding their subsidiaries in mainland China from 2017 to 2024, situated against a turbulent backdrop of the U.S.–China trade war and the COVID-19 pandemic. The results indicate that high convertibility at the corporate, partner, and market levels could reduce a firm’s propensity to persist with a loss-making subsidiary. These findings underscore the efficacy of convertibility as a cross-level factor that facilitates de-escalation.
