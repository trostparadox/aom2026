Digital Strategic Attention, Resistance, and Recovery in Supply Chains

OSCM
Hantong Zhao (Harbin University of Commerce)
Why do firms with similar supply chain structures display sharply different resilience during the same disruption? Drawing on the attention-based view and organizational information processing theory, we argue that digital strategic attention to operations-relevant technologies shapes pre-shock investments and coordination routines that matter under shock. We develop a context-constrained text-mining measure of digital strategic attention from Items 1 and 7 of U.S. firms’ 10-K filings, combining seeded word-embedding expansion, a supply-chain context dictionary, and a ±10-token proximity rule. Using 1,250 U.S. manufacturing firms (2015Q1–2023Q4) and exploiting COVID-19 as an exogenous disruption, we estimate a difference-in-differences model with firm and quarter fixed effects. High-attention firms exhibit about a 12% reduction in inventory volatility after 2020Q2. In a Cox model of 980 firms experiencing a shock-induced decline in inventory turnover, high-attention firms recover 42% faster. The findings highlight managerial attention as a cognitive microfoundation of operational resilience and demonstrate the value of context-constrained text mining.
