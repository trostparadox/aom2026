Integrity versus Competence Misconduct in Family Firms

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Author: Anmol Pandey The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley

Session overview

Family firms are often portrayed as more ethical than non-family firms, yet prominent scandals challenge this assumption. Drawing on socioemotional wealth (SEW) theory, this conceptual study argues that family involvement has divergent effects across types of corporate misconduct. Integrating Connelly et al.’s (2016) distinction between integrity versus competence-related misconduct, we propose that family firms are less likely to engage in integrity-related misconduct due to strong ethical climates, but more likely to experience competence-related misconduct because of weaker adoption of high-performance human resource practices (HPHRPs). We develop four propositions outlining these direct and mediated relationships, offering a nuanced explanation for mixed findings in prior research and advancing theory on family firm ethics.
