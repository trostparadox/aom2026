Session overview

Entrepreneurship scholarship advances when researchers engage with the realities of entrepreneurial practice. While our field has long treated practice as a source of theoretical inspiration, the actual craft of transforming practitioner insight into scholarly contribution remains unevenly understood. Few researchers, and even fewer transitioning practitioner-scholars, receive explicit guidance on how to systematically convert rich, practical insight into viable theoretical contribution. At the same time, the boundary between practice and academia is shifting, with experienced entrepreneurs increasingly engaging the scholarly community through executive and DBA programs, teaching roles, and ecosystem leadership. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) addresses that gap by focusing on the co-creation of entrepreneurial knowledge: how practitioner experience can serve as a legitimate foundation for theory-building, and how scholars can use field engagement to deepen conceptual and empirical contributions. Through a combination of short panel presentations and highly interactive roundtable discussions, participants will learn how to identify theoretical mechanisms in lived entrepreneurial experience, navigate the challenges of researching in the field, and collaborate effectively across the practitioner–scholar boundary. The session will culminate in a co-created “Practitioner–Scholar Toolkit” for field-driven knowledge generation, designed to support research, teaching, and ecosystem engagement across the entrepreneurship community.
