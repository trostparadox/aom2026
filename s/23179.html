The Fabric of Decarbonisation Knowledge – The Organizational Manufacturing of Performative Metrics

CTO
Virginie Cauderay (University of Potsdam (Hasso Plattner Institute)); Thomas Haskamp; Falk Uebernickel (ZHAW School of Management and Law)
While an accounting-based perspective has become central to aligning economic activities with the planetary boundary of climate change, the reliability of decarbonisation data and metrics is increasingly being questioned, particularly in the assessement of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from value chains (scope 3). Building on prior literature in management and Information Systems (IS) research, this study seeks to better understand the limitations of the so-called data-driven mitigation mechanisms, core to the accounting-based perspective, by exploring how firms manufacture their decarbonisation metrics. Using a two-phase qualitative study, we identify three mechanisms that shape the manufacturing process of decarbonisation metrics. First, the specifying mechanism, aimed at increasing data accuracy. Second, the assuring mechanism, aimed at ensuring data reliability. Third, the estimating mechanism, aimed at enabling calculations through assumptions. Our findings highlight that decarbonisation metrics are not mere reflections of physical realities but are actively shaped by companies to satisfy stakeholders’ expectations for performative metrics. We thus argue for the need to consider the underlying socio-technical influences inherent to digital resources in the quantification process of corporate environmental burdens. With this research, we contribute to the emerging discourse questioning the implications of positioning data as a central component of environmental mitigation mechanisms.

Harnessing Generative AI: Ambidexterity, Risk, and Ethics in Supply Chain

CTO
Khalid Mehmood (Hubei Engineering University); Dr Qasim Ali Nisar (RMIT University); Amir Mehmood (Emaan Institute of Management and Sciences, Karachi, Pakistan); Hafeez Ahmad (International Islamic University)
Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative force in the manufacturing supply chain, offering substantial potential to enhance efficiency and performance. The present study investigates the influence of Generative AI on supply chain performance, emphasizing the serial mediating roles of supply chain ambidextrous innovation (explorative and exploitative) and supply chain risk management strategies (robustness and resilience). Furthermore, the moderating effect of ethical climate on the relationship between Generative AI and supply chain ambidextrous innovation is examined. Adopting a quantitative, three-wave time-lagged research design, data were collected at one-month intervals from supply chain managers in manufacturing firms across China and India. The results demonstrate that Generative AI significantly fosters both exploitative and explorative innovation within supply chains. In turn, these forms of ambidextrous innovation positively influence supply chain risk management strategies, specifically, robustness and resilience, which subsequently enhance overall supply chain performance. Moreover, the findings confirm the serial mediating roles of ambidextrous innovation and risk management strategies in linking Generative AI to supply chain performance. The study further reveals that an ethical climate strengthens the positive association between Generative AI and ambidextrous innovation. Cross-country analysis indicates that Chinese firms outperform their Indian counterparts in Generative AI adoption, supply chain exploration, performance, robustness, resilience, and ethical climate perceptions, while no significant difference is observed in supply chain exploitation capabilities.

Business Intelligence and Sustainability Performance: An Attention-Based View (WITHDRAWN)

CTO
Adel Beldi (IESEG School of Management); Kerim Karmeni (EMLV Business School, France); Shah Imran Ahmed
This study explores the impact of business intelligence (BI) on sustainability performance, specifically through the utilization of performance measurement systems (PMS). Anchored on the Attention-Based View (ABV), this research examines how both interactive and diagnostic uses of PMS mediate the relationship between BI adoption and sustainability outcomes. Drawing upon survey data collected from 203 top managers and executives across French firms, we find that BI usage significantly enhances sustainability performance. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that both interactive and diagnostic applications of PMS positively mediate this relationship. In line with the ABV, the study concludes that BI can strengthen sustainability performance by enhancing PMS capabilities, particularly in structuring and directing organizational attention towards sustainability priorities. Otherwise, the simultaneous use of both diagnostic and interactive PMS hurts sustainability performance. These findings highlight the critical role of BI and PMS in advancing sustainability objectives, suggesting that the benefits of PMS are amplified when interactive and diagnostic uses are applied independently.

Beyond Smart Machines: Human–AI Co-Actualization in Building Digital Resilience

CTO
Dandan (Wendy) YE (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Martin J Liu (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Jun Luo (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Pengtao Jiang (NingboTech University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in manufacturing operations, yet its implications for digital resilience remain insufficiently understood. While AI can enhance sensing, prediction, and response, it can also drift, erode human judgement, and introduce new operational vulnerabilities. To explain these mixed outcomes, this study examines how digital resilience emerges through ongoing human–AI interaction in AI-intensive manufacturing settings. Drawing on a multiple-case study and and affordance–actualization theory, we theorise human–AI co-actualization as a recursive socio-technical process through which human actors and adaptive AI systems jointly enact, recalibrate, and transform action possibilities over time. Our findings show how first-order AI affordances evolve into second-order affordances that underpin digital resilience, while also generating dis-affordances that constrain diagnostic capacity and vigilance. In doing so, we extends affordance–actualization theory by explaining how AI affordances evolve over time and by incorporating AI agency, reconceptualising actualization as human–AI co-actualization. It further advances digital resilience research by showing that resilience is a cumulative socio-technical capability rather than an immediate outcome of AI deployment. Finally, it contributes to the human–AI collaboration literature by explaining how hybrid intelligence evolves from task-specific interactions into system-level configurations over time.

Corporate Digital Transformation and Sustainable Development Performance: A Peer Effects Perspective

CTO
Jianwu Jiang (Shenzhen University); Fan Zhang (Shenzhen University); Nadilai Aisihaer
The sustainable development implications of digital transformation have emerged as a critical breakthrough for achieving high-quality development in enterprises. Peer enterprises’ mutual learning and imitation in digital transformation exert a substantial influence on corporate sustainable development performance. Drawing on Chinese A-share listed enterprises from 2014 to 2023, this study investigates the existence and formation mechanisms of peer effects in digital transformation, explores the underlying channels through which these peer effects influence corporate sustainable development performance, and examines the heterogeneity of such effects across technological attributes, ownership structures, and firm sizes. The findings are as follows: (1) Corporate digital transformation exhibits significant peer effects; (2) Peer effects in digital transformation significantly promote enterprises’ sustainable development performance, with the finding remaining robust to a battery of robustness and endogeneity tests; (3) These peer effects enhance sustainable development performance through channels including the promotion of green innovation, improvement of internal efficiency, and mitigation of financing constraints; (4) Heterogeneity analysis reveals that the positive effect of digital transformation peer effects on sustainable development performance is significantly stronger among non-state-owned enterprises, small enterprises, and high-tech enterprises than among state-owned enterprises, large enterprises, and non-high-tech enterprises.
