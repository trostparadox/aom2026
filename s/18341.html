Session overview

This symposium advances a capabilities-based perspective on neurodiversity in organizations, reframing neurological differences as sources of human capability rather than individual deficits requiring accommodation. Drawing on Amartya Sen’s Development as Freedom framework, the symposium conceptualizes the exclusion of neurodivergent individuals as the result of structural and cultural “unfreedoms” that constrain participation, advancement, and recognition—particularly for individuals with nonapparent disabilities such as autism, ADHD, and dyslexia. Integrating research on stigma, invisible disability, and organizational models of disability (medical, social, and social relational), the symposium examines how neurotypical norms embedded in hiring, evaluation, and leadership practices systematically disadvantage neurodivergent professionals. Contributions highlight the role of concealability, bias, and style-based assessments in sustaining underemployment and marginalization. The symposium further explores evidence-informed organizational practices—including alternative assessment methods, strengths-based evaluation, inclusive leadership, and flexible work design—that expand individual capabilities while enhancing organizational effectiveness. Together, the presentations offer a unifying theoretical lens and a future research agenda for neurodiversity scholarship.
