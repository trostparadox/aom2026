Session overview

Social cohesion, the degree to which members of society trust each other, share a sense of belonging, and work toward a common good (Chan et al., 2006; Child, 1976; Friedkin, 2004) is under severe strain globally. Rising polarization, economic pressures, and declining institutional trust have amplified calls for business organizations to play a proactive role in addressing this societal grand challenge (George et al., 2016). This panel aims to improve conceptual clarity, examine links to management theories and frameworks, and explore the scope and boundaries of integrating social cohesion into management research. It highlights social cohesion as a critical yet missing conversation in management and aims to identify pathways for future research on social cohesion in management studies.
