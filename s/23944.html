Digital Transformation and Sustainable Compliance: The Roles of Role Clarity and Self-Efficacy

TIM
MUHAMMAD ALI (Sichuan University of Science and Engineering); Kanwal Iqbal (COMSATS University Islamabad); Shareen Iqbal (National University of Science and Technology); Syed Hamad Hassan SHAH (South China Normal University)
How digital transformation translates into employees’ environmentally responsible behavior remains underexplored, particularly in service settings where sustainability depends heavily on frontline compliance. This study examines whether employees’ perceptions of digital transformation shape green compliance behavior in tourism organizations and under what conditions this influence is most effective. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources and Role Theory, we propose that digital transformation enhances green compliance by clarifying employees’ environmental roles, while individual green self-efficacy determines whether such clarity is acted upon. We test this moderated mediation model using multi-wave, multi-source data collected from 224 employee-supervisor dyads in Chinese tourism firms. The findings show that stronger perceptions of digital transformation are associated with higher green role clarity, which in turn predicts greater green compliance behavior. Moreover, green self-efficacy strengthens the relationship between role clarity and compliance. When employees feel confident in their ability to perform environmentally responsible tasks, role clarity is more likely to translate into compliant behavior. Consistent with this pattern, the indirect effect of digital transformation on green compliance through role clarity emerges only at moderate and high levels of green self-efficacy. Overall, the study highlights the importance of role-based and psychological mechanisms in explaining how digital transformation shapes sustainability-related behavior at the employee level.
