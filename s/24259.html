Epistemic Burden and Cognitive Regimes of AI at Work: When AI Offloads, Scaffolds, or Transforms

CTO
HAKAN SANRI (University of Glasgow); Anna Morgan-Thomas (University of Glasgow); Alison Smart (University of Glasgow)
Organizations increasingly deploy AI in the name of efficiency and automation, yet field evidence shows that AI does not produce a uniform cognitive effect. Drawing on a comparative study of ten algorithmic startups, we develop a variance theory of how AI stabilizes distinct cognitive regimes of offloading, scaffolding, or transformation and how these are dependent on an organization’s epistemic burden, the load of producing, warranting, and defending actionable truth under model mediation. Where throughput and scale overwhelm human attention, organizations relocate high frequency inference into pipelines, repositioning humans toward oversight and exceptions and yielding cognitive offloading. When decisions must travel credibly across technical and organizational boundaries, firms build shared evidentiary surfaces that coordinate and legitimate action, producing scaffolding. Finally, where truth is contestable and errors are consequential, teams move beyond output acceptance to engage models as epistemic objects. Sustained work with failures and warrants reshapes professional reasoning, producing cognitive transformation. The model offers an explanation for heterogeneous AI impacts and a diagnostic for designing AI governance.
