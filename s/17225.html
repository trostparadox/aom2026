Session overview

The translation of scientific insights into organizational practice is a longstanding challenge across management research. Although practitioner engagement has always been central to the advancement of the field, academic knowledge often remains difficult to access, interpret, or apply in real-world settings. These tensions become particularly visible in research associated with CTO and TIM scholarship, where scholars frequently work in domains characterized by rapid technological change, practice-embedded phenomena, and evolving organizational processes. In such contexts, research depends on access to organizational settings, technological infrastructures, and practitioner expertise, while practitioners simultaneously seek evidence-based insights that can inform strategic and operational decision making. This interdependence creates substantial opportunities for mutual learning yet translating these opportunities into meaningful and sustained exchange often proves challenging, not because of a lack of ideas but because established structures and routines hinder effective implementation. The PDW addresses this persistent issue by bringing together scholars, industry leaders, consultants, and editors who work at the interface between academic research and managerial relevance. Through an interactive fishbowl conversation, contributors will explore how knowledge circulates between academia and practice, what expectations and constraints shape these processes, and why translation frequently falters despite shared interests and numerous proposed models for engagement. The discussion encourages participants to surface tensions, question assumptions, and articulate promising avenues for more meaningful collaboration. In a second step, participants will join thematic roundtables that build on these reflections and examine concrete mechanisms to strengthen knowledge translation, whether through research design or communication formats. The PDW invites researchers at all career stages to critically reflect on their own approaches and to co-develop innovative strategies for enhancing the practical relevance and impact of CTO and TIM scholarship.
