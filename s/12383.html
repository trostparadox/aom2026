Session overview

Discussion and use of AI is at an all-time high for practitioners, researchers, consumers, and students. One might struggle to think of a category of products that has similarly impacted our world; 95% of US adults know something about AI (Kennedy, Yam, Kikuchi, Pula, & Fuentes, 2025), 88% of companies have adopted AI for at least one business function (Singla, Sukharevsky, Yee, Chui, & Hall, 2025), 90% of college students use AI (Legatt, 2025), Chat GPT has about 800 million weekly active users (Zao-Sanders, 2025), and researchers are both struggling with what this means for research and how they can capitalize on this product. In recent years, top journals in the field of management have seen a rise in publications pertaining to AI. Between the Journal of Applied Psychology, Journal of Management, Academy of Management Journal, Administrative Science Quarterly, and Personnel Psychology, there have been 57 articles published in which AI is focal to the study (or studies). It is refreshing to see academia not lagging industry in ways it usually does when it comes to adoption of new policies, practices, or technology. However, pertinent considerations regarding ethics have been unremarkable and relatively absent compared to the myriad of symposia, talks, and administrative encouragements to adopt and use AI. We are at a critical juncture in which we must address ethics of AI in tandem with its increased adoption and understanding. In this symposium, we discuss the ethics of AI from teaching, research, philosophical, and legal perspectives.
