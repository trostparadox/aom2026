First Impressions Last: Exploring When and Why AI-Human Collaboration Sequence Enhances Creativity

OB
Zijing Wang (Wuhan University of Technology); Sibo Huang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Zhiqiang Liu
Idea diversity is widely recognized as a foundational antecedent of creativity, and artificial intelligence (AI) theoretically possesses an unparalleled capacity to generate such diversity. Yet, the creative potential of integrating AI into creative work is often not fully realized in practice. We attribute this shortfall to a critical but underexamined design feature of AI-human collaboration: the sequence in which AI and humans generate ideas. Drawing on anchoring theory, we propose that AI-human collaboration sequence and trust in AI jointly influence creativity. Across two simulation-based experiment (Study 1 and Study 2) and a field experiment (Study 3), we found that: (1) In AI-first sequence (i.e., AI provides the initial ideas), higher trust in AI strengthens anchoring on AI-generated suggestions, thereby constraining idea diversity and creativity. (2) In human-first sequence (i.e., humans generate ideas first), higher trust weakens anchoring on initial human ideas and facilitates greater idea diversity and creativity. By highlighting the joint roles of collaboration sequence and trust in AI, our work advance understanding of effective AI-human collaboration in creative processes, and offer practical insights for optimizing such collaboration to maximize creative yield.
