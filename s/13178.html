Session overview

Employees attempt to influence the moral behavior of others, often with great success. However, emerging research also suggests that employees face substantial interpersonal challenges when morally influencing others. Moral influencers often face retaliation and social derogation. At times, moral influence can ironically backfire and cause others to rationalize unethical behavior. Despite this emerging work, notably less research has explored these challenges, leaving our understanding of the challenges associated with moral influence unclear. Without a greater appreciation of these challenges, organizational research risks painting an overly optimistic picture of moral influence, which may undermine the utility and promise of this area of study. In our symposium, we introduce a series of papers that address this need and unpack the challenges associated with moral influence. Work in this symposium takes an interdisciplinary perspective and a multi method approach to examine a range of challenges that individuals face when attempting to influence others morally. Through diverse samples and methods, these papers highlight insightful and surprising challenges, such as the downside of anonymity, different cultural thresholds for accepting half-truths, and the tension between fear and admiration of a courageous act. In doing so, this symposium aims to shift conversation from how individuals successfully morally influence others to instead appreciate the complexities and costs of attempting to do so.
