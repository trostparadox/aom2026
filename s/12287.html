The Crossover of Life Satisfaction: How Entrepreneurs Affect Their Close Social Network

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Authors: Maximilian Weldert University of Münster; Philipp Schäpers University of Münster; Henrik Heinemann University of Münster

Session overview

Over the past decade, entrepreneurship research has increasingly paid attention to well-being. Entrepreneurs do not operate in a vacuum, but are part of far-reaching social networks, yet research still largely overlooks the effects of entrepreneurial well-being on other stakeholders. Building on the spillover-crossover model, we examine whether entrepreneurs’ life satisfaction is associated with the life satisfaction of close standing persons (friend, colleague, partner, or family member), and whether this association differs from non-entrepreneurs. Drawing on a dataset from 150 entrepreneurs and 135 non-entrepreneurs, plus 193 peer-ratings, we find that entrepreneurs reported higher life satisfaction than non-entrepreneurs. Using peer ratings, we find a robust crossover effect: participants’ life satisfaction significantly predicts peers’ life satisfaction across the full sample. Peers of entrepreneurs reported higher life satisfaction than peers of non-entrepreneurs. Spillover indicators (income satisfaction, family and friends time) are positively associated with entrepreneurs’ life satisfaction, which in turn relates to peers’ life satisfaction in a spillover–crossover model.
