Session overview

Digital innovation is increasingly characterized not by discrete adoption decisions but by the erosion of a clear “adoption moment.” Across platform updates, embedded AI capabilities, evolving configurations of AI engagement, and shifting governance in standard-based ecosystems, technological change often arrives as a continuous condition rather than a choice made at a point in time. This presenter symposium develops a multi-level account of what happens to strategy, agency, and governance when organizations cannot locate innovation in a single decision episode. The first paper theorizes “hypercontinuous innovation,” explaining how continuous updating and compatibility-preserving architectures reshape the temporal structure of innovation and the organization of innovation work. The second paper introduces “innovation injection,” examining how vendor-driven updates embed new capabilities —often discovered post-deployment—and how organizations attempt to reassert accountability and control through shutdowns, boundary work, and improvised governance. The third paper shifts to established firms’ efforts to scale AI, arguing that effective AI adoption unfolds through evolving configurations of organizational attributes over time rather than through best practices or one-time adoption choices. The final paper examines standard-based ecosystems in which firms make a single entry decision and thereafter innovate through ongoing interdependence; institutional patent (in)validation reallocates innovation space and reshapes who can contribute to technical change. Together, the symposium clarifies how the disappearance of an adoption moment reconstitutes innovation as an ongoing, distributed process by reframing core assumptions about adoption, implementation, and control in contemporary digital environments.
