Newcomer Self-Presentation: A Temporal Perspective

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Author: Nan Wang Lingnan University

Session overview

Newcomers’ self-presentation has been viewed as a strategic effort to foster positive impressions of competence, warmth, and morality for desirable social adjustment outcomes. This study challenges this static view by re-conceptualizing newcomer self-presentation as an ongoing process that forms cumulative impression commitments over time. Newcomers face a fundamental dilemma between value-claiming and bearing the costs of sustaining those claims. Early self-presentation sets expectations about newcomers’ identity and appropriate behaviors, which in turn generate commitment trajectories that constrain their subsequent interactions and identity enactment. This study further investigates self-promotion, self-depreciation, and self-verification as three primary self-presentation logics, proposing that each initiates distinct patterns of commitment by prioritizing value claiming and cost containment differently. Furthermore, job characteristics that influence the extent to which newcomers can shape impressions may function as structural amplifiers or attenuators of cost accumulation over time. By viewing self-presentation as a cumulative and constraining process, this study reframes newcomer socialization as a dynamic trade-off between early acceptance and sustainable integration.
