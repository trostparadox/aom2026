Session overview

Entrepreneurs in emerging economies often confront institutional environments characterized by weak formal rules and reliance on informal substitutes for resource access. Yet access to these informal arrangements is uneven, creating systematic disparities in who can mobilize capital, legitimacy, and support. This symposium advances a positional framework centered on the concept of the outsider-within—entrepreneurs who participate in markets but remain excluded from the informal networks that govern access to critical resources. We theorize that outsider-within entrepreneurs face a distinctive double bind: they cannot rely on formal institutions due to institutional voids, nor can they access the informal substitutes available to insiders. Across diverse empirical settings, the papers examine how this double bind constrains entrepreneurial action and shapes strategic responses. Contributions explore how outsider-within actors navigate corruption pressures, mobilize social and role identities, deploy multimodal signals to align with audience norms, and develop adaptive capacities in response to climate shocks. Together, the symposium shifts attention from demographic disadvantage to structural positionality, identifies the mechanisms through which institutional constraints operate, and illuminates the varied strategies entrepreneurs use to cope with exclusion. The session offers a unified theoretical lens for understanding marginalized entrepreneurship and highlights the limits of individual adaptation absent more inclusive and equitable institutions.
