Taming Toxic Power: Peer Support as a Buffer for Emotion-Driven Withdrawal in Vietnam

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Chia-Wu Lin National Dong Hwa University; THAO THI MY NGUYEN; Hsiang-Pin Yu National Dong Hwa University; Mandla Kantuseya Gama National Dong Hwa University

Session overview

TestWhile research on abusive leadership is extensive, the discrete emotional mechanisms driving specific withdrawal behaviors remain underexplored. Grounded in Affective Events Theory (AET) and Social Support Theory, this study investigates how anxiety and anger mediate the relationship between abusive leadership and two distinct forms of withdrawal: presenteeism and absenteeism. Utilizing a two-wave survey of 402 employees in Vietnam, we analyze the data using PLS-SEM. The results reveal that anxiety mediates the path from abusive leadership to presenteeism, whereas anger mediates the path to absenteeism. Crucially, coworker support serves as a vital boundary condition, significantly buffering both emotional transmission pathways. This research contributes to Human Resource Management (HRM) by mapping discrete emotions to specific behavioral outcomes and highlighting peer solidarity as a strategic resource in high power-distance, collectivist cultures. For HR practitioners, the findings underscore the necessity of moving beyond generalized wellness programs toward targeted emotional interventions and the cultivation of supportive peer environments to mitigate the hidden costs of toxic leadership.
