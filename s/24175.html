Feminist Legacy Before Feminism: An Anticipatory Case Of Gender, Work And Emancipation

MH
Giuseppe Modarelli (University of Turin); Valerio Brescia (University of Milan)
The study aims to demonstrate how the analysis of archival documents (payroll records) can reveal power dynamics and structural inequalities rooted in history, as well as highlight the precursors of ideological movements emerged centuries later. Specifically, it aims to elevate Isabella Morra as an icon of avant-garde feminism, using her tragic story as an emblematic case study. The research adopts a diachronic and synchronic approach based on documentary analysis and grounded in “new” accounting history. The authors use as primary sources the accounting records and payrolls relating to the Italian poet Isabella Morra’s service as a lady-in-waiting in the 16th century. This method allows for a-posteriori hermeneutic reconstruction, transcending the numerical data to observe the social, political and organizational context of the time. Using accounting documents and payroll records, the hermeneutics of a-posteriori reinterpretation elevates the figure of the poet Isabella as an anticipatory fulcrum of modern feminist thought. Economic independence, work as a liberating concept from patriarchy and gender issues are concepts that accurately reflect the key figure of Isabella, albeit in her sad story as a Renaissance icon of anticipatory feminism. Contemporaneously with the emergence of accounting as a technique, accounting documents themselves reflect the practice’s enlarged role as a social technology. The findings highlight how the violence suffered by the poet is linked to structural barriers that remain relevant today (femicide, gender pay gap etc.). The originality lies in placing the analysis of feminism and women’s work in the 16th century, a much earlier period than traditional literature focusing on the 19th and 20th centuries. The work innovates the field of Management Studies by proposing an alternative reading of history through accounting, seen not as a mere technique, but as an “introspective” and “surjective” tool for interpreting the present and the future, shaping a better world.
