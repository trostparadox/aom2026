Controlled Experiments in Uncontrolled Environments: The Limits of A/B Testing in Startups

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Authors: Travis Howell Arizona State University; Ryan Allen Brigham Young University; Anavir Shermon; Todd Hall University of Kansas

Session overview

Startups increasingly rely on controlled experiments like A/B tests to navigate uncertainty. We argue that the rigor these methods demand often clashes with the volatile realities of entrepreneurship. Taking seriously the view of “entrepreneurs as scientists,” we contend that experiments that produce useful data require “methodological fit” with the environment. Using panel data on 1,558 U.S. e-commerce startups from 2017–2023, we find that while A/B testing boosts performance on average (consistent with prior literature), its benefits are contingent on the extent to which the environment satisfies the statistical assumptions required for valid inference. Tests are more effective when customer bases are large and stable, products are modular, and customers are relatively homogeneous. When these conditions are violated, the benefits of adoption are significantly weakened or reversed.
