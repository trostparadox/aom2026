Role of Servant Leadership and Organizational Long-Term Orientation in Shaping OCBIs

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 10
OB

People

Author: Eda Aksoy Koç University

Session overview

Organizational citizenship behaviors toward individuals (OCBIs) create a social dilemma: helping coworkers yields collective, longer-term benefits but imposes immediate personal costs. Building on stewardship theory and situational strength theory, we examine how servant leadership and organizational long-term orientation (LTO) jointly shape employees’ OCBIs when employees’ personal work horizons are short. We conceptualize turnover intentions as a personal indicator of a short temporal work horizon and argue that, in low-LTO contexts, employees with higher turnover intentions are less likely to reciprocate servant leaders’ prosocial behaviors with helping. In contrast, when an organization provides strong long-term cues (high LTO), servant leadership and LTO align to form a strong situation that reduces behavioral variability and buffers the adverse implications of turnover intentions for OCBIs. Survey data were collected from 488 full-time employees across industries and municipalities in Turkiye. Results show positive main effects of servant leadership and LTO on OCBIs, and a significant three-way interaction among servant leadership, turnover intentions, and LTO. When LTO was high, the servant leadership-OCBI relationship did not differ across employees with high versus low turnover intentions. When LTO was low, servant leadership related more strongly to OCBIs among employees with low turnover intentions than among those with high turnover intentions. Together, findings highlight the importance of aligning organizational temporal orientations with leadership approaches to maximize the effectiveness of prosocial leadership in fostering coworker-directed citizenship.
