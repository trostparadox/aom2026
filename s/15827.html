Session overview

Rapid advances in artificial intelligence (AI) have prompted widespread claims that many professional and managerial roles will be automated or fundamentally transformed. While such narratives often emphasise job displacement, they underplay the critical role of management education in preparing graduates with AI-proof capabilities-skills and forms of judgment that cannot be easily automated, such as sense-making, ethical reasoning, leadership, systems thinking, creativity, and human-centred decision-making. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) responds to this challenge by supporting management educators in redesigning curriculum, pedagogy, and assessment to deliberately develop AI-complementary and AI-resilient managerial capabilities. Drawing on research from management education, higher education, and the future of work, the PDW reframes AI not as a replacement for management graduates, but as a catalyst for rethinking what, how, and why we teach management. Designed as an interactive How-to Workshop with a co-design studio, the PDW will enable participants to: (1) critically examine dominant “AI replaces jobs” narratives; (2) identify core AI-proof managerial capabilities; (3) explore pedagogical and assessment strategies that leverage AI while preserving human judgment and responsibility; and (4) co-design curriculum and assessment innovations suited to their institutional contexts. Participants will leave with concrete tools, curriculum design principles, assessment exemplars, and an evaluative framework that can be immediately applied across undergraduate, postgraduate, and executive management education. The PDW directly addresses MED priorities on AI, pedagogical innovation, faculty development, impact, and the future relevance of management education. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
