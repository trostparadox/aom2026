Do Firms Respond to ESG Performance Feedback? Evidence from EMS Certification Adoption Decisions

STR
Abhinay NEDUNURU (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode); Nycil George
Although Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) has moved from peripheral rhetoric to the core of firm evaluation, our understanding of how firms translate the ESG performance feedback into strategic organizational responses remains limited. We address this by extending the Behavioral Theory of the Firm (BTOF) and testing how non-core goal performance feedback leads to a non-core action pathway, namely, how discrepancies between a firm’s ESG performance and aspirations predict the adoption of Environmental Management Systems (EMS). Using a sample of listed U.S. firms, we find that negative discrepancies are associated with a higher likelihood of EMS adoption, whereas positive discrepancies have a dampening effect on this adoption. We also demonstrate how board gender diversity moderates the relationship between performance feedback and EMS adoption by strengthening the dampening effect of positive discrepancies on EMS adoption.
