Session overview

Research practices aimed at integrity, robustness, and ultimately the impact of scientific findings have gained increasing attention among management scholars. While many approaches like pre-registration and registered reports, causal identification procedures like instrumental variable approaches, or replication approaches like direct or conceptual replications are increasingly familiar terms among many scholars, their use remains still scarce and scattered. While different “camps” suggest how to improve the credibility of our field, the advocates for different solutions have talked so far mostly in isolated siloes rather than in a joint community. The present PDW, therefore, aims to accomplish two goals: First, it provides PDW participants with a hands-on introduction to new developments and best practices across a variety of methodological approaches in management research that aim to improve research credibility. Second, the PDW contributes to developing a close-knit “credibility community” in which researchers across management disciplines and paradigms come together to jointly create scientific insights with high credibility, robustness, and transparency. Ultimately, through the joint advancement of methodological competencies and sustained cross-field collaboration, this PDW seeks to strengthen rigorous theory testing and promote a more cumulative, transparent, and policy-relevant management science.
