Who Do We Trust with Our Data? Legitimacy Judgments and Sectoral Trust in Healthcare Ecosystems

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5

Authors: Aigerim Dairabekova University of Oulu; Fan Wang University of Oulu

Session overview

Digital healthcare increasingly relies on ecosystemic arrangements that enable the sharing and integration of personal health data across organizational boundaries. While such arrangements promise innovation and efficiency, their success depends critically on legitimacy judgments. Drawing on Suchman’s (1995) distinction between moral, cognitive, and pragmatic legitimacy, this study examines how individuals evaluate health data sharing across public, private, and public-private healthcare sectors. Using qualitative data from 56 international master’s students enrolled at a Finnish university, we analyze respondents’ willingness to trust different sectoral actors with their personal health data based on scenario evaluations. Our findings show that legitimacy judgments are sector-contingent and multi-dimensional. Public healthcare organizations benefit from strong moral and cognitive legitimacy rooted in public interest and institutionalized expectations of care, but face pragmatic challenges related to infrastructure and interoperability. Private actors are primarily evaluated through pragmatic legitimacy associated with innovation and efficiency, yet face moral and cognitive skepticism due to profit motives. Public-private ecosystems generate the greatest ambiguity, requiring active legitimacy work to reconcile competing expectations. The study contributes to Organization and Management Theory by highlighting the micro-foundations of legitimacy in emerging digital health ecosystems and demonstrating how sectoral institutional logics shape trust and acceptance of data-driven innovation.
