The Reciprocal Relationship between Feedback-Seeking Behavior and Job Performance

OB
Hui Liu (Macau University of Science and Technology); Xiyang Zhang (Tianjin University); Wenchi Zou (Macau University of Science and Technology); Xin Wang (Tianjin University); Yu Wei (Tianjin University)
Existing research has shown that the results of the relationship between feedback-seeking behavior and job performance are conflicting. To address these inconsistencies in prior research, we draw on self-regulation theory to propose a moderated model examining the reciprocal relationship between feedback-seeking behavior and job performance. We identify two critical boundary conditions that shape this regulatory loop: job uncertainty as a situational moderator and feedback orientation as a dispositional moderator. Specifically, we theorize that job uncertainty affects how effectively different feedback-seeking strategies (feedback inquiry and feedback monitoring) are translated into employees' subsequent job performance. Completing the reciprocal cycle, we further posit that feedback orientation determines the extent to which prior performance outcomes motivate employees to engage in future feedback inquiry and monitoring. Data from two experience sampling studies provide support for our theoretical model: a daily diary study with 882 observations from 107 employees and a weekly survey with 423 observations from 108 employees. Overall, our findings demonstrate the cross-level moderating role of job uncertainty and feedback orientation in the self-regulatory process of feedback seeking, thereby offering a novel explanation for the relationship between feedback-seeking behavior and job performance.
