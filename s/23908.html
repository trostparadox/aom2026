How AI Penetration Changes the Effect of Information Disclosure on Innovation Performance

TIM
Min Guo (Xi'an University of Posts and Telecommunications); He Zhai; Hui Liu; Mingzhen Zhang; Jingbei Wang
Against the backdrop of pervasive artificial intelligence (AI) penetration, traditional corporate disclosure strategies are undergoing a paradigm shift. Drawing on panel data for Chinese A-share listed firms from 2009 to 2024, we construct text-based measures of disclosure transparency and disclosure quality, as well as a firm-level AI penetration index, and estimate negative binomial regression models to test our hypotheses. The results indicate that both transparency and quality of information disclosure exhibit significant inverted U-shaped relationships with firms’ innovation performance. Moreover, AI penetration positively moderates these relationships: higher levels of AI penetration strengthen the innovation-enhancing effects of both disclosure dimensions. These findings call into question the conventional wisdom of opacity as a protective strategy and suggest that AI penetration can function as a dynamic capability that helps firms better manage proprietary costs while enhancing their capacity for organizational learning from disclosure. We advocate a shift from “talking much but saying little” to disclosures that are both informative and readable, balancing substantive content with cognitive accessibility. Our results enrich research on information disclosure, AI, and innovation, and offer timely implications for disclosure frameworks and corporate communication strategies in the digital economy.
