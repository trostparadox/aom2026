Transforming Disciplinary Silos: Boundary Spanning Practices in Interprofessional Education Planning

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Author: Kyle G Dobbeck Montclair State University

Session overview

Interprofessional education (IPE) has become a cornerstone of health professions curricula and now includes disciplines across universities, yet implementing IPE requires substantial collaboration across traditionally siloed programs. Faculty and staff must navigate regulatory and institutional structures to co-create effective interprofessional learning experiences. Boundary spanning is required; that is, forging common ground through two-way social exchanges across disciplinary boundaries is essential. This qualitative case study examined the structures and practices that enable or constrain interprofessional curriculum planning at a university academic medical center. Using Strong Structuration Theory (Stones, 2005) as a theoretical lens, the study followed an IPE planning group from inception through execution of an IPE event. Findings revealed that collaborative curriculum planning develops progressively through four relational enactments: passivity, familiarizing, connecting, and synthesizing. These relational enactments are shaped by three structural factors: roles, interpersonal relationships, and group norms. The study demonstrates that curriculum collaboration transforms boundaries rather than merely crossing them. Boundary spanning is enhanced as structural constraints and the boundaries between interacting agents are changed through reciprocal social interactions. As boundaries change, constraints on and barriers to planning efforts are alleviated. This implicates the need for ongoing social interaction among planning participants to sustain curriculum initiatives. Findings contribute to educational scholarship by revealing how structures enable or constrain collaboration and by providing a developmental framework for understanding how curriculum planning groups develop. Practical implications include guidance for curriculum leaders for fostering collaborative capacity, identifying structural enablers and constraints, and supporting faculty development for interprofessional work.
