When Cognitive Biases Build Structure: Procedural Rationality as a Missing Link in Decision Quality

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Authors: Teodora Globarevic University of Montenegro; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro; Sara Dragicevic University of Montenegro; Milena Mugoša University of Montenegro; Stefan Jovetic University of Montenegro, Faculty of Economics

Session overview

Drawing on ecological rationality, this study conceptualizes commonly labeled cognitive biases (overconfidence, representativeness, availability, anchoring) as heuristic tendencies that may help impose structure on complex decisions. We examine whether employees’ perceptions of managers’ cognitive-bias tendencies relate to perceived decision quality through procedural rationality and whether perceived environmental uncertainty conditions this pathway. Cross-sectional survey data from 414 employees in Montenegro captured evaluations of their direct managers’ bias tendencies, procedural rationality, decision quality and perceived environmental uncertainty. This employee-rated design offers a distinctive vantage point by capturing how managers’ decision processes are observed and experienced by key stakeholders, rather than relying on managers’ self-reports. Using OLS path models with bootstrapped indirect effects (PROCESS Model 7), results indicate that higher perceived cognitive bias tendencies are associated with higher procedural rationality and procedural rationality is positively related to higher decision quality. Procedural rationality partially mediates the relationship between cognitive-bias tendencies and decision quality. Contrary to expectations, perceived environmental uncertainty does not significantly moderate the indirect pathway: the positive indirect effect via procedural rationality is evident across low, medium and high uncertainty. By leveraging an employee-rated perspective in a transitional economy context, the study responds to calls for greater contextual diversity and highlights procedural rationality as an actionable lever for strengthening decision quality even when environments are perceived as uncertain.
