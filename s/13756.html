Digital Dilemmas: How New Technologies Reinforce and Disrupt the Social Fabric of Emerging Markets

Authors: Denise R. Dunlap; Farzad Nazari University of Massachusetts Lowell

Session overview

Digital technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), mobile banking, and telehealth are transforming social dynamics worldwide. Their impact is particularly profound in emerging markets (EMs). Unlike developed countries, with robust formal institutions, EMs are characterized by weak formal institutions where informal networks are relied upon for economic coordination, resilience, and daily routines. We argue that EMs face a significant dilemma wherein incremental technological changes can lead to unpredictable outcomes. In some cases, they create efficiency, foster social inclusion, broaden access to essential resources/services, and strengthen community resilience. Conversely, they risk fragmenting social cohesion and potentially, eroding the relational ties that compensate for institutional fragility. This research advances a novel conceptual framework that elucidates the mechanisms under which digital innovations reinforce or disrupt informal institutions. We suggest that the effects of digital innovation are neither inherently positive nor negative, but are shaped by technology design, sociocultural context, and the strength of informal institutions. Our research offers guidance on navigating these dilemmas to ensure that future innovative endeavors do not undermine the resilience and well-being of EMs.
