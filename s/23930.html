What Drives Commercialization and Research Impact Among Early and Mid-Career Researchers?

TIM
Forough Zarea Fazlelahi (Queensland University of Technology)
Governments worldwide invest substantial public funding in university research, yet empirical evidence on what drives commercialization and technological impact among funded researchers remains limited. Drawing on an individual ambidexterity perspective, this study examines how researchers balance exploratory scientific production and exploitative commercialization activities. The analysis uses a uniquely constructed dataset linking more than 90,000 Australian Research Council (ARC)–funded publications with global patent-to-paper citation records from the Reliance on Science dataset. The final dataset covers 2,876 Discovery Early Career Researcher Award (DECRA) grants awarded between 2011 and 2025 and 2,120 ARC Future Fellowship (AFF) grants awarded between 2009 and 2025. These grants generated 675 patents and 1,671 patent citations associated with DECRA-funded research, and 516 patents and 1,065 patent citations associated with AFF-funded research. The results show that scientific productivity and scholarly influence are the strongest and most consistent predictors of both patent production and technological impact, supporting the view that exploratory performance underpins successful exploitation. In contrast, institutional status, gender, and research orientation do not exhibit consistent effects. These findings contribute to university technology transfer research by providing large-scale evidence on individual ambidexterity and offer insights for the evaluation and design of national research funding programs.
