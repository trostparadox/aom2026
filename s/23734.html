Responsibly Handling Deviant Behavior Through Creativity: Creating a Psychological Safe Environment

ENT
Ali Hasan Mumtaz (Harbin Institute of Technology); Ziyi Huang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Li Zhang (Harbin Institute of Technology)
Responsible leadership (RL) plays a complex role in promoting employee innovation, particularly when innovation involves rule-breaking or creative deviance. Drawing on Social Exchange Theory (SET), this study proposes a model linking RL, psychological safety, work engagement, and creative deviance. We argue that RL fosters psychological safety through trust-based reciprocity, enabling prosocial deviance, while its emphasis on stakeholder norms may simultaneously deter such behavior. Work engagement is posited as a moderator, amplifying the link between psychological safety and creative deviance. Survey data from 320 employees in Pakistan were analyzed using confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modeling. Findings reveal that RL enhances psychological safety, which positively mediates its effect on creative deviance, despite RL’s direct negative influence on deviance. Work engagement strengthens this mediation. The study reconciles conflicting views on leadership and deviant innovation, offering theoretical insights and practical implications for fostering climates that balance ethical responsibility with boundary-pushing creativity.
