Negotiation Spillovers: Scale Development and Impact on Subjective Value

CM
Apostolos Tsatsoulis (Athens University of Economics and Business); Ilias Kapoutsis (Athens University of Economics and Business)
Spillovers in negotiation processes comprising elements such as emotional contagion, behavioural mimicry, and cognitive convergence are particularly consequential in high-uncertainty business negotiations. Despite growing theoretical interest, the field lacks an instrument to gauge the magnitude of such phenomena. We address this gap by developing and empirically testing the Negotiation Spillover Inventory (NSI), a scale designed to quantify the perceived magnitude of spillovers in business negotiations. In Study 1, we establish the psychometric properties and nomological network of the NSI using a quasi-experimental methodology. We conceptualize the NSI as a hierarchical model that includes an overarching spillover factor and three first-order dimensions: affective, behavioural, and cognitive. In Study 2, we test the NSI in a simulated sequential entrepreneurial negotiation involving 68 participants in Round 1 and 67 participants in Round 2, all enrolled in a business school negotiation course. Our analysis indicates a significant positive relationship between negotiation spillovers and subjective value generated across both rounds, moderated by psychological resilience in the first round. Contrary to our hypotheses, spillovers do not predict objective value in either round. Our research offers several contributions. Methodologically, the development of the NSI advances negotiation theory and offers a novel tool for quantifying spillover phenomena in negotiation processes. Practically, quantifying spillovers enables negotiators to optimize their negotiation performance by developing spillover-informed negotiation strategies. Overall, our paper establishes spillovers as important elements of negotiation success, opening new avenues for negotiation research and practice.
