Opening the Dialogue or Closing It? How Task Characteristics Shape Newcomer-Incumbent Interactions

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Author: Joanne Cao Cornell University

Session overview

Interactions with incumbents are widely understood to provide newcomers with informational guidance and social support, yet these two functions are often examined in isolation. We still lack a within-person explanation for why, for the same newcomer, some interactions remain one-way and instructional, while others become two-way and facilitate social assimilation. I argue that the task characteristics play a critical role in shaping whether incumbents create dialogic opportunities as they interact with newcomers. Specifically, I propose that when newcomers undertake tasks characterized by high uncertainty, such as explorative or coordinative work, incumbents are more likely to navigate interactions around control, emphasizing instruction and correction. In doing so, incumbents constrain opportunities for reciprocal dialogue. I examine this argument in a deep-learning code review setting, where newcomers must submit proposed code changes for incumbent evaluation. Using panel data of code reviews nested within 50 deep-learning engineers over a six-month period, I find evidence consistent with this constraining mechanism: incumbent code reviews elicit less newcomer input when newcomers work on explorative or coordinative tasks. I discuss implications for research on newcomer socialization and organizational learning.
