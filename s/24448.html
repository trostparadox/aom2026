How and When Identity Leadership Motivates Public Employees to Take Charge?

PNP
Shanghao Song (Capital University of Economics and Business); Chenhui Zuo (Beijing Normal University); Yunsheng Shi (Beijing Normal University)
Motivating public employees to proactively do taking charge behavior represents a significant practical challenge for both public administration research and practice. Most scholars have discussed the factors shaping taking charge behavior from an individual perspective, while few studies have explored the significant role of situational factors such as leadership. Based on the social identity theory, this study discusses the influence mechanism and boundary conditions of identity leadership on the taking charge behavior of public employees. Through a multi-wave questionnaire survey research (N = 299), this study found that identity leadership can promote the taking charge behavior respectively through the mediating role of felt accountability and work meaningfulness. Furthermore, the public service motivation of public employees can serve as an important boundary condition to enhance the mediated relationship. This study enriches the theoretical understanding of public employees’ taking charge behavior and provides practical guidance for fostering such behavior in public organizations.
