Session overview

Leadership development has attracted substantial scholarly interest over the past half-century. Despite tremendous progress, it remains a nascent field of study, with key theoretical, definitional, and empirical questions unresolved. Moreover, the rapidly changing organizational landscape, in which new technologies are fundamentally altering how organizations and their members relate to their work and to one another, both necessitates and enables radical new approaches to developing leadership. As we turn the page on the first quarter of the twenty-first century, the proposed symposium offers scholars and practitioners the chance to take stock of existing progress and share ideas about next steps. We present four papers — two conceptual and two empirical— from leading voices in the field that explore leadership development using a variety of theoretical perspectives, levels of analysis, and research designs. Following the presentations, David Day, who has contributed both foundational ideas and provocative new perspectives to the leadership development literature, will lead the audience in an interactive discussion.
