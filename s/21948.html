Thriving in Midlife: Navigating Paradoxical Tensions During Career Transitions

Session overview

In contemporary organizational landscapes characterized by volatility, unpredictability, complexity and ambiguity (VUCA), midlife women professionals often face complex career transitions that demand significant identity reconstruction and the management of competing personal and professional commitments (Burke et al., 2025). This poster submission presents a mixed-methods study exploring how a "paradox mindset"—the cognitive capacity to embrace and integrate contradictions—serves as a critical internal resource for thriving during these disruptive times. Grounded in Robert Kegan’s Constructive-Developmental Theory and Self-Determination Theory (SDT), the research examines how the satisfaction of core psychological needs—competence, autonomy, relatedness, and social contribution—mediates the relationship between a paradox mindset and thriving (vitality and learning) at work. By shifting the focus from early-career development to the unique career transitions of midlife, this study offers innovative insights into the micro-level mechanisms of organizational change. It addresses how individuals interpret and engage with disruption, moving beyond reactive survival to proactive thriving. Findings aim to provide rigorous and relevant guidance for organizations seeking to support professional women through transformational career shifts, ultimately linking individual growth to broader systemic resilience.
