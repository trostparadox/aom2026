Broadening Our Lens: Four Theoretical Perspectives on Industry Incubation

ODC
Gaomin Liu (RMIT University); Glenn Hoetker (Melbourne Business School, University of Melbourne)
The emergence of new industries is preceded by an incubation stage, where Moeen and Agarwal (2017) give a short definition: “the period between a technological breakthrough and the first instance of its commercialisation”. Choices made in the incubation stage impact the long-term evolution of the industry. Accordingly, there is a growing body of literature on the incubation stage from scholars across multiple disciplines. Reflecting this diversity of disciplinary approaches, the literature remains fragmented and dispersed. In response, this study synthesises leading theoretical perspectives—technology realism, economic realism, cognitive interpretivism, and social constructivism—to shed light on the processes characterising the industry's incubation phase. Through a comprehensive literature review spanning multiple management disciplines, it proposes a four-stage framework encompassing idea generation, resource allocation, social network formation, and institutional legitimacy emergence, demonstrating how firms navigate the industrial-level evolution. By connecting each theoretical angle and industry stage, this paper clarifies the underlying assumptions made by the different theoretical perspectives, explores the implications of those assumptions for our understanding of the incubation period, and, most importantly, demonstrates that the application of those perspectives to stages of industry incubation to which they have previously not been applied generates new research questions and theoretical insights.
