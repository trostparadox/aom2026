Asymmetric Peer Influence in Corporate Political Stance Adoption: Evidence From Hong Kong

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Author: Zhongyu Zhao The University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Firms increasingly adopt political stances, but we know little about whether proximate firms influence each other's stance decisions. Using Hong Kong's novel data, we examine spatial peer effects in restaurants' political stance adoption. Our sample comprises 147,600 restaurant-month observations across 18 districts. We estimate linear probability models with firm fixed effects and district-by-time fixed effects, using lagged neighbor stances to establish temporal ordering. We find striking asymmetry: progressive stance adoption shows strong positive peer effects at hyper-local distances, while conservative stance adoption shows no positive peer effects at any distance. Both ideologies show negative cross-ideology effects. Chain restaurants amplify progressive peer influence specifically but not conservative or opposing effects. Results are robust across distance thresholds and chain definitions. This study documents that horizontal peer influence matters for corporate political behavior beyond firm-stakeholder relationships, and that organizational structure moderates peer responsiveness in ideology-specific ways.
