Configurational Pathways to High Performance in Cross-Border Digital M&As of Digital Enterprises

IM
Jichen Li (Shanghai Business School)
Digital M&As serve as a critical strategic tool for digital firms to strengthen digital competitiveness and boost performance, yet the complex causal mechanisms underlying performance variability in digital M&As remain underexplored. This study adopts an asymmetric configurational perspective to unpack the multifaceted drivers of M&A performance among digital acquirers. Based on a sample of 150 Chinese digital firms, we employ an integrated methodological framework combining Necessary Condition Analysis (NCA) and Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA) to identify the configurational pathways shaping M&A performance outcomes. The findings reveal five distinct pathways and four configurations conducive to high M&A performance, alongside five configurations that impede the achievement of superior performance. This research offers actionable implications for digital firms, advocating for tailored M&A strategies that integrate multi-dimensional factors rather than a one-size-fits-all approach, with M&A process legitimacy highlighted as a core determinant of high performance. Theoretically, this study advances the understanding of digital M&A performance mechanisms by reconciling seemingly contradictory findings in the digital economy context and integrating multiple theoretical lenses, thereby providing a holistic view of the complex causal relationships in digital M&As.

How Internationally Experienced Executives Shape Enterprise Digital Transformation

IM
Tahir M. Nisar (University of Southampton); Nick Hajli (Loughborough University)
As economic activity becomes increasingly globalized, top managers with overseas study or work experience are playing an ever more consequential role in shaping firms’ strategic responses to technological change. This study examines how top managers’ international experience influences enterprise digital transformation in China. Drawing on upper echelons theory, the paper theorizes that international experience constitutes a critical cognitive and strategic resource that shapes executives’ interpretations of digital opportunities, risk preferences, and long-term strategic orientation, thereby positively affecting firms’ digitalization decisions. Using a quantitative research design, the study analyzes panel data from Chinese A-share listed companies over the period 2014-2023. The empirical results show a robust and positive relationship between top managers’ international experience and the level of enterprise digitalization. This relationship remains stable across multiple model specifications and robustness checks, confirming the reliability of the findings. Further heterogeneity analysis reveals that the effect of international experience varies significantly across regions, with stronger effects observed in less developed, non-eastern regions. The study makes several theoretical contributions. First, it extends upper echelons theory into the domain of digital transformation, demonstrating how managerial international experience functions as a mechanism through which executive cognition and strategic decision-making shape firm-level digital change. Second, by uncovering region-based heterogeneity, it advances a more context-sensitive understanding of executive influence, highlighting the institutional embeddedness of upper echelons effects. Empirically, the paper enriches the literature by linking international managerial experience to digital outcomes in an emerging economy context.

Empowering Change: Key Individuals’ Impact on an MNE’s Digital Transformation

IM
William W. Baber (Kyoto University); Aya Samy Ibrahim Abdelfatah (Kyoto University); Arto Ojala (University of Vaasa)
This study examines the mechanisms enabling successful digital transformation (DT) in a large, legacy financial institution operating in a highly regulated environment. Focusing on a major Japanese “megabank,” the research addresses how scope, pace, leadership, and institutional conditions shape DT outcomes, particularly after prior large-scale transformation failures. Drawing on institutional logics, the study conceptualizes DT as a socio-technical process involving not only technology adoption but also deep organizational and cultural change. Using a qualitative case study methodology, the authors analyze interview data from senior executives and managers responsible for DT initiatives. The findings show that transformation progressed through a deliberately incremental, stepwise approach rather than radical overhaul. Powerful senior leaders enabled change by leveraging hierarchical authority while fostering bottom-up project successes that gradually reduced resistance. Crucially, internal stakeholder alignment was not a prerequisite for progress; instead, alignment emerged over time as a consequence of incremental successes and organizational restructuring. The study highlights the role of institutional logics in linking environmental pressures to managerial decision-making and organizational change. By illustrating how institutional inertia can be overcome through small, cumulative interventions, the research contributes to DT literature by challenging assumptions about the necessity of early alignment and offering insights relevant to large, risk-averse organizations.

Overseas Returned Entrepreneurs Boost the Digital Transformation of Private Enterprises

IM
Jing HE (Guizhou University of Finance and Economics); Xiaogang He; Jinxia Peng
Based on the imprint theory and combines the core viewpoints of cognitive theory, this paper constructs a theoretical model of how returnee entrepreneurs influence the digital transformation of private enterprises. Using data from Chinese listed private companies, this study finds that: First, the overseas returnee entrepreneur contributes to promoting the formulation and implementation of digital transformation in private enterprises, a conclusion that remains robust after applying multiple testing methods. Second, the effect of returnee entrepreneurs is moderated by the inclusiveness of the company's senior management, namely, the overseas duration of senior executives and the proportion of returnee executives from highly innovative countries significantly strengthen the positive impact of returnee entrepreneurs on digital transformation. Third, heterogeneity analysis indicates that the promoting effect of returnee entrepreneurs on corporate digital transformation is more pronounced in regions with higher marketization, shorter distances from the company’s location to the nearest port, higher regional openness. This study holds significant theoretical and practical value for deepening the understanding of international human capital mobility and the adoption of digital technologies.
