Developing Instructional Capability for Future Employability

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Author: Ruei-Teng Hung Chaoyang University of Technology

Session overview

This study examines how instructional capability developed prior to organizational entry translates into future employability among pre-service teachers, with a focus on the mediating role of teaching self-efficacy. Drawing on a pre-entry human capital development perspective, data were collected from 119 sophomore pre-service English teachers enrolled in an 18-week required teaching practice course emphasizing lesson planning and teaching-aid design. Instructional capability was assessed through expert ratings of video-recorded teaching performances, while teaching self-efficacy and future employability were measured using self-report questionnaires. Regression and mediation analyses revealed that instructional capability positively predicts future employability indirectly through teaching self-efficacy, rather than through a direct effect. These findings underscore the importance of psychological mechanisms in translating pre-entry capability development into employability outcomes and highlight the need for training designs that integrate performance competence with professional confidence in early-stage talent development contexts.
