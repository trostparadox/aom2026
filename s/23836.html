The Cross-Level Effects of Leadership Emergence on Teamwork Effectiveness

OB
Shucong Guo (Hainan University); Jingtao Fu (Hainan University); Keting Guo (Hainan University)
Leadership emergence has become increasingly important in contemporary teams. Drawing on social information processing theory, this study examines the cross-level mechanisms through which leadership emergence affects team effectiveness, focusing on team task performance and team creativity via team behavioral integration. Using a multilevel structural equation modeling approach, we analyzed two-wave matched data from 379 frontline employees nested in 76 teams, collected through a social network method. The results show that leadership emergence is positively associated with team behavioral integration, which in turn mediates its effects on both team task performance and team creativity. In addition, task uncertainty strengthens the positive relationship between team behavioral integration and team task performance. By identifying team behavioral integration as a key explanatory mechanism and task uncertainty as an important boundary condition, this study advances understanding of how leadership emergence contributes to team effectiveness and offers practical insights for managing teams in uncertain task environments.
