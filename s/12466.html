Session overview

Innovation and entrepreneurship are increasingly recognized as central processes through which societies address grand challenges such as climate change, public health crises, digital transformation, and economic development. While scholarship in entrepreneurship and technology and innovation management has traditionally focused on private firms, startups, and market-based activity, a growing body of evidence suggests that many consequential entrepreneurial and technological advances originate in, or are enabled by, public-sector organizations. Governments and public agencies frequently engage in entrepreneurial activity by setting strategic priorities, investing in uncertain technological trajectories, shaping markets, and orchestrating innovation ecosystems. Inspired by a newly published book Innovation and Entrepreneurship in the Public Sector (Oxford University) authored by Drs. Wendy Chen and David Audretsch, this Professional Development Workshop (PDW) brings together scholars from entrepreneurship, technology and innovation management, and public management to examine why and how innovation and entrepreneurship in the public sector matter for entrepreneurship and management theory, empirical research, and teaching. The workshop highlights entrepreneurial processes that unfold under conditions of political oversight, indirect incentives, and institutional constraint. Through multiple perspectives, the PDW explores how public-sector contexts extend existing theories of opportunity formation, resource mobilization, and innovation strategy. We designed this PDW as an interactive forum rather than a traditional panel. Five presenters, including the moderator, will offer concise framing remarks and perspective-driven presentations, followed by structured discussion with participants. Emphasis will be placed on unresolved theoretical questions, methodological challenges, and opportunities for cross-disciplinary research.
