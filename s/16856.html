Uncertainty Framing in Entrepreneurial Narratives: Evidence from Founder Podcasts

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
ENT

People

Author: Philipp Dahl Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich

Session overview

Entrepreneurs often tell stories to explain what they do and why their venture matters. But entrepreneurship is full of uncertainty about markets, technology, demand, and execution. We study how entrepreneurs talk about uncertainty in a natural setting: founder podcasts. To examine uncertainty talk “in the wild,” we analyze 5,609 founder podcast episodes and link linguistic uncertainty framing to venture funding outcomes from Crunchbase (funding within 12 and 24 months, and eventual funding). We operationalize four families of uncertainty framing—cognitive, affective, behavioral, and environmental—and estimate logit models with rich controls and fixed effects. Two framings robustly predict funding: cognitive-positive framing (structured reasoning under uncertainty) and environmental framing (situating the venture in external contingencies). Emotion alone doesn’t reliably predict funding, but emotion-heavy stories with little structure tend to be followed by slower funding. Overall, it’s not about sounding optimistic, it’s about making uncertainty understandable. These findings position uncertainty framing as an observable marker of entrepreneurial sensemaking competence.
