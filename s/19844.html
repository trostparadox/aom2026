Mixed Signals: Interpreting and Responding to Ambiguous Organizational Cues

Session overview

Organizations and their members frequently communicate with one another through ambiguous signals whose meanings must be interpreted by the receiver. Whether in official communication or day-to-day employee interactions, the interpretation of even minimally ambiguous cues can vary dramatically across recipients and contexts. This symposium brings together five studies that each examine how individuals interpret and respond to cues, revealing both the challenges these cues pose and the opportunities they create for more effective management. Each study demonstrates that organizational signals—whether welcoming language in job postings, feedback from grant reviews, training participation requirements, moral disagreements with colleagues, or personality ratings from different perspectives—are rarely received as intended. Instead, their meaning is constructed through complex psychological processes shaped by social identity, prior experience, relational context, and perspective. Together, these studies advance our understanding of how cues influence perceptions and behaviors in organizational settings and offer practical insights for designing clearer, more effective organizational communications and practices.
