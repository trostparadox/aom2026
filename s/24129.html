Commoning the Smart City: Citizens’ Assemblies as Modern Agoras in Times of Climate Crisis

OMT
Ilona Beliatskaya (Estonian Business School); Kätlin Pulk (Estonian Business School)
This paper explores how a citizens’ assembly can act as a modern agora that reorients smart city agendas toward commons-based and climate-responsive governance. Drawing on an exploratory single-case study of the Tallinn Citizens’ Assembly 2023, which was held while the city served as European Green Capital, the research investigates how participatory democracy, technological mediation, and environmental governance intersect in practice. Grounded in participant observation and supplemented by publicly available materials post-event, the analysis followed the development of deliberation and its translation into municipal policy. The findings reveal that the assembly enabled citizens, experts, and the city government to collaboratively shape ideas and actions for a more climate-responsive and inclusive urban future. It illustrates how democratic innovation can help shift smart city governance toward more participatory and collaborative practices.
