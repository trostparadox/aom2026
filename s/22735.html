Coping Under Tension: How Higher Education Faculty Regulate their Identity During GenAI Integration

OB
Menglu Yang (Durham University); Chrysostomos Apostolidis (Durham University Business School)
As generative AI (GenAI) transforms higher education (HE), faculty are trying to understand how it impacts their teaching and research. To understand this phenomenon, we conducted an inductive qualitative study using semi-structured interviews with 33 faculty members in mainland China. We developed a process model that explains how academic faculty experiences and manages tension over time. Our model identifies four factors triggering tension in faculty work, namely, Institutional expectations, Value–role concerns, unreliability and social proof. The use of AI both empowers and disempowers faculty members, resulting in what we call a role identity trap, which is mainly characterized by cognitive dissonance and emotional ambivalence. In their efforts to regulate this dissonance and escape the identity trap, faculty members may follow three tension-specific identity-regulation pathways: approach-oriented coping, boundary regulation, and avoidance-oriented coping. Collectively, we extend cognitive dissonance theory in the context of academic professional identity and GenAI by conceptualizing these experiences as recurring and identity-relevant. These insights inform how HE institutions can build sustained support that enables faculty to integrate GenAI responsibly, whilst safeguarding professional integrity. Key Words: Higher Education Faculty, GenAI, Empowerment, Disempowerment, Identity Trap, Tension

Humanizing Algorithmic HRM: Leader Support as a Buffer for Employee Well-Being

OB
Kashif Ullah Khan (Wenzhou-Kean University); Fouzia Atlas (Wenzhou-Kean University); Qurat-ul-ain Talpur (ESSCA School of Management)
The current research explores the dark side of AI usage in HR practices at work. Specifically, using the Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) model, it examines how AI-driven HRM, as a job demand, can lead to employee work exhaustion. It further presents the mediating role of work rumination, explaining how AI-driven HRM remains linked with work exhaustion. Additionally, leaders may play a crucial role in managing such stressful work conditions. This research tests the buffering role of leader emotional support on the relationship between AI-driven HRM and work rumination. Three studies using mixed methods provide support for the hypothesized model. Study 1, which included vignette experiments conducted with 158 employees, confirmed that individuals in a high AI-driven HRM condition were more likely to experience work exhaustion than their counterparts. Study 2, with a sample of 173 employees, employed a 2x2 experimental design with AI-driven HRM and leader emotional support conditions, confirming that employees in the high AI-driven HRM-low leader emotional support condition were significantly more likely to ruminate about work-related issues than those in the other conditions. Study 3, which used three-wave time-lagged surveys among 349 employees, showed that AI-driven HRM is significantly associated with employee work exhaustion. It has validated the significant mediating role of work rumination on the linkage between AI-driven HRM and work exhaustion. Furthermore, the findings of the moderated mediation analysis showed that leader emotional support significantly buffers the effect of AI-driven HRM on work rumination, weakening the indirect effect on work exhaustion under high levels of leader emotional support.

Efficiency Paradox of AI Adoption: Unpacking the Enabling and Burdening Mechanisms of Job Crafting

OB
Yanyuan Qi (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Yifei Wang (Tongji University)
As artificial intelligence (AI) is widely integrated into the workplace, understanding the implications for work experience and performance has become an important research agenda. Existing research paid little attention to work efficiency — an immediate and process-oriented indicator that captures how effectively employees accomplish their daily tasks. This oversight is notable because enhancing efficiency is a goal of AI adoption. Drawing upon the Job Demand-Resource theory, this study unveils both the bright and dark sides of AI adoption for work efficiency. Specifically, AI adoption influences work efficiency through both enabling (i.e., job crafting) and burdening (i.e., job demand) pathways, and these effects hinge on how employees perceived the technological affordance of AI it, particularly its transparency. We tested hypotheses using a two-wave, time-lagged survey study. The results showed that AI adoption impaired work efficiency by increasing job demand, but enhanced work efficiency by fostering job crafting. Moreover, perceived AI transparency moderated both indirect effects: it weakened the negative indirect effect through job demand, while strengthening the positive indirect effect through job crafting. Our findings contribute to the literature by highlighting AI adoption as a double-edged sword for work efficiency and identifying perceived AI transparency as a critical boundary condition shaping its impacts.

Workplace Artificial Intelligence Relieves Emotional Labor and Enhances Mental Health

OB
Kaixian Mao (Renmin University of China); Huidi Lu (University of Oxford); Ziwei Shen (Peking University); Shiyong Xu (Renmin University of China)
This paper examines whether and under what conditions artificial intelligence (AI) assistance improves employee mental health. Empirical evidence derived from both secondary data and controlled experimental settings indicates that workplace AI assistance has a positive influence on mental health, particularly when used for social emotional tasks. One explanation is that by reducing the emotional labor usually demanded in these activities, AI helps decrease feelings of emotional exhaustion. Interestingly, the effects vary by emotional demandingness of the tasks and employees’ communication abilities. Individuals with lower communication ability benefit more overall, but mainly when emotional demands of the tasks are moderate, whereas those with higher ability benefit primarily under high emotional load. These findings highlight the potential of AI-based tools in alleviating work-related stress, while also pointing to varied impacts among different employee subgroups.
