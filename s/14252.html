Session overview

The diffusion of purpose-oriented business models and sustainability frameworks has intensified scholarly attention on how for-profit firms generate social and environmental impact. However, skepticism toward corporate sustainability claims about impact generation open opportunities to further investigate the integrity of measurement dynamics in business context (Rawhouser et al., 2019). Recent research on sustainability decoupling highlights the risks of firms relying primarily on symbolic communication rather than substantive practices (Delmas & Burbano, 2011; Kim & Lyon, 2015; Wu et al., 2022; Gull et al., 2023; Cepêda et al., 2025). Therefore, emerging standards and regulations, such as the B Corp Certification v2.1 framework and the European Union’s Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), reduce information asymmetries and strengthen the credibility of firms’ sustainability claims (European Commission, 2022; B Lab, 2025). This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) focuses on sustainability measurement as a mechanism differentiating the purpose-driven strategies in for-profit firms, especially in certified B Corporations (Marquis, 2020). By foregrounding measurement, this PDW examines whether evolving frameworks can discourage symbolic compliance and foster meaningful alignment between purpose, strategy, and impact (Marquis & Lee, 2015; Lucas et al., 2022). It situates B Corp certification within a broader institutional landscape, including regulatory frameworks and global initiatives such as the Sustainable Development Goals (George et al., 2022). Through panel discussions and a paper development session, the workshop advances theory and research on sustainability measurement, double materiality, and impact authenticity, contributing to conversations on how measurement systems can support substantive organizational transformation rather than mere compliance. The first part of the PDW is open to the general public and welcome all researchers interested in B Corps, purpose-driven organizations, social enterprises and social entrepreneurship; The second half of the PDW is by invitation only for those who submit working papers and are accepted for restricted feedbacks and discussions in groups. Submissions for the paper sessions are due by July 1st, 2026.
