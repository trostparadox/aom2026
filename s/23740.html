Innovative Entrepreneurial Management: Empirical Evidence from Brazil’s Third Sector

ENT
CLEDINEI CLÓVIS MELO CAVALHEIRO; Suzete Antonieta Lizote (Universidade do Vale do Itajaí - Univali); Patrick Zawadzki (University of Western Santa Catarina); Sayonara de Fátima Teston
This study investigated how social innovation relates to value creation and entrepreneurial orientation from the perspective of employees of a third-sector organization. The empirical context was the International Federation Fe y Alegría in Brazil, whose mission involves social development and popular education in contexts of vulnerability, making it relevant to understand how innovative practices connect to value generation and to a more entrepreneurial organizational culture. The research adopted a quantitative, relational, cross-sectional design, and the data were analyzed using univariate and multivariate techniques. Three research hypotheses were proposed and supported. Positive relationships were confirmed between social innovation and value creation; between social innovation and entrepreneurial orientation; and between value creation and entrepreneurial orientation. As a contribution, the study strengthens empirical evidence that social innovation may operate as an organizational mechanism simultaneously associated with value creation and the strengthening of entrepreneurial behaviors, indicating implications for the planning and management of social organizations. By showing that these factors are already perceived by employees from different areas, the study suggests incorporating them into the management model, with the potential to consolidate a culture oriented toward innovation, value, and entrepreneurship.
