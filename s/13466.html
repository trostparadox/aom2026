Making and Giving Sense in Post-Merger Integration: A Multilevel Leadership Analysis

Authors: Renata Costa Fundação Dom Cabral; Felippe Oliveira University of Dundee; Alice Erthal Fundacao Dom Cabral

Session overview

International mergers and acquisitions (M&A) remain a high-stakes strategic maneuver characterized by high failure rates, often attributed to the neglect of "human integration" in favor of financial synergies. This research conceptualizes post-merger integration (PMI) not as a monolithic technical restructuring, but as a stratified negotiation of meaning operating across distinct hierarchical levels. Employing a longitudinal, single case study of a $1.8 billion acquisition in the industrial ingredients sector, this study investigates the micro-foundational processes of how leadership constructs, translates, and reframes meaning during integration. By triangulating data from 255 meeting transcripts, field journals, and documents over a fifteen-month period, we reveal a profound structural divergence in how the acquisition was experienced. Findings indicate that Global leaders engaged in "institutional sensegiving," constructing abstract, heroic narratives of historic legitimacy. In contrast, Operational teams largely rejected high-level rhetoric, engaging in "pragmatic sensemaking" anchored in daily realities and validating integration only through tangible "quick wins." Crucially, the study identifies Regional leaders as the essential linchpin of organizational continuity. Caught between abstract global promises and operational anxieties, these intermediate managers performed intense emotional labor—specifically "emotional hiding" and "emotional reversal"—to bridge the dissonance. We argue that organizational cohesion during M&A depends less on strategic perfection and more on the capacity of middle management to perform this critical translation work, transforming uncertainty into a shared sense of belonging.
