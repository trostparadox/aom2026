Session overview

Taxation has traditionally been treated as a compliance obligation or a cost-minimization exercise. However, the contemporary tax environment is increasingly shaped by complex interactions among governments, multinational organizations, investors, employees, civil society, and supranational institutions. Stakeholder theory offers a compelling lens to reframe taxation as a domain of strategic engagement rather than a purely technical or legal matter (Freeman, 1984). When organizations and tax authorities treat tax-related decisions and policies as opportunities to actively involve, address and balance the competing interests of multiple stakeholders, we can perceive taxation also as strategic stakeholder engagement. This moves beyond seeing taxation as a mere compliance or financial issue, instead framing it as a way to build trust, legitimacy, and long-term value by recognizing, supporting, and maintaining dialogue with all affected parties (Kujala et al., 2022). Strategic stakeholder engagement in taxation involves understanding and balancing stakeholders, some of which may be overlooked, and adapting strategies to fit institutional and cultural contexts, which can lead to improved firm performance and stronger relationships with both internal and external stakeholders (Gupta et al., 2020).
