Cosmetic Equality: How Individual-Focused Gender Initiatives Leave Power Structures Intact

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1

Author: Alexandra Pfisterer Friedrich-Alexander University of Erlangen-Nuremberg

Session overview

In German university hospitals, many physicians—especially women—report questioning or leaving clinical career paths due to a lack of distributive fairness, dysfunctional hierarchies, and hostile work environments. Although women constitute two thirds of medical students and nearly half of the physician workforce, they exit clinical work earlier and report higher rates of burnout, depression, and emotional exhaustion. Although these developments create increasing pressure on university hospitals, the organizational mechanisms that shape unequal access to power, resources, and career opportunities have remained remarkably stable. Drawing on 39 interviews with physicians across hierarchical levels, this study illustrates how patronage and informal power networks influence everyday clinical work, have impact on physicians’ motivation and influence whether they pursue, postpone, or abandon certain career paths.Interviewees frequently describe equality measures as ineffective and equality officers as insufficiently empowered to intervene in cases of discrimination or power abuse. To explain this functional failure, the analysis highlights how unequal task allocation and the self-elimination of outgroup members operate as mechanisms of organizational boundary work that benefit elite ingroups (Lorber, 1993; Seibel, 1994). The study also discusses how individual-focused interventions may unintentionally reinforce inequality by obscuring structural barriers. Finally, the findings show how the organizational positioning of equality officers can create conflicts of interest that limit their ability to act, illustrating how organizations can structure equality work in ways that prevent it from influencing core processes of power and resource distribution.
