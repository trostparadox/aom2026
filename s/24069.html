Does TMT Stability Help or Hurt Operational Efficiency? A Relational Embeddedness Perspective

OSCM
Xintong Liu (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); En Xie (Tongji University); Huahua LI
While existing research emphasizes how the succession and characteristics of individual executives influence firm performance, the collective dynamics of the top management team (TMT), which can shape strategic choices in important ways, remain relatively underexplored in the context of operational efficiency. This study shifts the focus from individual-level appointments to team-level collective stability. Integrating upper echelons theory with a relational embeddedness perspective, it examines whether an optimal level of TMT stability exists to maximize operational efficiency, revealing a potential “too-much-of-a-good-thing” effect in executive team dynamics. The conceptual framework is tested on panel data for 3,306 Chinese listed firms from 2011 to 2022, using fixed-effects models and instrumental-variable regressions to mitigate endogeneity. The results reveal an inverted U-shaped relationship between TMT stability and operational efficiency. While moderate stability fosters trust, coordination, and knowledge sharing, excessive stability engenders cognitive rigidity and social inertia, eventually diminishing efficiency. Furthermore, high customer concentration and bankruptcy risk serve as critical boundary conditions that postpone the inflection point where stability begins to yield detrimental effects. Our study not only advances existing knowledge on the operational implications of TMT but also broadens the application of the relational embeddedness perspective by underscoring the importance of intra-firm social dynamics in driving operational efficiency. The findings also have practical implications, providing guidance on optimizing TMT stability in varying contextual conditions to enhance operational performance. Keywords Operational efficiency; Top management team stability; Customer concentration; Bankruptcy risk
