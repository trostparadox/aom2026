Session overview

Universities have long celebrated research-based teaching as a cornerstone of academic legitimacy, yet the reverse dynamic—teaching as a catalyst for research—remains strikingly underexplored. This symposium challenges conventional knowledge flows by positioning pedagogy as a fertile ground for theory development. We focus on organizational paradox theory, which illuminates how actors navigate persistent tensions such as efficiency versus innovation or centralization versus autonomy. Distinguished scholars and scholar-practitioners on this panel will engage participants in a debate about the promises and pitfalls of teaching-based research for advancing paradox theory. The discussion will highlight research designs, ethical considerations, and methodological pathways for transforming teaching into a site of theorizing. Our aim is to spark a scholarly conversation on reversing the research–teaching nexus, advancing a symbiotic cycle that deepens both academic inquiry and educational practice, and turning classrooms into living laboratories.
