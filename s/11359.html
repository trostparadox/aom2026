Scoop up the Moon: An Insider’s Reflection on Counseling Profession in China’s Psy-Boom

Author: Rongrong luo Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University

Session overview

In an endeavor to reflexively locate the social (re)production of my individual experiences, I probed into the paradox of the “psy-boom” in contemporary China. Despite the meteoric rise of psychological counseling over the past two decades, its legitimacy remains contested. I discovered that occupational members strive for professionalization but find themselves caught in a state of “professional liminality”, situated betwixt and between more established professions. Through a Bordieuan lens, I saw this occupational state results from its members’ uncritical adoption of the symbolic systems of these proximate professions and the doxa of the broader society. I discuss how these social agents, in their attempts to maintain or advance their positions in the field, become complicit in reproducing their own subordination. I analyzed my embodied transitions into and out of this occupation to uncover the underlying process in which this complicity was created by the mutual conditionality of habitus and field. A key implication is that the unreflective support of professional ideology limits the emerging occupation's capacity to recognize and legitimize its own forms of field-specific expertise. This realization invites to rethink the non-neutral concept of “profession”, thereby opening new ground for dialogue between critical management studies and mainstream management theory.
