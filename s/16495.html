Enacted Agency: Enabling Control and the Cultivation of Civic Entrepreneurship

Authors: Yutong SUN Department of Management and Marketing, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Yong HUANG The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

Scholarship on community and governance typically dichotomizes formal bureaucratic institutions and informal community practices, treating the latter as predicated on a static endowment of resident agency. This study challenges that static conceptualization by examining how agency can be systematically cultivated through external intervention. Drawing on a micro-ethnography of the "Mini-Venture" initiative in Chengdu, China, we analyze a policy experiment where street-level bureaucrats and designers acted as agents of enabling control. We argue that resident agency is a developmental outcome fostered through intentional sensegiving, which instills an entrepreneurial logic into civic practice. Building on research regarding opportunity recognition, bricolage, and uncertainty, we inductively develop a three-tier framework of enacted civic agency: community self-service, community self-entertainment, and community entrepreneurship. Our findings reveal that while agency is stratified by community endowment, it is fundamentally an accomplishment of "supervised sensemaking" rather than a pre-existing condition. This study elucidates the micro-foundations of informal governance, demonstrates that formal institutions can serve as architects of the civic autonomy upon which they depend.
