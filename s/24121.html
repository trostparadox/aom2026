The Promise and the Pain of Ambidexterity: How ACAP and Middle Managers Prevent Idea Frustration

OMT
Luiz Fernando Fogaça Vieira (Mackenzie Presbyterian University); Silvio Popadiuk (Mackenzie Presbyterian University)
Contextual ambidexterity—the simultaneous pursuit of exploration and exploitation—has become an imperative for organizations seeking innovation. However, this theoretical essay argues that when organizations encourage autonomy and idea generation without the corresponding capacity to process these inputs, a "communicational void" emerges, which employees perceive as a violation of the psychological contract. Integrating literatures on ambidexterity, absorptive capacity (ACAP), and sensemaking, we propose a conceptual model wherein ACAP functions not merely as a technical learning mechanism, but as a critical socio-emotional "buffer" that mitigates employee frustration. Central to this model is the role of middle managers, who act not as mere information conduits but as architects of ACAP, using sensegiving to contextualize the acceptance or rejection of ideas and maintain organizational trust. Theoretically, this work contributes by (1) reconceptualizing ACAP as a fundamentally human and relational capability, countering its reification in recent literature; and (2) proposing that Potential ACAP can serve as a strategic substitute for personnel turnover, challenging the classical view that knowledge renewal requires replacing individuals. We conclude that stimulating employee voice without strengthening managerial absorptive capacity converts cultural encouragement into emotional rupture.
