AI Adoption Without Slack: How Resource Constraints Reshape Organisational Use of AI

Author: Mncedisi Christopher Mabhele University of Cape Town

Session overview

Research on artificial intelligence (AI) adoption commonly assumes that organisations possess sufficient organisational slack to support experimentation, learning, and failure without immediate consequences. Within much of the digital innovation literature, AI adoption is theorised as an iterative process enabled by surplus financial, human, and temporal resources. This poster challenges that assumption by foregrounding the absence of organisational slack as a constitutive condition shaping AI adoption. Drawing on organisational slack theory and institutional perspectives, the poster advances a conceptual distinction between slack-enabled and slack-constrained AI adoption. Slack-enabled adoption is characterised by experimentation, parallel systems, and tolerance for inefficiency during learning. Slack-constrained adoption, by contrast, is selective, risk-averse, and deeply embedded within existing organisational routines, where failure threatens operational continuity or institutional legitimacy. In such settings, AI is frequently mobilised to stabilise operations, manage regulatory demands, or sustain performance under conditions of scarcity rather than to explore novel capabilities. The poster positions resource-constrained organisations as theoretical stress tests for dominant models of AI adoption. Rather than treating these organisations as laggards or empirical outliers, it argues that they expose hidden assumptions and boundary conditions in prevailing theories of digital innovation. Attention is given to regulated organisational environments in the Global South, where organisational slack tends to be limited. By foregrounding resource constraint as a shaping force rather than a deficit, the poster contributes to organisation and management theory by reframing how AI adoption is conceptualised under conditions.
