Session overview

The role of associate editor (AE) is critical to the profession of Management and Organizational Science – they decide how journal articles should be improved and act as gatekeepers for their publication. Thus, AEs are at the heart of our knowledge production systems, and their decisions materially impact the careers of scholars. Being an AE is also very demanding with the heavy and complex workloads and increasing expectations in terms of timelines and accountability. Yet the role of AE is largely unremunerated, unrecognized and often unacknowledged by employers. It can be stressful for many reasons, not the least is the rate of rejection of colleagues’ papers. It often lacks structured support mechanisms as most journals do not have the resources or bandwidth to run formal training programs, and many editorial teams are geographically dispersed which limits the possibility of mentoring and on the job learning. Furthermore, as AEs are supposedly appointed because of their expertise, many might feel uncomfortable in reaching out to their fellow editors. As such it can be a lonely experience, especially for new appointed AEs. The purpose of this PDW, therefore, is to provide experience, strength and hope for those in or desiring to be in the AE role. We offer a series of carefully structured activities designed to build AE skill and resilience. These will be guided by the facilitators and other invited experienced editors who will contribute to panel discussions and plenary debates. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
