Unveiling the Opening of Corporate Political Strategizing Under Stigmatization by the Government

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
SAP

People

Authors: Juan Sebastian Aristizabal EAFIT University; Maria Andrea De Villa EAFIT University

Session overview

Open Strategy research has advanced our understanding of the opportunities and challenges involved in opening strategy-making to internal and external actors. However, Open Strategy research has largely overlooked the contextual setting in which the opening of strategy takes place. An interesting strategic domain to study the enactment of openness is corporate political strategizing, as it has been typically portrayed as a top management affair that is concealed and secretive. Particularly, under a context of stigmatization by the government towards the private sector, some firms are changing the way they are ‘doing’ their corporate political strategizing by opening up this traditionally closed strategic domain. Hence, we seek to better understand how firms are opening up their corporate political strategizing in response to a context characterized by organizational stigmatization from the government. In doing so, we aim to examine the revelatory case of Grupo Sura, one of the largest Latin American investment holding companies offering healthcare insurance, to provide a processual account (2018-2026) of how a firm opens up over time the practices involved in its corporate political strategizing in reaction to government stigmatization. We will adopt a combined view of Strategy as Practice and Process, to address data collection and analysis. Our findings aim to contribute to understanding how contextual conditions can lead to openness in a traditionally closed strategic domain. They also aim to extend our understanding of how the contemporary practices involved in corporate political strategizing are drastically changing.
