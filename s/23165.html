Readiness Pathway: How Implementation Intensity Shapes Technology Integration and Efficiency in I4.0

CTO
Benjamin R Wixson (Case Western Reserve University); Kalle Lyytinen (Case Western Reserve University); Miyeon Jung (Case Western Reserve University)
Research on digital transformation has emphasized which Industry 4.0 (I4.0) technologies firms adopt, but less is known about how implementation approach shapes conditions enabling successful integration. We conceptualize implementation approach as implementation intensity, defined by the scope and speed of technology rollout, and argue that it influences performance indirectly by mobilizing organizational readiness rather than acting as a direct performance lever. Using structural equation modeling on survey data from 386 U.S. manufacturing professionals, we test a sequential model in which implementation intensity increases a readiness capability composed of absorptive capacity, financial preparedness, workforce adaptability, and leadership support. Readiness enhances technology integration feasibility, which improves operational efficiency. Results support this pathway: implementation intensity increases readiness, readiness predicts integration feasibility, and integration feasibility predicts operational efficiency, while direct effects of implementation intensity are nonsignificant. These findings indicate that I4.0 outcomes depend less on radical versus incremental approaches and more on how scope and speed are used to build sociotechnical readiness capabilities linking implementation strategy to integration and operational improvement.

Digital Transformation Paradoxes and Coping Strategies in Manufacturing Incumbents

CTO
Ei (Emily) Shu (North China University of Technology)
Although previous studies have emphasized the widespread impact of digital technologies, research remains scarce in the realm of radical innovation efforts in the context of digital manufacturing in established firms. To address this gap, this study adopts a paradox lens to specifically investigate paradoxical tensions and coping strategies associated with a traditional apparel manufacturing firm’s transformation from mass production to digitally-driven customized production systems. By revealing how the core paradox between mass production and customization sparks other paradoxes and emerging tensions, this study provides a more nuanced view on the complex challenges traditional manufacturing firms face when introducing disruptive digital technologies. The findings also contribute to the paradox theory by revealing a three-way knotted tensions in the specific context of manufacturing incumbents’ digital innovation endeavors

Performing the Data Alchemy: Digital Resource Recombination and Value Theorizing Under Uncertainty

CTO
Zhijian Yang (Zhejiang University); Fuyicheng Lou (Zhejiang University)
Digital resource recombination lies at the heart of innovation. Yet firms face substantial uncertainty when recombining resources such as digital data whose value is ambiguous and volatile ex ante. Drawing on the Theory-Based View, we conceptualize digital resource recombination as an iterative process of value theorizing and experimentation. Through a longitudinal case study of a Chinese digital firm, Merit, we trace how the firm recombined data resources across layered-modular architectural contexts. We consequently identify four recombination approaches (distilling, deploying, channeling, and assembling), and delineate how digital resource recombination was steered by evolving theories of value recursively forged through abductive reasoning. This study advances research on digital resource recombination by explicating an imbricated iteration between resource portfolios and firms’ theory of value. We also extend the burgeoning Theory-Based View literature via unfolding the abductive reasoning as the momentum for advancing theory and producing value out of uncertainty.

Situated Sensegiving: How Frontline Practices Resolve Digital Service Stagnation

CTO
Hsin Kuan (Yuan Ze University); Rueylin Hsiao (National Chengchi University); Chao Yi Huang (National Chengchi University)
Why do digital services remain unused even as organizations continuously improve their functionality and interfaces? This study examines adoption stagnation, a condition in which systems are technically usable yet fail to enter everyday practice. Drawing on an in-depth qualitative case study of long-inactive investors (“dormant users”) in the securities industry, we examine how stalled digital innovation is reactivated through frontline work rather than redesign or persuasion. Grounded in situated practice theory, this study shows that adoption stagnation arises not from users’ lack of understanding, but from a misalignment between digital services and users’ sensemaking about risk and everyday order. Tracing frontline interactions, we identify situated sensegiving as a practice-based mechanism through professionals rework meaning in action. Sensegiving, therefore, does not occur prior to action but is accomplished in practice. This study contributes to theory in three ways. First, it relocates adoption analysis from user behavior to frontline situated practices. Second, it advances sensemaking research by theorizing sensegiving as embedded in practice rather than detached from cognition. Third, it extends digital innovation research by showing that progress depends on frontline work re-anchoring technology within existing practices. When adoption stagnates, sense is earned through situated interaction rather than given before action.

A Theory-Driven Framework of Digital Resilience Across Institutional Contexts

CTO
Aqsa Bilal Hussain (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Jingchun SUN (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Bilal HUSSAIN (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Dr. Muhammad Shaique (Sukkur Institute of Business Administration)
Global shocks such as COVID 19 expose a puzzle: firms with comparable digital maturity exhibit divergent resilience. This study theorizes that resilience arises not from a single maturity trajectory but from institutionally contingent configurations of digital capabilities. Integrating dynamic capabilities and institutional theory, we advance a contingency framework in which sensing, seizing, and transforming are differentially enabled by specific capabilities under distinct regulative, normative, and cultural cognitive logics. Empirically, we employ an interpretable, attention based deep learning model for tabular data together with game theoretic feature attribution to analyze a panel of 26,000 firm year observations for listed firms in the United States and China spanning pre and post COVID periods. The analysis uncovers robust cross national asymmetries. In the United States, resilience is most strongly associated with digital platform integration and flexible information technology infrastructure, enabling rapid recombination of channels, work arrangements, and ecosystem ties. In China, resilience is primarily driven by data analytics maturity and digitally integrated supply chains, which support high frequency monitoring and coordinated response in more relational and state influenced settings. We contribute a theory driven framework specifying how institution specific capability bundles translate digital transformation into resilience, challenge Universalist prescriptions, and delineate boundary conditions. Methodologically, we show how interpretable machine learning can surface context specific pathways rather than merely predict outcomes. Practically, we offer guidance on capability prioritization by country context and highlight policy levers that shape national resilience through the incentives they create for capability development.
