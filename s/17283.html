The Interactive Effects of Goal Orientation and Attachment Style on Creative Behavior

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Author: Hyunjoo Lee Sungkyunkwan University

Session overview

Drawing on goal orientation theory and attachment theory, this study examines how individuals’ creative behavior is shaped by the interaction between goal orientation and attachment style. Using a laboratory experiment with 121 Korean undergraduate students, three types of goal orientation—learning goal orientation (LGO), prove performance goal orientation (PPGO), and avoid performance goal orientation (APGO)—were manipulated, along with two attachment styles: anxiety and avoidance. Results from a 3 × 2 between-subjects design indicate that under LGO and PPGO conditions, individuals high in attachment anxiety demonstrate higher levels of creative behavior than those high in avoidance. In contrast, under APGO conditions, individuals high in attachment anxiety exhibit lower creativity than those high in avoidance. These findings highlight attachment style as a critical interpersonal boundary condition in the goal orientation–creativity relationship, extending both goal orientation and attachment theories by integrating motivational and relational perspectives on creativity.
