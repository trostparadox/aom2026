When Sleep Backfires: The Role of Moral Ambiguity

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM

People

Authors: Jure Andolšek School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Christopher Barnes University of Washington; Matej Cerne School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana

Session overview

We examine how sleep shapes unethical behavior in high-stakes decision environments characterized by moral ambiguity. Drawing on ego-depletion–based self-regulatory models, prior research has largely portrayed sleep as a uniformly protective resource that constrains unethical conduct. We extend this perspective by integrating Cognitive Load Theory and dual-process models of judgment to develop a context-sensitive model of sleep and unethical behavior. We argue that sleep increases deliberative capacity, but that deliberation is not inherently ethical; when situations afford justificatory latitude, sleep-restored cognitive resources may facilitate moral rationalization rather than ethical sensemaking. We propose two longitudinal experience-sampling studies conducted during the 2026 national and municipal election cycles in Slovenia, combining objective sleep measurement with daily surveys to capture within and between-person decision dynamics. By shifting attention from whether cognitive capacity is restored to how it is deployed, this work advances a more novel, context-sensitive account of the ethical consequences of sleep.
