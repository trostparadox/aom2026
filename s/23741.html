Motivational Factors of Investors in Equity-Based Crowdfunding: A Process-Oriented Perspective

ENT
Sophie Kutzsch (Dresden University of Technology); Martina Pieperhoff (Technische Universität Dresden)
Equity-based crowdfunding (ECF) has emerged as an important entrepreneurial financing mechanism, enabling early-stage ventures to raise capital from diverse investors. While prior research has largely focused on campaign creators, platforms, or isolated investor motives, little is known about how different motivational factors interact and unfold throughout the ECF investment decision-making process. This study addresses this gap by examining the role of financial and non-financial motivations across key stages of investor decision-making in ECF. Using an exploratory qualitative approach, we conduct semi-structured interviews with ECF investors and apply inductive analysis to develop a propositions model illustrating how instrumental, intrinsic, and participative motivational factors emerge and interact. Our findings identify 27 interrelated factors. Instrumental motivational factors relate primarily to expected financial returns under conditions of high uncertainty. Intrinsic motivational factors reflect value alignment, meaningful engagement, and personal relevance, while participative motivational factors stem from enjoyment, curiosity, and involvement in entrepreneurial processes. These factors overlap and dynamically shift across different decision-making stages. This work contributes to the literature on alternative finance and investor behavior by offering a process-oriented perspective on investor motivation in ECF. We derive six propositions and provide insights for platforms and campaign creators seeking to address heterogeneous investor motivations.
