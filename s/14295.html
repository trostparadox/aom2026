Session overview

Leadership judgment is increasingly formed in classrooms where AI systems organize attention before students begin thinking. When learners encounter AI generated recommendations, rankings, and confidence scores, these cues shape what seems relevant and what disappears from view. Judgment often begins before students recognize that their attention has already been shaped. This workshop addresses a central pedagogical challenge in management education: how to teach leadership judgment when artificial intelligence actively participates in its formation. Drawing on emerging scholarship on AI mediated leadership judgment, the workshop treats judgment as an intentionally designed pedagogical moment rather than an assumed byproduct of analytical skill development. Through a structured experiential sequence, participants make the algorithmic influence on attention visible and redesign existing curricula to cultivate reflexive judgment and human accountability in AI influenced learning environments.
