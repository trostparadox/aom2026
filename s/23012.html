Beyond “If”: How, Where, and Why Artificial Intelligence is Reconfiguring GVCs

IM
Ye He (University of Warwick); Weihang Huang (University of Birmingham); Xiaocan Yuan (Aston Business School); Hossam Zeitoun (University of Warwick)
The proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) is poised to reconfigure the global operations of multinational firms, yet its precise impact on global value chain (GVC) dynamics remains theoretically and empirically underexplored. This study addresses this gap by applying a novel methodology to measure and categorize firm-level AI adoption. Using a deep learning approach (e.g., BERT model) on the text of US firms’ 10-K filings, we construct multi-dimensional indicators for the intensity, diversity, and strategic type of AI adoption. Challenging the traditional view that AI adoption enhances operational stickiness in existing production locations, our research offers a different view on the contingent relationship between AI adoption and GVC relocation and tests it empirically. We argue that the impact of AI is not uniform, but that its effect is contingent on the type of AI adopted, the firm’s strategy, its industry context, and its position within the supply chain network. This study contributes to the literature by providing an evidence-based framework for understanding which types of AI adoption, and under what strategic conditions, are reshaping the global corporate landscape.

Navigating Complexity in IB Through Data: A Machine Learning Augmented Approach

IM
Yulun Ma (Tsinghua University); Oli Mihalache (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Mashiho Mihalache (EDHEC Business School); Arjen Van Witteloostuijn (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
International Business (IB) research traditionally uses theoretical simplification to model complex global environments. This approach, while parsimonious, struggles to capture the nuanced, non-linear interdependencies inherent in multinational enterprise (MNE) decisions. In this study, we propose and apply a "machine learning-augmented approach" to address this gap. Combining the exploratory capacity of machine learning with the explanatory power of interpretable ML techniques on data from Japanese MNEs, our method lets theory emerge inductively from data before abductive refinement. We investigate central strategic decision on the relationship between the number of subsidiary (both globally and within their industry) and equity ownership in foreign subsidiaries. The analysis uncovers non-linear relationships and interaction effects among firm, subsidiary, industry, and host-country factors that conventional models miss. Our study demonstrates how this data-driven, abductive process yields more contextually nuanced insights and provides a practical methodological framework for embracing real-world complexity without sacrificing theoretical rigor in IB.

Data Matters! Why Firms Conduct Foreign Digital Innovation in the Digital Era

IM
Tianyu Gong (Shenzhen University); Xiaolei Wang (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); En Xie (Tongji University); Li Zhang (Tongji University)
Previous research has identified technology-seeking, market-seeking, and intellectual property right (IPR)-seeking as the main drivers for firms to pursue foreign non-digital innovation. With the rise of digital innovation in the digital era, this study highlights the unique role of data for digital innovation in digital era. In context of global data regulation complexity, we suggest that a country’s stringent domestic data regulations may push local firms to seek alternative data sources for digital innovation in foreign countries with more lenient data regulations. This effect becomes less relevant for firm’s foreign non-digital innovation but more salient with stricter regional enforcement of domestic data regulation, larger data volumes in foreign countries, and more advanced data analytic capability by local firms. By using the Chinese Cybersecurity Law (CSL) as a regulatory shock, we employ a difference-in-differences design to test our hypotheses using panel data from publicly listed internet firms in China from 2013 to 2019. Our study reveals data-seeking as a new mechanism for firms to conduct foreign digital innovation in digital era.

From Local to Global: How Leading Firms Internationalize Through Digital Platform Construction

IM
Yanping Chen (University of Electronic Science and Technology of China); Max Von Zedtwitz (University of St.Gallen); Yanbo Wu; Zhichun Dong (University of Electronic Science and Technology of China)
Digital platforms are reshaping how leading firms expand globally by enabling novel forms of organizational connectivity, resource integration, and strategic control. While prior research has explored the role of digital platforms in internationalization, limited attention has been paid to the underlying mechanisms through which platform construction enables global expansion. This paper investigates how a leading enterprise achieves internationalization through building digital platforms, using a longitudinal single-case study of BOE Technology Group. A process-oriented qualitative approach was adopted, involving archival analysis and 32 in-depth interviews. The findings identify three core mechanisms through which digital platforms facilitate internationalization: (1) building a digital platform to integrate and reshape international market resources; (2) expanding organizational boundaries and capabilities through the platform; and (3) redefining internationalization strategy through platform governance. These mechanisms illustrate how digital platforms serve as both technical infrastructure and strategic enablers for cross-border coordination, innovation, and capability reconfiguration. This study advances theoretical understanding by revealing the dynamic interplay between platform construction and internationalization in leading firms, and provides a process model to capture how digital platforms reshape global expansion pathways.

When Data Privacy Regulation Hurt Performance? Evidence from Mobile App Markets in Emerging Markets

IM
Mingtao Xu (Tsinghua University); Wenzhe Xu (Tsinghua University)
Data privacy regulations define digital property rights, which should benefit efficiency. However, such gain shall not be taken for granted, especially in emerging markets where the awareness of property rights is also evolving. We study how these laws affect users’ awareness of digital property rights and subsequent app firm performance for in Southeast and South Asian markets using 10 years of weekly data on 934 apps across 11 markets. Exploiting staggered policy promulgation and enactment, we find that: (1) at the extensive-margin, downloads, installs, and app openings decline significantly, while intensive-margin behaviors such as session frequency and duration remain stable; (2) user ratings drop sharply upon policy implementation, whereas core behavioral metrics adjust more slowly; (3) domestic apps are more negatively affected, while apps with larger pre-existing user bases experience smaller declines. These results advance literature by demonstrating how digital property rights and institutional contexts jointly shape heterogeneous user behavior and firm adaptation in digital markets.
