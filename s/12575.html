Session overview

The availability of large-scale public biobanks and the advancement of health and genomic sciences have opened new vistas for management research. While fields like economics and psychology have long integrated physical health and biological markers, management scholarship—particularly in HR and OB— has yet to fully utilize these rich resources. This PDW aims to demystify the use of health-related and genetic databases (e.g., AddHealth and UK Biobank) for organizational studies. We address the "technical wall" that scholars face in an era where AI and automated procedures are commoditizing traditional survey methods. By incorporating lifestyle and biological "ground truth," researchers can explore unique questions regarding leadership, occupational choice, employee well-being, and the interplay between nurture and nature in the workplace. This session brings together experts to discuss: (1) the landscape of available lifestyle and biological databases; (2) the process of gaining access to sensitive data; and (3) the integration of physical health and genetic markers into organizational theories. The workshop features a 45-minute expert panel followed by a 45-minute hands-on practice session. Participants will leave with a practical roadmap for accessing data and a conceptual framework for applying biological insights to HR and OB research, fostering a new wave of interdisciplinary, multi-level scholarship that addresses the complexities of human behavior in modern organizations.
