Fix the System, Not Women: Navigating Barriers in Australian Digital Startup Ecosystem

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4

Authors: Madiha latif RMIT University; Leila Afshari RMIT University; Tracy Lynn Taylor RMIT University

Session overview

This study explores the ongoing underrepresentation of women in Australia's digital startup scene, where women make up almost one- quarter of founders in the ecosystem, yet all-women founding teams receive just 2% of venture capital funding. Using the Individual Differences Theory of Gender and IT (IDTGIT), we conducted 51 in-depth interviews with Australian women digital entrepreneurs to examine how individual traits, personal influences, and environmental factors shape their entrepreneurial experiences and results. Applying Reflexive Thematic Analysis and the Gioia methodology, we find a significant tension between structural barriers and personal agency. Environmental factors—involving gendered questioning by investors, male-dominated industry cultures, and policy gaps—form systemic barriers that individual efforts cannot fully remove. Personal traits show high levels of entrepreneurial resilience (69 mentions) and strategic use of digital technology (97 mentions), but these are mainly adaptive responses to structural inequalities, not solutions. Notably, analysis of the Individual Identity dimension shows five specific barriers but no enablers, highlighting that entrepreneurs facing multiple forms of marginalization encounter layered challenges that current support systems fail to address. Two main themes emerge: resilience as resistance (viewing resilience as active agency against constraints) and collective individualism (showing how individual effort relies on social support). This research extends the IDTGIT framework to entrepreneurial settings, offers a multi-level model that connects personal differences with environmental factors, and provides evidence for structural changes that could unlock an estimated $71-135 billion annually in gender parity potential.
