Author: Sha Wang Maastricht University

Session overview

Co-located multicultural entrepreneurial teams increasingly rely on real-time translation tools, yet we know little about how translation media shape interactional processes that underpin entrepreneurial team confidence. Drawing on Communication Accommodation Theory and Cognitive Load Theory, we examine how two translation tools, Augment Reality translation glasses (ARTG) versus a mobile translation app (MTA), relate to perceived appropriate accommodation and, in turn, cross-cultural entrepreneurial self-efficacy (CCESE). We report a team-based experiment in which 57 participants formed 16 cross language venture teams and completed a Business Model Canvas task using either ARTG or MTA. Perceived appropriate accommodation was captured via dyadic partner ratings and aggregated to the individual-level. Multilevel baseline adjusted models show that perceived appropriate accommodation positively predicts post-task individual CCESE (b = 2.721, p = .041), net of baseline CCESE and tool condition. A cross-level interaction provides suggestive evidence that this accommodation to CCESE link is stronger under ARTG than under MTA (interaction b = 6.018, p = .066; simple slope under ARTG is positive and significant). Cognitive load analyses indicate that higher within-team language-related intrinsic load predicts lower perceived appropriate accommodation (b = -0.0278, p = .0028), while between-team entrepreneurial content-related germane load shows a marginal positive association with accommodation climate (b = 0.0494, p = .050). Finally, the second language (L2) proficiency exhibits a non-linear boundary condition for the language intrinsic load to accommodation link. Overall, translation media may not directly change perceived accommodation in co-located teams, but may condition when accommodative interaction translates into entrepreneurial team confidence gains.
