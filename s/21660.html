Session overview

Resource constraints are becoming the operating reality for many entrepreneurs, intrapreneurs, teams, and organizations facing funding gaps, institutional voids, volatility, crises, and intensifying societal demands. Yet much of our theory and many of our research practices still presume slack, stability, and well-resourced actors. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) advances theory building and research design for studying entrepreneurial action and innovative organizational behavior under constraint. Integrating perspectives from entrepreneurship, organizational behavior, strategy, social issues, and innovation studies, we focus on how actors mobilize, recombine, and legitimate resources (material, social, cognitive, and digital) and why some constrained innovations generate resilience and renewal, while others produce fragility, burnout, mission drift, or inequity. The PDW is intentionally developmental and interactive where participants will engage in facilitated roundtables anchored by a small set of “anchor” abstracts (attached as appendices), cross-table synthesis, and a methods/theory clinic that emphasizes boundary conditions, multi-level mechanisms, and credible designs for researching scarcity settings. Participants will leave with (1) sharper conceptual mechanisms and scope conditions for “doing more with less,” (2) design templates for multi-level and process research in constrained environments, and (3) a community of potential collaborators for future AOM submissions and journal-oriented projects. Attendees are welcome to bring copies of their works in progress for discussion; however, this is not obligatory.
