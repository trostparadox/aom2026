Situating Organizational Justice: Administrative Staff Experiences in Chilean Public Education

CMS
Francisca Alvarez Figueroa (Pontificia Universidad Católica de Valparaíso); Jenny K. Rodriguez (Alliance Manchester Business School, University of Manchester)
The study examines context based understandings of organizational justice to generate new theoretical insights and inform future research directions. Drawing on 48 semi structured interviews with managers, professionals, and administrative support staff across four public education providers in Chile, the study adopts a qualitative design and applies thematic analysis. The findings identify three forms of context based organizational justice. First, deferential organizational justice, characterized by uneven opportunities for engagement and participation shaped by perceived status hierarchies. Second, differential organizational justice, marked by informality and cronyism in the enactment of procedures and protocols. Third, dividendal organizational justice, defined by a favor exchange system governing the allocation of opportunities and resources. The study engages with organizational justice as a contextually constructed phenomenon, an approach largely neglected in research grounded in traditional organizational justice frameworks. By adopting a contextual analytical lens, the study interrogates how experiences and interpretations of justice are produced within specific socio-historical, economic, and political contexts. The study makes three theoretical contributions. First, it conceptualizes organizational justice as a contextual construct, highlighting the analytical necessity of embedding contextual conditions to develop more nuanced understandings of justice. Second, it demonstrates how context-specific cultural values and perceptions of fairness interact to shape interpretations of organizational justice. Third, it advances a typology that captures how this interaction structures distinct forms of organizational justice. The study concludes by outlining a research agenda for advancing a context-based approach in organizational justice scholarship.
