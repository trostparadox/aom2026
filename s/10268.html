Session overview

The global energy transition is unfolding within an increasingly fragmented and threat-laden global context. Despite widespread net-zero commitments of governments and Multinational Enterprises (MNEs), recent evidence suggests that many entities in the global arena are retreating from or failing to meet their energy transition goals, as institutional fragmentation and extreme threats intensify. In this context, as central entities in this transition, MNEs confront a strategic challenge embedded in the energy trilemma: the opposing pulls of environmental sustainability, energy security, and energy equity, which are exacerbated by geopolitical conflict, regulatory divergence, climate shocks, and cascading crises. This panel symposium examines how MNEs navigate the energy trilemma under fractured global conditions and what this reveals about the capabilities, governance arrangements, and strategic choices required for effective action. Bringing together leading scholars in international business, strategy, and sustainability, the panel explores the reconfiguration of global value chains, the development of organizational resilience, the role of advanced technologies in decarbonization, and the application of systems-level frameworks as avenues for future research and practice. In doing so, the symposium advances theory and sets a future research agenda for managing the energy transition in turbulent global environments.
