Aligning Human Judgment with Algorithmic Uncertainty in AI-Assisted Recruitment

CTO
Gayathri Raghavapurapu (XLRI Xavier School of Management)
The greater integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in recruitment has amplified debate about how human judgment and algorithmic decision support should be combined in high-stakes hiring decisions. Although AI systems promise efficiency and scalability, organizations continue to observe substantial variation in recruitment outcomes across recruiters and contexts, even when similar tools are used. While prior research on AI in recruitment has focused on the areas of adoption, predictive performance, explainability, and fairness, there is little research explaining how recruiters cognitively interact with AI-generated recommendations while making a recruitment decision. This study addresses this gap by developing a theoretical framework that explains how recruiters allocate cognitive effort while making recruitment decisions supported by AI, under conditions of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. Drawing on classical theories of bounded rationality, Judgment and Decision-Making Under Uncertainty, and human-automation interaction, the study introduces Cognitive Effort regulation as a metacognitive process through which recruiters regulate attention and judgment in response to underlying algorithmic uncertainty. The resulting process model explains why similar AI recruitment systems may produce divergent hiring outcomes across individuals and organizations. The study advances sociotechnical understanding of human-AI collaboration in digital HRM by shifting attention from AI adoption and explainability toward the coordination of human judgment and algorithmic uncertainty and the design of AI-supported recruitment practices.
