Authors: Massimiliano Dell'Orto Politecnico di Milano - School of Management; Antonio Ghezzi Politecnico di Milano; Andrea Rangone

Session overview

Extended Reality (XR) technologies have attracted significant attention as potential drivers of organizational transformation, yet managerial and scholarly discussions remain disproportionately focused on the metaverse and consumer applications. As a result, the role of XR in enterprise contexts, where operational benefits are well documented, but adoption remains limited, has been underexplored. Building on the transformative business models literature, this study investigates how firms successfully integrate XR into their business models beyond isolated pilot projects. Drawing on an in-depth multiple case study of five manufacturing firms operating across different sectors, the analysis is based on 20 semi-structured interviews complemented by secondary data. The findings show that XR-enabled business model transformation unfolds through a structured yet iterative process comprising two interrelated phases: exploration and exploitation. During exploration, firms experiment with XR while developing strategic intent, co-specializing internal and external capabilities, and overcoming cultural, technical, and organizational barriers. In the exploitation phase, XR becomes embedded into operational routines, generating measurable performance improvements and activating reinforced learning processes that support continuous expansion and refinement. The study contributes to the literature on transformative business models by offering a process-oriented, technology-specific account of how XR enables operational transformation over time. For managers, the findings highlight the importance of treating XR as a strategic tool for addressing concrete operational challenges and sustaining commitment beyond experimentation to realize long-term value.
