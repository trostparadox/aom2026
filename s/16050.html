The Rise of Indie Brands in K-Beauty: Exploring Key Topics through Text Analysis

Session overview

This study focuses on analyzing the K-beauty industry as it has transformed around indie brands and interpreting the topics that have driven these changes. Despite the increasing importance of indie brands in the K-beauty industry, existing studies have primarily focused on large corporations, export performance, or changes in single firms’ business models, and have relied on survey or interview-based methodologies. To address this gap, this study applies Latent Dirichlet Allocation topic modeling to K-beauty news articles collected from the BIG KINDS database. As a result, six major topics in the K-beauty industry were identified. In addition, interviews with K-beauty industry experts were conducted to validate the findings. Furthermore, the six topics were classified and interpreted through the lens of the Dynamic Capabilities Framework. This study offers both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, this study expands K-beauty research by moving beyond a large-corporation-centered perspective to capture the industry’s recent shift toward indie brands. In addition, by interpreting six topics through the dynamic capabilities framework, this study advances the use of dynamic capabilities research from the firm level to industry-level transformation processes. Methodologically, this study differs from prior survey and interview-based research by employing article-based topic analysis to capture industry change. Practically, the findings suggest implications for the sustainable development of the K-beauty industry.
