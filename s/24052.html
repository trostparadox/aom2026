Gender Balance and Value Creation: Re-Examining the Norwegian Board Quota

SIM
Vartika Chandra Saman (The City College of New York of the City University of New York)
This paper re-examines Norway’s corporate board gender quota as a long-term governance reform at the intersection of business and society. Introduced as a mandatory requirement for public limited companies to appoint at least 40 percent women to their boards, the quota was initially framed as a controversial intervention into corporate autonomy. While early research focused on compliance and firm performance, this study shifts attention to how such regulation reshapes governance practices, legitimacy, and value creation over time. Drawing on thirty-two semi-structured interviews conducted in 2017 with board members, policymakers, senior executives, and academic experts, the paper offers a retrospective analysis nearly a decade after full implementation. Using an interpretive qualitative approach, the study shows that gender balance altered governance practices and expectations without fully dismantling informal power relations. The value associated with gender balance was articulated primarily in institutional and relational terms, including legitimacy, accountability, and trust, rather than short-term financial outcomes. By integrating critical mass, social identity, and legitimacy theories, the paper highlights the importance of evaluating governance reforms over time.
