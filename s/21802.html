Exploring Growth Variances in Youth Entrepreneurship Education: A Bayesian Multilevel Approach

Session overview

Entrepreneurship education (EE) equips individuals with essential skills, entrepreneurial mindsets, and innovative capacities that contribute to economic growth and social progress. Building on its long-standing presence in higher education, EE has recently been introduced at the high school level, where it is gaining recognition for addressing adolescents’ developmental needs and fostering entrepreneurial intentions during this formative stage. However, youth EE’s reliance on experiential learning paradigms often lacks rigorous evaluation frameworks that capture exposure to entrepreneurship through social learning, making it difficult to comprehensively assess learning outcomes. This pilot study addresses this limitation by implementing a Bayesian multilevel, longitudinal design across four measurement points to explore the effects of an EE program on entrepreneurial cognitions and error management strategies. It further examines how individual psychological characteristics (conscientiousness and imaginative capability) shape different developmental trajectories. The results provide preliminary evidence supporting the feasibility and value of Bayesian multilevel modeling for capturing personal and contextual variation in learning growth. In line with the heterogeneity revolution in behavioral science, the study underscores the importance of tailoring learning pathways to accommodate diverse learner profiles. Together, the findings provide an evidence-based foundation for future large-scale studies aimed at enhancing inclusivity, adaptability, and impact in youth EE design and participation recruitment.
