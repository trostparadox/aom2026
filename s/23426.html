The Impact of HR Inducements on Employee Attitudes and Retention in the Public and Nonprofit Sector

PNP
Jaehee Jong (Northern Illinois University); Jungtaek Lee (Dongguk University)
This study investigates whether standard human resource (HR) inducements—specifically fringe benefit quality and remote work arrangements—yield universal outcomes across organizational sectors. While conventional HR theory suggests these inducements foster commitment and loyalty, this research examines how the benefits-outcome relationship is unexpected within the unique institutional contexts of the public and nonprofit sectors. The study applies signaling theory and social exchange theory to explain how the psychological contract is reconfigured by sectoral boundaries. Drawing on pooled data from the General Social Survey (GSS) waves spanning 2002–2024, the study employs an empirical strategy combining regression adjustment with propensity score matching. The analysis distinguishes between materially induced rewards (fringe benefits quality) and autonomy-induced arrangements (remote work status) to evaluate their impact on job satisfaction and turnover intention across for-profit, public, and nonprofit contexts. Our results reveal significant sectoral variations in how employees interpret and respond to HR inducements. In the public sector, the relationship between fringe benefits quality and job satisfaction is non-significant, while higher benefit quality correlates with increased turnover intention. This supports the employability paradox, where benefits may function as an exit facilitator rather than a retention tool. Conversely, remote work acts as a high-value compensatory reward in the nonprofit sector, while its effect in the public sector remains insignificant. The findings suggest that public agencies should prioritize human capital investments that are uniquely valuable to the public service context to mitigate the unintended exporting of talent. Meanwhile, nonprofit leaders should leverage remote work as a primary, cost-effective lever for retention and satisfaction. .

Rethinking Traditional Project Management for Grand Challenges: A Conceptualization-Oriented Review

PNP
Ivan Baptista (University of the West of Scotland); Hazhar Faris (University of the West of Scotland); Anupam Dey (University of the West of Scotland)
Global development projects delivered through public sector institutions and partnerships and not for profit projectized organisation face persistent performance challenges despite the alignment they demonstrate with the sustainable development and the SDGs agenda. They face a paradox also; despite this alignment they demonstrate limited integration of project sustainability management principles. A particularly troubling gap given their mandate to deliver economic, social and environmental benefits in developing and emerging contexts. This conceptualization-oriented review examines project management methods in the international development sector an bridges this with the project sustainable management literature to examine how project sustainability school can contribute to address systematic challenges in global development project delivery. The research contributes to the evolving discourse on project sustainability management by addressing a significant gap in both theory and practice: the integration of sustainable project practices in public and not-for-profit (NFP) projects in global development at the lifecycle level and as key enablers of sustainable development. Drawing on thematic synthesis of international development literature, a systematic literature review on development project management (67 papers, 1990-2022) and project sustainability management (62 papers, 2015-2025) we identify critical disconnects. The global development literature on public and NFP project management extensively documents management failures, how traditional project management practices fail to support sustainability objectives and synthesised themes or drivers on critical areas that could enhance their success rates. Amongst preliminary findings there is support for a management approaches that move beyond “time”, “cost” and “scope” and which consider the integration of project sustainability considering social, economic and environmental dimensions within complexity of these projects. Thematic analysis also reveals that while other sectors are actively operationalizing sustainability through frameworks, project processes, lifecycles and governance mechanisms, in project studies on global development projects, public and NFP projectized organizations, the sustainability integration in these contexts, largely lags, with most literature being developed in other sector. While still in progress, the study reports on a focused review that will require further validation prior to the development of conceptual and theoretical models. The review argues that the sustainability school can serve as a critical bridge, injecting methodological agility and a genuine sustainable development orientation to these “gran challenge” sustainability projects. By reconceptualizing global development project management through a sustainability lens, this study proposes a research agenda and a conceptual pathway for achieving more sustainable interventions.The review lays the foundational groundwork for empirical work validation and the eventual development of conceptual and theoretical models for sustainability-oriented project practice integration in public and not for profit projects within the sector.Furthermore, it proposes a research agenda that addresses: 1) the geographic knowledge gap resulting from global north dominance in the sustainable project management (SPM) literature, 2) the under-conceptualization of project sustainability management within global development frameworks, across both public and not-for-profit projects, 3) the under-theorisation of project level-mechanism sustainability mechanisms – where theory remains fragmented, limiting operationalisation across project lifecycles.

How and When Public Engagement Shapes Organizational Identification Among Top Managers

PNP
Tipeng Chen (Arizona State University)
Understanding how public organizations can foster organizational identification among agency top managers is critical, as such identification underpins proactive managerial behavior and leadership that ultimately shape organizational performance. This study examines when and how public organizations’ engagement with the community influences organizational identification of agency top managers. Drawing on a four-wave, nationally representative survey of U.S. local government department heads conducted between 2012 and 2018, the study finds that the frequency of public organizations engaging the community has a positive effect on top managers’ organizational identification after addressing endogeneity using instrumental variable estimation. The analysis further identifies key input-, organizational-, and individual-level boundary conditions. The positive effect strengthens when top managers have broader spans of control and longer tenure, but weakens when input from public engagement is more strongly attributed to the agency. This study advances understanding of the antecedents of organizational identification among top managers by linking it to their organizational experience related to public value– oriented. Findings also suggest that fostering citizen–state interactions can be an effective strategy for public organizations to strengthen organizational identification among the public sector workforce.
