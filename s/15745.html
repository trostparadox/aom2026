Session overview

Scholars’ careers involve many opportunities to have an impact. We can have an impact by writing academic papers, teaching courses, and developing new cases and other teaching materials. One possibility pursued by a growing number of scholars is to write books for general readership, often referred to as trade books. These books are grounded in science but written with a broad audience in mind. There are many benefits to writing a trade book. It is an opportunity to reach a large audience, to bridge research and practice, and to develop a new skillset. Yet it is also laborious to write a trade book and requires an interested scholar to wade into unfamiliar waters. Interested authors must navigate a new world of agents, editors, marketing departments, and publicists with their own opaque rules and norms. The purpose of this PDW is to help audiences who might be interested in writing trade books by demystifying the process. The PDW will have two phases. First is a moderated discussion between the PDW organizers and five esteemed panelists, all of whom have written one or more successful trade books. Then, we’ll break into small groups so that audience members have an opportunity to ask questions at small tables. We’ll ask each table to list its top three tips for aspiring trade authors and end our discussion with a review of these top tips.
