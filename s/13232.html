Integrated Model for Optimal Order Quantity in Manufacturing with Final and Semi-Finished Products

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
OSCM

People

Author: Iman Ghalehkhondabi Howard University

Session overview

This paper investigates the problem of meeting customer demand in a price-sensitive market where purchasing behavior is influenced by price, customization availability, and delivery speed. We study a supply chain that offers two product options: (i) ready-to-purchase standardized items that require no waiting time and (ii) customized products assembled from semi-finished items after an order is placed. Because the two offerings provide different value propositions, they are sold at different prices, and their demand rates depend on price, promised lead time, and the degree of pre-completion embedded in semi-finished products. We develop an optimization model that maximizes supply chain profit by jointly determining retail prices, the retailer’s order quantity from the supplier, and the completion level of semi-finished inventory. To address demand uncertainty and ensure a desired service level, the model incorporates chance-constrained programming to satisfy a specified portion of market demand with high probability. The proposed framework yields an optimal mix of completed and semi-finished products that balances responsiveness and customization while managing market sensitivity and uncertainty.
