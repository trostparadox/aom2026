From Tool to Teammate: A Grounded Theory Study of AI-Mediated Organizing in Creative Production

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5

Author: Zhao Zheng INSEAD

Session overview

The human-AI relationship is shifting from operator-tool to manager-team. As multi-agent AI systems execute creative tasks with unprecedented precision, organizations face a fundamental reconstitution of structure, coordination, and human roles. Traditional creative work contains substantial execution content —data synthesis, layout iteration, code debugging—that AI can now automate, concentrating human value in strategic judgment, aesthetic selection, and ethical deliberation. This transformation challenges classical organizational theory: when AI instantly decomposes objectives, executes tasks in parallel, and synthesizes outputs, the rationale for conventional hierarchies becomes uncertain. This grounded theory study investigates how AI-native creative production organizations reconstitute their structures and coordination mechanisms. Through interviews, ethnographic observation, and documentary analysis of emerging AI-driven content studios, we examine three questions: How do formal structures change when AI assumes creative execution? What coordination mechanisms manage human-AI workflows at scale? How do workers construct new professional identities? We propose that humans are evolving toward three roles: task designers (defining problems), process supervisors (ensuring quality), and value arbiters (making ethical and aesthetic decisions). This study contributes empirically grounded theory on organizing in the AI era.
