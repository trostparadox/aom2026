Challenges with Curvilinearity: When Curves are on Both Sides of the Equation

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
RM

People

Authors: Dane Patrick Blevins University of Central Florida; MAHSA MOHSENIBEIGZADEH University of Central Florida

Session overview

Curvilinear relationships are common in organizational research, yet empirical models increasingly combine curvilinear predictors with intrinsically nonlinear dependent variables, such as bounded, censored, or proportion-based outcomes. We argue that this practice creates serious inferential problems by confounding substantive curvature with nonlinearity induced by outcome scaling or link functions. As a result, estimated turning points become unstable, coefficient interpretations misleading, and evidence for non-monotonic effects overstated. We develop a framework for identifying where curvature should reside in a model, emphasize post-estimation inference using predicted values and marginal effects, and propose alternative specifications that improve interpretability and theoretical alignment.
