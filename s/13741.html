Session overview

This workshop provides an interactive forum for management educators, administrators, and scholars to examine how artificial intelligence (AI) is fundamentally challenging the assumptions and necessary conditions that underpin experiential learning (EL) in higher education. Through structured dialogue centered on critical questions rather than predetermined answers, participants will explore the paradox emerging in management education: as AI makes knowledge work seemingly effortless, are students reluctant to invest the emotional effort required for deep experiential learning? During this Professional Development Workshop, participants will examine how AI's rapid adoption intersects with rising student anxiety, varying levels of engagement in emotionally challenging activities, and shifting faculty responses—from enthusiasm to opposition. Utilizing a framework focused on three key community stakeholders (e.g., students, faculty, and administrators), the workshop will: (1) surface diverse perspectives on how AI is reshaping the emotional landscape of experiential learning; (2) distinguish between productive discomfort and barriers to learning in an AI-augmented environment; (3) explore whether the solution lies in changing assessments, pedagogy, and/or the EL environment (4) co-create guiding principles for navigating experiential learning in an era where AI appears to offer an alternative to the proximity, struggle, and emotional investment that EL traditionally demands. This inquiry-based session aims to build community and catalyze future collaboration among educators grappling with these urgent questions. Participant evaluations are invaluable.
