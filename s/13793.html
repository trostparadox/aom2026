Exploring a Chinese Approach to Sustainability: An Anthropological Insight into Hong Kong's SMEs

Author: Kong Man Joey Ng Hong Kong Metropolitan University

Session overview

Sustainable development is a central global concern in business and management research (Allen et al., 2018; Pizzi et al., 2020; García-Sánchez & Aibar-Guzmán, 2021; Lim & Tseng, 2023). The United Nations (UN) advances a comprehensive socio-environmental agenda through the 2030 Agenda and its 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which promote integrated economic, social and environmental progress worldwide (United Nations, 2020). Sustainability has been emphasized on the Hong Kong government agenda since 1999, when long-term environmental quality and resource management were framed as core elements of a “world-class” city strategy (HKSAR, 2022). SDSN Hong Kong has established under the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network, in which it mobilizes cross-sectoral expertise to localize and support SDG implementation (SDSN Hong Kong, 2025). Despite rising public awareness, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Hong Kong have been slow to embed sustainability in their practice. Resource constraints, short-term survival pressures, the post pandemic’s economic disruptions and perceptions of sustainability as elite or rhetorical discourse continue to limit SME engagement (Tso et al., 2011; Banerjee, 2003; Seefried, 2015; Chowdhury et al., 2023). This research examines the applicability of sustainability to Hong Kong SMEs by asking: How do SME members sustain and develop their businesses after COVID-19? Using ethnography, it draws on data from four SME field sites collected since 2023. The study interrogates the relevance of a mainstream Western sustainability framework and develops an alternative Chinese theoretical framework grounded in Confucian and Daoist thought. By comparing these frameworks, the research contributes a culturally informed understanding of sustainability and offers evidence-based insights for policymakers and SME stakeholders seeking to promote sustainability in Hong Kong.
