Why Students Fail to Integrate What They Learn: Absorptive Capacity and Reflective Writing

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: Tian-Jyun Gong National Chung Cheng University; Hsu-Chu Wu National Sun Yat-Sen University

Session overview

International Business (IB) courses are inherently integrative, requiring students to synthesize functional business knowledge with IB theories and complex global contexts. However, students frequently struggle to bridge prior knowledge and new concepts, a difficulty that manifests as a deficiency in individual absorptive capacity (AC). This study addresses this pedagogical gap by integrating a multi-pedagogical approach—comprising games, case discussions, and video analysis—with a structured reflective writing intervention. Drawing on the 5R framework (Reporting, Responding, Relating, Reasoning, and Reconstructing), the study examines whether structured reflection enhances students’ ability to acquire, assimilate, transform, and apply knowledge. Using a quasi-experimental design in an undergraduate IB course, 33 of 36 students completed matched pre- and post-semester surveys. Data sources included self-reported measures of absorptive capacity and learning outcomes, longitudinal assessments of reflective journals, and objective classroom performance indicators. The results show a statistically significant increase of more than one point in overall absorptive capacity on a seven-point Likert scale, with the strongest gains in knowledge transformation and exploitation. Longitudinal analysis further indicates that peer-learning interventions, such as the World Café, significantly reduced the learning curve associated with reflective writing. These findings suggest that while diverse teaching methods generate rich experiential inputs, it is the deliberate practice of reflective writing that functions as the cognitive mechanism converting experience into learning capability. This study contributes to management education by providing an empirically grounded framework for cultivating higher-order thinking and integrative competencies in future business leaders.
