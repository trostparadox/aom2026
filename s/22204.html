Session overview

This panel symposium critically examines the most effective online resources for teaching global management in contemporary business education. As management educators increasingly rely on digital content to address rapidly evolving geopolitical, economic, technological, and cultural dynamics, the quality, credibility, and pedagogical integration of online resources have become central to effective global management instruction. Bringing together five experienced faculty members from Hult International Business School—Robert Neer, Rajendra Shirole, Katarzyna Bachnik, Pamela Campagna, and Niland Mortimer—the symposium showcases a curated set of high-impact online resources, including websites, newsletters, data platforms, multimedia case repositories, interactive tools, and open-access research. Drawing on their diverse disciplinary perspectives, panelists demonstrate how these resources can complement traditional cases and textbooks, enhance experiential and comparative learning, and support the development of global awareness, analytical reasoning, and cross-cultural competence. The symposium goes beyond listing tools to address critical pedagogical questions: How can instructors assess the reliability, bias, and limitations of online materials? How can digital resources be integrated without overwhelming students or diluting conceptual rigor? How can resources be adapted for different course levels, delivery formats, and institutional contexts? Through concise presentations, comparative discussion, and active audience engagement, the panel provides evaluative frameworks and practical examples of classroom application. Participants will leave with concrete recommendations and strategies for selecting and deploying online resources to strengthen global management education in an increasingly complex and information-rich environment.
