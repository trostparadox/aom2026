AI-Driven Entrepreneurship and Poverty Reduction in LMICs: A Review and Framework

ENT
Xiao Chen (Jiangsu Normal University)
Poverty remains a pressing global challenge, with over 700 million people living below the $2.15/day international poverty line. Entrepreneurship is widely recognized as a sustainable, empowering pathway to poverty alleviation, enabling marginalized groups to secure self-employment, build stable incomes, and address the structural roots of poverty. Yet, marginalized entrepreneurs in low- and middle-income countries confront systemic barriers, including information asymmetry, resource scarcity, and institutional gaps. Advancements in artificial intelligence, including machine learning, the Internet of Things, and big data, have emerged as a transformative solution to these barriers, equipping disadvantaged entrepreneurs with capabilities once limited to high-income contexts. However, the literature on AI-enabled entrepreneurial poverty alleviation is fragmented, as studies tend to focus on narrow sectors or regions, lack cross-domain theoretical integration, and rarely address the heterogeneous impacts on subgroups. To fill the gaps, this review integrates 91 studies published between 2017 and 2025, to map three key dimensions: theoretical mechanisms linking AI to poverty reduction, contextual enablers and constraints, heterogeneous impacts. This review contributes to management literature by developing a unified theoretical framework, synthesizing cross-sector evidence, and proposing a targeted research agenda. For practitioners, it offers actionable strategies to design inclusive AI initiatives, advancing scholarly understanding and practical action for AI-driven entrepreneurial poverty alleviation in low- and middle-income countries.

Decentralized Entrepreneurial Financing: An Entrepreneurial Ecosystems Perspective

ENT
Jacquoia Hunter (Syracuse University)
Decentralized finance (DeFi) has emerged as an innovative approach to funding new ventures, promising to democratize access to capital by removing traditional intermediaries through blockchain technology. This systematic literature review synthesizes 94 empirical studies on DeFi for entrepreneurial financing through an entrepreneurial ecosystems framework. The review reveals that while DeFi has not delivered on its democratizing promises, specific ecosystem conditions enable productive outcomes. The findings identify critical success factors across ecosystem elements: regulatory clarity in formal institutions, cryptocurrency market conditions driving demand, and social media driven networks and knowledge sharing. Importantly, the literature shows that productive DeFi ventures often reintroduce forms of centralization, such as venture capital backing, suggesting complementarity rather than substitution with traditional financing mechanisms. The review also highlights significant gaps in understanding physical infrastructure, cultural factors, talent availability, and long-term economic impacts. This review proposes a research agenda emphasizing total ecosystem analysis and measurement of aggregate economic outcomes to better understand when, how, and for whom DeFi enables productive entrepreneurship.

Entrepreneurs’ Linguistic Cues and Financing Outcomes: Evidence from Crowdfunding

ENT
Dalee Yoon (University of Hawaii at Mänoa); Joonhyung Bae (Sungkyunkwan University); Youngsang Kim (Sungkyunkwan University)
This study extends the literature on entrepreneurial financing by examining the role of linguistic cues as costless signals to external investors. Drawing on signaling theory and entrepreneurial financing research, we investigate how entrepreneurs’ use of a collectivism-oriented language relates to entrepreneurial financing performance. The findings indicate that entrepreneurs’ tendency toward collectivism, as reflected in their narratives, can influence external funders’ investment decisions because such tendencies can reduce information asymmetry by signaling the entrepreneurs’ adaptability, responsiveness, and trustworthiness. This further suggests that this positive effect is amplified (mitigated) when a venture is in its early (late) development stage. We test our hypotheses using a dataset of game developers’ funding performance on the Kickstarter platform.

New Venture Growth After Crowdfunding: An Integrative Literature Review

ENT
Jackson Ojodomo Akor (University of Louisville)
Crowdfunding is a multidisciplinary phenomenon and has become a mainstream financing pathway for new ventures. A campaign's end is a venture's true beginning, highlighting the importance of understanding post-crowdfunding growth for entrepreneurship scholars. While prior studies have examined antecedents of crowdfunding success, the current fragmentation of the literature limits our understanding of how different types of crowdfunding may lead to distinct post-campaign growth trajectories. This review examines the current state of research on the post-crowdfunding life of new ventures. An integrative review of 91 peer-reviewed empirical articles on post-crowdfunding across different crowdfunding typologies and 12 business-related fields was conducted. This review delivered two key contributions: it maps how the literature on the growth trajectory of new ventures after crowdfunding has developed theoretically and methodologically. Secondly, the study combines empirical articles published between 2014 and 2025 across diverse business domains to present a theoretical process model of the growth trajectory of new ventures post-crowdfunding, anchored in the theory of firm growth (Penrose, 1959). Based on a structured synthesis of a fragmented field, this paper presents future research directions for scholars interested in crowdfunding's evolving role in entrepreneurial financing.
