Threat or Catalyst: The Effect of U.S.–China Tension on Chinese Firms’ Digital Innovation

OSCM
Li Yin (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Jing Dai; Jinan Shao (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Zelong Yi (Shenzhen University)
The escalating U.S.-China tension has strained the nerves of global trade and significantly shaped firms’ strategic decisions. Drawing on strategic growth option theory, this study investigates how Chinese manufacturing firms leverage digital innovation as a growth option in response to bilateral political tension. Using a panel dataset of 14,140 firm-year observations from 2,096 Chinese listed manufacturing firms between 2008 and 2023, we employ fixed-effects regression models to test our hypotheses. The results show that U.S.–China tension is positively associated with firms’ digital innovation. Moreover, digital leadership and operational capability strengthen this positive relationship, whereas TMT heterogeneity weakens such association. By conceptualizing digital innovation as a strategic growth option amid U.S.-China tension, this study contributes to the political tension and operations management literature and extends strategic growth option theory to a bilateral, macro–micro context. The findings suggest that managers should view U.S.- China tension not merely as an obstacle but as a catalyst for digital innovation and strategic renewal.

Supply Chain and Propagation of International Trade Shocks

OSCM
Ruoming Lyu (Tsinghua University); Cong Peng (Peking University); Jiexin Wang (University of Oxford)
External shocks can rapidly alter firms’ costs and demand conditions, prompting operational adjustments. Prior research shows that trade policy shocks influence sourcing, production, and market strategies, propagate through supply chains, and manifest in logistics flows, but real-time and system-wide response dynamics remain underexplored. In April 2025, U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods spiked to historically high levels, affecting both trade and domestic logistics. Using a comprehensive, high-frequency truck GPS and freight dataset (covering 97% heavy-duty trucks’ commercial trajectories in China), we examine the immediate impact of the tariff, with an event-study framework identifying causal effects and indicators of supply chain linkages capturing heterogeneous responses. We find that the tariff caused a sharp but short-lived contraction in domestic freight activity, followed by a rapid rebound, with variations across supply chains reflecting both direct and indirect operational adjustments by firms. Our study bridges theory and empirical evidence, providing novel empirical evidence of immediate operational responses, documenting heterogeneous propagation along supply chains, and linking trade shocks to real-time adjustments.

The Impact of Knowledge Network on Firm Sustainability: The Context of Third-party

OSCM
Cheng Qian (Central University of Finance and Economics); Qiuhuan Lin (Renmin University of China); Kangkang Yu (Renmin University of China); Kangni Zhao (Central University of Finance and Economics); Xinqi Guo (Renmin University of China)
This study adopts a knowledge network perspective to investigate the impact of third-party certifiers’ knowledge quantity (KQ) and knowledge heterogeneity (KH) on sustainable performance of the certified firms. This study constructs an inter-organizational certification network based on based on 2920 valid observations from 359 listed agribusinesses in China from 2010 to 2023 to measure the KQ and KH of the third-party certifiers. The analysis used fixed-effects regression models to evaluate the impacts of these knowledge attributes on the sustainable performance of the certified firms, as well as the moderating role of operational resilience (OR). Results reveal inverted U-shaped relationships between third-party certifier’s KQ or KH and sustainable performance of the certified firms. Furthermore, high OR flattens the curve for KH and shifts its inflection point to the right. From a knowledge network perspective, this study identifies the inverted U-shaped impact of knowledge attributes, highlighting the importance of third-party certifiers as knowledge intermediaries in enhancing sustainable performance. Furthermore, it establishes operational resilience as a critical capability for certified firms to buffer knowledge shocks, thereby clarifying the boundary conditions of knowledge transfer.

A Sensemaking Framework for Supply Chain Reconfiguration Amid Geopolitical Tension

OSCM
Yan Chen (The University of Manchester); Fahian Huq (Manchester University); Grigory Pishchulov
Global supply chains are increasingly weaponized in great-power rivalries, transforming commercial decisions into matters of national security. While existing research typically frames geopolitical phenomena through risk or disruption lenses, it tends to treat them as episodic deviations, failing to explain why firms facing identical geopolitical pressures adopt divergent reconfiguration strategies for their supply chains. To address this gap, we propose a ‘tension’ lens that conceptualizes these phenomena as enduring structural conditions. We reconceptualize geopolitical tension as a field-level conflict between a market logic prioritizing economic efficiency, and a state logic prioritizing alignment with national security and sovereignty interests. Integrating the institutional logics perspective with sensemaking theory, we develop a conceptual framework showing how organizations interpret this conflict through an identity-shaped enactment-selection-retention (ESR) process. We identify three distinct constructed realities — efficiency-oriented, alignment-oriented, and flexibility-oriented — that render specific supply chain reconfiguration strategies sensible. Our framework contributes to management scholarship by shifting analytical attention from mitigating risks to navigating structural geopolitical tension, revealing that strategic heterogeneity in supply chain reconfiguration stems not merely from calculative risk assessment but from differential construction of meaning around conflicting institutional demands.
