Suicide Bereavement at Work: A Multilevel Model of Survivor Experiences and Recommendations

DEI
Rita Rueff-Lopes (ESADE Business School - Ramon Llull University); Ferran Velasco (EADA Business School); Josep M. Sayeras; Ana Junça Silva (ISCTE - University Institute of Lisbon)
Returning to work after losing a loved one to suicide is a profoundly complex and underexamined process in organizational life. Despite growing awareness of mental health at work, suicide bereavement remains marginal in research and practice. Drawing on in-depth interviews with survivors and an interdisciplinary expert panel, this study develops a multi-level model of grief disruption during work reintegration and identifies survivor- and expert-informed organizational best practices. Findings reveal that survivors navigate intersecting organizational, interpersonal, and intrapersonal challenges, often in the absence of structured postvention support. These dynamics produce interrelated emotional, physical, social, and occupational repercussions that extend well beyond the immediate period of bereavement. The model underscores the need for multi-level organizational interventions, co-developed by survivors and experts, including compassionate and informed leadership, tailored bereavement policies, suicide-specific training for managers and peers, and the integration of postvention into broader wellbeing strategies. Survivors also underscored the value of peer-led initiatives and collective rituals of care to counter isolation and silence. By situating suicide bereavement as both a personal and organizational event, the study advances grief and organizational scholarship and calls for empathetic, systemic infrastructures that affirm the humanity of those grieving within workplaces.
