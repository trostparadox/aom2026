When Work Follows Home: The Differential Effects of AI and Human Self-Disclosure on Rumination

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

People

Authors: Da Gyeong Lee Seoul National University; Ejun Jang Seoul National University; Jin Nam Choi Seoul National University

Session overview

As employees increasingly turn to generative AI for emotional expression after work, understanding how AI versus human self-disclosure shapes post-work rumination and its associated stress has become critical. Drawing on the Transactional Model of Stress and the Challenge–Hindrance Stressor Framework, this study distinguishes affective rumination (AR) and problem-solving pondering (PSP) as cognitive pathways linking stressors to burnout. Two-wave survey data from 194 employees reveal that AI self-disclosure amplifies AR’s link to hindrance stress, whereas human self-disclosure weakens PSP’s link to challenge stress, thereby diminishing its protective effect against burnout. These findings underscore AI’s threat-focused cognition versus humans’ emotional buffering in shaping employee well-being.
