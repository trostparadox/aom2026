Unpacking Ethical Leadership Abroad: Dual-Level Mechanisms Influencing Expatriate Performance

IM
Yuntao Bai (Xiamen University); Yu Chen (Xiamen University); Li Lin (Nanjing Agricultural University)
Expatriate employees often work in ethically and culturally unfamiliar settings, where clear moral guidance is essential for their success. While a substantial amount of research has examined the advantages of ethical leadership on employee outcomes, these studies have predominantly concentrated on teams in domestic context. Surprisingly little research has investigated the role of ethical leadership in global situations, especially in facilitating the adjustment of expatriate employees. Drawing on social learning theory, this study posits that ethical leadership plays a central role in expatriates’ socialization process through both individual- and team-level mechanisms, specifically, we propose a multilevel conceptual model in which, at the individual level, perceived ethical leadership influences expatriates’ adjustment and subsequent performance, moderated by individual altruism. At the team level, we investigate the impact of team ethical leadership on collective-oriented teamwork process, with collective altruism serving as a moderating factor in this path. Analyses with three-wave paired data of 92 team managers and 283 expatriate employees from three multinational pharmaceutical companies’ subsidiaries across Europe and Asia support the dual-level roles of ethical leadership in facilitating expatriates’ successful adjustment.. This research enhances ethical leadership theory in international contexts and provides practical guidance for leading and developing ethical expatriate teams.
