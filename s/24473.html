Job Satisfaction in Healthcare: The Role of Violence, Presenteeism, and Demanding Workplaces

HR
Ylenia Curzi; Francesca Nepoti (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia); barbara pistoresi (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia); Erica Poma (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia)
Purpose Grounded in the Job Demands-Resources theory, this paper investigates how organizational characteristics affect job satisfaction (JS) among healthcare professionals working in Emergency Departments (EDs), with a particular focus on two mediators: future violence concern (FVC) and presenteeism. Design/methodology/approach A mixed-method approach is employed. First, partial least squares structural equation modelling is conducted on quantitative data from a questionnaire administered to a sample of 131 healthcare professionals from two Italian EDs. Second, 26 interviews were carried out with EDs staff and management to gain deeper insights on unexpected results. Findings Results show that most job demands are linked to increased future violence concern and presenteeism. Job resources, conversely, are not significantly related to these mediators, except for relational skills that reduce FVC. Both FVC and presenteeism negatively impact JS and mediate the effects of demands and interpersonal skills. Practical implications The study suggests that tackling extreme work in EDs requires systemic interventions beyond organizational boundaries. Structural overcrowding must be addressed through stronger coordination with territorial emergency actors; otherwise, the sustainability of autonomy and supervisor support, key for attracting and retaining EDs professionals, remains at serious risk. Originality/value This study contributes to JD-R literature by integrating two underexplored mediators, FVC and presenteeism, into the analysis of JS in EDs. It offers new theoretical and practical insights into how emotionally intense and structurally constrained environments impact workers’ wellbeing.
