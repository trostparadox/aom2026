Strategizing Under Trade Disruptions: Firms’ Adaptive Responses to Sanctions and Tariffs

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3

Authors: Laurenz Tinhof Vienna University of Economics and Business; Anna Andronovich Vienna University of Economics and Business; Constantin Lackinger WU Vienna

Session overview

Recent geopolitical tensions have turned restrictive trade policies to central instruments of foreign policy, exposing firms to recurrent and often unpredictable trade policy shocks. While extnt research documents negative firm-level effects, our understanding of individual firms adaptive responses and their performance implications remains vague. This study addresses this gap by examining how firms’ adaptive strategies mediate the effects of restrictive trade policies on firm outcomes. Using a transaction-level dataset with near-census coverage of Austrian firms’ imports, exports, and production from 2013 to 2023, we analyze firm responses to exogenous trade policy shocks stemming from U.S. protectionist measures and EU sanctions on Russia. We classify firm adaptations into trade diversion, domestication, circumvention, and production reduction by comparing firm-specific trade networks before and after policy implementation. Employing a structural equation modeling approach with an instrumental variable to address endogeneity, we assess the effectiveness of managerial responses in mitigating policy impacts.
