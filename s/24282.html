Near and Dear? CSR Geographic Expansion and Breakthrough Innovation

OMT
Chenhao Hu (Shandong University); Jie Lyu (Shandong University); Wenhe Cheng (Shandong University); Zeyu Chen (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Traditional legitimacy perspective suggests that firms should strengthen the local embeddedness of their CSR activities to gain entrenched recognition. However, the impact of CSR geographic expansion—extending CSR efforts beyond the home region—and its ability to secure heterogeneous legitimacy support has been largely overlooked. This study addresses this gap by examining how CSR geographic expansion influences a firm’s capacity for breakthrough innovation. Drawing on legitimacy theory, we argue that CSR geographic expansion activates two competing legitimacy mechanisms: the legitimacy-breakthrough mechanism, which facilitates innovation by broadening access to diverse legitimacy endorsements, and the legitimacy-barrier mechanism, which introduces escalating legitimacy frictions in distant institutional environments, ultimately constraining breakthrough innovation. Using panel data from Chinese listed firms, we find an inverted U-shaped relationship between CSR geographic expansion and breakthrough innovation Additionally, we show that AI adoption, CEO geographic diversity, and local CSR endorsement moderate this relationship. This study provides fresh insights into how firms can strategically manage CSR expansion geographically to balance legitimacy acquisition and innovation, contributing to the literature on CSR-innovation linkage and legitimacy theory.
