A Curvilinear Relationship Between Family-to-Work Conflict and Participative Leadership via Guilt

OB
HAO WANG (State Grid Shandong Electric Power Company Training Center); Mian Zhang (Tsinghua University); Xiyue Ma (Tsinghua University); Leijie Liu (Tsinghua University)
This study explores how leaders cope with family-to-work conflict by altering participative leadership behavior. Based on the resource perspective and guilt’s reparative mechanism, we propose a curvilinear mediation model between a leader’s family-to-work conflict and participative leadership behavior via family-to-work guilt. Using time-lagged survey data collected from 99 team leaders and their 451 team members, we found that a leader’s family-to-work conflict has a U-shape relationship with participative leadership behavior and family-to-work guilt mediates the relationship. We also found that such indirect, curvilinear relationship is stronger under high levels of family sacrifice team climate. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.
