The Effect of Idiosyncratic Deals on Career Outcomes

CAR
Xinyu Liu (The University of Hong Kong); Chun Hui (The University of Hong Kong); Cynthia Lee (Northeastern University); Denise M. Rousseau (Carnegie Mellon University)
Although studies have linked idiosyncratic deals (i-deals) to favorable attitudinal, motivational, and behavioral outcomes, little research exists regarding how i-deals influence employee careers. We hypothesize that i-deals promote boundaryless career opportunities, which in turn facilitate career success. We also propose that i-deal recipients in organizations with strong career management practices are more likely to accumulate boundaryless career opportunities and achieve career success. We first develop and validate a scale, Boundaryless Career Opportunities, across multiple samples: one for item generation, another for content validation, and two for exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses. We then conducted a study of full-time employees each using three-wave, multisource data to test the full model. Our findings show that i-deals enhance career success through boundaryless career opportunities. The effects of i-deals are stronger when employees’ organization supports career management.

Requesting I-deals After I-deals: An Action Regulation Perspective

CAR
Raymond Loi (University of Macau); Stephanie W.X Wang (University of Macau); Gabby K.P. CHOI (University of Macau); Fangjian Wu (University of Macau)
Existing research on i-deals has either primarily focused on one form of i-deals or simultaneously examined the dual effects of different i-deals on career outcomes, often overlooking employees’ evolving needs to request multiple types of i-deals. Building on action regulation theory, this study investigates how employees manage their serial requests of flexibility i-deals and development i-deals. We propose that receiving flexibility i-deals enhances employees’ psychological job control, which in turn promotes their seeking developmental i-deals. Furthermore, we examine the moderating roles of leader-member exchange (LMX) and future focus, suggesting that both contextual and individual factors strengthen the indirect relationship between flexibility i-deals and seeking developmental i-deals via psychological job control. Using three-wave survey data collected from 423 full-time employees in China, our findings support the mediating role of psychological job control. The indirect effect of flexibility i-deals on developmental i-deals is strengthened under high LMX and strong future focus. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.

A Silver Tongue Isn’t Always Enough: The Conditional Effect of Upward Ingratiation on Promotability

CAR
Ni Lu (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics); Riguang Gao (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics)
Upward ingratiation—characterized by flattery, conformity, and other-enhancement behaviors aimed at gaining favor from superiors—is often perceived as weak or opportunistic. Counterintuitively, it frequently predicts accelerated promotion. Grounded in socioanalytic theory, this study examines how and when upward ingratiation influences promotability. We propose that such ingratiation signals loyalty and role-congruent deference, thereby fostering higher-quality leader–member exchange (LMX). Enhanced LMX, in turn, increases promotability. Crucially, the signaling efficacy of ingratiation depends on employees’ political skill (Sibunruang & Kawai, 2023); only politically adroit individuals can craft ingratiating messages perceived as sincere rather than manipulative, maximizing LMX gains. Using a three-wave, time-lagged design with data from 199 supervisor–subordinate dyads, we found support for our hypotheses. Results revealed that upward ingratiation positively influenced promotability through elevated LMX quality, and this indirect effect was stronger for employees with higher political skill. These findings contribute to organizational behavior literature by elucidating the mechanism linking impression management tactics to career outcomes and identifying political skill as a key boundary condition determining their effectiveness.

The Impact of Job Rotation on Employee Objective Career Advancement in Dual Ladder System

CAR
Ning Huang (Nanfang College of Sun Yat-Sen University); Yan Qiao (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University)
Despite a broad scholarly and practical consensus that job rotation serves as a catalyst for employee career advancement, empirical evidence remains limited and marked by puzzling inconsistencies. Based on a 16-year organizational archival data, we distinguish between within-functional and cross-functional rotation, analyzing their effects on professional and managerial promotion. Findings reveal that both within-functional rotation and cross-functional rotation faciliates employee career advancement in dual ladder systems. Furthermore, we found that the career benefits of job rotation are contingent upon the hierarchical level of the rotated employees. Specifically, entry-level employees gain greater opportunities for professional promotion through within-functional rotation, whereas middle-level employees achieve higher chances of managerial promotion through cross-functional rotation. This study contributes to the literature on job rotation, employee mobility and career development by demonstrating the nuanced effects of job rotation types across different career paths and therefore reconciling existing conflicting empirical evidence.

Board Chairman's Actual Tenure, Expected Tenure and Firm Digital-Intelligent Transformation

CAR
Yangfan Han; Yifei Huang (Central South University); Liu Liu
This study examines how chairman tenure influences firms’ digital-intelligent transformation, highlighting leadership horizons beyond static demographics. It further explores how internal governance and external market pressures shape these effects and under what ownership contexts they hold. Using panel data of Chinese A-share non-financial listed firms from 2010 to 2019, we measure digital-intelligent transformation through text mining of annual reports. Fixed-effects regressions and robustness checks validate the results. Longer chairman tenure is positively associated with higher levels of digital-intelligent transformation. Market competition amplifies these effects, while shareholding balance enhances the role of actual tenure but weakens expected tenure. Importantly, the effects are significant only in non-state-owned firms. This study advances understanding of how executive time horizons shape digital strategies, extending tenure research into the digital-intelligent transformation domain. It also reveals how internal governance and external market pressures condition these relationships, offering theoretical and practical insights for governance and digital strategy.
