The Legitimacy Paradox: Decoupling Between Professionalization and Informality in Family Business

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Authors: Salma FATTOUM University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Western Switzerland (HES-SO); Juergen Fritz University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Western Switzerland (HES-SO); Caroline Nicolas Université de Sherbrooke

Session overview

Why do most family successions remain informal despite the hyper-professionalization of successors? This study investigates the construction of legitimacy during successions. Drawing on a sample of 499 Swiss family firms, our findings reveal a "legitimacy decoupling": while 90% of successors possess external professional experience and 85% hold higher education degrees (individual legitimacy), 70% of transition processes remain non-formalized (under-institutionalization).We demonstrate that this persistent informality is not a management deficit but rather a deliberate strategy of "non-decision making." This strategy aims to preserve Socioemotional Wealth (SEW) and defer the identity-related tensions of the incumbent leader. By moving beyond mechanistic views of succession planning, this research contributes to literature by framing succession as a management of tensions between acquired meritocratic authority and tacit relational structures.
