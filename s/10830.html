Session overview

Leadership and followership are increasingly understood as dynamic and socially embedded phenomena that emerge through ongoing interactions among group members. Building on leadership identity construction theory, which conceptualizes leader and follower identities as co-constructed through processes of claiming and granting, this presenter symposium offers new empirical insights into how leadership and followership emerge within groups and teams. Across presentations, contributors investigate how individual characteristics (e.g., risk aversion, overconfidence), social identities (e.g., age, gender), and behavioral cues (e.g., informal leadership behaviors) shape individuals’ likelihood of claiming leadership or followership roles, as well as how others interpret these claims and grants. Collectively, the papers advance theory and research on leadership emergence by integrating perspectives on cognition, identity, and social interaction.
