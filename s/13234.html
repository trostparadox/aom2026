Concealing or Confessing? Gender Differences in Impression Management During Family-to-Work Conflict

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

Authors: Viva Nsair Western Michigan University; Zahra Heydarifard Bryant University

Session overview

Family-to-work interruptions are common, yet little is known about how working parents communicate these situations in real time at work. Drawing on impression management and attribution theories, we investigate parents’ responses to situation that when family demands disrupt work by distinguishing between behavioral choices and communication strategies. Using a policy capturing design, we varied work urgency, negative feedback history, and organizational climate. Findings from 106 full-time working parents assessing 32 scenarios reveal that situational urgency strongly influences behavioral choices, while organizational climate and negative feedback shape communication strategies. Specifically, urgency triggers heightened impression management through increased ingratiation, suppression, and apologies. Unsupportive climates increase suppression of family reasons. Gender differences emerge in communication: women and men make similar behavioral choices when facing identical scenarios, but women conceal family reasons more often while also expressing supplication more frequently. These findings challenge deficit models that assume women prioritize family over work and instead point to gendered expectations about how work-family conflict should be communicated. We discuss implications for theory on role salience, attribution, and workplace norms, as well as for designing parent-inclusive organizational cultures.
