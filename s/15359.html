Session overview

Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems are redefining the role of managers in organizations. Managerial functions long regarded as exclusive to human managers —such as performance monitoring, corrective oversight, task allocation, and decision-making—are increasingly replaced by AI, a phenomenon known as algorithmic management. Yet research has focused primarily on workers' experiences, leaving unexplored how managers themselves navigate this redistribution of responsibilities. This knowledge gap has practical consequences: managers serve as critical brokers in algorithmic management implementation, and without their support, these systems fail to take hold. This panel symposium examines how managers interpret, adapt to, and actively shape their reconfigured roles. Leading scholars will discuss recurring patterns of managerial adaptation, the organizational and technological conditions that generate them, and empirical approaches for studying these dynamics. Topics include: managers as algorithmic brokers balancing worker wellbeing with system implementation; the emergence of hybrid management structures; visibility loss in GenAI adoption; managers' legitimacy judgments of algorithmic control; and the reshaping of managerial discretion and responsibility. The session will clarify key mechanisms shaping managerial authority and accountability under AI-mediated supervision.
