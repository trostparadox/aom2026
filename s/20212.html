Session overview

Homophily—the tendency to prefer and connect with similar others—has long been described as the “iron cage” shaping our interpersonal relationships across social contexts. Scholars from sociology, psychology, and management have extensively documented its pervasiveness and consequences. However, only recently research has begun to explore the exceptions to this pattern, highlighting the possibility and benefits of forming diverse personal ties. This paper symposium advances our understanding of who connects across differences and why, as well as how individuals experience and sustain diverse informal relationships, such as friendship and advice ties, particularly within organizational settings.
