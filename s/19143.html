Matrescence at Work: Understanding Self-Concept Shifts Among Women Gig Workers

Session overview

Matrescence refers to the significant physical, psychological, and social transition into motherhood, involving hormonal changes, identity shifts, and emotional transformation. Motherhood is a life-changing experience for working women, affecting their physical, emotional, personal, and professional lives. Mothers are still seen as less capable, devoted, worthy of job development, and paid less. This tension forces many women to step off traditional career paths by taking breaks or exiting the workforce entirely, thereby limiting their leadership prospects. The concept of matrescence captures this transformative period. Defined as “a developmental stage during which a woman moves from pre-conception to pregnancy and birth, surrogacy or adoption, to the postnatal period and beyond”. While considerable scholarship has examined women in the workforce, limited attention has been given to how self-concept evolves during matrescence among women in the gig economy. This paper aims to address this gap by exploring the identity transformation of women gig workers navigating motherhood. A qualitative inquiry will be undertaken with women across multiple digital labour platforms. Semi-structured interviews will capture their lived experiences, focusing on challenges, identity negotiations, and the intersections of caregiving with professional aspirations. Implications for organizations and policymakers, the findings can inform initiatives that promote positive self-concept development among women gig workers undergoing matrescence. Identity change can be facilitated when women are supported in setting and pursuing personal goals. By doing so, the gig economy can move beyond replicating traditional gendered inequalities and instead foster inclusive pathways for women’s leadership and professional growth. Keywords: Gig workers, Self Concept, Matrescence, Motherhood penalty, Self Concept
