Beyond the Institutional Shield: The Universal Advantage of Open-Source AI Strategy in Global Market

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5
TIM

People

Author: WENXIN ZHENG Fudan University

Session overview

As Generative AI reshapes the global digital economy, multinational enterprises face a critical strategic dilemma: maintain proprietary control (closed-source) or release model weights to the public (open-source)? Conventional international business theory views open-source strategies primarily as an “institutional shield”—a defensive mechanism to navigate strict local data regulations (e.g., GDPR) by enabling local deployment. We challenge this contingent view through a two-stage game-theoretic model and a global empirical analysis. By modeling the trade-off between deployment costs and technological sovereignty, we theoretically predict that technical efficiency, rather than regulatory arbitrage, drives open-source adoption. We test this using a novel dataset of 708 AI foundation models tracked across 46 countries. Our high-dimensional fixed effects (HDFE) analysis reveals a substantial “Open Source Premium,” where open-weights models enjoy approximately 180% greater global adoption than their closed counterparts, ceteris paribus. Crucially, we find a null interaction effect between openness and regulatory stringency, rejecting the “institutional shield” hypothesis. These findings suggest that the advantage of open-source AI is universal, driven by a global demand for technological sovereignty and deployment efficiency. Open source is not merely a shield for compliance; it is a universal sword for global market dominance.
