Configuration, Scale, and Survival in Capital-Intensive Sustainability Ventures: Evidence from CEA

STR
Se Ho Cho (North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University)
Capital-intensive sustainability ventures often attract significant investment, yet they frequently experience very different commercial outcomes. This study examines why commercially similar controlled-environment agriculture (CEA) ventures follow divergent trajectories, even though they operate with comparable production technologies. Rather than relying on technology-centered explanations, we adopt a configurational perspective. This perspective allows us to examine how business model design and capital intensity interact with scaling strategies, and how these interactions shape patterns of organizational fragility, resilience, and, in some cases, adaptation. We conduct a comparative case analysis of six commercial CEA ventures in North America. Three of these firms entered bankruptcy or court-supervised restructuring, while three continue to operate and expand. Cases were selected using theoretical replication logic in order to maximize outcome contrast, while keeping the technological domain broadly constant. Drawing on hierarchical documentary evidence, we reconstruct firm-level business models and identify recurring configurations associated with distinct outcomes. The findings suggest that rapid, venture-style scaling combined with high leverage tends to increase organizational fragility. By contrast, market-anchored and adaptive configurations appear better able to support resilience under conditions of volatile energy prices and rising operating costs.
