Session overview

Spatial analogies and the “landscape” metaphor are pervasive in strategic management, organization theory, and innovation studies, linking micro-level explanations of search to macro patterns of change. Yet, empirical work has struggled to directly capture the spatial logic these models imply. Studies that rely on categorical proxies (patent classes, citations, keywords) obscure the geometry of opportunity spaces and make distance and structure hard to measure consistently. Recent data and computational advances make addressing this gap feasible. This symposium brings together four studies that bridge the theory and empirics of search landscapes. The papers vary in settings and approaches, but together demonstrate how landscapes can be estimated and analyzed. In doing so, the symposium advances theoretical, methodological, and empirical aspects of studying search landscapes.
