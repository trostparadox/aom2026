Learning-by-Exporting, Upgrading-by-AI: A Reciprocal Model of AI Capability and Export Intensity

IM
Ruiyi (Vivian) Luo; Yun Zhou (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Tianle Yang (Zhejiang University of Technology)
Extant international business (IB) literature incorporates digital technologies as enablers of firm international expansion but overlooks how the latter may affect digital capability development. In this study, we theoretically develop and empirically test a mutual reinforcement model between artificial intelligence (AI) capability and export intensity in the context of the Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) firms which are characterized by complex formulations, high regulatory scrutiny, and deep cultural embeddedness. By investigating a sample of 67 publicly listed TCM firms between 2010 and 2024, we substantiate the mutual influences between AI capability and export performance. This study enriches the perspective on dynamic capabilities in a unique context at the intersection of AI, emerging markets, and cultural-heritage industries. It also offers practical insights for executives and policymakers, in particular in indigenous industries as to how AI-enhanced exporting performance in turn facilitates AI technology upgrading.

AI Competence as an Organizational Capability: Examining Workforce Adjustment and Labor Productivity

IM
Jong Min Lee (Yonsei University); Baiyang Li (Yonsei University)
Organizations are increasingly investing in artificial intelligence (AI), yet little is known about how AI shapes employment through firm-level organizational decisions in an international context. Drawing on compensation theory, we conceptualize AI competence as an organizational capability and examine its influence on employment growth and labor productivity, as well as the moderating role of industry competition and firms’ degree of internationalization. Using panel data on 1,839 Chinese international firms, yielding 5,689 firm-year observations between 2018 and 2024, we find that higher firm-level AI competence is associated with lower employment growth. This negative effect is stronger in less competitive industries, where short-term pressures to maintain headcount are weaker. In contrast, the relationship is attenuated among more internationalized firms, where coordination demands and institutional heterogeneity limit their ability to reduce workforce size. Moreover, the results suggest that the productivity consequences of AI-induced employment adjustment are dynamic and short-lived, rather than persistent over time. Taken together, these findings advance compensation theory in an international business context by demonstrating that the employment effects of firm-level AI competence are contingent on industry structure and firms’ degree of internationalization.

How Intelligent Manufacturing Drives Internationalization Breadth? A Dual Parallel Mediation Model

IM
Peien Chen (Tongji University); Weicheng Chiu (Tongji University); Mengmeng Shan (Fudan University); Luu Thi Nguyen (Tongji University)
This study investigates the impact of digital transformation on emerging market multinationals enterprises' (EMNEs) ability to overcome coordination complexity and legitimacy deficits during international expansion, with a specific focus on the strategic role of intelligent manufacturing. We synthesize dynamic capabilities theory and institutional theory to propose a dual-path mechanism framework, wherein intelligent manufacturing acts as the core strategic infrastructure for cross-border governance. To establish causal inference, we leverage China’s Intelligent Manufacturing Demonstration Program as a quasi-natural experiment and employ a staggered difference-in-differences methodology. The findings confirm that intelligent manufacturing significantly expands EMNEs’ internationalization breadth, operating through two distinct channels: an internal competence channel, where digital integration enhances innovation-oriented dynamic capabilities, and an external legitimacy channel, where improved Environmental, Social, and Governance performance provides credible accountability signals. By revealing the linkage between technological upgrading, governance capabilities, and institutional acceptance, this study contributes novel empirical evidence to the international business literature on EMNEs’ global expansion strategies.
