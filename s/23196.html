Emotional Storytelling as Entrepreneurial Agency:Evidence from MigrantEntrepreneurs’ Use of LinkedIn

ENT
Deepa Subhadrammal (University of Technology, Sydney); Martin Bliemel (University of Technology, Sydney); Jochen Schweitzer (University of Technology, Sydney)
Digital platforms have become central arenas where entrepreneurs communicate their ventures, engage stakeholders, and make sense of entrepreneurial life. For migrant entrepreneurs, whose ventures are shaped by migration-related constraints, fragile legitimacy, and emotionally demanding conditions, professional social media platforms such as LinkedIn offer both opportunities and risks. While prior research recognises the importance of emotions and narratives in entrepreneurship, less is known about how migrant entrepreneurs work with emotions over time, and how emotional storytelling constitute entrepreneurial agency in constrained contexts. Drawing on a qualitative, multi-case study of migrant entrepreneurs operating in the Australian entrepreneurial ecosystem, this paper examines emotional storytelling on LinkedIn as a processual enactment of entrepreneurial agency. The study combines backstage interviews capturing lived emotional experiences with frontstage interviews and LinkedIn posts reflecting public storytelling practices. Using reflexive thematic analysis informed by a dramaturgical lens, the analysis traces how emotional experiences are first interpreted in backstage contexts, then selectively articulated in frontstage digital spaces, and subsequently give rise to interactional and relational outcomes. The findings reveal a recurring process in which migrant entrepreneurs engage in emotional sensemaking, calibrate emotional expression in response to audience and stakeholder evaluation, and enact recognisable entrepreneurial roles that enable interaction. Through this process, emotional storytelling generates action possibilities for future, including visibility without legitimacy loss, opportunity emergence through relational engagement, and the formation of belonging and community. The study shows how emotional storytelling operates as an ongoing practice that sustains entrepreneurial engagement under uncertainty. By foregrounding the process through which emotional experiences become agentic storytelling, this study advances research on entrepreneurial agency and extends research on emotions and migrant entrepreneurship.

Emotional Triggers of Entrepreneurial Learning Cycles: What Can We Learn from Migrant Entrepreneurs

ENT
Deepa Subhadrammal (University of Technology, Sydney); Martin Bliemel (University of Technology, Sydney); Jochen Schweitzer (University of Technology, Sydney)
Entrepreneurial learning is widely conceptualised as experiential, cognitive, and identity-based, yet the emotional mechanisms that shape how entrepreneurs learn over time remain under-theorised. This gap is particularly salient for migrant entrepreneurs, who often encounter challenges such as uncertainty, cultural dislocation, and systemic exclusion. Drawing on 37 in-depth interviews with first-generation migrant entrepreneurs in Australia and using constructivist grounded theory, this study develops a process model that explains how emotional experiences both trigger and structure entrepreneurial learning. The analysis reveals a six-phase, emotionally sequenced process comprising emotional disruption, emotional regulation, emotion-centred reflection, affective reframing, experimentation, and integration. Emotional disruption, stemming from experiences (such as rejection, discrimination, visa precarity, or workplace displacement), functions as the catalyst for learning, while regulation enables entrepreneurs to regain sufficient emotional clarity for sensemaking. Reflection and reframing represent the core learning mechanisms through which entrepreneurs reinterpret the emotional meaning of adverse experiences, shifting from fear and self-doubt toward agency and purpose. Experimentation and integration show how these reframed emotions translate into behavioural testing, identity reconstruction, and emotional competencies such as resilience, emotional self-efficacy, and boundary setting. The study advances entrepreneurial learning theory by demonstrating that emotion is not a peripheral influence but the architecture through which learning unfolds, offering a processual account of how migrants transform emotional adversity into adaptive entrepreneurial action.

Entrepreneurial Pathways and Later Midlife Well-Being (WITHDRAWN) (WITHDRAWN)

ENT
Bach Nguyen (University of Exeter)
This paper investigates how different patterns of entrepreneurial engagement across the life course shape well-being in later midlife. Drawing on the Conservation of Resources theory and utilising 50-year longitudinal data, we identify three entrepreneurial pathways— lifelong, early, and periodic—and assess their impact on mental, physical, and emotional well-being. Our instrumental variable analysis reveals that periodic entrepreneurship yields the most consistently positive well-being outcomes, whereas early engagement is associated with long-term emotional strain. Lifelong entrepreneurs report higher subjective mental well-being but worse physical and emotional functioning. This paper advances a life-course perspective, underscoring the trade-offs inherent in entrepreneurial careers.

When AI Feels: How Emotionally Aware AI Reshapes Opportunity Belief Formation

ENT
Yu Pei (Jilin University); Siwen Wu; DALEI YANG (State University of New York at Binghamton); Yi Deng (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Opportunity beliefs refer to entrepreneurs’ judgments about whether a nascent idea merits pursuit and play a central role in entrepreneurial action under uncertainty. While prior research shows that emotions shape how uncertainty is interpreted during opportunity evaluation, this understanding remains incomplete as entrepreneurs increasingly engage with emotion aware artificial intelligence (AI) systems that participate in sensemaking. Building on opportunity belief theory and the Affect as Social Information perspective, this conceptual paper develops a process framework that explains how emotional awareness influences opportunity belief dynamics through opportunity objectification. By translating diffuse emotional experiences into interpretable cues, emotion aware AI conditions sensemaking pathways that shape opportunity objectification along technical and market dimensions. Confusion and surprise tend to activate exploratory learning and belief revision, whereas desire and positive affect are more likely to foster causal reasoning and belief stabilization. When emotional cues are prematurely reinforced or insufficiently examined, opportunity objectification may result in belief distortion. Overall, the framework positions opportunity objectification as a mediating process linking emotional awareness to dynamic belief outcomes and clarifies the catalytic rather than deterministic role of AI in entrepreneurial sensemaking. Keywords: Opportunity beliefs; emotion-aware AI; emotional externalization; entrepreneurial sensemaking; opportunity objectification

Role of Connectors, Diaspora Networks, and Co-Evolution in Building Resilient Innovation Ecosystems:

ENT
Norris F. Krueger (Kyushu University); Suresh Kumar (New Jersey Institute of Technology)
Economic, community, and workforce development models have evolved significantly over decades from nearly nonexistent to focuses on large firms, then to business attraction, then retention and expansion and now some that include business incubation. Given the disproportionate impact of new and small business, it is surprising that while support for them is becoming more commonplace, the dominant logics and allocated resources remain largely fixated on business attraction, retention and expansion and industrial era education and career planning, led from the top down by governments, higher educational institutions, large firms and other entrenched players. We provide here a conceptual overview of the emerging model of the ecosystem builder model that is driven by the increasingly critical role played by connectors, diaspora networks, and bottoms-up information flows. We then do a deep dive into what we know to be true about vibrant entrepreneurial ecosystems with roots in India, such as The Indus Entrepreneur (tie.org), the Karnataka Digital Economy Mission (KDEM.org), and Global Alliance for Mass Entrepreneurship (https://massentrepreneurship.org/). We conclude with expert commentaries from a diverse set of knowledgeable and productive professional ecosystem builders from India. Building innovation ecosystems with intentionality is emerging as the future of how to most effectively grow entrepreneurial economies and entrepreneurial communities.
