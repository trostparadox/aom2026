Artificial Intelligence for Climate Policy Adaptation: Evidence from Firms

TIM
Junjun Zhou (Central South University); Yangjie Wang (Central South University); Tao Zhang; Kuo-Jui Wu (Hainan University)
Although artificial intelligence (AI) has been widely studied for improving firms’ operational efficiency, direct evidence on its role in adapting to climate policy remains limited. Our work fills this gap by examining the contribution of AI to firms’ green product innovation under climate policy shocks, with a particular focus on automobile manufacturing firms that are highly related to AI technologies. We measure firms’ AI levels using AI patent data extracted from the China National Intellectual Property Administration, and assess their responses to climate policies by examining the impact of the Dual- Credit Policy (DCP) shock on green product innovation. Using an interaction regression model based on a difference-in-differences approach, we find that AI enhances firms’ innovation-oriented adaptation to climate policies by promoting green product innovation. After the implementation of the DCP, firms with AI technologies, on average, introduced 0.093 more green vehicle models per month than other firms, reflecting a faster pace of green innovation. The mechanism analysis reveals that this effect can be attributed to AI fostering innovation in key technologies for green vehicles, such as batteries, electronic control systems, and intelligent configurations. Further analysis reveals a noteworthy result: firms with AI technologies not only introduce green vehicle models more actively, but these new products also achieve better sales performance in the market. Our findings provide new managerial implications for the coordinated design of climate policies and AI technology strategies.
