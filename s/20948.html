Session overview

Affecting millions of individuals worldwide, violence generates both immediate threats and enduring socioeconomic consequences. Developing a richer understanding of its forms, mechanisms, and implications can enable management scholarship to produce insights that inform not only theory but also policy, organizational practice, and the needs of affected communities. Building on this context, this symposium aims to advance a research agenda that treats violence as a complex, historically embedded, and institutionally mediated phenomenon. It focuses on how violence interacts with institutions, organizations, and individuals to produce variation in organizational outcomes across markets and societies. Rather than viewing violence solely as an episodic shock, this symposium emphasizes its longer-term legacies, emotional residues, and structural consequences. The four papers in this symposium approach this agenda from complementary theoretical perspectives and empirical settings, spanning contexts from post–civil war Colombia and Palestinian entrepreneurship in Jordan to global markets and contemporary U.S. organizations. Together, they underscore the breadth and urgency of studying violence as a phenomenon-driven research domain. By bringing these perspectives into dialogue, this symposium seeks to stimulate a new scholarly conversation that problematizes violence as a key feature of organizational environments and enriches management theory through insights on coercion, emotion, inequality, and adaptation under heightened disruption.
