Session overview

This symposium examines how marginalized identities shape organizational experiences across evaluation, creative production, and career trajectories, highlighting when and how inequality is reinforced versus mitigated. The papers address these questions across a diverse set of organizational contexts using experimental, qualitative, and archival methods. Together, they examine how work arrangements can reduce access barriers and stigma for people with disabilities, how organizational characteristics shape sanctioning decisions in public schools, when neurodivergence can be reframed as a source of distinctiveness in evaluation, how marginalized producers contribute to creative output, and how repeated exposure to tokenism amplifies career penalties over time. Collectively, these papers demonstrate that inequality is neither inevitable nor uniform; understanding when and how it is reinforced versus reduced requires attention to the interplay among identity, organizational context, and stage of interaction.
