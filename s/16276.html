Session overview

This symposium explores how entrepreneurial finance shapes the emergence and evolution of new industries. While prior research has emphasized technological innovation and institutional support, it has largely treated financing as exogenous to the emergence process. This panel challenges that view by positioning investor organizations - VCs, CVCs, angel investors, and others - as generative actors who actively curate experimentation and influence which technologies, markets, and organizational forms survive. The symposium also considers how markets for entrepreneurial finance themselves emerge, particularly in contexts with weak institutions or limited technological complexity. Drawing on global perspectives, panelists will examine how investor heterogeneity, local institutional conditions, and cross-country variation shape both financing markets and the industries they support. In doing so, the session advances new conceptual and empirical agendas at the intersection of entrepreneurial finance, industry emergence, and institutional theory - offering fresh insights for the Organization and Management Theory, Strategic Management, and Technology & Innovation Management communities.
