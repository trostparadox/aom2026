Gendered Appointments of Board Secretaries During Crises: Evidence from China

DEI
Xinqi Wang (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Nana Liu (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Ruchunyi Fu (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics)
The glass cliff literature suggests that women are disproportionately appointed to precarious leadership positions during organizational crises, yet it often treats leadership as a homogeneous construct. We examine whether crisis-related gendered appointments extend to a distinct functional executive role in China: the Board Secretary, who oversees disclosure and serves as the key interface with investors and regulators. Leveraging China’s Special Treatment (ST) designation as an institutionalized indicator of organizational crisis, we develop a legitimacy-based argument that firms under heightened scrutiny prefer male Board Secretaries as a symbolic signal of stability and control, consistent with prevailing gender-role ideology. We further posit that this tendency is weaker when governance is stronger, such as under concentrated ownership and greater institutional investor presence. Using Chinese public firms from 2008–2023, we find strong support for these predictions, highlighting that gendered crisis appointments are role-contingent and institutionally bounded.
