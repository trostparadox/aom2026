Session overview

This symposium advances a solution-focused agenda that highlights how organizations can reduce workplace mistreatment by acting on multiple, interconnected leverage points. Rather than focusing exclusively on narrowly defined or one-size-fits-all interventions, the symposium integrates evidence on how early-formed beliefs, supervisor behaviors, and organizational contexts shape whether mistreatment is confronted, prevented, or transmitted across levels. Across four complementary papers, the symposium examines how leaders’ equality-valuing behavior activates employees’ willingness to confront sexism, how leaders’ motivation to regulate hostile impulses can be strengthened, how compassion-based micro-interventions can buffer the trickle-down effects of mistreatment, and how emerging technologies can recalibrate attributional processes surrounding ambiguous mistreatment. Together, these papers demonstrate that meaningful reductions in workplace mistreatment require coordinated solutions that span leadership behavior, employee responses, and scalable delivery mechanisms.
