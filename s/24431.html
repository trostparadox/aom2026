Homeomorphic Trickle-Down of Entrepreneurial Passion: The Moderating Role of Organizational Memory

ENT
Qinqin Cao (University of Science and Technology Beijing); Jingjing Li (Beijing University of Technology); Shuai Peng
The effective transmission of entrepreneurial passion is critical for sustaining organizational vitality and strategic alignment. Yet many entrepreneurial firms display passion faultlines—enthusiasm at the top, fatigue in the middle, and apathy at the frontline—undermining development capacity. Prior work has largely emphasized heteromorphic outcomes of passion (e.g., effects on behavior or performance), leaving vertical, homeomorphic transmission—downward cascades that preserve form—underexamined. Drawing on social learning theory and the trickle-down framework, we develop a homeomorphic trickle-down model in which top leaders’ entrepreneurial passion shapes employee passion via middle managers, with organizational memory as a contextual moderator. Survey data from 387 respondents in 58 entrepreneurial firms form matched top leader–middle manager–employee triads. Multilevel analyses show that (1) leaders’ Passion for Inventing and Passion for Developing diffuse downward through social learning; (2) middle managers serve as a key bridge; and (3) organizational memory strengthens these cross-level effects by facilitating recognition and internalization of passion signals. This study advances emotional trickle-down typologies, enriches entrepreneurial passion research, and clarifies the logic of sustainable emotional inheritance.
