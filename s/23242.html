AI Initiatives and Financial Distress: The Role of Resource Reallocation Capabilities and Industry

STR
Kyungmi Lee (University of Wollongong); Kumar Krishna Biswas (University of Wollongong); Yiyang Bian (University of Wollongong in Dubai); Sudipta Bose (The University of Newcastle)
Artificial intelligence (AI) initiatives are increasingly central to firms’ strategies, yet their dark side and organizational consequences remain underexplored. While extant research has largely emphasized the positive firm outcomes of AI initiatives, it has overlooked the possibility that AI initiatives may also generate financial risks. Drawing on organizational inertia theory, we argue that AI initiatives, driving core technological changes can outpace firms’ ability to realign complementary routines, coordination structures, and resource-allocation processes, thereby increasing vulnerability to financial distress. We further theorize that this effect depends on firms’ internal adjustment capacity and external temporal pressures. Using a panel dataset of 34,616 observations from U.S. publicly listed non-tech firms between 2000 and 2020, we examine how AI initiatives—measured through AI-related disclosures in firms’ 10-K filings—affect firms’ financial distress. We find that AI initiatives are associated with higher financial distress. This relationship is weaker for firms with stronger resource reallocation capabilities but amplified in high-velocity industries. This study contributes to the dark side of AI initiatives by shifting attention to firms’ exposure to financial risk and shows how AI initiatives interact with organizational inertia to produce organizational strain.

Building Strategic Dynamism with AI Capabilities: The Contingent Role of AI Use

STR
Sebastian Bauer (Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz); Jana Oehmichen
In response to efforts to build AI capabilities, there is an ongoing scientific debate about the benefits of this technology. While existing research primarily focuses on potential productivity gains, we adopt a strategic perspective. AI enhances the capacity to analyze a firm's strategic positioning by processing internal and external data more efficiently. Drawing on dynamic capabilities theory, this improves the ability to adapt strategy to suit the firm's environment. Thus, we hypothesize a positive relationship between a firm's internal AI capabilities, reflecting the availability of AI-specific human capital, and its strategic dynamism – firms’ ability to reallocate resources and shift priorities. However, implementing specific AI solutions, which we define as AI use, ties up the capacity of AI specialists and leads to increasing standardization. We therefore propose that AI use weakens the expected positive relationship of internal AI capabilities. Based on a sample of 451 firms from the S&P 500 between 2014 and 2022, our results support the theorized positive relationship of internal AI capabilities on strategic dynamism, as well as the moderating role of AI use. We contribute to strategy theory and provide practical implications to the discussion on the benefits of AI.

Optimal AI Signaling: AI Orientation and Market Value

STR
Fangyan Niu (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Yaqun Yi (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
As firms integrate artificial intelligence (AI) into their strategies, how investors evaluate these commitments remains unclear, with prior research yielding contradictory results regarding firms’ AI initiatives and market value. This inconclusive pattern suggests that investors’ evaluation of firms’ AI engagement may be more complex than what linear models based on discrete adoption indicators can capture. Drawing on signaling theory, we theorize and demonstrate an inverted U-shaped relationship between firms’ AI orientation and market value. Moderate AI emphasis effectively signals organizational capability and enhances firm valuation, whereas excessive AI orientation triggers investor skepticism regarding overcommitment and resource misallocation. Further, we suggest that signaler characteristics moderate this curvilinear relationship. Managerial myopia attenuates the pattern, while insider ownership amplifies both positive and negative market responses. Analysis of Chinese high-tech listed firms supports our predictions. Our results thus reconcile contradictory evidence on the value of AI, reveal an optimal threshold for AI signaling intensity, and show how firm characteristics condition market evaluation of strategic orientation toward emerging technologies.

Competing in Information-Rich Environments: Managerial Experience and Analytics Technology Adoption

STR
Hisan Yang (Louisiana State University); Hangjun Cho (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Jongsoo Jays Kim (Korea University)
When competitors possess comparable analytics, what determines differential organizational performance? We theorize that managerial experience amplifies analytics value. Through repeated exposure to consequential decisions, experienced managers accumulate decision pattern libraries enabling faster interpretation of analytical signals and coordination capabilities enabling swifter organizational execution. We test this theory using the NBA’s 2013 league-wide adoption of player-tracking technology as a quasi-natural experiment. This universal adoption created an exogenous information shock where all teams simultaneously gained identical analytics while coaches varied substantially in accumulated experience. Analyzing game-level data from 2002-2020, we find that post-adoption, experienced coaches achieve higher win probability through superior performance in time-pressured structured decision windows: they more effectively protect halftime leads (but not reverse deficits), produce stronger third-quarter margins, maintain optimal lineups longer in critical periods, and extract greater value from timeouts. These mechanism-consistent patterns reveal how experienced managers convert analytics into performance advantages through rapid interpretation and effective execution. Our findings demonstrate that in analytics-rich competitive environments, accumulated managerial experience becomes increasingly valuable for translating information abundance into performance differentials.

Policy Tailwinds and Digital Divides: How Can AI "Break Barriers" in R&D Internationalization?

STR
Xue Liang (Zhejiang University of Technology); Zaiyang Xie
Abstract: Despite AI embedding into R&D internationalization at an unprecedented speed and acting as the "meta-technology" to trigger the new technological revolution, how it reshapes global R&D footprint amid globalization remains an unsolved "black box". Drawing on data from Chinese listed manufacturing firms spanning 2016-2022, this study employs the dynamic capability theory to empirically unravel the mechanisms through which the application of AI influences firms' R&D internationalization. The findings indicate that AI applications significantly advance enterprises' R&D internationalization primarily through three pathways: enhancing their strategic sensing capabilities, strengthening their timely decision-making capabilities, and empowering their transformation implementation capability. Government digital support positively moderates the relationship between AI application and enterprises' R&D internationalization, while digital distance plays a negative moderating role in this relationship. Further heterogeneity analysis shows that the above effects are significantly present in non-state-owned enterprises and the born global enterprises. This study reveals the internal logic of AI-driven enterprise R&D internationalization, which has important theoretical and practical significance for promoting the application of AI technology and the implementation of internationalization strategy.
