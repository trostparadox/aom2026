Exploring Entrepreneurial Financing Outside of Traditional VC Industries

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Authors: Ojaswi Malik University of Washington; Benjamin L. Hallen University of Washington; Sung Ho Park University of Oregon

Session overview

Entrepreneurial financing is essential for starting and scaling startups, yet most evidence on startup funding patterns is derived from sectors where venture capital is prevalent and necessary, such as software and biotechnology. With lower costs of starting a business and emergence of new technologies and alternative financing options, however, it is unclear whether previously identified financing patterns generalize to many modern forms of entrepreneurship. We examine this question by conducting exploratory analyses to document financing patterns in the modern direct-to-consumer (DTC) e-commerce sector and compare them with patterns in the biotech and software sectors. Our findings show that in contrast to traditional venture sectors, startups in the DTC e-commerce sector make much greater use of debt funding and tend to already be generating revenue when raising external funding. Other patterns of venture finance, however, continue to hold including strong geographic and social embeddedness. Overall, this study contributes to the literatures on resource mobilization and entrepreneurial finance, by documenting changing patterns of funding in the broader universe of startups.
