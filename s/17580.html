Menopause as a State-Dependent Personal Demand: A JD-R Synthesis

Authors: Sarvenaz Taridashti Montclair State University; Manuel F. Gonzalez Montclair State University

Session overview

This systematic review synthesizes 26 empirical studies examining the influences of menopause on work-related well-being, through the lens of the Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) framework. Findings show that psychological and cognitive symptom severity, rather than menopausal stage, predicts stress, burnout, and engagement. Supporting a JD-R perspective, high job demands amplify symptom burdens, whereas job resources such as autonomy, coworker support and flexibility mitigate them. The review identifies critical gaps in theory, measurement, and design, including over-reliance on generic constructs, cross-sectional methods, and Western samples. We call for menopause-specific operationalizations and life-course extensions of the JD-R model.
