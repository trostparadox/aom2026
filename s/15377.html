Peers, Leaders, and AI: How LMX, TMX, and Team Composition Shape AI Adoption in PBOs

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4
OMT

People

Authors: Sara Baskir Onur Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf; Larissa Pomrehn TU Dortmund University; Josephine Mago Moritz

Session overview

Organizations increasingly deploy artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance performance, yet employees struggle to integrate AI tools into everyday work. Drawing on leader–member exchange (LMX) and team–member exchange (TMX) theories, this study examines how relational dynamics shape AI adoption in a global digital consulting firm organized as a project based organization (PBO). Based on 41 semi structured interviews, the findings show that structurally constrained LMX shifts influence from leaders to peers, making TMX quality and team composition key drivers of AI uptake and human–AI collaboration. Strong TMX in cross functional teams enables collective experimentation and deep integration of AI tools, whereas weak TMX in siloed teams, combined with distant leadership and ambiguous governance, leads to fragmented or minimal AI use. The study develops a framework and a typology of four AI adoption profiles—Collective High Adopters, Isolated Adopters, Fragmented Adopters, and Minimal Adopters—explaining divergent AI uptake patterns in PBOs.
