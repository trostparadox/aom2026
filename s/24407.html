The Costs and Benefits of Advice Centrality: A Social Exchange Perspective on Task Performance

OB
Xingxin Fu (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); Wei Zeng (Hunan University)
This study examines when and why advice network centrality enhances or undermines task performance by adopting a social exchange perspective. Although central positions in advice networks provide access to valuable information and influence, social exchange theory (SET) suggests that increasing centrality also entails growing obligations and relational burdens. Integrating these mechanisms, we propose an inverted U-shaped relationship between advice network centrality and task performance. Moreover, we argue that this relationship depends on team-level friendship reciprocity, a context characterized by mutual trust and support. Using two-wave, multilevel data from 376 employees nested within work teams in a large retail organization, we find support for our hypotheses. Results show that advice network centrality exhibits an inverted U-shaped relationship with task performance. Furthermore, higher levels of team friendship reciprocity shift the inflection point of this relationship toward lower centrality and raise the peak performance at moderate centrality. These findings advance social exchange and network research by demonstrating that the performance consequences of network centrality reflect a balance between exchange benefits and burdens, and that relational climates reshape this balance.
