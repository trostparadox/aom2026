Between Commitment and Consequence How DEI Policies in Academic Journals Shape Knowledge Production

DEI
Elisabeth Bethge (Friedrich Schiller University Jena); Laura Wolfinger (Friedrich Schiller University Jena)
Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) guidelines have rapidly diffused across academic journals, yet it remains debated whether these policies primarily signal normative alignment or produce measurable organizational consequences. We develop and test a stepwise model linking the degree of DEI policyf formalization, the extent to which guidelines move from aspirational statements to enforceable and monitorable practices, to journals’ societal impact. Building on legitimacy arguments that distinguish symbolic conformity from substantive implementation, we theorize that more formalized DEI policies foster structural openness in knowledge production, reflected in authorship diversity and international collaboration, which in turn increases sustainable knowledge production and ultimately political visibility (policy citations). We evaluate these relationships using a journal-level dataset of 155 peer-reviewed journals from Arts and Humanities, Social Sciences, and Education. The results indicat that DEI formalization is associated with greater structural openness and that sustainable knowledge production is strongly linked to political visibility. The study contributes to research on DEI governance and legitimacy by (1) connecting journal-level DEI governance to downstream societal impact outcomes, (2) clarifying when DEI commitments might operate as fragile symbolic signals versus when they align with observable shifts in participation and the societal reach of published knowledge, and (3) introducing a replicable measure of DEI policy formalization in academic publishing.
