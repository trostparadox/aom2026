How Mentoring Role Modeling Shapes Innovation: Psychological Safety and Job Crafting as Pathways

Session overview

Mentoring is widely viewed as a positive developmental resource, yet less is known about how mentors translate guidance into employees’ innovative work behavior. Drawing on role modeling and social learning perspectives, this study proposes that mentors’ role modeling function supports innovation through two complementary pathways. First, mentors may foster team psychological safety, creating conditions in which employees feel safe to express and develop new ideas. Second, mentors may encourage employees to proactively increase challenging job demands through job crafting, thereby expanding learning and experimentation opportunities. These ideas were examined using matched employee-supervisor survey data collected in late December 2025 in Montenegro. Employees reported mentoring role modeling, perceived team psychological safety, and challenging job crafting, while supervisors rated employees’ innovative work behavior. Results indicate that mentoring role modeling relates to innovative work behavior indirectly rather than directly. Specifically, mentoring role modeling is associated with higher psychological safety and greater engagement in challenging job demands, both of which are positively related to innovative work behavior. Conceptually, the findings position mentoring role modeling as an upstream developmental resource that promotes innovation by shaping employees’ psychological safety climate perceptions and proactive work redesign, rather than by exerting a direct influence on innovative behavior.
