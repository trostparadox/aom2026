“Proving Before Improving”: A Theoretical Framework of Identity-Contingent Leader Reactions

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Nikhil Awasty University of New Hampshire; Shubha Sharma University of Tulsa

Session overview

Employees routinely surface early-stage ideas requiring leader input, yet leaders vary in whether they respond collaboratively (co-developing) or confrontationally (demanding proof). We develop the Identity-Contingent Development Framework (ICDF) theorizing this variation as interactional developmental inequality. Drawing on status characteristics theory, leadership prototypes, and shifting standards, the ICDF argues that race-gender status shapes implicit thresholds for when an idea is treated as improvable versus not yet credible. The ICDF predicts within-category heterogeneity via identity stances: among White women, race-to-innocence versus intersectional allyship; among Black/Brown men, assimilation versus resistance; among Black/Brown women, tempered identity-shifting versus agentic authenticity. These reactions accumulate across episodes, translating interactions into systematic differences in developmental investment, mentoring intensity, and leader-member relationships. The framework predicts downstream effects on creative performance, well-being, and relational standing. Collaborative reactions foster idea generation and learning; confrontational reactions increase proof burdens, undermine self-esteem, and increase stress. The ICDF contributes to multiple theories and offers practical levers to equalize co-development access in organizations.
