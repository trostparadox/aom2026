A Constructivist Grounded Theory Investigation of Factors That Support Remote Workers’ Performance (WITHDRAWN)

HR
Vineet Arora (Not Associated); Amit Shukla
The rise of remote work, accelerated by technological advancements and the COVID-19 pandemic, has reshaped how work is organized and carried out. In response to this shift, the present study examines the factors that help remote employees manage challenges and maintain effective performance. Using a constructivist grounded theory approach, semi-structured interviews were conducted with 37 full-time remote workers to identify the factors that enable employee performance in remote work settings. The findings point to a range of individual and organizational enablers. At the individual level, readiness for remote work, meaningful work and mindfulness, emerged as key factors. At the organizational level, remote work-specific human resource practices, including growth-oriented, empowerment-oriented, and integration-oriented practices, along with supportive supervision, were found to be key enablers of remote workers’ performance. Grounded in the lived experiences of remote workers, this study offers contextually grounded insights for organizations seeking to strengthen the conditions that help employees navigate remote work effectively.

How I-Deals Shape Network Centrality and Citizenship in Culturally Diverse Workgroups

HR
Farid Jahantab (Sam Houston State University); Maria Del Carmen Triana; M. Fernanda Garcia (The University of Texas at El Paso)
In this study, we integrate idiosyncratic deals (i-deals) research with the social network perspective to suggest that flexibility i-deals have implications for employees’ centrality in workgroup influence network, which in turn affects employee organizational citizenship behavior (OCB). We further theorize that workgroup power distance and collectivism moderate the relationship between flexibility i-deals, influence network centrality, and OCB. Social identity theory integrated with social exchange theory provides the conceptual foundation for our predictions. Multi-level modeling using time-lagged multi-source data from 442 employees and 85 supervisors demonstrated a positive relationship between flexibility i-deals and influence network centrality, with influence network centrality transmitting the indirect effect of flexibility i-deals on OCB. Moderation analyses further revealed that workgroup power distance strengthens the relationship between flexibility i-deals and influence network centrality, whereas workgroup collectivism weakens this relationship. Finally, results of Monte Carlo simulations indicated that the indirect effect of flexibility i-deals on OCB via influence network centrality was stronger under higher workgroup power distance and lower workgroup collectivism. Supported by our study results, we assert that i-deals should be examined within the broader context of workgroup membership and social networks.

Working With GenAI: How Human-AI Relatedness and Enjoyment Boost Employee Performance

HR
Marta Mas-Machuca (International University of Catalonia); Frederic Marimon (Universtitat Internacional de Catalunya); Anna Akhmedova (International University of Catalonia)
The increasing utilization of generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools in the workplace is undeniable. However, differing viewpoints persist regarding the adoption, potential benefits, and pitfalls of GenAI in organizational settings. To shed light on this issue, this article investigates how the integration of GenAI technologies influences employee performance, with particular attention to the roles of hedonics and job satisfaction. The study employs a mixed-methods approach: the quantitative phase, conducted in December 2023, uses structural equation modeling to analyze data from 251 professionals in Spain, followed by a qualitative phase in April 2024 involving 16 Spanish managers and employees through focus groups. The quantitative results indicate a direct and positive impact of GenAI use on employee performance, mediated by hedonics and job satisfaction. Findings from four focus groups reveal that GenAI enhances productivity by fostering factors such as enjoyment, surprise, and satisfaction. Importantly, the integration of GenAI is not solely a technical challenge; it also requires embedding these technologies into employees' daily routines to enrich their experience and satisfaction. Furthermore, a positive perception of GenAI is essential for organizational competitiveness. When employees have positive experiences and higher satisfaction with GenAI adoption and use, their productivity increases. These insights provide valuable theoretical and practical implications for improving employee satisfaction and performance.

When Different Minds Meet: How Social Support is Co-Constructed between Supervisors and Employees across Concealable Differences

HR
Tanya Alaaldin (The London School of Economics & Political Science); Caleb Phillips (The London School of Economics & Political Science); Elizabeth E. Stillwell (The London School of Economics & Political Science); Anders Thomassen (London School of Economics and Political Science)
Supervisors are a crucial and often primary pathway of support their employees, yet even their well-intentioned efforts frequently miss the mark. This can be particularly consequential for employees whose lived experiences differ fundamentally from their supervisors' own, a gap that grows wider when those differences are concealable and mutual understanding cannot be assumed. Drawing on grounded theory analysis of 41 interviews with autistic employees and autistic supervisors in mixed-neurotype workplace relationships, we examine how supervisors come to provide meaningful support. We find that effective support does not emerge from good intentions or formal accommodations alone, but through iterative relational work we call social support co-construction, the ongoing effort through which supervisors and employees build the mutual understanding necessary for support to be aligned with employees' needs. Our model reveals that the relational foundation supervisors build with employees is not a precondition for support but is rather its fundamental core.

Task I-Deals, Burnout, and Performance: A Multilevel JD-R Perspective

HR
Farid Jahantab (Sam Houston State University); M. Fernanda Garcia (The University of Texas at El Paso); Maria Del Carmen Triana
This study extends job demands-resources (JD-R) theory by investigating the effects of task i-deals on employee outcomes within the context of workgroup membership. We examine whether, how, and when task i-deals shape employee burnout and task performance. We propose that task i-deals reduce burnout and, in turn, enhance task performance, while recognizing that the three subdimensions of burnout (i.e., emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and professional efficacy) may operate differently. Multi-level modeling using time-lagged, multi-source data from 551 employees and 113 supervisors nested in 113 workgroups revealed that task i-deals were negatively associated with emotional exhaustion and indirectly improved task performance through reduced emotional exhaustion. Results of a Monte Carlo simulation further indicated that this indirect effect was stronger in contexts of higher supportive climate and lower team orientation. Additionally, multilevel path analysis showed that task i-deals interacted with supportive climate to diminish employee cynicism. Finally, we found that task i-deals enhanced employee professional efficacy. We conclude by discussing theoretical contributions, practical implications, and avenues for future research.
