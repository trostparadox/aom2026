When Industry Experience Becomes Logic: Business Model Design in Four Entrepreneurial Cases

TIM
SHUILIAN WANG; XIAOHUI YANG; SHASHA LIU; Fengyun Wei (Dalian Maritime University)
This study examines how entrepreneurs’ industry experience shapes the decision logics guiding initial business model construction. Drawing on a qualitative four-case comparison and imprinting theory, we conceptualize industry experience along two dimensions—industry relatedness (insider vs. outsider) and experience structure (depth vs. breadth)—and distinguish four founder profiles: Industry Specialists, Cross-Industry Leaders, Cross-Industry Pathfinders, and Industry Verticals. Our findings show that different experience configurations systematically channel entrepreneurs’ reliance on effectuation versus causation during business model construction. The mechanism hinges on the strength and configuration of cognitive and capability imprints. Building on this mechanism, we advance four propositions that specify stage-contingent logic use across value proposition establishment and transaction structure design: Industry Specialists rely on causation in both phases; Cross-Industry Leaders combine causation (value proposition) with effectuation (transaction structure); Cross-Industry Pathfinders employ effectuation throughout via resource bricolage; and Industry Verticals adopt the reverse hybrid—effectuation for value proposition establishment and causation for transaction structure design. These insights deepen understanding of business model construction under uncertainty and inform entrepreneurs, investors, and training programs on logic–experience alignment.
