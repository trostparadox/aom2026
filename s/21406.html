Developing Transferable Competences through Blended Intensive Program: A Social Presence Perspective

Session overview

Blended Intensive Programs (BIPs), developed under the Erasmus+ initiative, combine virtual collaboration with short-term physical mobility and are increasingly promoted as innovative tools for internationalising higher education. However, empirical and reflective insights into how BIPs can be systematically integrated into existing curricula to support the development of transferable competences remain limited. This poster presents an educational design and implementation case of integrating a BIP into a graduate-level Human Resource Management (HRM) module at a business school in Northern Europe. The programme, titled Managing Virtual, Interdisciplinary and Multicultural Teams, involved 45 master’s students from 12 higher education institutions across 10 countries. The BIP was embedded into the course Human Resource Management in International Organisations and structured around three phases: virtual preparation, an intensive on-site week, and post-intensive team-based project work with Lithuanian organisations. Mixed international student teams collaborated on real organisational challenges, supported by academic staff, organisational mentors, and former programme participants. The poster highlights how this integrated design fostered the development of key transferable competences, including intercultural communication, teamwork, critical and problem-solving skills, time management, and self-regulation. Drawing on educators’ reflective analysis, the study also identifies key challenges related to coordination, workload intensity, and the inclusion of local working students. The contribution of this poster lies in presenting a transferable BIP integration model for management education, offering practical and pedagogical implications for educators seeking to embed international, competence-oriented learning experiences into existing programs.
