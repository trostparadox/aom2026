Investigating When and Why Leader Passion Expression Promotes Versus Inhibits Employee Job Crafting

OB
Jiyu Li; Zhenyuan Wang (East China Normal University)
Existing research consistently shows that leaders’ expressions of passion foster positive emotional contagion, invigorating employees and enhancing performance. However, based on the Emotion as Social Information (EASI) model, we challenge this prevailing view by uncovering the potential negative effects and boundary conditions of leader passion expression, suggesting it can operate as a double-edged sword. We argue that the impact of leader passion expression depends on employees’ perceptions of leader-employee goal congruence. When congruence is high, leader passion expression can effectively enhance employees’ feelings of energy through emotional contagion, facilitating promotion-focused job crafting aimed at growth. Conversely, when congruence is low, employees may interpret a leader's passion as a signal of pressure, leading to a significant correlation with perceived workload. This can trigger prevention-focused job crafting aimed at resource conservation. Our hypotheses receive empirical support from a scenario experimental study (Study 1) and a three-wave lagged survey (Study 2). These findings highlight both the advantages and disadvantages of leader passion expression, emphasizing the importance of leader-employee goal congruence in shaping the interpretation of this unique emotional influence.

Divergent Effects of Prosocial Leadership on Job Crafting

OB
Yoshihiro Ito; Rebecca Badawy (University of Pittsburgh)
In increasingly volatile and unpredictable work contexts, employees are often required to adapt their roles rather than rely on stable routines. Job crafting represents one important way employees proactively adjust their work to meet changing demands, yet less is known about how leadership cues shape these behaviors. Grounded in conservation of resources theory, this study examines how two prosocial leadership approaches, servant leadership and leader mindfulness, are uniquely related to employee job crafting and whether these relationships differ by gender. Using survey data from 191 full time U.S. employees across multiple industries, we estimated a regression model including both leadership behaviors, their interactions with gender, and relevant controls. Results show that perceived servant leadership is positively related to job crafting, whereas perceived leader mindfulness is negatively related to job crafting, revealing a counterintuitive pattern for a leadership behavior typically viewed as supportive. Gender moderated the relationship between leader mindfulness and job crafting, but not the relationship between servant leadership and job crafting. These findings highlight the importance of moving beyond uniform assumptions about supportive leadership and underscore the need to consider for whom and under what conditions leadership behaviors encourage employees to actively shape their work.

The Cross-Level Impact of Self-Sacrificial Leadership on Employee Job Crafting

OB
Yanting Zhu; Wenhao Luo (North China University of Technology)
Digital transformation is a common challenge faced by enterprises today, as key elements within organizations and the work environment are continuously reshaped by artificial intelligence technologies. Managers need to adopt effective leadership styles to enhance corporate performance and foster positive employee work behaviors. Self-sacrificial leadership, as a collective-oriented leadership style, can positively influence employee proactive work behaviors. However, few studies have explored its cross-level effects and mechanisms on different types of employee job crafting at the team level. Based on the Conservation of Resources theory, this study conducted a multilevel path analysis on 356 dyad data from 10 Chinese companies with a time-lagged research design. The results indicate that at the team level, self-sacrificial leadership positively influences employee promotion-focused job crafting but negatively affects their prevention-focused job crafting. Promotion collective regulatory focus mediates the relationship between self-sacrificial leadership and promotion-focused job crafting, as well as the relationship between self-sacrificial leadership and prevention-focused job crafting. Team leader competence, as a key team resource, positively moderates the relationship between self-sacrificial leadership and promotion Collective Regulatory Focus, and further moderates the mediating role of promotion Collective Regulatory Focus in the positive relationship between self-sacrificial leadership and promotion-focused job crafting. The findings contribute to enriching research on self-sacrificial leadership and job crafting, expand the application scenarios of the Conservation of Resources theory at the team level, and provide practical insights for organizations in cultivating self-sacrificial leadership and encouraging employee promotion-focused job crafting under current business environments.

Upward LMXSC and Divergent Job Crafting Paths: Dualistic Work Passion and Internal Locus of Control

OB
Yingnan Dong (Waseda University); Yuhee Jung (Waseda University)
In dynamic and uncertain organizational environments, employees are increasingly engaging in job crafting as proactive agents shaping their work. Although prior research has distinguished between approach and avoidance job crafting, the reason why employees adopt different strategies under similar conditions remains unclear. Drawing on the social comparison theory and the conservation of resources theory, this study examined how upward leader– member exchange social comparison (LMXSC) shapes differentiated job crafting pathways through distinct forms of work passion. Using three-wave time-lagged data from full-time employees in China (N = 760), the study’s results showed that upward LMXSC undermines harmonious work passion, thereby reducing approach job crafting while simultaneously enhancing obsessive work passion, which promotes avoidance job crafting. Moreover, an internal locus of control exhibits a path-dependent double-edged effect: it buffers the negative impact of upward LMXSC on harmonious passion while it amplifies its positive effect on obsessive passion. By conceptualizing upward LMXSC as a salient contextual signal, this study advances the job crafting literature and challenges the assumption that an internal locus of control is uniformly beneficial.

From Obligation to Agency: A Daily Diary Study of Deontic Justice Enactment and Job Crafting

OB
Neha Tripathi (National University of Singapore); Yunyi Shao (Zhejiang University); Yukun Liu (ShanghaiTech University)
Job crafting, the proactive redefinition of one’s work tasks, relationships, and role meanings, is a critical mechanism through which employees align their work with evolving needs and values. While prior research has emphasized individual traits and job characteristics as antecedents, less is known about the interpersonal behaviors and psychological conditions that enable employees to craft their jobs. Drawing on Parker et al.’s (2010) proactive motivation framework, we propose that daily deontic justice enactment, defined as fair treatment of others grounded in moral obligation, enhances employees’ engagement in job crafting by activating three core motivational pathways. Specifically, we theorize that justice enactment provides a reason to craft by affirming one’s moral purpose, fosters can do motivation by cultivating psychological safety, and builds on energized to motivation through a sensitizing effect that amplifies the motivational salience of repeated moral action. We tested our model using a 10-day experience sampling study. Multilevel analyses supported our hypotheses: On days when employees enacted justice, they reported higher psychological safety, which in turn was associated with increased job crafting. Moreover, this indirect effect was stronger following prior days of high justice enactment, consistent with the proposed sensitizing mechanism. These findings position deontic justice enactment as a dynamic, self-reinforcing behavior that shapes the motivational and psychological conditions for proactive role redesign. Our work contributes to theory on job crafting, psychological safety, and organizational justice by highlighting the role of morally motivated interpersonal behavior in sustaining daily proactivity.
