Organizational Form and ESG Evaluation: Business Groups and Rating Divergence

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1

Author: Yi Tang The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

Why do ESG ratings of the same firm often diverge sharply across rating agencies? Prior research attributes this divergence mainly to rater-specific methodologies or firm-level disclosure strategies, implicitly assuming that firms are discrete, transparent units of evaluation. This study challenges that assumption by advancing an organizational-structural explanation. Focusing on business groups (BGs), I argue that BG affiliation systematically problematizes ESG evaluation by blurring organizational boundaries and obscuring responsibility attribution. Because affiliated firms operate under centralized group governance while being evaluated as legally independent entities, ESG outcomes become difficult to assign to the focal firm versus the group as a whole. This structural ambiguity generates persistent disagreement among ESG raters. Building on business group research, information asymmetry, and CSR decoupling theories, I develop a set of propositions that specify when and why this ambiguity is amplified or constrained. In particular, I theorize the roles of affiliate hierarchical position, intragroup transaction intensity, and public attention as key conditions shaping evaluative disagreement. Using the context of Chinese listed firms, where business groups are prevalent and ESG ratings are increasingly influential, this study contributes to the ESG ratings literature by introducing organizational form as a foundational driver of rating divergence. It also extends business group research by identifying a previously overlooked external consequence of group structures: fragmented and contested ESG evaluations.
