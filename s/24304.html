Interactive Antecedents and Reinforcement Processes of Overwork Among Gig Workers

OB
Jun Yang (Tongji University); Dongdong Li (Shanghai University); Zhenghao Xu (Shanghai University); Andong Dong (Shanghai University); Bin Wang (Monash University); Le Li (Shanghai University)
The gig economy has made overwork widespread, leaving many workers trapped in a persistent cycle of high labor intensity and limited skill development. While overwork in gig settings largely reflects self-discipline exercised under relative autonomy, existing research has concentrated on platform mechanisms, paying insufficient attention to workers’ internal motivational drivers. Drawing on trait activation theory, we adopt a trait-situational interaction perspective to identify the joint antecedents of gig workers’ overwork as well as its reinforcement processes. Two empirical studies were conducted to test our hypotheses. Study 1 (N = 307) showed that work gamification amplified the effect of achievement motivation on overwork, whereas task unpredictability intensified the impact of scarcity mindset. Study 2, based on a seven-day experience sampling design (N between = 155, N within = 736), revealed that daily overwork carried over to the next day through pride and perceived income level, producing cross-day accumulation effects. Our research advances the overwork literature by identifying its’ interactive antecedents, we also extend trait activation theory to the digital gig-work context. Practical implications for platform labor management and worker well-being are also discussed.
