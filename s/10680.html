Session overview

This PDW builds on a recent Strategic Organization Special Issue (Sergeeva et al., in press) examining how intelligent technologies—particularly AI— reshape strategy and organization scholarship. Through ten articles and an editorial, the special issue explores how these technologies' potential to reconfigure tasks traditionally requiring human intelligence distinguishes them from previous technologies that enhanced physical action. This raises fundamental questions about organizational capabilities, knowledge creation and transfer, coordination, learning, business models, and organizational boundaries. This PDW brings together distinguished special issue authors with participants across AOM divisions to explore how scholarship must adapt to intelligent organizing. Papers address how machines perform tasks previously requiring human intelligence, how human-technology hybrids alter intelligent work, and how technology enables new organizational forms—challenging scholars to reconceptualize core constructs centered on humans. The workshop has two parts. Part 1 features impulse presentations by special issue editors framing intelligent organizing's implications for core strategy and organization constructs. Participants engage with these provocations and connect them to their research. Part 2 involves 54 pre-registered scholars (early-, mid-career, and senior) in roundtables with leading scholars, rotating across two of nine topic areas exploring how intelligent technology challenges existing frameworks. The interactive format enables participants to grapple with fundamental questions about technology's constitutive role in strategic organization.
