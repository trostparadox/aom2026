Parallel Paths to Retention: Motivation and Justice during Agile Transformation

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Authors: Nicoline Scheidegger Zurich University of Applied Sciences; Konrad Rietmann ZHAW School of Management and Law

Session overview

The introduction of new compensation systems presents organizations with significant challenges—particularly during agile transformations when status changes and income losses may occur. Building on Self-Determination Theory and Organizational Justice Theory, we develop and test a dual-process model that conceptualizes motivation and justice as independent, parallel mechanisms shaping employee responses to compensation system change. Using survey data from a service company undergoing agile transformation that replaced individual bonuses with collective, company-wide bonuses and competence-based job levels, we examine how these mechanisms relate to turnover intention. Results show that self-determined motivation and perceived justice independently predict turnover intention, with neither process mediating the other. In line with the compensation context, distributive justice emerges as the strongest justice predictor, alongside procedural and informational justice, while interpersonal justice shows no incremental effect. The near-zero correlation between autonomous motivation and distributive justice further supports the assumption of two distinct psychological evaluation processes. Theoretically, our findings challenge dominant mediation-based integration attempts and demonstrate that motivation and justice operate through parallel affective–self-regulatory and cognitive–evaluative pathways during organizational change. Practically, this implies that motivating work design cannot compensate for unfair compensation, nor can fair pay substitute for autonomous motivation—both must be addressed simultaneously.
