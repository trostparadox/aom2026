Session overview

How jobs are perceived shapes how work and workers are evaluated, trusted, and rewarded in organizations and society. While organizations, policy makers, and public discourse often treat job characteristics as objective features, jobs are frequently interpreted through the social and cognitive lenses of skill and status. These perceptions can introduce challenges for both workers and organizations, particularly as technological change reshapes how work is valued and performed. In this symposium, we examine how perceptions of job skill requirements and occupational status influence judgments of worker competence, credibility, and value, including instances where evaluations may be inaccurate or biased. Collectively, these papers highlight job perception as a consequential yet understudied psychological process that shapes status hierarchies, evaluation, and worker welfare, offering insights for organizational practices related to job design and framing, legitimacy and credibility assessments, and employee development.
