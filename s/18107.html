Session overview

Many students are eager for opportunities to participate in competitive events, such as case study competitions, pitch competitions, sales competitions, and strategy competitions, that round out their learning and anchor their educational resumes. However, getting started with facilitating these types of experiences can be daunting for faculty, particularly those early in their careers or from institutions with limited resources. The purpose of this PDW is to share our experiences as early-career faculty with advising teams of students to compete in the annual International Collegiate Business Strategy Competition (ICBSC) in Anaheim, California. The ICBSC is a comprehensive, computerized business strategy competition that gives students hands-on experience with running their own company and experiencing the challenges of collective decision-making amidst intense and shifting competitive pressures. The ICBSC is "as close as you can come to running your own business without risking your own money." In this interactive PDW, with opportunities for both presentations by the organizers and open discussion with the audience, we share ideas and discuss how we have generated support from university administrators and raised money from sponsors, how we select student team members, how we leverage the competition as a semester-long class experience, and how we address institutional barriers and other challenges. We highlight the benefits and growth to students from participating in the competition, as illustrated by past participant feedback, and plan to set aside time for individualized/small group discussion and brainstorming to facilitate participation in other "signature" competition experiences for students. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
