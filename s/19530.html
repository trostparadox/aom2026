Who Should Be Warned With What? Heterogeneous Effects of Warning Messages in Personality Assessment

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Author: Liyan Xi Singapore Management University

Session overview

Warnings have been widely studied as interventions to reduce applicant faking in pre-employment personality assessments. Prior research shows that warning messages can mitigating faking, but their effects vary with message framing. Prevention-focused warnings emphasize detection and negative consequences and are generally effective in reducing score inflation, yet they may undermine applicants’ perceived fairness. In contrast, promotion-focused warnings highlight the benefits of honest responding and are perceived as fairer but show weaker effects on faking. Moreover, individual characteristics, such as faking tendency, affect how applicants respond to warnings. This study examines whether causal machine learning (ML) can be used to move beyond uniform warning interventions by delivering appropriate warning messages to different applicants. Using data from a field experiment in a selection context, we apply causal ML to estimate and compare individual-level heterogeneous treatment effects of prevention-focused and promotion-focused warnings on changes in personality test scores and perceived fairness, and to assess whether and which type of warning should be delivered to a given applicant. We find that this targeting strategy can better balance the dual goals of reducing faking while maintaining perceived fairness. The implications for personnel selection research and practice are discussed.
