When Safety Narrows the Match: Online Dating Regulation and Racial Integration in Marriage

DEI
Hyeonsik Shin (University of Wisconsin-Green Bay); Min-Seok Pang (University of Wisconsin-Madison); Eunji Huh (University of Wisconsin-Green Bay)
As meeting romantic partners online has surpassed meeting partners offline in the United States, public and policy interest has increased in how online dating platforms (ODPs) shape social integration and diversity outcomes. Recently, several U.S. states have introduced the Online Dating Safety Act (IDSA), which requires ODPs to disclose potential risks associated with online dating. However, little is known about how such regulation affects intramarriage and intermarriage patterns that are central to racial inclusion and boundary crossing, and thus highly relevant to diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) initiatives. Using metropolitan statistical area–level data from IPUMS USA, combined with public news sources and Google Trends, we examine the impact of the IDSA on marriage outcomes. We find that following the enactment of the IDSA, the intermarriage ratio decreases by 0.332 percentage points two years later, while intramarriage remains unaffected. Further analyses suggest that the IDSA increases the perceived search costs of seeking partners from out-groups, narrowing users’ choice sets and disproportionately discouraging interracial dating. This study contributes to the information systems (IS) literature by examining how digital platform regulation shapes DEI-relevant social outcomes, particularly interracial marriage, which is widely viewed as an indicator of social inclusion. We further show that regulation differentially affects user behavior depending on platform features: after the IDSA becomes effective, users reduce engagement with search-based platforms while continuing to rely on recommendation-based platforms. These findings have important implications for platform design and for policymakers seeking to improve online safety without unintentionally constraining pathways to social diversity and inclusion.
