Session overview

Newer firms, outsiders, and innovative outputs face unique barriers such as evaluative heterogeneity and uncertainty in gatekeepers; novelty generates dissensus when people prefer consensus (Harvey & Mueller, 2021; Johnson & Proudfoot, 2024; Boudreau et al. 2016). As the quotes above reflect however, it is the offerings that are both new and controversial that may offer the greatest returns, i.e. those offerings that are the most difficult to evaluate because they most diverge from existing categories. Venture capitalists must parse jungles of ambiguity in their hunt for unicorns. Scholars must theorize about when categories, status, gatekeepers and consensus (or lack thereof) alternatively help and hinder innovation. Categorization can be a barrier to new entrants or it can be strategized to create new opportunities (Pontikes, 2018). Likewise, status can enable risks and big swings not possible without it or can calcify and canalize actors into the status quo, just as gatekeepers can champion-or spurn- outsiders. These practical and theoretical tensions generate pressing questions about when each type of outcome is more likely to occur. The purpose of this panel symposium is to engage scholars who have been and continue to tackle these difficult but important topics in a formal, moderated, interactive discussion.
