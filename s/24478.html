From Theory to Play: Challenge-Based Game Design for Circular Economy Competence Development

MED
Cristian R. Loza Adaui (THI Business School - Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt); Maud Schmiedeknecht; Holger Tobias Hoppe (Ingolstadt University of Applied Sciences)
Challenge-based and game-based learning can together create conditions for deep engagement with complex concepts, including sustainability and the circular economy. This paper uses qualitative case study research methods to answer the following research question: How does the implementation of challenge-based and game-based learning in an intensive, intercultural, and interdisciplinary summer school enhance students’ understanding of the R-principles of circular economy? The study examines a pedagogical intervention within a real-world setting. The empirical context is provided by the SustainED Summer School at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt, Germany, which convened 20 bachelor students from Germany and Brazil during June 2025 for an intensive week of learning focused on sustainability and systems thinking. Within this program, one student group developed, as a final deliverable, a serious game that recalls participants’ previous gaming experiences and translates circular economy R-principles into a concrete, new interactive learning experience. The setting of an intensive, international classroom functions as a laboratory for experimentation, where intercultural and interdisciplinary collaboration, together with challenge-based and game-based learning, cultivates individual skills as well as strategic and systems-thinking competencies essential for addressing polycrisis challenges. The study contributes to understanding how innovative, experiential pedagogies can support the learning of complex sustainability concepts.
