The Hidden Psychological Costs of Complex AI Use: Evidence From Experience Sampling

Session overview

Employees increasingly relying on AI-based tools to support task execution (Tang et al., 2022). Prior research has largely emphasized the instrumental benefits of AI use, documenting positive effects on efficiency and performance related outcomes (Bankins et al., 2024; Noy & Zhang, 2023). However, much less is known about how everyday AI use affects employees’ psychological functioning and mental well-being (Bankins et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2024). Drawing on Self Determination Theory (SDT) (Ryan & Deci, 2000), we examine how daily AI use influences employees’ competence experiences and task performance, and how these effects depend on task complexity. SDT posits that experiences of competence constitute a fundamental psychological need that supports well-being (Ryan & Deci, 2024). We argue that AI use can enhance competence and task performance by enabling employees to accomplish work more effectively (Tang et al., 2022). However, when AI is used extensively in complex, cognitively demanding tasks, it may constrain employees own efforts, thereby weakening competence experiences and diminishing performance related benefits, ultimately undermining well-being (Cai et al., 2025; Hermanns & Teubner, 2025; Jia et al., 2024). We test our hypotheses using an experience sampling design capturing a total of 1,150 daily observations from 164 participants. This study advances understanding of how AI reshapes psychological need fulfillment in everyday work and highlights the importance of task context for understanding the mental well-being consequences of AI mediated work (Bankins et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2022).
