Not All Breaks Are Equal: An ESM Study of How Lunch Break Activities Shape Daily Energy Through Flow

OB
Wan-Jing April Chang (National Tsing Hua University); Ya-Jen JOe Cheng (National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University)
Despite a growing body of research on work recovery and flow experience, there is limited information on the nature of breaks and the underlying mechanism between at-work recovery and its consequences, as well as antecedents and flow links. Considering the importance of mental health in the workplace, this study extends the work recovery literature by first, examining whether the influence of break activities during a workday on end-of-workday energy levels is different depending on the nature of activities; and second, exploring the mediating role of flow experience between types of within-day breaks and mental-health-related consequences while engaging in work activities. This study adopts an experience sampling method to collect information from 84 full-time employees. We sent survey links to participants’ mobile devices three times per day, during a two-week workday period. Hierarchical linear modeling and structural equation modeling were employed to test the hypotheses. The results show that work activity positively relates to exhaustion, and relaxation activity is negatively associated with exhaustion. Furthermore, flow experience mediates these relationships by reversing the work-exhaustion relationship from positive to negative and changing the work-vigor relationship from nonsignificant to significant. These mediation effects are found for work and relaxation activities, but not for social activities. The study discusses theoretical and practical implications, limitations, and future research directions.
