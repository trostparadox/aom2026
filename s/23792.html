The Invisible Work of Resilience: How Employees Quietly Repair Dysfunction

OB
Debanjana Deb Biswas (Amity University, Kolkata)
Workplace dysfunction, often manifesting as incivility, ambiguity, interpersonal friction, and leadership gaps, creates hidden burdens for employees who often step in to maintain stability. While organizational resilience research has examined how systems and leaders respond to disruption, little is known about the invisible labor employees perform to quietly repair everyday dysfunction. This study develops a grounded theory of Invisible Resilience Work (IRW), the subtle and often unnoticed cognitive, relational, and emotional efforts through which employees stabilize their work environment. Using a constructivist grounded theory approach, we conducted 60 in-depth interviews across diverse sectors in India, supported by field notes and reflexive memos. Our analysis reveals a three-phase process through which employees enact IRW: (1) Detecting Dysfunction, involving continuous scanning for breakdowns or emotional tension; (2) Absorbing Shock, characterized by buffering harmful interactions, taking on additional tasks, and using strategic silence to prevent escalation; and (3) Quiet Repairing, where employees restore communication, rebuild morale, and foster micro-level psychological safety. The findings reveal an inherent tension: although IRW helps teams function, it imposes a significant resilience tax on employees, contributing to emotional exhaustion and identity strain while remaining unrecognized by organizations. The study contributes a novel process model of employee-driven resilience repair, extends theorizing on resilience, silence, and emotional labor, and highlights the relational and cultural foundations of resilience in collectivist contexts. We offer implications for reducing reliance on hidden labor, promoting equitable responsibility for resilience, and designing supportive organizational systems. The study concludes by identifying pathways for future research on quantifying IRW and its long-term outcomes. [Keywords: Resilience labor, Invisible work, Workplace dysfunction, Grounded theory, Employee coping, Organizational behavior]
