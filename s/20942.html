Dialectical Conflict Management: A Framework for Managing Workplace Values Conflict

Authors: Sivahn Barsade Barli; Corinne Bendersky

Session overview

Modern workplaces face a rise in interpersonal values-based conflict where deeply disagreement over issues that represent or evoke deep ideological values (e.g. conflicts over gender equity, poverty, religion) (Wade-Benzoni et al., 2002). These conflicts tend to evoke intense moral emotions such as disgust and contempt (Pratt et al., 2025). Traditional conflict management, rooted in the Dual Concerns Model (Pruitt & Rubin, 1986), fails in these contexts because it assumes a willingness to compromise and involves tactics such as integrative problem solving and perspective taking which require significant cognitive resources that are inaccessible when experiencing intense emotions (Carnevale & Probst, 1998). Given this, the standard prescriptive advice for these workplace conflicts is avoidance (e.g. ‘keep politics out of work’ or ‘just focus on the task’) (Thomas, 1992). However, this strategy is both unfeasible in interdependent contexts and depletes individuals of the psychological resources needed to complete their work tasks. We address this challenge by developing a theory of effective conflict management based on Dialectical Behavioral Therapy (DBT), a cognitive behavioral therapy designed to treat severe emotion dysregulation by encouraging patients to accept and embrace opposing thoughts, emotions, and behaviors (Linehan et al., 2007). Our proposed framework – Dialectical Conflict Management – integrates principles of DBT to existing conflict management strategies. This integration shines a light on boundary conditions of popular conflict management strategies while identifying new opportunities for intervention in moralized conflicts
