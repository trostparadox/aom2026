Authors: Diana Warner University of Maryland College Park; Eri Shay University of Maryland College Park; Nikki Frias; Paul John Hanges University of Maryland College Park; Dritjon Gruda CPBS/Maynooth U.; John Michael Kauth

Session overview

This study investigates the comparison between human and artificial intelligence (AI) in analyzing leadership traits within global literature. Using the Project GLOBE’s 112 leadership traits, we compared how human coders and OpenAI’s ChatGPT-5.2 rated summaries of 62 books, each representing a different country. Human coders demonstrated moderate inter-rater reliability (ICC (3,k) = .453, p < .001), indicating consistent interpretation of the traits. However, the agreement between human and AI coders was low (ICC (2,k) = .076, p < .001). Furthermore, a hierarchical cluster analysis found that 78.6% of traits were placed in different clusters for human structures compared to AI structures. While human coders displayed convergent ratings patterns, AI applied a different pattern to the same literary content. These findings highlight a significant interpretive gap between human and AI content analysis, raising important questions about the validity and reliability of using AI for cultural and leadership research.
