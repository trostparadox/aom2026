Session overview

Deep tech ventures are at the forefront of transforming economies and addressing grand societal challenges. While research has established that deep tech startups are distinct due to long development cycles, high technological uncertainty, and acute funding barriers such as the valley of death, our understanding of their entrepreneurial dynamics remains limited. We still lack clarity on which founder, team, and ecosystem factors actually drive progress across stages, because deep tech ventures are hard to measure, involve opaque technologies, and require integrating scientific, managerial, and commercial perspectives. At the same time, deep tech is no longer a “pre-AI” phenomenon: generative AI is increasingly reshaping how frontier ventures are built and evaluated. Founders use AI systems for scientific discovery, rapid prototyping, experimentation, and decision support, while investors increasingly deploy AI-assisted screening, due diligence, and risk assessment, potentially altering what counts as a credible signal of quality, traction, and technological defensibility under uncertainty. A future research agenda should therefore combine (a) core deep tech constructs and longitudinal designs with (b) a dedicated methodological and theoretical view on GenAI-enabled entrepreneurship and innovation systems. This includes developing shared taxonomies and comparable datasets that capture how deep tech teams evolve, how investors assess frontier technologies in AI-saturated evaluation pipelines, and how innovation systems can better support science-based venture creation. Methodologically, the agenda should advance AI-enabled research infrastructures, global observatories that integrate patents, publications, funding histories, and venture signals at scale, while explicitly addressing validity, bias, and governance challenges introduced by automated classification and inference. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW), “Global Deep Tech and Impact Forum: Mapping Science-Based Innovation and Frontier Technologies Worldwide,” provides an interactive forum to bring together scholars and practitioners working on deep tech entrepreneurship, entrepreneurial finance, and innovation ecosystems. It outlines a shared vision for a Global Deep Tech and Impact Council and provides a structured forum to identify emerging methodological approaches, key research gaps and challenges, and priorities for a future research agenda. The session invites participants to jointly define core constructs, map relevant global data sources, and explore opportunities for collaborative and comparative research. Drawing on insights from diverse regional and technological ecosystems, participants will co-develop concepts for cross-country studies, shared methodological standards, and sustainable research infrastructures. The workshop focuses on three key areas: - Emerging methods: Developing shared conceptual frameworks and analytical approaches that clearly define deep tech domains and allow for consistent, comparable measurement. - Key research gaps and challenges: Limited understanding of deep tech funding trajectories, investor evaluation of frontier technologies, and the coordination challenges between private and public actors across stages.
