How Environmental Shocks Influence Downsizing

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: TzeShiang Lin University of South Carolina; Jana Boeckx IESEG School of Management; Kristof Struyfs IESEG School of Management; Anthony J. Nyberg University of South Carolina

Session overview

Negative environmental shocks heighten firms’ dependence on external resource flows and intensify uncertainty, increasing the likelihood of employee downsizing. Integrating resource dependence theory (RDT) with human capital resources (HCR) theory and circular economy (CE) strategy, we hypothesize and find that shocks increase downsizing, but that CE strategy implementation attenuates this effect by reducing external input dependence. We further find that firms with greater HCR dependence are less likely to downsize following shocks, consistent with the higher strategic costs of human capital outflows. Evidence from 1,524 listed firms (10,213 firm-years; 2015–2022) supports these claims.
