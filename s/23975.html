Channeling Attention at Home: Product Scope and Domestic Context in New Venture Internationalization (WITHDRAWN)

STR
Abu Rehan Abbasi (Indian Institute of Management Kashipur)
Prior research suggests that broad product scope can hinder international expansion in established firms by increasing coordination requirements. Whether this relationship holds for new ventures remains unclear. Moreover, despite the prominence of business groups (BGs) in emerging markets (EMs), studies of new venture internationalization rarely consider BG affiliation as an institutional context. Drawing on the attention-based view, this study examines how product scope influences internationalization in EM new ventures and how this relationship depends on BG affiliation. We analyze an unbalanced panel of 6,964 Indian new ventures observed over 24 years using Tobit estimation with firm-level clustered standard errors. Unlike established firms, where product and international diversification often act as substitutes, broader product scope promotes internationalization among EM new ventures. However, diversified BG-affiliated ventures internationalize more slowly than diversified non-affiliates, as BG affiliation substitutes for weak formal institutions and redirects managerial attention toward domestic opportunities. The study demonstrates that product scope and internationalization are complementary in new ventures, challenging dominant assumptions derived from research on established firms. It further reveals a paradoxical role of BG affiliation: while BGs provide resources and institutional support, they can also constrain early internationalization by anchoring managerial attention domestically.
