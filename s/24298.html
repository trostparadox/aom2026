Steering with Trust: The Consensus–Transformation Dilemma in High-Trust Societies

TIM
Anna Smolander (Aalto University)
Transformative Innovation Policy (TIP) assumes that high-trust, consensus-oriented political economies possess the coordination capacity required to steer innovation toward societal goals. Emergent technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI) present a decisiveness gap where the burden of consensus can dilute directional intent. We examine how directionality is governed in high-trust environments through a longitudinal analysis of Finland’s AI mission (2013–2025). Utilizing an abductive qualitative design and a corpus of 296 policy and intermediary documents supplemented by expert interviews, we identify a Consensus–Transformation Dilemma: the foundations of systemic trust that enable coordination can simultaneously impede prioritisation and radical socio-technical reorientation. We find that Finland manages this through domesticated disruption, where transformative intent is neutralized through four mechanisms: intentional strategic vagueness, institutional stickiness, professional interpretive drift, and leadership fragmentation. We label this pattern a Nordic Trust Anomaly, in which trust functions both as a coordination resource and as institutional glue that stabilizes legacy arrangements. This study contributes a framework of Trust-Moderated Directionality (TMD) to TIP scholarship, qualifying the assumption that broad participation necessarily strengthens directionality. We argue that in high-trust systems, coordination can lead to strategic stasis if prioritisation is not institutionally legitimized. We conclude by identifying boundary conditions for mission performance and proposing adaptive decisiveness as a pathway for small open economies to maintain transformative velocity without eroding systemic trust.
