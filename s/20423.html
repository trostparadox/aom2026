Session overview

With political polarization on the rise in many parts of the world, organizations and employees are increasingly grappling with the challenges posed by political tensions in the workplace. This symposium examines how workers experience, navigate, and manage political discourse and identity-based conflict in organizational life, with particular attention to the interpersonal, structural, and organizational processes that shape whether political differences are suppressed, escalate into division, or–under certain circumstances–can be engaged more constructively. Bringing together four complementary papers, this symposium conceptualizes political polarization as a behavioral and organizational phenomenon, rather than solely an attitudinal one. The papers span multiple levels of analysis, from interpersonal processes and institutional choice architecture to inclusion dynamics and person-organization fit, to show how organizational systems translate political differences into concrete outcomes for employees and organizations. Collectively, the symposium advances theory on polarization, inclusion, and identity management while identifying pathways for sustaining disagreement without undermining cohesion or well-being in politically diverse workplaces.
