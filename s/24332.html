How AI Usage Fuels Service Improvisation: A Conservation of Resources Perspective

CTO
Xin Chen (Renmin University of China); Xiaoyu Li (China University of Mining and Technology (Xuzhou)); Zhihao Wu (Jinan University)
Drawing on the Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, this study investigates how AI usage enables service improvisation. Using a three-wave survey design with 370 tourism employees, we examine the underlying micro-mechanisms and a critical boundary condition of the focal relationship. The findings indicate that AI usage liberates employees from routine tasks, thereby enhancing psychological availability. This psychological state of readiness creates the necessary conditions for cognitive engagement, which ultimately drives service improvisation. Moreover, the utility of AI in replenishing resources is significantly stronger when employees face high task uncertainty. Theoretically, this study elucidates the micro-foundations of AI usage effectiveness by verifying the sequential transition from resource readiness (psychological availability) to active deployment (cognitive engagement). Furthermore, by identifying task uncertainty as a critical boundary condition, we demonstrate how the resource-replenishing utility of AI is amplified from a marginal benefit in routine tasks to a vital necessity in uncertain contexts.
