When Proactive Peers Collide: Proactive Personality Congruence, Status Conflict, and Performance

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Authors: Aashima Gaba Indian Institute of Management Lucknow; Kashish Kumar Truefoundry Inc

Session overview

Organizations increasingly select and encourage proactive employees because proactivity is widely associated with positive work outcomes. However, little is known about the implications of having multiple proactive individuals working closely together. Drawing on dominance complementarity theory, we examine how proactive personality congruence in coworker dyads influences status conflict and dyadic performance. We argue that, unlike leader–follower dyads, where proactive personality congruence is typically beneficial, similarity in proactive personality among peers may heighten competition for influence, thereby increasing status conflict and negatively affecting dyadic performance. Using multi-wave, multisource, dyadic data, we test a model in which proactive personality congruence predicts status conflict, which in turn affects dyadic performance. We further examine task interdependence as a boundary condition, proposing that the effects of proactive personality congruence on status conflict are attenuated when coworkers’ tasks are highly interdependent. Our findings challenge the dominant assumption that proactive personality similarity is uniformly advantageous and highlight the relational and contextual conditions under which proactivity among peers may undermine performance
