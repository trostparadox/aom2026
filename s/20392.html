Staying or Leaving? Psychological Capital and Migration Intentions

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Authors: Thi Minh Trung Ho International University of Sarajevo; Emil Knezovic International University of Sarajevo; Šejma Aydin International University of Sarajevo

Session overview

This study investigated whether psychological capital is related to migration intentions and if this relationship is mediated by self-perceived employability. Using cross-sectional survey design, data were collected from working-age population in six developing countries (Bosnia and Herzegovina, Colombia, Jordan, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Vietnam). The data collection resulted in 1,413 valid responses, with a minimum threshold of 200 per country. Hypotheses were tested via structural equation modeling. The results demonstrate that psychological capital is positively related to migration intentions, while self-perceived employability mediates the relationship. The study contributes to migration research by extending the aspirations–capabilities framework to individual psychological resources and identifying self-perceived employability as a key explanatory mechanism in developing-country contexts. The results have limited generalizability and causal inference arising from the use of snowball sampling, cross-sectional design, and skewness of the sample in favor of more educated and employed individuals.
