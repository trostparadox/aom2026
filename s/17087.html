Session overview

Social media platforms are content-heavy and rely on high user engagement to foster network effects and be effective. Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI), capable of generating and distributing content, increasingly dominates social media feeds, diminishing human-generated content on platforms. However, GenAI- overpopulated social media platforms could have massive negative effects, potentially breaking user engagement, network effects, and thus the platform. Prior work on GenAI and AI-generated content (AIGC) largely examines isolated posts, innovation capabilities or disclosure effects. Yet, little is known about how social media platforms are affected by AIGC. These studies investigate effects serving as a proxy for how AI content is perceived. However, varying proportions of AIGC within a feed influence users’ engagement through key perceptual mechanisms. Thus, these studies do not address how much AI in social media platforms is too much. Building on a pre-study to align visual stimuli, this study examines how varying AIGC proportions within a feed influence user engagement and perceptual evaluations. Methodologically, the study builds on a within-subjects experiment, exposing participants to social media feeds differing in their percentage of AIGC. This design enables the examination of how feed- level AIGC composition affects engagement through shifts in users’ perceptual responses. By moving beyond individual AI capabilities to feed-level exposure, this study will provide an account of user engagement in AI-shaped social media environments, contributing to debates on platform-level strategies responding to AIGC.
