Silicon Mentor, Human Anchor: Can People Fix AI-Driven Role Ambiguity?

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Author: chenyan wang Nanjing University

Session overview

As organizations integrate Artificial Intelligence (AI) into onboarding, the shift toward AI-dominated mentoring presents unique challenges for employee socialization. Drawing on Role Theory and "Intelligent Augmentation," this study investigates how the proportion of AI mentoring influences new hires' role ambiguity and subsequent affective commitment. We further examine the moderating role of human mentor support (psychosocial and role-modeling).Preliminary qualitative findings from 11 semi-structured interviews reveal a "logic gap" in AI mentoring: while AI excels in explicit knowledge transfer, its lack of emotional depth creates a "cultural vacuum," heightening role ambiguity. Crucially, human mentors act as "cultural translators" and "mental anchors," buffering the negative impact of AI reliance. By shifting the focus from "Human vs. AI" to a synergistic "Human-AI" paradigm, this research offers empirical insights into balancing technological efficiency with the "human touch." Practically, it guides HR practitioners in designing hybrid mentoring systems that leverage AI for scalability while preserving human mentors for strategic guidance and emotional support.
