Examining Context, Microaggressions, and Health Behaviors Among LGBTQIA+ Employees

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Joseph Regina Rutgers University-Camden; Tammy D. Allen University of South Florida; Soner Dumani Human Resources Research Organization

Session overview

Minority stress theory states that individuals from marginalized groups experience additional stressors compared to their peers. One such marginalized group is the LGBTQIA+ community who can experience microaggressions while at work. Highlighting that LGBTQIA+ experiences can vary based on state norms in the United States, we examine the role of state legislation related to LGBTQIA+ inclusion as a contextual factor related to workplace microaggression experiences as well as how experienced microaggressions relate to unhealthy behavior enaction in the form of alcohol and nicotine consumption. Further considering how identity-related factors can modify experiences, we consider self-reported work identity importance as a moderator of the relationships from microaggressions to unhealthy behavior enaction to inform on a potential boundary condition of the hypothesized relationships; specifically, we posit relationships will be most detrimental among those with higher relative to lower work identity. Hypotheses were evaluated using a three-timepoint sample of 244 full-time employed individuals who self-identified as a member of the LGBTQIA+ community and a 5,000 bootstrap path model. Results suggest state-level inclusion was negatively related to experienced microaggressions. Additionally, the interaction of microaggression and work identity related to alcohol consumption, such that the relationship was positive among those with a higher work identity but not significant among those with a mean-level or lower work identity. Implications for minority stress theory, individual wellbeing, and the importance of considering contextual factors within research on LGTBQIA+ work experiences are discussed.
