Artificial or Superficial? Investigating Cognitive Effects of Generative AI in Management Education

MED
Michal Szymanski (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign); Carmit T. Tadmor (Tel Aviv University); Carlo Brighi (Ivey Business School)
This study explores how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) influences team performance and individual learning in management education. Drawing on Elaboration Likelihood Theory (ELM), we examine whether GenAI alters students’ task engagement, potentially reducing their reliance on effortful, self-generated elaboration during complex problem solving, with different consequences at the team and individual levels. In an experiment in a field classroom setting, ninety-four undergraduate business students, working in 16 client-based consulting teams, were assigned either to use GenAI tools or to complete a complex problem-solving task without them. GenAI-supported teams produced issue trees that were broader in conceptual scope, suggesting that GenAI can enhance the breadth of collective outputs. However, individuals in non-GenAI teams demonstrated significantly higher recall accuracy (both immediate and delayed), slightly higher confidence in the team’s solution, and greater cognitive elaboration spillover to a subsequent analytical task, indicating longer lasting individual-level learning. The results suggest a trade-off between collective breadth and individual-level cognitive engagement in GenAI-assisted teamwork. Implications for management educators center on designing “productive friction” in classroom settings to preserve reflection, reasoning, and collaborative learning in a GenAI-enriched – rather than GenAI-dominated – environment.
