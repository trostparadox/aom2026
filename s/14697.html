When Generative AI Becomes a Colleague: Early Identity Tensions in Academia

Authors: Neris Güven University of Amsterdam; Almasa Sarabi University of Amsterdam; Wendelien van Eerde University of Amsterdam; Corine Boon University of Amsterdam

Session overview

Generative AI (Gen-AI) introduces uncertainty into both what professionals do and who they are at work by augmenting and reframing tasks traditionally tied to professional expertise. Yet existing research has largely approached Gen-AI through an efficiency-oriented lens, treating its identity implications as future concerns. Addressing this gap, we qualitatively examine how professionals make sense of their professional identity as Gen-AI becomes embedded in everyday work. Based on preliminary findings from eight academics, we identify three key tensions. First, although Gen-AI is initially perceived as reducing workload, participants describe a reconfiguration of effort, as time saved is offset by new cognitive demands such as evaluating outputs and heightened perfectionism. Second, participants express ambivalence toward Gen-AI use in identity-relevant domains: they engage AI in tasks they find meaningful or central to skill development, sometimes relying on its output while simultaneously seeking to strengthen their own expertise. Third, although participants state that Gen-AI does not fundamentally alter workplace social dynamics, their practices suggest otherwise: many turn to “always-available AI colleague” for communication and early-stage brainstorming, while expressing concerns about reduced help-seeking, informal learning, and social bonds. Across these tensions, participants anticipate shifts in professional value, emphasizing the growing importance of critical thinking and social capital. Together, these findings highlight the meaning-related challenges of Gen-AI integration and the value of a professional identity lens for understanding AI-enabled work.
