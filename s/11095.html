Session overview

The field of organization design is experiencing a resurgence marked by diverse theoretical agendas and renewed practical relevance. A recent trend, however, has emphasized how design features constrain the behavior of individuals, teams and organizational units, while downplaying how those actors are embedded in—and interact within—larger organizational systems. For example, an emphasis on predicting an agent’s behavior has diverted attention from the emergent, collective dynamics and unintended consequences inherent in organizational systems. In parallel, a focus on internal structures has largely ignored design principles that better integrate internal structures with external environmental pressures. Information processing perspectives have often treated information and incentive-based design principles separately, even as AI reshapes both. Our goal is to identify and feature promising new lines of research along these lines, and to help Ph.D. students and junior faculty conceive of and develop high quality scholarship in this vein of research. By bringing together a diverse set of well-established and younger scholars that approach the field from different, but complementary perspectives and by including short, in-depth presentations, open Q&A, and discussion, we seek to encourage a constructive multi-way conversation and provide participants with very clear direction on the greatest opportunities for research on the toughest challenges in organization design today.
