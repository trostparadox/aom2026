The Impact of Digital Transformation Misalignment in Supply Chain on Green Innovation

OSCM
Jing Ning (Ocean University of China); Wenjie Tang (Ocean University of China); Xiaole Wan (Ocean University of China); Mengjie Xi (Ocean University of China)
With the vigorous advancement of digital transformation, digital transformation misalignment has gradually become evident. Based on absorptive capacity theory, this study investigates the impact of digital transformation misalignment between focal firms and their suppliers on corporate green innovation. Using a dataset of 2966 Chinese listed firms from 2011 to 2023, the results demonstrate a robust inverted U-shaped relationship between digital transformation misalignment and green innovation. Furthermore, this study identifies the moderating effects of degree centrality and geographic distance on this relationship. A firm’s high degree centrality within the supply chain network acts as a buffer, flattening the inverted U-shaped curve. Conversely, greater geographic distance steepens the inverted U-shaped relationship. These findings resolve existing inconsistencies regarding the impact of digital transformation on green innovation by highlighting the "optimal gap". This study suggests that firms should strategically select the suppliers and manage digital differentiation with partners, thereby providing guidance for optimizing supply chain management.
