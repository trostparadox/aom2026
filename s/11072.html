Open Strategy and Firm Performance in Dynamic Environments: Evidence From German-Speaking Countries

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
SAP

Author: Christian Bruck WU Vienna University of Economics and Business

Session overview

Research on open strategy, defined as inclusive and transparent strategy-making, has grown rapidly in recent years. Yet, much of the existing work relies on anecdotal illustrations or qualitative case studies. As a result, we have a limited causal understanding of how open strategy affects different dimensions of firm performance and interacts with environmental dynamism. To address this gap, we conducted a survey of the 100 largest corporations in German-speaking countries, obtaining 153 responses from middle and top managers on open strategy, environmental dynamism, and firm performance. Our analysis yields three key insights: (1) Using factor analysis, we clearly distinguish two firm-level strategy-making approaches – conventional strategy vs. open strategy – in our sample; (2) regression analyses of these approaches on different dimensions of firm performance (market, financial, and innovation performance) show that open strategy consistently outperforms conventional approaches; and (3) higher environmental dynamism weakens the positive effect of open strategy on performance while strengthening the effect of conventional strategy. For managers, these findings suggest that opening the strategy process can enhance firm performance in stable environments, whereas in more dynamic environments, a balance between conventional and open strategy is required. Overall, our study highlights the importance of considering environmental dynamism when deciding whether to pursue open strategy initiatives.
