Heuristic–Emotion Networks: A Hybrid AI-Augmented Analysis of Entrepreneurial Reasoning

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Meysam Manesh Fayetteville State University; Alicia Del Pardo Fayetteville State University; Assad Tavakoli Fayetteville State University

Session overview

Entrepreneurs operate in uncertain environments where decisions must be made quickly with limited information. Traditional methods to study entrepreneurs navigating uncertainty struggle to scale the nuanced interplay between cognition and affect. To uncover the cognitive and emotional structures of entrepreneurial reasoning, this study develops a hybrid analytical design that integrates large language models (LLM) with qualitative text analysis. We analyze a dataset from 700 interviews to reveal how heuristics emerge as adaptive logics that co-occur with emotional states. Our findings demonstrate that AI can bridge the methodological divide between interpretive depth and data-driven scalability in entrepreneurship research. By utilizing an AI-augmented pipeline, we visualize heuristic–emotion networks, revealing that heuristics such as satisficing, analogical reasoning, and effectuation are context-sensitive adaptive logics rather than fixed cognitive shortcuts. This research advances entrepreneurial cognition theory by demonstrating how AI-assisted methods uncover the micro-patterns of reasoning underlying early-stage decision-making. Specifically, we find that effectual logic (Koller et al., 2025) serves as a macro-level strategy while specific heuristics act as micro-level tactical manifestations of broader principles (Zhang et al., 2024). By automating the extraction of these cognitive episodes, we provide a scalable model for understanding the entrepreneurs at scale.
