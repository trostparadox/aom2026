Why Abusive Supervision Impacts Employees’ Creativity: A Meta-Analytic Review of Multiple Mechanisms

OB
Tongqing Zhu (Nanjing University); Su Cui (Nanjing University); Yina Mao (Nanjing University)
Although prior research has adopted various perspectives to explain the influence of abusive supervision on employees’ creativity, these studies have been largely conducted in separate and it lacks a comprehensive explanation of why this influence takes place. It is still unknown which of these mechanisms matters more. To address these issues, we propose an integrated model and used meta-analytic structural equation modeling (MASEM) to examine the five major mediating mechanisms through which abusive supervision influences employees’ creativity. We analyzed 493 primary studies that included 531 independent correlations (N = 221,270). The MASEM results reveal that (1) the total effect of abusive supervision on employees’ creativity is negative; (2) five mediating mechanisms (creative self-efficacy, leader-member exchange, knowledge hiding, intrinsic motivation, and psychological empowerment) mediate the influence of abusive supervision on employees’ creativity. Furthermore, the exchange perspective accounted for a greater proportion of abusive supervision’s effect on employees’ creativity than other perspectives. Based on these findings, we discuss implications and directions for future research.

Leader Reputation Strengthens the Unfavorable Effects of Abusive Supervision

OB
Flora Chiang (CEIBS); An-Chih Wang; Thomas A. Birtch (University of Exeter); Yanyu Chen (National Sun Yat-Sen University)
By integrating attribution theory with social information processing, we develop a cognitive framework to examine how leader reputation shapes subordinates’ responses to abusive supervision. We propose that when leaders are perceived as reputable, subordinates are more likely to make internal attributions for the abuse, which in turn negatively affect extra-role performance. Using a sample of 213 retail store employees, we find that leader reputation strengthens the relationship between perceived supervisory abuse and internal attributions. These attributions are subsequently associated with lower levels of coworker- and organization-directed citizenship behaviors. While internal attributions do not directly relate to in-role performance, supplemental analysis reveals that leader reputation moderates the direct effect of perceived abuse on job performance, consistent with our theoretical expectations.

A Qualitative Model of Sensemaking and Identity Work Following Abusive Supervision

OB
Marlee Eden Mercer
Abusive supervision threatens employees’ personal identities and work outcomes. However, individuals differ in how they interpret and respond to such experiences. Drawing on 28 qualitative interviews with employees who experienced abusive supervision, this study examines how differences in cognitive sensemaking gives rise to distinct identity work processes and divergent motivational and future work outcomes. Through inductive analysis, we identify two dimensions shaping responses: how individuals attribute the source of abuse (to a lone supervisor versus broader organizational or societal systems) and the extent to which they feel prepared by prior experiences. These dimensions yield four identity work profiles: Isolated Novices, Isolated Veterans, Societal Issue Novices, and Societal Issue Veterans. Although participants engaged in both restoration- and loss-oriented identity work, the focus and durability of these strategies varied across profiles. By showing that identity work following abusive supervision is patterned rather than idiosyncratic, this study advances theory on identity work, sensemaking, and the downstream consequences of abusive leadership.

Abusive Supervisors Shape Social Class Effects on Challenge Appraisal, Voice and Innovation Behavior

OB
Yun Chung (University of Idaho); Hana Johnson (Washington State University); Fong T. Keng-Highberger (Curtin University - Perth)
Widening social-class disparities create differential access to materials and resources, which fundamentally shape employees’ psychological orientations and behaviors at work. Integrating the social cognitive perspective with the behavioral activation and inhibition systems (BAS/BIS) framework, we examine how social class influences innovative work behavior (IWB) through challenge appraisal and voice for organizational issues and personal development, as well as the moderating role of abusive supervision. Using two preregistered, multi-wave, multi-source studies, we found that abusive supervision significantly moderated the relationship between social class and challenge appraisal. Specifically, higher abusive supervision widened the gap in challenge appraisals between upper-class and lower-class employees, strengthening the positive relationship between social class and challenge appraisal. In contrast, lower abusive supervision reduced class-based differences in challenge appraisal levels. We also found support for the moderated serial mediation, whereby the indirect effects of social class on IWB through challenge appraisal and voice behaviors were contingent on levels of abusive supervision.

The Impact of Harmful Leader Behavior on Hospitality Employees’ Turnover (WITHDRAWN)

OB
Meihong Chen (Xiamen University); Yijing Lyu; Qixing Huang (Xiamen University); XiaoSong Lin (Xiamen University)
In this research, we examine the connection between harmful leader behavior (HLB) and turnover of hospitality employees, including their leave intention and job search behavior (JSB). We propose that job embeddedness acts as a mediator in explaining how HLB affects employee turnover. Additionally, we explore the role of supervisor’s organizational embodiment (SOE) as a moderator, determining when these impacts are most pronounced. To validate our hypotheses, we analyze three-phase data encompassing 242 frontline employees from 27 hotels across China. Results show that harmful leader behavior (HLB) can positively influence frontline employees’ leave intention and job search behavior (JSB) through reducing their job embeddedness. Furthermore, supervisor’s organizational embodiment (SOE) can foster the adverse impact of harmful leader behavior (HLB) on employees’ job embeddedness. Finally, we also consider the implications from theoretical and practical aspects.
