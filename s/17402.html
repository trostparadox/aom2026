The Hidden Cost of Servant Leadership for Leaders

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9

People

Authors: Dahyun Lee Sungkyunkwan University; Jiseon Shin Sungkyunkwan University

Session overview

Servant leadership is widely viewed as beneficial for followers and organizations, yet its psychological costs for leaders remain underexplored. Drawing on the Job Demands–Resources model and Cognitive Appraisal Theory, this study conceptualizes servant leadership as a job demand and examines its effects on leaders’ role overload, work engagement, and turnover intention. Using survey data from 186 organizational leaders, the results show that servant leadership increases leaders’ role overload, which in turn reduces work engagement and increases turnover intention. Positive psychological capital buffered the effect of servant leadership on role overload and attenuated the indirect negative effect on work engagement, whereas follower dependency showed limited moderating effects. These findings highlight that servant leadership can entail psychological costs for leaders and underscore the importance of leader resources in sustaining well-being and motivation.
