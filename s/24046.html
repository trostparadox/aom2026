When the Environment Turns Hostile: The Impact of Local Crime on Firms’ CSR Engagement

SIM
Jingnan Li (London South Bank University); Hongyi Yang (London South Bank University)
This study explores a phenomenon in which firms scale back their CSR engagement. We focus on a negative institutional factor, specifically crime issues in the region where a firm is headquartered. Crime constitutes a pressing social issue that affects business activities and society at large. We argue that local crime discourages firms from contributing to their local communities and engaging with stakeholders, resulting in a negative spillover effect on overall CSR performance. Using panel data from S&P 1500 firms from 2010 to 2021, we find empirical support for this relationship. We also explore conditional factors, including firm performance relative to aspiration, board industry experience, and board nationality diversity. These factors relate to instrumental motives and corporate governance, which shape firms' practices and responses to the institutional environment. We use both fixed-effects and mixed-effects models to test hypotheses. Supplementary analyses show that the negative impact of local crime on CSR is more pronounced in high-CSR performing firms than in low-CSR performing firms. The contributions of our findings are discussed.
