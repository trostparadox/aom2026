Big Five Personality Traits as Predictors of Neurodivergence Disclosure

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Julia King; Priscilla Chaves Florida International University; Tamara Rubiera Florida International University

Session overview

Neurodivergent individuals face challenging decisions regarding workplace disclosure and accommodation requests. Despite increased attention paid to the topic in recent literature, and neurodivergence affecting around 20% of the population, few individuals have disclosed neurodivergence in the working population. Disclosure and accommodation-requesting behaviors facilitate access to support at work, but are associated with high levels of perceived risk, including stigma and potential negative career consequences. We examine whether personality traits predict intent to disclose neurodivergence and intent to request accommodations during the job application process among neurodivergent individuals. Participants were students at Florida International University (n = 430) who completed an abridged measure of personality grounded in the Five Factor Model and responded to two questions: When you apply for your next job, will you request accommodations during the application process? and When you apply for your next job, will you request accommodations… during the application process? Results suggested that personality traits do not predict neurodivergent individuals’ disclosure and accommodation request behaviors, but Conscientiousness and Openness predict unwillingness to disclose or request accommodations in future job application processes, though effects were small.
