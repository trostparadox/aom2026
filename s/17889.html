Insights on Stakeholder Relationships for Enhanced Participatory Project Evaluation and Management

Authors: Žan Pirc School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Metka Tekavcic School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana

Session overview

In complex, multi-stakeholder environments, diverse and dynamic stakeholder perspectives must be considered (De Brucker, Macharis, & Verbeke, 2013). This supports the successful attainment of project goals and affirms the ethical imperative of considering affected stakeholders as “the right thing to do” (de Gooyert, Rouwette, van Kranenburg, & Freeman, 2017). Building on stakeholder theory in the context of project evaluations, we introduce a novel approach to stakeholder analysis that generates participatory insights on mutual relationships among project stakeholders, conceptualized as stakeholder balance. This addresses a gap in participatory evaluation – how to weight stakeholder groups themselves. We propose a nuanced participatory assessment of stakeholder balance, considering stakeholders’ mutual perspectives on their relationships and addressing strategic bias, which remains a challenge in participatory evaluation (Hajkowicz, 2012; Macharis, Turcksin, & Lebeau, 2012). We illustrate how stakeholder balance, by recognizing and revealing relationships and stakeholder dynamics, facilitates project management and enhances participatory evaluation by objectivizing and structuring stakeholder weighting. In particular, we discuss how participatorily derived relative balance weights can complement multi-criteria project evaluation and other participatory evaluation approaches.
