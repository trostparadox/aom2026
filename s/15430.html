Social Media as a Catalyst for Green Entrepreneurship

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4

People

Authors: Laura Mary Ierfino Trent University; Kartik Rao Trent University

Session overview

We examine how audience-driven culture shapes new venture creation. Entrepreneurship research has largely treated entrepreneurs as skilled cultural operators who seek legitimacy for their ideas at the field level, while portraying audiences as relatively passive evaluators. The emergence of social media has however created new arenas where consumers and other audiences can share assumptions, vocabularies and practices; arenas whereby cultural mechanisms are shared in ways that we argue influence entrepreneurial activity. Yet, despite the unprecedented scale and speed of communication on social media, its impact on new market activity has remained underexplored in current literature. We address this gap by examining how social media audience frames influence nascent entrepreneurial activity that contributes to market emergence. Empirically, we study Ontario’s renewable energy market from 2006 to 2023, a sustainability-oriented setting in which entrepreneurial entry is observable through government licensing. To capture audience-driven culture, we qualitatively analyze posts on X (formerly Twitter) and develop quantitative measures of the frames expressed by social media audiences, then test how these frames relate to subsequent licensing activity. We also compile complementary data on Fortune 500 press releases from PR Newswire and traditional news coverage from major Ontario outlets via ProQuest’s Canadian Newsstream to compare the influence of social media framing with signals in corporate communications and traditional media. In doing so, we examine whether framing effects vary across different types of social media stakeholders, such as consumers and environmental advocates, and identify which cultural elements are most conducive to venture creation in an emerging sector.
