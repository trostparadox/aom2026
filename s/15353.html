Sustaining Career Longevity: How Respectful Leadership Shapes Retirement Attitudes in China

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Authors: Zhehua Hu Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University; Muzi Li Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University; Shulin Li Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University

Session overview

The recent shift in China’s statutory retirement age—gradually extending it by three years—represents a significant disruption to late-career trajectories and may be perceived by many employees as a psychological contract breach. For employees who view late-career employment as a burden rather than a source of dignity, the extension is often experienced as a "sentence" rather than an opportunity for career longevity. Drawing on Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) and Social Exchange theories, this study investigates how respectful leadership acts as a critical resource in fostering career sustainability by shifting employee attitudes toward delayed retirement. Using data from 224 full-time employees (aged 40–55) in Chinese state-owned enterprises, the results indicate that respectful leadership is negatively related to retirement eagerness, thereby fostering a more positive orientation toward an extended career path. Furthermore, perceived organizational status was found to strengthen this effect, suggesting that an employee’s social standing within the career hierarchy moderates their willingness to remain in the workforce. The findings suggest that respectful leadership enhances task performance by alleviating the desire for early career exit. These results highlight the essential role of the immediate supervisor in mitigating resistance to retirement policy shifts. Practically, organizations should implement respectful leadership training and career transition programs—such as mentorship and strategic advisory roles—to help aging employees successfully navigate the evolving retirement landscape while maintaining professional engagement.
