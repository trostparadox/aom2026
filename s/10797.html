Beyond the Office: How Work Nostalgia and Activity Participation Shape Retirees' Mental Well-Being

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
HCM

People

Author: Lecheng Chen Shandong University

Session overview

Against global aging and evolving retirement policies, the retirement transition has become a critical life stage, yet how pre-retirement organizational experiences shape post-retirement psychological adaptation remains underexplored. Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, this study aims to test a serial mediation model where pre-retirement affective organizational commitment (AOC) and work centrality (WF)—two core work-related resources— influence post-retirement life satisfaction (SWLS) and depression (GDS) via work nostalgia and mental/physical activity participation Using convenience sampling, we recruited 223 Chinese retirees (65.47% female; mean age=70.18±6.35 years) and adopted hierarchical regression and serial mediation analysis for data analysis. Results showed that 46.19% of retirees reported work nostalgia (M=9.00±4.62) and 27.35% reported retirement loss (M=8.17±4.03), with males having a significantly higher proportion of work nostalgia than females (p=0.038). Pre-retirement AOC (ß=0.228, p<0.05) and WF (ß=0.227, p<0.05) were significant positive predictors of work nostalgia, and their predictive effects mutually weakened each other (ß=-0.083, p<0.05). Additionally, work nostalgia and mental/physical activity participation sequentially mediated the effects of AOC/WF on post-retirement life satisfaction (b=-0.191, p<0.05) and depression (b=-0.093, p<0.01), forming four complete serial mediation paths. This study enriches the literature on retirement psychological adaptation by unpacking the sequential resource dynamics mechanism from a COR theory perspective, extends work nostalgia research by identifying its key work-related antecedents and their interaction, and provides actionable insights for pre-retirement organizational support and post-retirement social policies to promote elderly mental health.
