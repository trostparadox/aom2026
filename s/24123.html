Dynamic Capabilities in Urban Mobility Governance: a Knowledge-Based Theoretical Integration

OMT
Maria do Carmo Rodriguez (Mackenzie Presbyterian University); Silvio Popadiuk (Mackenzie Presbyterian University); GILBERTO PEREZ (Mackenzie Presbyterian University)
Urban mobility governance faces increasing complexity driven by climate pressures, technological disruption, and social inequality. They demand more than infrastructural provision or incremental regulatory adjustment; they require public authorities to continuously learn, coordinate across organizational boundaries, and adapt institutional arrangements under conditions of uncertainty. These dynamics require governments not only to provide infrastructure but also to cultivate adaptive capacities grounded in learning, coordination, and knowledge creation. Although dynamic capabilities (DCs) offer a powerful framework for explaining adaptive performance, their application to public governance remains conceptually fragmented and insufficiently theorized, particularly in complex, multi-actor policy domains such as urban mobility. This study develops an integrative theoretical framework that explains how knowledge-based strategic assets function as micro foundations of dynamic capabilities in public mobility governance. It argues that absorptive capacity, knowledge sharing processes, organizational ambidexterity, and social capital strengthen governmental ability to interpret emerging mobility trends, co-design policy solutions, and reconfigure institutional arrangements through distributed and interorganizational learning processes, in response to evolving societal demands. The proposed framework conceptualizes urban mobility governance as a knowledge-intensive and adaptive ecosystem, in which dynamic capabilities emerge from recursive learning loops that operate across organizational and interorganizational interfaces. By linking these learning mechanisms to policy adaptation, the model advances public management theory by extending the understanding of dynamic capabilities into the governmental domain and by clarifying how learning-centered knowledge assets enable sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring under institutional fragmentation, political constraints, and path dependence. The essay thus contributes a coherent conceptual foundation for future theoretical refinement and empirical examination of learning-centered approaches to urban mobility governance.
