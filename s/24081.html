Performance Feedback Duration and Strategic Change: The Contingencies of Managerial Characteristics

ODC
Shufeng Xiao (Shandong University); Chencheng Qiu (Shandong University); Hongyan May Qu (Middlesex University)
Research on the behavioral theory of the firm has long examined how performance feedback influences strategic change; however, empirical findings regarding the effects of feedback intensity remain inconsistent. We address this issue by shifting attention to the duration of performance feedback and arguing that sustained performance feedback conditions constitute powerful organizational stimuli that guide strategic responses. Using a longitudinal dataset of publicly listed firms, we show that prolonged underperformance increases the likelihood of strategic change, whereas prolonged overperformance reduces it. To illuminate the top management team level mechanism underlying these effects, we integrate upper echelons theory and examine how managerial optimism and managerial ownership power condition firms’ reactions to sustained feedback. Our findings reveal that optimism weakens, while ownership power strengthens, the influence of feedback durations on strategic change. This study advances behavioral theory by demonstrating the temporal and top management team level contingencies through which sustained performance feedback translates into organizational adaptation.
