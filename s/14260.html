Surviving is Not Enough: Explaining Black Women Leaders’ Organizational Commitment

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Catrina Palmer Johnson Kent State University; Kemi Anazodo University of Windsor; Nicole C. Young Franklin & Marshall College; Simona Hite-Womack

Session overview

Black women leaders are marginalized and stigmatized within organizations. Negative experiences may be even more prevalent in the current environment, as organizations have largely dismantled diversity, equity, and inclusion programs, which signaled and provided support. For the Black women who remain with their organizations, we suggest a nuanced understanding to their organizational commitment. We present a typology that highlights four categories of organizational commitment: thriving, persisting, maintaining, and surviving. Each of these categories is explained by considering the level of perceived organizational support along with the level of perceived team support. As Black women leaders’ level of success and productivity shift as they vacillate between these categories of organizational commitment.
