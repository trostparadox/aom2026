Session overview

Sports serve as “living laboratories”, offering novel settings to study management issues beyond traditional organizational and industrial environments. Among the sports examined in management research, paddling has attracted growing attention due to its versatility. Prior research has shown how rowing provides novel insights into organizational puzzles such as the cooperation–competition paradox, performance enablement, institutional plasticity, and organizational sensemaking. Related activities within paddling—such as kayaking, canoeing, stand-up paddleboarding, and rafting—remain comparatively underexplored in management research, yet they may offer valuable insights into group coordination, teamwork, leadership, self-awareness and the mental stamina required for sustained performance. In business schools and academic institutions, deans, scholars, and managers operate in competitive and demanding environments. They are expected to collaborate effectively and perform collectively, much like high-performance athletes striving for success. This PDW is intended for board members of the OMT, IAOM Divisions and the Sports and Management (S&M) Community of the Academy of Management (AOM). We aim to critically examine organizational challenges currently affecting AOM divisions, using paddling as an empirical context. The PDW also seeks to foster connections with scholars in Latin America, an increasingly important region for both the OMT Division and the S&M Community. Drawing on paddling as an experiential lens, this Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to question and critically examine organizational phenomena currently affecting AOM leaders. To enhance the quality of discussions, strengthen personal relationships, and promote overall learning and development, participants will engage in a private learn-to-paddle experience, followed by a structured debriefing session focused on management topics inspired by this activity.
