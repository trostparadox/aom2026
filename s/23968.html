Theorising Involuntary Resources and Advancing a Framework for Consumer-Firm Resource Co-Integration

STR
Salinda Weerasinghe (Queensland University of Technology); Sandeep Salunke (Queensland University of Technology)
Service Dominant logic (S-D logic) positions resource integration, particularly Consumer-Firm Resource Co-integration (CRC), as a central mechanism of value co-creation. Within this paradigm, CRC, defined as the relational interaction in which firms and consumers incorporate each other’s resources, has emerged as a distinct research stream. However, existing work largely confines CRC to voluntary resources, those that are intentionally and knowingly provided, and focuses primarily on its drivers, consequences, and co-integration potential. Contemporary resource literature increasingly reveals the presence of involuntary resources, those that are not consciously offered, yet shape value outcomes. The omission of these resources in prior conceptualizations, combined with the absence of a clear definition and dimensionality of CRC, has constrained its development as a robust construct for advancing theory and practice. To address this gap, we draw on the S-D logic and resource integration literature to theorize involuntary resources and to conceptualize CRC as a unified, dyadic process comprising four steps: resource access, match, connect, and reconcile. We qualitatively validate this conceptualization in the Australian banking sector using document analysis. Our findings show that banks and consumers systematically co-integrate both voluntary and involuntary resources by following these four steps. Overall, we extend S-D logic by explicitly incorporating involuntary resources into resource co-integration and offering a structured conceptualization of CRC with four propositions, thereby enabling a broader understanding of value co-creation beyond voluntary resources. Keywords: Consumer resource, Resource integration, Involuntary resources, Dyad, Value-in-use.
