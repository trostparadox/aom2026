Session overview

Business school classrooms are increasingly diverse across gender, religion, nationality, and socio-economic background. While this diversity enhances learning, it also creates conditions for emotionally charged, polarized, and sometimes disruptive classroom conversations. Faculty are often expected to facilitate discussions on inequality, identity, power, ethics, and global conflict without having received formal preparation in managing disagreement, emotional responses, or positionality in the classroom (Brookfield & Preskill, 2016; Sue et al., 2009). Research on psychological safety and inclusive learning environments suggests that productive learning requires not the absence of conflict, but structured approaches that allow disagreement without harm (Edmondson, 2019; Arao & Clemens, 2013). This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) equips management educators with practical frameworks, facilitation strategies, and classroom design tools to manage challenging in-class conversations while maintaining analytical rigor and inclusive participation. Drawing on insights from management education, social identity theory, and critical pedagogy (Tajfel & Turner, 1986; Hooks, 1994), the PDW emphasizes teaching inquiry skills and decision logics rather than political or moral positions. Through short presentations, guided discussion, and a hands-on design activity, participants develop adaptable discussion structures and assignments suitable for undergraduate, MBA, postgraduate, and executive education contexts. Presenters have extensive experience leading challenging discussions in extremely diverse student groups at a business school with locations on three continents. Participant evaluations are invaluable.
