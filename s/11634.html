Session overview

This symposium advances theory and research on image management in the workplace. Although research on image management has gained traction in the literature, recent qualitative reviews have noted the need for scholarly attention to identifying varied factors that elicit image management processes and to incorporate novel theoretical frames to explain underlying image management processes and behavioral reactions. The papers in this symposium address this research agenda by integrating image management principles with other theoretical explanations (e.g., codeswitching, resource allocation, stress-appraisal) to identify antecedents (i.e., abusive supervision, advice triggers, coworker busyness, coworker busyness talk, social gaffes, traditional professional norms) that influence (2) different image management processes (e.g., authentic/identity considerations, codeswitching motives, image perceptions, inspiration, interpersonal awareness, rumination) and (3) cognitions triggered by image appraisals (problem-solving pondering and affective rumination) that impact (4) outcomes (e.g., advice giving, citizenship, codeswitching, counterproductive work behavior, helping, impression management behaviors, performance), and by (5) highlighting critical moderators that influence the image management process (e.g., competitive work environment).
