Open Strategizing as a Legitimacy Process: Evaluation, Contestation, and Practice

Author: Yinfeng Shen The University of Sydney

Session overview

Open strategy has emerged in response to growing expectations that organizations should be more transparent, participatory, and accountable in how they formulate strategy. While prior research has examined how organizations “open up” strategy and the dilemmas that follow, open strategy is often implicitly treated as a pathway to legitimacy, with legitimacy understood as a beneficial outcome of inclusion and transparency. Less is known about how openness itself becomes evaluated, contested, and rendered legitimate in practice, particularly in pluralistic contexts where multiple audiences apply divergent criteria of appropriateness. This paper reframes open strategizing as a legitimacy process rather than a normative ideal or design choice. Drawing on a qualitative, process-oriented study of open strategizing in a public-sector organization operating under conditions of heightened visibility and evaluative plurality, the study examines how open strategy becomes legitimized and delegitimized over time. The analysis is based on 18 months of participant observation, interviews, and documentary data, enabling close examination of how actors evaluate openness as it unfolds through situated strategizing practices. The early findings identify five interrelated domains through which legitimacy judgments are produced in open strategizing: the legitimacy of participants, strategic topics, the strategizing process, the organization as a responsible actor, and the outputs of strategizing. Together, these domains show how openness extends evaluation beyond strategic outcomes to include who participates, how decisions are made, and whether engagement leads to consequential action. By conceptualizing open strategizing as a legitimacy process, this study contributes to strategy-as-practice research by explaining how openness becomes consequential through ongoing evaluation and contestation rather than assumed acceptance. It also highlights pluralistic contexts as a critical condition shaping contemporary strategizing.
