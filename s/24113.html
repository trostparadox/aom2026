Workplace Cultural Identity Integration Among Host Country Nationals in Multinational Corporations

IM
Hui Young Suh (Sogang University); Jae Youn Chang (Sogang University)
Workplace cultural identity integration (WCII) captures the extent to which host country nationals (HCNs) in multinational corporations (MNCs) perceive their national and corporate cultural identities as compatible and psychologically integrated at work. This study conceptualizes WCII as an organizationally embedded psychological process and examines its antecedents and behavioral consequences among HCNs. Using survey data from 9,072 HCNs across 119 subsidiaries in 55 countries, combined with longitudinal administrative records on employee turnover, psychometric analyses demonstrate that WCII operates as a unidimensional individual-level construct and exhibits a saturated subsidiary-level representation. Multilevel analyses show that WCII is positively associated with perceived organizational prestige and procedural justice at the individual level, as well as expatriate knowledge transfer at the subsidiary level, whereas cultural distance is negatively associated with WCII. Cox proportional hazards models further indicate that higher WCII is associated with a lower hazard of employee turnover over a three-year period, controlling for age, tenure, and job satisfaction, with stronger effects observed during early and later tenure stages. These findings position WCII as a distinct psychological resource linking organizational contexts to employee retention in culturally complex multinational workplaces.
