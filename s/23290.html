Micro-Processes of Coordination in Entrepreneurial Ecosystems: An Emerging Economy Perspective

ENT
Virginio Avila Lema (Universidad Católica Boliciana); Nikolay Anguelov Dentchev (Vrije Universiteit Brussel); Jorge Salas Vargas
Entrepreneurial ecosystems are described as an interdependent group of actors, but little is known about the microprocesses through which these actors coordinate activities in practice, particularly in emerging economies characterized by institutional fragility and limited formal authority. This study examines how coordination is executed at the micro level among ecosystem institutions during the early stages of entrepreneurial ecosystem development. Drawing on an qualitative study of an entrepreneurial ecosystem in Bolivia, based on 55 semi-structured interviews, the analysis adopts relational coordination theory as a sensitizing perspective to examine how communication and relationships shape coordination across organizational boundaries. The findings show that coordination emerges through relational micro-processes that combine shared goals, role alignment, and problem-solving communication, rather than through isolated or standardized mechanisms. In addition to relational coordination practices identified in prior research, the study highlights the empirical findings micro-processes that stabilize collaboration while preserving institutional autonomy. Conceptualizing coordination as a relational and communicational group of microprocesses, this study extends relational coordination theory to entrepreneurial ecosystem contexts and contributes to a further understanding of how collective entrepreneurial action is organized in emerging economies. The findings offer insights for practiciones and policymakers seeking to support early-stage ecosystem coordination under conditions of institutional uncertainty.

Student-Managed Venture Funds as Drivers of Entrepreneurial Ecosystems

ENT
Joanne Jin Zhang (Queen Mary University of London); Jing Zhang (Old Dominion University)
It has become increasingly important to recognise the role of students in co-creating education programmes, as such approaches can enhance learning outcomes while simultaneously fostering ecosystem development in and around universities. This study examines how student-managed venture funds (SMVFs), a novel entrepreneurship education programme, foster entrepreneurial ecosystem development through individual and collective learning. We develop a typology of SMVFs based on the level of autonomy in learning and the level of institutional support, and advance a theoretical proposition that different types of programmes have various impacts on ecosystem development along three dimensions – knowledge, resource provision, and network development. We use four cases to illustrate these dynamics. The study offers insights for higher education institutions (HEIs) on designing engaged and impactful programs, enabling robust ecosystem growth for student entrepreneurs, investors, and alums. In doing so, it responds to calls for action-oriented approaches in entrepreneurship education and provides an innovative pathway for educators and policymakers seeking to foster entrepreneurial ecosystem to drive social change and economic growth.

Uncovering Relational Structures in Underdeveloped Entrepreneurial Ecosystems: Evidence from Morocco

ENT
Aziza Sellah (Cadi Ayyad University); Syeda Nimra Batool (Royal Holloway - University of London); Said Ouhadi (Cadi Ayyad University)
Research in entrepreneurial ecosystems has multiplied in recent years. Yet, ecosystems in emerging economies are not fully explored. To address this gap, this study uses mixed methods to investigate network configuration in an underdeveloped ecosystem in Morocco in the municipality of Marrakesh. First, 250 sociometric questionnaires were administrated through google form for active stakeholders in Marrakesh ecosystem. The obtention of 180 response rates allowed us to perform the social network analysis of the study through Gephi 9.0. Second, we triangulated data by administrating 30 semi structured interviews with heterogonous group of entrepreneurs to reveal hidden ties in the ecosystem.
