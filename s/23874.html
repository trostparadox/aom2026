Managing the Unengaged: Ethical Leadership as a Buffer Against Quiet Quitting Among Machiavellian

OB
Hai Viet Nguyen; Hao-Chieh Lin (National Sun Yat-Sen University); Jia-Bo Guo
This study contributes to the ongoing conversation about how organizations can effectively manage Machiavellian employees. While prior research has shown that leadership can promote citizenship behaviors and reduce unethical intentions among Machiavellians, less is known about how to foster their genuine work engagement. Given that employee engagement is essential for organizational competitiveness, this gap warrants attention. Drawing on Social Information Processing theory and Conservation of Resources theory, we propose that Machiavellian employees are less likely to be engaged at work, which in turn increases their likelihood of quiet quitting. However, we argue that ethical leadership, particularly through transactional approach, can buffer this negative effect by enhancing work engagement and reducing the tendency to quietly withdraw. In doing so, our study offers new insights into how ethical leadership can not only manage Machiavellian behavior but also promote authentic contribution in the workplace.
