Session overview

Team research remains central to understanding modern organizations, yet early-career scholars face significant hurdles when entering this extensive and methodologically demanding field. Vast bodies of research on team diversity, team processes, and team dynamics create the perception that “there are countless possibilities yet no clear path forward,” making it difficult for early-career researchers to identify meaningful theoretical contributions. At the same time, high methodological expectations, including sophisticated study designs, extensive data collection, and multi-method approaches, often exceed the resources available to PhD students and junior faculty. These challenges are compounded by the fact that many critical methodological decisions in team research, such as how individual- level data should be aggregated to represent team-level constructs, are fundamentally theoretical rather than purely technical, yet are rarely taught explicitly. Further complicating this landscape, rapidly evolving tools, such as AI-assisted behavioral coding, confederate bots, and digital trace data, require constant technical upskilling. Ultimately, although preregistration and open-science initiatives enhance research integrity, they also impose additional procedural demands. This PDW aims to provide guidance for early-career team researchers to navigate these challenges by providing strategies for framing novel contributions in established domains, designing rigorous yet feasible studies, and prioritizing skill development in an era of rapid methodological innovation. Participants will leave with actionable guidance to enhance the rigor, creativity, and viability of their team research.
