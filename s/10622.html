Session overview

Strategy-as-Practice (SAP) research has generated powerful insights into how strategy is accomplished through the everyday actions, interactions, and tools of practitioners. Yet many instructors—whether doctoral students, early-career scholars, or experienced faculty—struggle to translate practice-based ideas into engaging classroom experiences that go beyond traditional lectures or case studies. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) responds to this need by showcasing award-winning pedagogical innovations from the SAP Teaching Innovation Competition and offering participants hands-on guidance for designing their own practice-based teaching activities. The PDW opens with dynamic “Innovation Spotlights,” where winners and finalists present high-impact teaching approaches such as strategy-practice simulations, sociomaterial exercises, micro-ethnographic activities, field-based assignments, visual and artefact-based methods, and routines-focused classroom interventions. These demonstrations highlight how practice-based concepts can be taught experientially across levels—from undergraduate to MBA and executive classrooms—and how instructors can adapt these innovations to different institutional and disciplinary contexts. Building on this inspiration, participants engage in guided design roundtables where they identify a practice-based concept they wish to teach, articulate a teaching challenge they face, and develop an early prototype of a teaching innovation using structured templates. Facilitators provide targeted feedback, helping participants integrate practice-based organization and management theory, learning objectives, and practical design considerations. The session concludes with structured peer coaching and collective takeaways, enabling participants to leave with actionable ideas, concrete pedagogical tools, and a clearer sense of how to craft practice-based learning experiences. Overall, this PDW empowers educators to bring practice-based organization and management theory to life in the classroom and strengthens the community of scholars committed to high-quality, practice-oriented strategy teaching.
