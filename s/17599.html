Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative technology reshaping work and organizations (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021; Lebovitz et al., 2022). While algorithms offer meaningful efficiency gains, improved decision-making, and competitive advantage (Krakowski et al., 2023), they also introduce new forms of complexity for managers by fundamentally reshaping managerial work (Jarrahi et al., 2023). We conceptualize managers as organizational actors who link macro-level organizational goals and societal/regulatory requirements to micro-level work practices. Considering the role of managers in translating strategies into concrete decisions about task allocation, coordination, control, and people management, as well as outcomes, we focus on managers who are responsible for making AI workable and useful. The complexity of managerial work in AI-enabled organizations arises not only from the opacity of algorithms (Lebovitz et al., 2022) but also from the need to navigate shifting role boundaries, negotiate authority between humans and algorithms, and address employees’ reactions to AI-mediated control and evaluation (Castrillon, 2025). This professional development workshop (PDW) contributes to the discourse by highlighting these tensions and underscoring the need for stronger conceptual frameworks and actionable tools to support managers. Integrating perspectives from technology, management, and organizational behavior, we explore how managers can effectively fulfill their roles in contexts where learning algorithms, e.g., generative and agentic AI, increasingly influence their core functions. The PDW combines expert presentations synthesizing emerging research with interactive, scenario-based group discussions. Participants will leave with actionable frameworks for addressing AI integration challenges, strategies for fostering effective human–AI collaboration, and a strengthened scholarly network. This interactive PDW is situated at the intersection of management and technology domains. Researchers and practitioners from different divisions and interest groups, particularly from Organizational Behaviour (OB), are welcomed to participate.
