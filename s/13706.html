Session overview

Contemporary cooperative systems among organizations and individuals increasingly operate through networks of interdependent relationships, rendering them highly productive yet acutely vulnerable to cascading failures. Decades of network research have conceptualized network change as gradual and incremental, focused largely on microdynamics of tie formation and dissolution, and with an interest in private advantage accruing to individuals or organizations. Yet increasingly frequent shocks, such as pandemics, wars, climate disruptions, and political sanctions, reveal a new network reality characterized by punctuated, system-wide change with implications for collective outcomes. This panel symposium applies a robustness–resilience perspective on networked cooperative systems to address this gap. We define robustness as a whole network’s capacity to sustain collective outcomes immediately following a shock, and resilience as the network’s capacity to recover or surpass those outcomes over time. Bringing together insights from interorganizational and interpersonal networks, the panel examines how shock properties, whole-network topologies, and individual actor responses jointly shape collective outcomes. Panelists will explore theoretical tensions, methodological implications, and practical design challenges associated with networks that are not only efficient in stable times, but capable of withstanding and recovering from disruptions.
