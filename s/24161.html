Digital Emotional Contagion: Evidence from Twitter Sentiment Transmission Patterns

CTO
Lin Wang (Communication University of China); Jiarui Li (Communication University of China); Ziyi Zhang (Communication University of China); Zhiyong Li (Communication University of China); Ying Yue (Communication University of China); Donghan Wang (Communication University of China)
Social media platforms have transformed emotional transmission processes, yet existing research focuses on group-level phenomena while neglecting micro-level mechanisms governing individual tweet-pair interactions. This study examines emotional continuity within Twitter retweet pairs, investigating how original tweet sentiment and intensity influence subsequent retweet expressions. Drawing on emotional contagion theory, we test hypotheses examining emotional continuity effects, intensity changes, and moderating roles of user characteristics and content types. Using hierarchical regression analysis on tweet-pair data focusing on positive-to-positive and negative-to-negative transmission pathways, we find strong emotional continuity with positive emotions showing strongest persistence. Contrary to expectations, retweet intensity is significantly lower than original tweets, revealing "intensity decay" rather than amplification. User-level moderators operate differentially: network centrality, account type (bot vs. human), and mutual following relationships all significantly moderate emotional continuity. Content type (political vs. non-political) also exhibits significant moderating effects on emotional transmission patterns. These findings advance emotional contagion theory by identifying boundary conditions in digital environments and challenge assumptions about emotional amplification in social media. Results provide evidence-based strategies for emotional ecosystem governance and affective polarization mitigation.
