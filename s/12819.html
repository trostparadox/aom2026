Session overview

Leadership and management education increasingly turn to mindfulness, spirituality, and ethics to respond to accelerating social, ecological, and technological crises. Yet dominant approaches often abstract mindfulness from intersectional power relations, ecological embeddedness, and epistemic conditions shaped by inequality and algorithmic mediation. As a result, mindfulness is frequently framed as an individual self-regulation technique rather than a justice-oriented epistemic capacity that sustains ethical judgment promoting peace, dignity, and well-being. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) advances an integrated framework connecting intersectional mindfulness, expansive leadership identity, ecological sustainability, and epistemic resilience. Drawing on four interrelated research streams: (1) expansive leadership and socio-ecological identity development, (2) holistic approaches to mindfulness emphasizing culture, power, and lived experience, (3) mindfulness as a catalyst for sustained pro-environmental behavior, and (4) epistemic drift and epistemic resilience in algorithmically mediated organizations—this workshop reframes mindfulness as epistemic infrastructure. Leadership identity is conceptualized as constituted through intersecting social locations (race, caste, gender, class, migration), ecological embeddedness, historical accountability, and vulnerability to epistemic erosion under AI-mediated decision environments. Mindfulness functions not as coping alone, but as a capacity that preserves reflective judgment, interpretive plurality, and ethical responsibility in contexts marked by uncertainty, automation bias, and moral overload. Designed as a 90-minute research- and pedagogy-centered PDW, the workshop supports participants in developing justice-, sustainability-, and epistemic-resilience–oriented research agendas and pedagogical designs. Participants refine research questions, theoretical positioning, and methodological approaches for studying spiritually grounded leadership development, while engaging pedagogies that cultivate ecological awareness, ethical agency, and durable judgment in the face of climate anxiety and algorithmic authority. The PDW aligns closely with the Management, Spirituality, and Religion (MSR) division’s commitment to spirituality as lived practice, leadership as moral responsibility, and management scholarship as a vehicle for social, ecological, and epistemic justice.
