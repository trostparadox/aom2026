Gendered Prescribing Responses to Medicare Part D Reforms

Authors: Pooria Assadi California State University-Sacramento; Shinjae Won Ewha Womans University

Session overview

Healthcare policies often assume uniform provider responses, yet professionals differ systematically in how they interpret and enact policy incentives. We examine how provider gender shapes prescribing behavior under two major U.S. policy shifts: the Affordable Care Act (ACA), which expanded prescription drug access, and the Comprehensive Addiction and Recovery Act (CARA), which imposed constraints to curb opioid misuse. Drawing on institutional theory and social role theory, we argue that female providers, socialized toward communal, risk-averse, and guideline-adherent behaviors, navigate policy-induced abundance and constraint more consistently than male providers. Using Medicare Part D data from 2013 to 2022 covering approximately 630,000 providers, we test gender differences in generic prescribing, opioid use, antipsychotic costs, and antibiotic costs. Difference-in-differences models reveal that female providers increase generic prescribing more than male providers following ACA implementation and reduce antibiotic costs more after CARA, particularly in solo practices. Evidence for reduced opioid prescribing among female providers is present in solo settings, while no gender differences emerge in antipsychotic costs. Our findings advance institutional and gender theory by demonstrating how gendered micro-level prescribing practices condition the effects of healthcare policy and contribute to responsible prescribing amid regulatory change.
