Session overview

Markets increasingly allocate attention and resources based not only on what producers do, but on where they sit in a relational landscape of comparison. Yet “being different” is not a unitary strategy: market position can be defined as similarity or dissimilarity to particular others (e.g., exemplars, acquirers, or one’s prior self) and as typicality or distinctiveness relative to category prototypes. This symposium brings these two vocabularies into direct dialogue and asks when they align, when they conflict, and how they are activated by the evaluative architecture of markets. Across four papers spanning awards, entrepreneurial search, platform narratives, and acquisitions, presenters show how positional advantages depend on evaluators’ goals, available reference points, and the institutional infrastructure that makes comparison legible. Together, the papers illuminate how intermediaries, labels, and audience relationships reshape the meaning of fit and difference, with implications for who is rewarded, who is discounted, and how competitive landscapes evolve.
