Session overview

What does it look like to organize in ways that are deeply human, and to create value grounded in human-centered principles? This PDW aims to bring together scholars with interests in a human-centered view on management and entrepreneurship, a perspective that takes a holistic, empathetic approach to understanding people, and views individuals as complex and holding idiosyncratic values and having multiple overlapping concerns and challenges. Whereas most theories of value creation operate at higher levels of theoretical abstraction (e.g., firms or ecosystems), we focus on the role of people who have unique concerns and perspectives. While this human-centered approach to entrepreneurial problem solving is one that educators intuitively understand and try to teach, taking a human-centered perspective is just as critical to research on value creation and organizing as it is to entrepreneurship and management practice. We thus call for a more dedicated conversation about infusing a human-centered approach into our research, teaching, and service, focusing on processes of value creation—to more effectively address the complex issues we face in pluralistic societies. This 90-minute PDW carries forward the theme that seeking to understand nuances of a person’s behavior rather than abstracting it away (as in economic agents or sociological actors) can help organizations create (novel) value (Christensen, Hall, Dillon and Duncan 2016). In other words, we seek to focus on the ways in which research on, and practice of entrepreneurial value creation can benefit from an even deeper focus on people with their lifeworld (see e.g., Dell’Era & Landoni, 2014; IDEO, 2025; Town et al., 2024). In this session, we will discuss how we can better implement a human-centered approach to writing, teaching, and consulting.
