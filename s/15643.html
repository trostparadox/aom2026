Session overview

While network research has extensively documented how structural configurations shape individual outcomes, understanding network dynamics also requires examining the psychological mechanisms that drive how individuals build, maintain, and leverage social connections. This symposium brings together four papers that advance a psychologically grounded view of network evolution by illuminating micro-processes governing network change—from tie initiation to maintenance, from ego-driven choices to alter-imposed constraints, and from instrumental to relational brokerage. Across longitudinal field studies, experiments, and a quasi-random network intervention, the papers show how affective threat construal, gendered relational behavior, and personality-driven negotiated agency shape network structure and organizational outcomes.
