Organizational Control Responsibilization in a Self-Managing Organization

CMS
Valentina Voimavuo (Aalto University School of Business)
The persistence of normative control in organizations, despite its widespread critique, remains a topic of interest and concern. Building on the seminal work by Kunda, which highlighted the potential overt authoritarian nature of corporate culture management, this paper examines current manifestations of organizational control and its responsibilization to employees. This study argues that the current form of normative control is both more intense and subtle, making it increasingly difficult for employees to resist or challenge. The paper posits that the responsibilization of control to individual employees blends the roles of controller and controlee within one person, inducing internal conflict and stress. Furthermore, employees face the dual expectations of self-control and self-promotion, demanding not only the execution and supervision of tasks but also the portrayal of competence and accomplishment. The study conducts a critical discourse analysis of culture books of a self-managing organization.
