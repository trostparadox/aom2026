Session overview

Political forces create novel challenges for organizations and entrepreneurs. In new institutional environments underpinned by political uncertainty, organizational strategies, legitimacy, and even survival become contested. Responding to the recent call for a “political turn” in management scholarship, this symposium highlights four papers that seek to understand how political power permeates organizations across different contexts. The studies show firms and entrepreneurs as political actors who must navigate political environments from failed to formalized to contest systems of domination in different ways. Our symposium, in particular, bridges research on corporate political activity, institutional theory, and entrepreneurship in illiberal and transitional settings. The symposium concludes with a discussion of the papers and outlines directions for future research on the interrelations of politics and organizing.
