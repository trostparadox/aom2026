Session overview

Human Resource Management (HRM) education faces diverse challenges across global contexts, from foundational principles to advanced, specialized courses such as International HRM, Training & Development, Compensation, Diversity & Inclusion, Labor Relations, and Workplace Safety. This symposium brings together scholars from several countries to explore how cultural, institutional, and technological factors shape both teaching and learning in HRM. Our discussion will highlight key issues such as limited access to non-U.S./W.E.I.R.D. (White, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) course materials, the professionalization of HR as a strategic function, and the role of certification standards in shaping curricula. While Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming HR practice and pedagogy, we aim to broaden the conversation beyond AI to include innovative approaches for engaging students, integrating global perspectives, and addressing resource constraints. By sharing experiences and best practices from different regions, this symposium seeks to foster dialogue on how educators can prepare future HR professionals for an increasingly complex and interconnected world. Attendees will gain insights into comparative teaching strategies, emerging trends, and collaborative opportunities for advancing HR education globally.
