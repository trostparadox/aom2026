Temporal Contingency in the Market Value of Board Gender Diversity

STR
Xiwen Zhao (University of Wisconsin-Whitewater); Daniel S. Halgin (University of Kentucky); Stephen P. Borgatti (University of Kentucky)
Despite extensive research, the relationship between board gender diversity and firm value remains contentious, with studies reporting contradictory positive, negative, and null effects. We propose that this inconsistency exists because the market valuation of diversity is not a fixed economic parameter, but an institutionally contingent evaluation. We argue that U.S. Presidents act as “macro-sensegivers” who activate competing institutional logics, signaling whether diversity should be viewed as a governance asset or an agency cost. Using panel data from 8,078 U.S. public firms (1989–2020), we find that investors systematically alter their valuation schemas based on presidential leadership: the “diversity premium” rises significantly over the tenure of Democratic presidents and turns into a “diversity discount” over the tenure of Republican presidents. These fluctuations are economically significant, generating swings in market capitalization independent of firm operational performance (ROA). We isolate presidential influence from broader societal trends by showing that these valuation effects are concentrated in states with “aligned trifectas” (where state government aligns with the White House) and are driven by tangible signals such as Cabinet appointments. We conclude that the economic value of corporate diversity is not structural, but is a politically constructed phenomenon that varies with the institutional environment.

Gender Penalty or Bonus? The Dual Effects of Board Gender Diversity On Analyst Recommendations

STR
Stephen Joel Smulowitz (Wake Forest University); Gosia Smulowitz (Wake Forest University)
We examine how board gender diversity creates both a penalty and a bonus with investors. Specifically, we predict that underperforming firms with more women on boards will receive harsher analyst recommendations (i.e., the “gender penalty”). This is because underperformance activates existing gender stereotypes, leading analysts to attribute poor results to perceived deficiencies in women directors’ leadership capabilities. Conversely, we predict that firms with higher R&D spending and more women directors receive improved analyst recommendations (i.e., the “gender bonus”). This occurs because analysts view women directors as providing better oversight of innovation investments, reducing concerns about wasteful spending. Using longitudinal data of 22,407 observations for 2,169 S&P 1500 companies for the years 1998-2022, we provide robust empirical support for our hypotheses. Our paper contributes new theory on conditional gender effects and provides practical guidance for boards navigating investor perceptions of gender diversity initiatives.

Employee Response to Mandatory Female Board Representation: Evidence from California Senate Bill 826

STR
Boshuo Li (University of Pittsburgh); Mark (Shuai) Ma (University of Pittsburgh); Victor Xiaoqi Wang (California State University-Long Beach); Betty Bin Xing (Baylor University)
Despite the heated debate over female board representation, little is known about how employees respond to mandated increases in female directors. We investigate this question in the context of California Senate Bill 826 (the “Women on Boards” legislation). Using a question-driven, abductive approach, we find a significant decline in employee satisfaction after a mandatory increase in female board representation. Post hoc analyses suggest that this effect likely reflects employees’ gender biases toward female directors rather than the qualifications of those appointed under the mandate. We further show that this effect is unique to mandatory gender quotas, as opposed to voluntary appointments of female directors, underscoring the distinctive consequences of regulatory interventions in board composition.

Seats of Change? Female Board Directors and Firm Performance in Pakistan- An Event Study Approach

STR
Atiyab Sultan
This paper explores the impact of female corporate board directors on firm performance and culture in Pakistan. Using data from 2012-23 on listed and unlisted firms in the country, we use an event study approach to assess the causal impact of the first entry of a female director on firm performance, exploring heterogenous effects on family and non-family firms. We extend this firm-level analysis using a director-level dataset that allows us to chart the specific characteristics and contributions of directors on different types of boards. We supplement this quantitative evidence with detailed interviews of 20 board directors as well as a larger survey of board directors. Our results show that the first introduction of a female director on a board has a positive and significant effect on firm assets, particularly in the case of family firms. Meanwhile non-family firms experience significant, positive effects on firm profitability, as well as on returns on assets and equity, effects that persist and accentuate in the years following the inclusion of a female director. Family firms, however, do not experience any significant effect on profitability. We argue that the inclusion of female board members in family firms often entails the appointment of passive directors that help consolidate firm ownership and control, manifested in growth in assets, but do not otherwise improve firm governance. In non-family firms, female directors are usually appointed based on their competence and expertise, leading to improvements in firm-level decision-making, leading to better performance outcomes across a range of measures.

Digital Orientation as a Strategic Posture: Gender Diversity and Performance in Innovative SMEs

STR
Ludovica Antenozio (Sant' Anna School of Advanced Studies of Pisa); Hussain Muhammad (American University of Ras Al Khaimah); DANIELA DI BERARDINO (Gabriele d'Annunzio University); Augusta Consorti (University G. D'Annunzio Chieti-Pescara)
Digital orientation has become a central strategic posture for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), particularly innovative ones. Despite growing evidence of the benefits of digital orientation, less is known about the governance conditions under which these benefits occur. This study investigates the relationship between digital orientation and financial performance, focusing on how board gender diversity shapes this association. Using longitudinal data from 250 Italian innovative SMEs over ten-year period and employing multiple model specifications, we find that digital orientation is positively associated with financial performance. However, the presence of women on the board introduces additional complexity, as board gender diversity shapes whether digital orientation translates into amplified gains or more muted outcomes, depending on firm-specific and environmental contingencies. By integrating digital strategy and governance, this study contributes to both academic research and managers, underscoring the importance of boards as active strategic actors in influence the performance implications of digital orientation in innovative SMEs.
