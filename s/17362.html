Resisting the Algorithm: Defining and Explaining AI Resistance in Entrepreneurship

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6
ENT

People

Authors: Naryoung Yu; Ting Li University of Western Ontario

Session overview

Despite widespread claims of artificial intelligence (AI) adoption, many entrepreneurs continue to resist using AI. Prior research has examined AI adoption and implementation challenges largely through organizational investment or project-based lenses, offering limited insight into how entrepreneurs engage with AI under conditions of extreme uncertainty, rapid pivots, and resource scarcity. As a result, the cognitive and strategic dimensions of entrepreneurial AI resistance remain underexplored. This work-in-progress addresses this gap through a structured review of the emerging literature on AI non-realization, asking: Why do entrepreneurs resist the use of AI during new venture development? Synthesizing insights across entrepreneurship, information systems, and AI governance research, we examine recurring patterns of intentional non-integration, delay, and selective engagement that constrain AI value realization in entrepreneurial contexts. Rather than uncritically adopting AI as a business component, entrepreneurs often limit or postpone substantive integration when anticipated value does not align with venture-specific capacities and developmental conditions. We define AI resistance as entrepreneurs’ intentional and strategic withholding, delaying, or limiting of substantive AI integration when AI is perceived to constrain professional discretion, blur accountability, compromise ethical identity or performance authenticity, or misalign with the venture’s digital capabilities and stage of development. By theorizing resistance as a strategic timing decision rather than a passive or irrational response, this review advances understanding of uneven AI adoption in entrepreneurship.
