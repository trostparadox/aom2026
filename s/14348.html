Session overview

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is becoming an important force in how entrepreneurial ventures search for opportunities, experiment with ideas, and build competitive advantage. By lowering the cost of knowledge work and enabling large-scale digital simulation, these technologies challenge long-standing assumptions about experimentation, learning, and differentiation in entrepreneurship and strategy research. This symposium examines how GenAI reconfigures core entrepreneurial processes, rather than treating it as a generic productivity tool. Drawing on complementary perspectives, the session explores how AI reshapes entrepreneurial entry and organizational form. It considers how digital learning alters the role of real-world experimentation and how standardized tools complicate startups’ efforts to differentiate. The symposium also examines how tasks are redistributed between humans and AI in creative, customer-facing work. Taken together, the papers show that AI can both enable and constrain entrepreneurial advantage. They offer a process-based account of when AI supports, substitutes for, or reshapes foundational entrepreneurial activities.
