Exploring How and When Workaholic Leadership Facilitates Newcomers’ Organizational Socialization

OB
Hui Deng (Beijing Forestry University); Jinmei Wu (Beijing Forestry University); Xian Cheng (Beijing Forestry University)
To explore the mechanism through which workaholic leadership affects newcomers’ organizational socialization outcomes, this study draws upon social learning theory to develop a moderated mediation model, with newcomer work engagement as the mediator and leader identification as the moderator. A survey of 375 newcomers from a clothing and textile organization in Shandong Province revealed that workaholic leadership was positively related to newcomers’ task mastery, role clarity, and social integration through enhancing work engagement. Furthermore, the results showed that leader identification not only amplified the positive effect of workaholic leadership on newcomer work engagement but also strengthened the positive indirect effect of workaholic leadership on newcomers’ organizational socialization outcomes via work engagement. These findings extend research on workaholic leadership and organizational socialization, providing valuable insights for managers to acknowledge and manage workaholic leadership styles, facilitate newcomers’ integration into the organization, and enhance overall organizational effectiveness.
