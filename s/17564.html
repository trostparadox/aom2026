Blockchain to Improve Prototype Procurement in Automotive Projects: A Transaction Cost Perspective

Authors: Manuel Sobrino Valladolid University; Natalia Martin-Cruz Valladolid University; Joaquin Adiego Valladolid University; Javier Pajares Valladolid University

Session overview

Digital technologies are increasingly reshaping how organizations coordinate complex, time-critical work across organizational and geographical boundaries. In the automotive industry, vehicle development projects rely heavily on the rapid procurement of customized prototypes, a process characterized by uncertainty, contractual incompleteness, and intense communication among multiple organizational units and external suppliers. Despite operating within large, vertically integrated firms, these procurement processes often generate substantial internal coordination and transaction costs. This study examines how blockchain technology functions as a digital governance and communication infrastructure in prototype procurement within automotive development projects. Drawing on Transaction Cost Economics, we analyze a real prototype request case and compare transaction costs under a traditional coordination model versus a blockchain-enabled scenario supported by smart contracts. Using detailed task-level time estimations and role-based cost rates across multiple countries, the study directly measures transaction costs rather than inferring them indirectly. Results indicate that the blockchain-enabled process reduces transaction costs by more than 50%, primarily by automating coordination, reducing communication frictions, and limiting ex post adjustments. By conceptualizing blockchain not only as a technical tool but as a sociotechnical mechanism that reshapes organizing and communication practices, this research contributes to the CTO literature on digital technologies and organizational coordination. The findings highlight how digital infrastructures can transform governance in project-based, distributed work settings.
