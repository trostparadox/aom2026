When Does Innovation Drive Small Firms’ Performance Growth? Evidence From AI Deep Neural FE-SEM

ENT
Jiabin Zhang (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
How small firms’ performance growth mechanisms differ from those of large firms is a central question in entrepreneurship and strategic management research. Based on the resource-based view (RBV), prior research shows that small firms’ flexibility supports innovation-driven growth, while large firms’ bureaucracy often limits it. Meanwhile, small firms usually lack internal financial resources, making external financing beneficial for innovation. However, this raises a fundamental but underexplored question: is external financing a necessary boundary condition for innovation-driven growth in small firms? In other words, can small firms rely on innovation to grow without external financing? We extend RBV by framing external financing as a trigger of resource reconfiguration in small firms and a necessary boundary condition for their innovation-driven performance growth. Without such financing, small firms cannot outperform large firms in innovation. We test our theory using unbalanced panel data from World Bank Enterprise Surveys, covering 30 countries and more than 46,000 firm-year observations from 2008 to 2019. We employ an AI-driven Deep Neural Fixed Effect Structural Equation Modeling (Deep Neural FE-SEM) to capture nonlinear relationships while maintaining causal inference. Classical fixed-effects regressions further confirm the robustness and stronger explanatory power of this model. This study contributes to RBV by identifying external financing as a necessary condition for resource reconfiguration and highlights its importance for managers and policymakers in supporting small-firm innovation and performance growth. Meanwhile, this study introduces Deep Neural FE-SEM to management research, showing its stronger explanatory power than classical regressions and encouraging future AI-based studies.
