Session overview

Since 2020, this ongoing Professional Development Workshop (PDW) series has explored singular models of consciousness to help individuals decipher their own singularity scenarios (Mathieu & Monneyron, 2015; Gautier & Voynnet-Fourboul, 2019). In 2021, we modeled the leadership of a world-renowned entrepreneur. In 2022, we explored Buddhist consciousness as a foundation for wise leadership. In 2024, we examined the spiritual dimension of singularity in the face of discrimination, using Nelson Mandela as an ethical and political archetype. In 2025, Gandhi’s model served to address contexts of injustice and denied dignity—where individuals are rendered invisible, silenced, or deprived of recognition. This year, we focus on a central leadership challenge: ethical decision-making under emotional and moral pressure. Through the lens of the Bhagavad Gita (Radhakrishnan, 1948), this PDW examines the inner conflict of Arjuna—a leader torn between competing duties—who engages in a transformative dialogue with Krishna. We explore how this spiritual encounter offers a model of decision-making grounded in responsibility, discernment, and inner freedom (Dane & Pratt, 2007). Participants will engage in a simulated leadership dilemma inspired by this narrative. We argue that responsible leadership in today’s volatile and ambiguous environment requires more than rational analysis —it calls for virtue ethics, introspective practice, and spiritual clarity (Sonenshein, 2007). This PDW connects Eastern wisdom traditions with contemporary organizational dilemmas. By linking inner work with outer action, this session contributes to the MSR community’s commitment to exploring the spiritual foundations of moral leadership, and to cultivating leaders capable of holding tension, uncertainty, and transcendence in their decision-making processes. This PDW is especially relevant for consultants, executive coaches, educators, and senior managers engaged in deep leadership development and ethical decision-making in high-stakes environments.
