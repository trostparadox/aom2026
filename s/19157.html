Session overview

Digital transformation is a defining theme in healthcare management, sparking intense interest in how organizations adopt new technologies, reorganize work, and pursue evolving notions of performance. Across systems and settings, health professionals, managers, and policymakers are confronting a world in which digital infrastructures, data flows, and algorithmic tools increasingly shape what healthcare organizations do—and what they are expected to achieve. Yet despite growing attention to digital innovation, the deeper question of how digitalization reshapes performance logics and performance work remains understudied. This is surprising, given that performance is not simply an outcome of digital transformation, but one of its primary drivers and consequences. In this PDW, we aim to develop a research agenda around the intersection of digital health and healthcare performance, providing interesting insights in different fields as innovation, management and entrepreneurship (Glover et al., 2024; Shane & Venkataraman, 2000). Rather than treating digital technologies as external tools layered onto existing systems, we take seriously their capacity to redefine professional roles, redistribute responsibilities, and transform the very criteria through which healthcare organizations evaluate success, quality, and value. Bringing together scholars with shared interests in digital transformation, performance management, and organizational change, the workshop seeks to illuminate both the opportunities and tensions introduced by digitally mediated performance regimes. Participants will gain a deeper understanding of how to formulate compelling research questions at this intersection, how to navigate empirical and methodological challenges inherent in studying digital ecosystems, and how to enhance collaboration with practitioners facing similar complexities on the ground.
