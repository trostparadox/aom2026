Session overview

We will introduce a trust-driven approach to designing and managing operations and supply chains that enhance performance, collaboration, and resilience. Traditional operational and procurement systems are largely built to mitigate risk through compliance and control. Not necessarily effective for error prevention, these systems often slow decision-making, strain supplier relationships, and inhibit cross-functional coordination. This workshop reframes trust as a critical operational capability, and one that can be intentionally designed into processes, governance structures, and communication practices. Drawing on research-informed frameworks and applied organizational experience (Mayer, Davis, & Schoorman, 1995; Zaheer, McEvily, & Perrone, 1998; Poppo & Zenger, 2002; Villena, Revilla, & Choi, 2011), the workshop explores how trust functions as an enabling mechanism across procurement, supplier onboarding, internal operations, and leadership decision-making. Participants will examine how trust-based design shifts compliance from a policing function to a shared commitment, enabling faster coordination, improved supplier performance, and greater adaptability in uncertain environments. The session bridges operations and supply chain research with management practice by translating trust from an abstract construct into concrete design principles, routines, and leadership behaviors. The workshop is designed for scholars interested in emerging research questions at the intersection of trust, operations, and supply chains, as well as practitioners seeking actionable approaches to redesign systems where trust becomes the operating logic that drives sustainable performance. Through interactive exercises, facilitated discussion, and applied reflection, participants will examine how trust affects efficiency, risk management, innovation, safety, revenues and sustainability outcomes in operations and supply chains.
