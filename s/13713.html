Market Actions and Competitive Spillovers: Walmart’s Wage Initiatives

Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6

Authors: Paul C Godfrey Brigham Young University; Joshua Powell Brigham Young University

Session overview

In recent decades, firms have increasingly made public commitments that extend beyond traditional price and output competition, addressing issues such as environmental sustainability, labor standards, and employee well-being. These commitments are often framed as expressions of corporate responsibility, yet they are also highly visible strategic actions that can reshape stakeholder expectations and competitive conditions. When made by dominant firms, such actions may reverberate well beyond the boundaries of the initiating organization, influencing how rivals position themselves and how entire industries evolve. Walmart provides a particularly salient setting for examining these dynamics. In 2005, the company announced a sweeping sustainability initiative that positioned it as a leader in environmental stewardship within discount retail. A decade later, Walmart publicly committed to a series of wage increases that were framed as long-term investments in employee well-being and workforce stability. These announcements were not quiet internal adjustments. They were widely publicized, explicitly justified, and directed toward a broad audience that included employees, investors, advocacy groups, policymakers, and consumers. As such, they functioned as strategic signals whose implications extended beyond Walmart’s own operations.
