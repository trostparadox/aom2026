Refashioning Urban Areas: Multi-Stakeholder Integration for Circular Fashion Marketing

MC
FAHIMEH KHATAMI (Bocconi University); Francesca Romana Rinaldi; Enrico Cagno (Politecnico di Milano)
Refashioning urban areas has become a growing topic of interest in academic research, particularly in the fields of management and sustainability. Urban areas are experiencing significant changes due to rapid consumption and innovation, leading to urgent demands for sustainable transformation. All these processes can be realized with collaboration among stakeholders, including organizations, institutions, and citizens in the fashion and design sectors. However, the precise roles, responsibilities, and impacts of stakeholders remain unclear. This paper aims to explore the potential of participatory action research as a transformative methodology for co-developing circular fashion marketing in urban areas. Current research identified the enabling conditions, systemic barriers, and actionable strategies for the fashion market to embed circular principles into fashion urban development, using a qualitative dataset from expert interviews in Italy. Findings indicated a positive trend among organizations, institutions, and citizens in embracing circularity, with particular emphasis on the integrative role of knowledge institutions in facilitating systemic change. These insights can offer significant implications for rethinking stakeholder roles and enhancing participatory frameworks in the urban circular economy. We proposed practical recommendations to inform multi-stakeholder governance, entrepreneurship, and innovation strategies for the fashion market, while outlining future research directions for sustaining circular fashion ecosystems.
