Session overview

The rapid diffusion of AI-enabled research tools is reshaping how scholars frame, articulate, and disseminate their research. This covers stages from idea generation, through data analysis, to theory development. These changes spark concerns among editors, reviewers, and even research funding agencies about the originality, rigor, and individual contribution of the scholar vs. the machine. As AI can help with speed, quality, and productivity of research activities, these developments surface not only methodological challenges but also deeper conceptual and normative tensions that cut across disciplines, often addressed in fragmented and isolated ways. This PDW calls scholars from diverse fields to engage in a focused, intellectually open dialogue on how originality, rigor, and scholarly contribution should be understood, evaluated, and advanced in the era of AI-enabled research. Through panel reflections and interactive discussions, the PDW examines points of convergence and tension in theory-building, empirical work, and emerging norms of transparency and disclosure. The goal is not to impose definitive answers, but to cultivate shared understanding, practical sensibilities, and forward-looking perspectives that can guide responsible, innovative, and impactful scholarships as AI becomes increasingly embedded in the research process.
