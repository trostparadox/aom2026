Manufacturing Financial Crisis? Financial Decoupling as Non-Market Strategy for Public Institutions

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
PNP

Authors: Sara Murphy Ivey Business School; Seemantini Madhukar Pathak Ivey School of Business; Dusya Vera Western University

Session overview

Can crying poor be strategic? Organizations typically exaggerate success to gain legitimacy, but we uncover a provocative reversal: public institutions may strategically underreport financial health to mobilize political pressure on government. In Ontario, Canada, following a 2019 tuition freeze, public universities claimed financial crisis while internal stakeholders questioned the authenticity of reported deficits. Using difference-in-differences analysis across 12 years of data comparing Ontario universities to control institutions, we examine how institutional shocks can trigger negative financial decoupling and how lobbying and media strategies correlate with this effect. Our preliminary findings reveal a striking gap: the policy shock produced modest negative actual financial impact, yet large deficits were circulated publicly. This research challenges conventional decoupling theory by demonstrating how public institutions may create the appearance of crisis, transforming financial reporting into a non-market strategy for policy change. The implications extend to any public institution navigating resource constraints and government relations.
