Session overview

Contemporary societies are undergoing profound and unprecedented transformations. Increasing political and social polarization has reduced consensus around responses to major societal challenges, while the widespread diffusion of digital technologies and artificial intelligence is fundamentally reshaping the nature of organizations and how they operate. As a result, much of the content and pedagogical approaches traditionally used in Management and Organization Studies (MOS) are increasingly insufficient for making sense of contemporary organizational realities. These challenges are further compounded by shifts in learning practices among new generations of students, who engage more strongly with visual and auditory modes of learning than with text-based materials. At the same time, the vast majority of teaching resources in MOS continue to reflect the experiences, assumptions, and institutional contexts of the Global North. Consequently, scholars teaching in Latin America are required to critically adapt and rework existing pedagogical materials in order to align them with the social, cultural, and organizational realities in which their students are embedded. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) seeks to explore how teaching in Management and Organization Studies can be innovated in response to these intersecting challenges, while producing forms of pedagogy that are meaningful and contextually grounded for students in Latin America. The workshop is structured in four stages: (1) thematic presentations by the workshop organisers, (2) facilitated roundtable discussions, (3) plenary discussions involving all participants, and (4) closing collective reflections. The PDW aims to offer practical insights and pedagogical strategies for teaching MOS in Latin America and to foster a community of scholars committed to critically rethinking and renewing teaching practices in the field.
