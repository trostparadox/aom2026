State or Market? The Emergence and Evolution of the Social Enterprise Field in South Korea

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Seminar B

Participant: Yeon Kyu Lee Aalto University

Session overview

Amid growing global uncertainty, states are increasingly reasserting themselves as active architects of markets and organizational fields. Yet organizational scholarship has often treated the state as a static or monolithic actor, obscuring how state roles evolve through recursive interaction with field participants. This study examines how sustained state involvement reshapes emerging fields through iterative, relational dynamics. We introduce the concept of double capture: a recursive process through which state actors seek to harness market mechanisms to advance public objectives, while market actors simultaneously appropriate state frameworks, legitimacy, and resources to pursue their own interests. We investigate this process through a longitudinal qualitative study of the construction of the social enterprise field in South Korea between 2003 and 2022. Drawing on 108 National Assembly minutes, over 286 policy documents, media materials, and 33 interviews with policymakers and field actors, we trace how state strategies evolved across three phases. Using temporal bracketing, we show how state actors repeatedly redefined policy aspirations, recalibrated state–field relations, and redesigned implementation mechanisms in response to unintended outcomes and field-level feedback. Central to this evolution was the category of “social enterprise,” which functioned as a boundary object linking these dimensions and enabling adaptive governance without wholesale policy abandonment. We find that double capture emerged through incremental recalibrations that shifted interdependence between state and field actors and produced hybrid governance arrangements via malleable boundary objects. This study theorizes double capture as a recursive mechanism of state–market co-evolution and reconceptualizes boundary objects as tools of strategic recalibration in state-led field construction.
