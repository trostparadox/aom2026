Writing Partners as Developmental Pedagogy in the Strategy Capstone

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Author: Darcy Kathryn Fudge Kamal California State University-Sacramento

Session overview

Undergraduate strategy capstone courses are intended to integrate analytical frameworks and cultivate students’ ability to communicate strategic conclusions persuasively. Yet these courses are often taught during students’ final semester, a period associated with declining motivation, fragmented attention, and reduced engagement in reflective writing. This paper presents Writing Partners, a community-engaged pedagogical intervention that pairs strategy capstone students, who are less traditionally represented in K-12 partnerships than STEM and humanities students, with 4th- and 5th-grade students through structured letter exchanges and a culminating campus visit. Writing to a young, authentic audience required graduating seniors to translate complex strategic concepts into accessible language, encouraging reflective thinking, early articulation of ideas, and renewed purpose. Drawing on theories of reflective thinking, writing-to-learn, and cognitive apprenticeship, Writing Partners provides a developmental micro-practice that reactivates foundational learning behaviors. Evidence from a pilot across two semesters of undergraduate strategy capstone sections suggests improvements in student engagement, clarity of strategic reasoning, and persistence in writing tasks. The project also generated faculty learning related to building community partnerships and cultivating educational networks. Finally, the paper situates Writing Partners within debates about writing pedagogy in the context of generative artificial intelligence.
