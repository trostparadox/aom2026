Session overview

Although the Impostor Phenomenon (a.k.a. “imposter syndrome” or impostorism) has been studied for nearly 50 years, its literature has been beset by the lack of theory and construct clarity. As a consequence, critical questions remain about the introspective and contextually embedded nature of this phenomenon. How might one think, feel, act, or even lead others, while experiencing impostorism at work? What boundary conditions shape its outcomes? This symposium features five cutting-edge studies that advance theory and insight into the Impostor Phenomenon. It discusses topics, such as: 1) how impostor feelings fluctuate in daily life, 2) how perceived competence buffers the negative effects of workplace impostor thoughts, 3) how impostorism might promote employees’ work engagement and recovery (under certain conditions), 4) leader impostorism as a double-edged factor in teams, and 5) the simultaneous impact of impostorism on person-job and person-organization fit perceptions. Together, these studies illustrate the dynamic, contextually triggered nature of the Impostor Phenomenon and illuminate its contingent effects on employees, teams, and organizations. All in all, this symposium packs a dynamite of insights into the impostor experience, igniting new and intriguing lines of inquiry into this ubiquitous workplace phenomenon.
