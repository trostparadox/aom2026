Session overview

We propose convening a symposium aimed at revitalizing the evolutionary tradition established by Joseph Schumpeter, who identified significant transformations in competitive and economic landscapes during the First Industrial Revolution, driven by the invention and commercialization of steam engines. Schumpeter recognized innovation and technological progress as key drivers behind economic growth and the expansion of firms. Building on Schumpeter’s foundational ideas, Richard Nelson and Sidney Winter extended this intellectual heritage by weaving in elements of Darwinian evolutionary theory. This evolutionary approach has proven highly effective in explaining long-term industry dynamics, intra/inter-industry and country differences in firm growth and performance, and the rise and fall of technologies. Despite this, the field has been dormant for the past twenty years. Research into innovation, entrepreneurship, and technological evolution now often lacks a solid theoretical foundation. Data-driven studies without theoretical underpinnings risk becoming “dusty bowl empiricism” that offers little real insight. Publishing bits and pieces of ideas without connecting the dots may fail to inspire future scholarship. By building a community around core theoretical ideas, we can put fragmented concepts together and take the lead in shaping an emerging field. We believe evolutionary economics is the field’s theoretical centerpiece, giving us an opportunity to shape its future direction. The symposium will gather experts studying innovation-based competition, intra/inter-industry and country differences in firm growth and performance, and the evolutionary technologies to invigorate the evolutionary approach. Panelists use various methods—including computational, analytical, and data-science approaches based on statistical physics—to examine how innovation, firm growth, and technologies evolve over time. The event aims to blend fundamental theories with new findings, spark conversation, and guide the future of strategy and technology innovation management.
