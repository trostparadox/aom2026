From Residues to Regenerative Inputs: LCA-Informed Business Models for Leek Biostimulants

Authors: Federico Zilia University of Milan; Luigi Orsi University of Milan; Luca Ferraro University of Milan; Jacopo Bacenetti University of Milan

Session overview

Circular bioeconomy initiatives increasingly valorize food-processing side-streams, yet “circular” does not automatically mean “sustainable”. Biomass upgrading can be energy-, water-, and material-intensive and may shift burdens from conventional agricultural hotspots to toxicity- or critical-material-related categories. This study develops and demonstrates a procedure that embeds quantified Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) evidence into sustainable business model (SBM) innovation for circular agri-food pathways. We illustrate the procedure that valorizes residues from fresh-cut leek processing into bioactive extracts used as a plant biostimulant in greenhouse lettuce cultivation. We combine cradle-to-gate LCA for two functional units (1 L of extract; 1 kg of lettuce at farm gate) with an ecosystem-oriented Sustainable Business Model Canvas (SBMC) and an Ecologies of Business Model Experimentation (EBME) lens. The LCA shows that the extract-based scenario reduces most Environmental Footprint midpoints (typically 6-18%), while revealing trade-offs in human toxicity and mineral/metal resource use. We translate these hotspots into business model levers (renewable electricity, safer chemistry, closed-loop water/solvent management, durable equipment, and third-party verification) and link them to value capture mechanisms that can finance mitigation during scaling. The contribution is a replicable “hotspot-to-canvas” method that turns LCA into an input for ecosystem governance and credible circularity claims.
