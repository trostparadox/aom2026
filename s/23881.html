Does Task Reflection Have a Downside? Linking Task Reflection to Work-Life Conflict

OB
Feiya Suo (Tibet University); Hongyu Zhang; Jia You (Shanghai Institute of Technology); Xiaobei Li (UNSW Sydney)
Task reflection is typically viewed as beneficial for learning and performance, yet its potential psychological costs remain underexplored. Grounded in cognitive appraisal theory of stress, we reconceptualize task reflection as a potential stressor that precipitates work-life conflict via work stress and test work effort as a boundary condition shaping this appraisal process. Analyzing two-wave field data from 274 working adults, we found that: (a) task reflection positively predicts work stress appraisals, (b) work effort attenuate this association; (c) work stress appraisals positively related to work–life conflict; and (d) the indirect effect of task reflection on work–life conflict through work stress varies depending on levels of worker effort. These findings clarify when and why task reflection becomes psychologically taxing and highlight work effort as a key personal trait that mitigates well-being depletion.
