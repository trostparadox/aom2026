Session overview

The role of context is touted as a core tenet of classic gender, stereotype, and leadership theories (Eagly & Karau, 2002). Yet, much of the classic gender and leadership research on the role of context has treated gender stereotypes as shifting primarily along a masculine/agentic versus feminine/communal dimension, often concluding that women’s representation and evaluation improve in more feminine or communal contexts, as typified by the numerical representation of women or tasks that are more traditionally associated with women (Eagly & Karau, 2002; Heilman et al., 2024). This symposium confirms, but also complicates, this binary view of context by showcasing four papers spanning contexts with distinct normative expectations, revealing the ways in which organizations and individuals navigate these unique contexts. Together, these papers show that gendered outcomes depend not simply on whether a context is masculine or feminine, but also on the specific public media scrutiny, regional gender egalitarianism, social judgment, and cultural logics embedded within the context. These papers also span multiple levels of analysis, from individuals to organizations to societies, using diverse methods (e.g., archival, quantitative, and qualitative) and settings across field and laboratory studies, offering a rich and multilayered examination of the topic. The symposium begins with an investigation of how organizations strategically respond to the context of public media scrutiny and regional gender egalitarianism by engaging in leadership change as reputational signals (Paper 1). The second paper turns to how women entrepreneurs navigate pervasive social judgment to pursue entrepreneurship and economic participation in rural India (Paper 2). Finally, Papers 3 and 4 focus on leadership evaluation, revealing how identical behaviors – such as possessing a calling work orientation or displaying dominance—carry divergent consequences for women and men depending on prevailing role and cultural expectations. Overall, these papers underscore the need for a more nuanced, norm-based understanding of gendered behavior and evaluation. Finally, the presentations will conclude with an audience discussion moderated by Dr. Xiao-Ping Chen, a distinguished scholar known for her groundbreaking research on diversity, leadership, and culture in the workplace.
