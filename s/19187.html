Session overview

Five noted academics explore several facets of geopolitics and globalization in the context of current disruptions, as well as responses and strategic adaptations by multinational enterprises (MNEs). Christopher Hartwell will begin the panel with a historical overview, examining differences as well as similarities between the current turbulent environment and past episodes where state intervention and political risk were also prevalent, and what lessons MNEs can learn. Ilya Cuypers argues that scholarly inquiries have addressed MNE reactions to overt or structural policy changes by governments, while inadequate attention has been paid to underlying causes such as nationalism and populism which have propelled the overt recent policies. A theoretical framework using three dimensions analyzes the core issue of nationalism. Yi Shen follows up on this with a conceptual model or novel typology for assessing social and political risks which exist concurrently but exert separate influences because different stakeholders are involved in each. The conceptualization also details how MNE responses should differ between episodic and chronic risks. Ricardo Buitrago, based on extensive empirical studies in 40 emerging countries including Latin America (e.g., Mexico, Brazil, Chile, etc.) proposes an intriguing paradox: Institutional strengthening (for example by nations signing International Investment Agreements (IIAs) in order to attract more incoming foreign direct investment (FDI) paradoxically also create a condition for increasing investor-state disputes and litigation. As institutions are strengthened in developing nations – incidentally, something that goes counter to the narrative of deglobalization – FDI investors are also enabled by IIAs to counter political change or risks at the national level. Buitrago argues that institutional quality aggregate indicators for countries may be useful for macro-level academic studies, but that it is now time to drill down to governance sub-indicators pertinent to sectors and nations relevant to each MNE’s particular investment. Farok J. Contractor’s presentation will specifically focus on how MNEs should adapt to recent geopolitical changes, via geographic diversification, decoupling (and its difficulties) given transaction costs and asset specificity, minimum economic scale (MES) considerations at the factory level (for each product type), increasing inventory holdings, the optimum number of suppliers in each global value chain (GVC), non-market strategies and lobbying, the use of advanced digital technologies to improve coordination at a distance between buyer and supplier, as well as to lower information asymmetry. Geopolitics and globalization are necessarily amalgamate and amorphous phenomena. It is hoped that this symposium will enable a deeper dive into their particular underlying variables and aspects, as they relate to International Management strategies. After brief presentations, sufficient time will be left for interaction with the audience.
