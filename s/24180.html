Too Direct to Endorse? Understanding Managerial Responses to Prohibitive Voice Directness

MOC
Sen Zhang (Kyoto Tachibana University); Mengru Zhao (University of Tsukuba)
While directness is generally valued in upward communication for enhancing clarity, its effects on prohibitive voice remain underexplored. Drawing on affective events theory (AET), we develop a dual-pathway model to examine how prohibitive voice directness influences supervisor voice endorsement through emotional mechanisms. Through a three-wave survey-based field study (Study 1) and a vignette-based experiment (Study 2) conducted in the United States, we demonstrate that prohibitive voice directness elicits greater supervisor hostility and less gratitude, which in turn reduce voice endorsement. Furthermore, we examine the moderating role of supervisors’ implicit followership theories (IFTs), revealing that the negative indirect effects of prohibitive voice directness on endorsement via hostility and gratitude are attenuated when supervisors hold positive rather than negative IFTs. We discuss theoretical and practical implications of our findings, limitations, and directions for future research.
