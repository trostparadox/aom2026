Session overview

Partnership ecosystems now represent a $4 trillion industry opportunity, with 75% of global commerce flowing through indirect channels (McBain, 2024). Yet AI partnership training is not part of formal discipline in universities, often taught as a course based on dated alliance materials. While firms invest heavily in partnership capabilities and train partner roles on the job (Brigman, 2017), formalized training remains underdeveloped. Prior research demonstrates that firms with systematic alliance management capabilities achieve significantly higher alliance success (Kale, Dyer, & Singh, 2002; Schilke & Goerzen, 2010), findings that take on new significance as AI ecosystems create distinctive power asymmetries and governance challenges (Gupta, Urmetzer, & Ansari, 2025; Jacobides, Brusoni, & Candelon, 2024). Yet such capabilities remain largely absent from formal business education. Some industry leaders have recognized this gap and started filling this void. Therefore, the purpose of this panel symposium is to engage leading developers of AI partnership training programs in a moderated discussion of (1) how AI partnership curricula are developed, delivered, and managed in industry settings; (2) what competencies these programs prioritize and why; (3) how training approaches evolve through implementation and feedback; (4) the limits of formal training and where experiential learning remains essential; and (5) how business schools might incorporate partnership management into their curricula. The panelists have collectively trained thousands of partnership professionals across the globe, offering unique insight into how AI partnership leaders are preparing for their roles as artificial intelligence reshapes how firms build, manage, and scale partnerships.
