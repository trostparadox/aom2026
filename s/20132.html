Session overview

Entrepreneurial ventures face persistent legitimacy challenges arising from novelty, resource scarcity, and institutional resistance. Entrepreneurial framing has emerged as a central mechanism through which entrepreneurs seek to mobilize stakeholder support, reduce opposition, and align new ventures with prevailing norms and expectations. Despite the growth of this literature, it remains fragmented across theoretical perspectives, empirical contexts, and methodological approaches, limiting knowledge development within the field of entrepreneurship. This PDW provides a systematic, theory-informed synthesis of entrepreneurial framing research and advances a unifying taxonomy of framing tactics for legitimacy acquisition. The PDW distinguishes micro-level framing persuasion rhetoric (logos-, ethos-, and pathos-based appeals), macro-level alignment tactics with dominant institutional discourses, and strategic playbook approaches that combine multiple tactics across venture stages and audiences. The PDW integrates conceptual input with interactive roundtable discussions tailored to the ENT Division. Participants will deepen their understanding of entrepreneurial framing as a core entrepreneurial process, exchange methodological and theoretical insights, and collaboratively identify promising avenues for future research. By consolidating dispersed insights and fostering cross-project dialogue, this PDW aims to strengthen theory building and empirical rigor in entrepreneurship research on legitimacy, framing, and stakeholder engagement.
