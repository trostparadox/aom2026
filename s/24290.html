Research on Sustainability Information Disclosure in Power Enterprises Based on Large Language Model

SIM
Li Zhang (Beijing Information Science and Technology University); Guangming Zhang (Beijing Information Science and Technology University); Xiaomin Lyu (Beijing Information Science and Technology University)
Traditional ESG evaluation methods often rely on the "disclosure equals fact" assumption, making it difficult to deeply identify issues such as semantic inconsistencies and commitment-action gaps in information disclosure. To address this, this study proposes the concept of "information disclosure integrity" and develops an intelligent evaluation system integrated with large language models (LLMs). Leveraging their capabilities in semantic understanding and logical reasoning, the system systematically assesses narrative-action consistency, qualitative-quantitative alignment, and information transparency within corporate disclosure texts. An empirical analysis based on ESG-related disclosure texts from 30 representative enterprises in China's power industry from 2020 to 2024 shows that: the overall disclosure quality of the industry has continuously improved, with its structure gradually optimizing from a "pyramid shape" to a "spindle shape"; hydropower and nuclear power enterprises lead in performance, new energy enterprises are growing rapidly, while traditional thermal power enterprises still have room for improvement. This study validates the role of large models in enhancing ESG evaluation effectiveness, providing a dynamic and actionable analytical tool for enterprises, investors, and policymakers.
