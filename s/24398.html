Finding Yourself in Motherhood: An Inductive Process Model of the Transition to Working Motherhood

DEI
Yang Du (Waseda University); Norihiko Takeuchi (Waseda University)
Our study explains why return-to-work experiences after childbirth vary and how women coordinate paid work and motherhood over time. Using grounded theory methods with interviews and online parenting-community materials, we develop an inductive process model with four elements: support ecology, orientation, identity shift, and outcomes. We show that a workable support ecology (especially, childcare coverage) often determines whether re-entry can begin and whether it can be sustained. When support is in place, mothers differ in how they engage the transition and whether they sustain a clear future at work. Identity change is additive and non-linear: mothers partially let go of some intensive caregiving expectations while gradually taking on a professional identity. We also identify “non-human support” as emotional grounding that can complement human support. These processes are associated with more stable cross-domain coordination versus a more fragile coexistence of roles, with shifts as conditions change. The findings highlight how uneven access to support ecologies can reproduce inequality in working lives and point to organizational practices that reduce stigma and increase usable support during re-entry.
