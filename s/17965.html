Session overview

Conflict is a defining feature of organizational life (Jehn, 1995; De Dreu & Gelfand, 2008). Employees regularly encounter disagreement, pressure, and misalignment—whether in deciding whether to speak up, how to express complex ideas, whether to resist questionable demands, or how to navigate administrative systems (Hirschman, 1970; Brown & Treviño, 2006; Morrison, 2014; Herd & Moynihan, 2019). Research on conflict has examined a range of behavioral responses, such as voice, dissent, and compliance, but conflict is also shaped by underlying cognitive, affective, and motivational processes that warrant greater theoretical attention. This symposium, advances the study of conflict by introducing novel psychological constructs that examine how conflict is experienced, expressed, and managed across interpersonal and organizational contexts.
