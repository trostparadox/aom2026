How Hospitality Employees Cope with Technostress: A Qualitative Multi-Case Study

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1

Author: Nazym Seitova Queen Mary University of London

Session overview

The rapid digitization of the hospitality and tourism industry has heightened employees' vulnerability to stress caused by technology, commonly known as technostress. Previous research has mainly concentrated on sectors with high digital adoption, like IT and education, which limits the understanding of coping mechanisms in customer-facing roles characterized by significant emotional labor and operational demands. Utilizing the Transactional Model of Stress and Coping and the Conservation of Resources theory, this study redefines technostress coping as a dynamic and context-sensitive process. The research is based on semi-structured interviews and non-participant observations involving 27 individuals (both frontline staff and managers) across 11 hospitality organizations in an emerging market. The findings reveal that technostress coping rarely occurs through isolated strategies. Furthermore, employees dynamically sequence and combine coping actions, such as problem-solving, emotional regulation, self-reliance, and support seeking, in response to digital constraints. The coping process was heavily influenced by factors including customer focus, limited resources, and managerial actions. Importantly, strategies like temporal coping, digital triage, and “reverse delegation,” where managers take on emotional and operational pressure to shield frontline employees, emerged as critical contextual factors. This study adds to the technostress literature by framing coping as a recursive, context-driven process that both reacts to and modifies digital work environments over time. On a practical level, the findings indicate that organizations should focus not just on minimizing techno-stressors but also on developing adaptive, emotionally supportive coping frameworks to enhance employee well-being and performance in digitally demanding service settings.
