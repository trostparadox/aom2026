The Independent and Interdependent Effects of Robotics and AI on Operational Efficiency

CTO
Nan Zhang (Guangzhou College of Commerce); Huiying Li (Guangzhou College of Commerce)
Robotics and artificial intelligence (AI), as two core intelligent technologies, are increasingly adopted by firms to enhance operational processes and performance. However, it remains unclear how their independent and joint deployment affects firms’ operational efficiency. Using panel data from publicly listed firms in China between 2016 and 2023, we examine the independent and interdependent effects of robotics adoption and AI engagement. The results show that, individually, both robotics adoption and AI engagement significantly improve operational efficiency. However, when deployed simultaneously, the two technologies exhibit a negative interaction effect, reflecting conflicting assumptions of robotics and AI regarding the role of human agency. We further find that this negative interaction effect can be mitigated by specific organizational resources, such as the presence of a Chief Information Officer (CIO) and higher levels of employee human capital. Overall, our study not only provides empirical evidence on the operational efficiency implications of robotics and AI but also highlights the importance of considering their potential interaction and the role of organizational resources in shaping such effects.
