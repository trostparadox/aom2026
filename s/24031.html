Boundary Conditions of Green Investors’ Influence on Greenwashing: Managerial Short-Term Pressure

ONE
Shaoqian Zhang (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences)
Existing literature on green investors and corporate greenwashing presents contradictory evidence: the disciplining perspective suggests green investors constrain symbolic claims through monitoring, while the catering perspective contends they inadvertently incentivize greenwashing by creating valuation premiums for symbolic behavior. Drawing on Upper Echelons Theory, we identify managerial short-term pressure as a critical boundary condition reconciling these perspectives, as executives' temporal orientation shapes how they interpret investor demands. Using 49,449 firm-year observations from Chinese listed companies (2008–2024), we find green investors significantly reduce greenwashing, but this effect attenuates and ultimately reverses under high managerial short-term pressure. We make three contributions. First, we reconcile conflicting evidence by establishing managerial short-term pressure as a boundary condition. Second, we use Upper Echelons Theory to demonstrate how temporal cognition filters stakeholder pressures. Third, we advance the field beyond if green investors matter to when and why. Practically, our findings indicate that disclosure mandates are ineffective without correcting the incentives behind short-termism.
