The Social Construction of Overconfidence

OSCM
Sergio Grove (The University of Texas at El Paso); Aaron Nelson (The University of Texas at El Paso); Manjot Singh Bhussar (University of Tennessee at Chattanooga); Andrew Schwartz (Elon University)
Do supply chains support confident CEOs when their displays of confidence are interpreted as unwarranted or reckless? Grounded in a micro-meso-level mechanism, we propose that displays of overconfidence function as a socially interpreted construct—one that suppliers evaluate against contextual cues of managerial competence and strategic reliability. We examine this question in a high-stakes interorganizational setting using a sample of 21,062 publicly traded buyer–supplier pairs from 1992 to 2023. Our results show that while suppliers support confident CEOs on average, this support is likely to be substantially lower in the presence of evidence consistent with unwarranted confidence, which we refer to as perceived overconfidence. These findings suggest that stakeholders with economic exposure and no formal hierarchy—such as suppliers—adaptively evaluate whether confidence reflects credible skill sets or unjustified overconfident behavior. The results advance the micro-meso-level literature by showing how individual-level CEO traits scale into interfirm governance. We reframe overconfidence research as a socially interpreted construct that operates across organizational boundaries, extending upper echelons theory into the behavioral dynamics of supplier relationships and supply chain coordination.
