Receptive Emptiness as a Cognitive, Generative, and Integrative Framework for Openness and Actions

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 6
MSR

People

Authors: Yunita Sofyan; Matej Cerne School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Irfan Jo Not Associated; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro

Session overview

This study introduces and validates a novel construct, receptive emptiness, to address a significant gap in psychology and management literature. While research increasingly integrates Eastern philosophy, emptiness has predominantly been framed as a negative, pathological state. In contrast, we define receptive emptiness as a cultivated, positive mental ground—cleared of habitual clutter—that optimizes the mind for engagement, derived from a synthesis of Buddhist, Daoist, and Confucian thought. Operationalizing the construct via Self-Determination Theory, we developed and validated a scale across multiple cross-cultural samples. Analyses confirmed a stable three-factor structure: cognitive openness, generative ideation, and relational harmony. The scale demonstrated strong psychometric properties, including convergent and discriminant validity, and proved to be a significant predictor of decision-making quality, cognitive flexibility, and relational harmony. This work provides researchers with a validated tool to investigate how foundational, open states of awareness shape downstream behavior, attitudes, and psychological processes.
