From Ethnic Ties to Export Markets: Strategic Adaptation Among Syrian Forced Migrant Entrepreneurs

ENT
Burcin Hatipoglu (UNSW Sydney); Kerem Gurses; Burçin Güçlü (ICN Business School); Eren Akkan (KEDGE Business School)
This study investigates the internationalisation strategies of Syrian forced migrant (FM) entrepreneurs in Türkiye, with a particular focus on how advice networks shape their market orientation. Grounded in social resources theory, we explore whether FM entrepreneurs seek business advice primarily from co-ethnic or local (non-co-ethnic) sources, and how these choices influence their orientation toward ethnic versus mainstream markets, ultimately affecting their export behaviour. Employing a mixed-methods sequential explanatory design, we analyse data from 203 survey responses and 20 in-depth follow-up interviews. Quantitative results indicate that reliance on co-ethnic advice networks is positively associated with a co-ethnic market orientation, which in turn reduces the likelihood of engaging in export activities. In contrast, stronger connections to local advisory networks are associated with a shift toward a mainstream market orientation and increased export engagement. Qualitative findings further illuminate how strategic engagement with local networks enables FMs to overcome institutional barriers, build legitimacy, and access broader market opportunities. This study contributes to the literature on immigrant entrepreneurship by highlighting the mediating role of market orientation and unpacking the unique challenges and adaptive strategies of FMs. It also provides practical insights for policymakers and support organisations seeking to enhance the cross-border potential of FM-led ventures.
