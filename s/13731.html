Session overview

Entrepreneurship and business education increasingly recognizes the importance of student well-being, resilience, and judgment under uncertainty (Shir et al., 2019; Wiklund et al., 2019). However, while research on eudaimonic well-being has gained traction, less attention has been given to the specific intellectual character required of entrepreneurs—and to the pedagogical practices by which faculty cultivate and model it (Ryff, 2019). This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) addresses how business educators can intentionally teach for intellectual character, particularly intellectual humility, curiosity, and gumption, rather than solely teaching about entrepreneurial outcomes (Ducharme, 2025). Designed as a developmental and experiential workshop, this PDW targets doctoral students, early-career faculty, and mid-career entrepreneurship educators seeking to refine their pedagogy. Participants will engage in structured reflection and interactive exercises that surface moments of intellectual and pedagogical discomfort—such as navigating authority, uncertainty, and the admission of limitations in the classroom. Rather than offering prescriptive solutions, the workshop places participants in the same kinds of productive discomfort they foster in early-stage entrepreneurs and business professionals. The PDW integrates four complementary perspectives: philosophical foundations of intellectual character in business, gumption and disciplined courage in entrepreneurial contexts, pedagogical experimentation with intellectual humility, and faculty modeling of intellectual character as entrepreneurial leaders. Through guided dialogue and applied activities, participants will develop shared language, classroom-ready exercises, and reflective tools that can be immediately adapted to entrepreneurship and experiential business courses. By centering faculty development and modeling as core mechanisms of entrepreneurship and business education, this PDW advances the ENT Division’s mission and offers a novel, interactive format that complements—rather than replicates—traditional conference sessions.
