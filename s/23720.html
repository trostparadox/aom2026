From Evil to Ideal: The Different Queens in the Female Leadership Spectrum

DEI
Juliana Mansur; Kelly Weires Avelino
This study proposes a framework mapping a spectrum of female leadership strategies—Evil Queen, Guardian Queen, Janus Queen, Maternal Queen, and Ideal Queen—grounded in the Queen Bee Phenomenon, leader identity construction, and four leadership mechanisms: To Be, To Do, To Think, and To Feel. The model situates strategies along a continuum from self serving competition to other serving collaboration. Competitive strategies reflect extreme agency and self group distancing used to secure individual legitimacy in hostile contexts. The Janus Queen occupies a hybrid zone of coopetition, balancing agency and communality to advance both self and ingroup. Maternal and Ideal Queens emphasize power sharing, mentorship, and activism for structural change. By reframing apparently dysfunctional behaviors as adaptive responses to scarcity and identity threat, the study challenges assumptions that such actions are innate female flaws. For organizations, the framework recommends practical interventions: reforming HR practices, valuing collaboration in promotion criteria, and developing socioemotional skills. Addressing structural barriers can unlock female leadership potential, stimulate innovation, and strengthen social impact. The framework serves as diagnostic and prescriptive tool for scholars and practitioners committed to transforming leadership cultures, advancing equitable change.
