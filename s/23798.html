ZHANG Haonan (Dalian University of Technology); haitong ? (Dalian University of Technology); haoyue wang (Dalian University of Technology); Zhenduo Zhang (Dalian University of Technology); Linzhuo Wang (BI Norwegian Business School)
Multiple project team membership (MPTM) serves as a critical human resource arrangement in matrix organizations. Existing research, while examining its impact on work contexts, has reported inconsistent findings regarding its effect on individual performance due to divergent theoretical perspectives. Addressing this gap, our study adopts an attribution theory lens to investigate how employees’ interpretations of the underlying logic behind MPTM influence their performance, specifically proposing the mechanisms of commitment attribution and control attribution. Furthermore, we adopt information gap perspective, viewed as an amplified characteristic in MPTM settings, into the attribution process. Using Bayesian network modeling and polynomial regression analysis, our results reveal a nonlinear relationship between MPTM and job performance, mediated by commitment attribution and control attribution. Additionally, information gap moderates the relationship between MPTM and both attributions, in that individuals experiencing a high information gap are more likely to perceive MPTM as a form of control attribution rather than commitment attribution. This current study enriches the MPTM literature by applying an attribution perspective to elucidate how interpretations of organizational arrangements determine employees’ subsequent behaviors. It thereby provides theoretical guidance for mitigating multi-identity challenges of employees and shaping cognitive appraisal of complex work arrangements for better employee performance.
