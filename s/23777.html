From Rejection to Understanding: Counterfactual Explanations in Human, Hybrid, and AI Hiring

HR
Vinicius Gracio Michelon (University of Liverpool); Yves R.F. Guillaume; Lilian Otaye-Ebede (University of Liverpool)
The popularity of AI-enabled hiring technologies has steadily increased in recent years. However, research shows that job applicants often perceive them less favourably than traditional human hiring. A 3 x 3 between-subjects pre-registered experiment (N = 384) was conducted to test whether providing counterfactual explanations to unsuccessful job applicants can mitigate these negative perceptions. Findings revealed that AI evaluations were perceived as less fair and less trustworthy than human decisions. Hybrid (human-AI) approaches were seen as less fair but similarly trustworthy compared with human decisions. Fairness and, to a lesser extent, trust partially mediated the relationship between evaluator type and organisational perceptions and behavioural intentions. Although rejected applicants perceived explainable AI systems less positively than similarly transparent human evaluators, counterfactuals significantly improved perceptions of both hybrid and AI, to the point that explainable AI and hybrid processes were often viewed more favourably than opaque human evaluators. Residual algorithm aversion persisted.
