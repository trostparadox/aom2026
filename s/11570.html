Session overview

Strategic uniqueness, the extent to which a firm’s strategy deviates from industry norms, has long been a central debate in organizational science. Research has long examined the tension between standing out to achieve competitive advantage and fitting in to secure legitimacy and survival. This unresolved tension has produced a paradox in the literature, with no clear consensus on the optimal degree of strategic deviation. Research across strategy, entrepreneurship, organization theory, and innovation reflects this divide, employing varied constructs such as uniqueness, distinctiveness, atypicality, and differentiation. This symposium responds to this fragmentation by bringing together four papers that offer diverse yet complementary perspectives on strategic uniqueness. Each paper advances the conversation by addressing different theoretical, empirical, and methodological approaches to strategic deviation. In addition to presenting their research, speakers will reflect on the state of their respective subfields. Collectively, the symposium aims to foster integration, clarify conceptual boundaries, and stimulate future research on strategic uniqueness.
