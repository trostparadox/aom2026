Accelerating Digital Offerings in Incumbent Manufacturers: A Process Model of Agile Transformation

TIM
Yorgo Bejjani; Marin Jovanovic (Copenhagen Business School); Wim Vanhaverbeke (University of Antwerp)
Digital transformation is a strategic imperative for manufacturers, yet many struggle to turn investments in analytics, cloud, and AI into digital offerings that scale. Agile is widely adopted as a remedy, but in manufacturing contexts marked by hierarchy, silos, and compliance constraints, Agile often yields prototypes without sustained adoption—an “Agile theater” risk. We ask: How can B2B manufacturing firms organize to accelerate digital offerings through Agile-enabled transformation? Drawing on a longitudinal qualitative case study of an aerospace manufacturer and three embedded projects, we develop a process model that explains how Agile becomes an organizational capability. The model identifies three organizing dimensions—Agile framing, Agile organization, and acceleration capability—and traces a shift across three phases: method-centric adoption with weak scaling pathways; orchestrated experimentation with stronger desirability learning and monetization probing but fragile integration; and institutionalized acceleration with routinized handoffs and a repeatable MVP-to-core pathway. Two transition mechanisms enable movement across phases: (T1) role clarification and protected experimentation space and (T2) integration mandate and decision-rights redesign. We contribute to research on Agile beyond software and digital servitization by showing how organizational systems—not Agile practices alone—enable digital offering acceleration in high-stakes B2B manufacturing settings.

Enhancing Innovation and Productivity in Advanced Manufacturing SMEs Through Artificial Intelligence

TIM
Bruno Alencar Pereira (Flinders University); Adela Jana McMurray (Flinders University); Nur Aini Oktiningrum Adinda (Flinders University); Graeme Keith Mitchell (Flinders University); Tim Van Erp (Flinders University); Andreas Cebulla (Flinders University); Angie Shafei (Flinders University); Josivania Silva Farias; Abner Santos Belém; Arlindo Galvão (Federal University of Goiás)
The adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in advanced manufacturing SMEs is an emerging topic in research and in practice. However, AI integration remains limited due to the lack of understanding of interconnected factors, such as technological readiness, organizational capacity, and the environmental context. This study introduces a novel evidence-based Technology-Organization-Environment -based conceptual framework to increase innovation and productivity through AI adoption in advanced manufacturing SMEs. The new conceptual framework was developed by grouping sub-dimensions and key factors from a systematic literature review and consolidated through an in-depth analysis of 31 interviews with advanced manufacturing SME owner CEOs, academics, and experts from Australia and Brazil. This multi-data approach provides a structured international perspective about AI adoption, capabilities, innovation and productivity outcomes. The novel framework is divided into the three dimensions of technology, organization, and environment, and nine sub-dimensions comprising thirty key factors. The data analysis results indicate that AI adoption and successful outcomes in advanced manufacturing SMEs depend on the interconnected factors, of leadership, strategic management, and skills which emerged as critical enablers alongside data integration, security, and perceived value. This study contributes theoretically to the AI adoption literature by extending and tailoring the existing TOE framework with empirically validated factors suitable for advanced manufacturing SMEs. The study’s practical impact lies in offering guidance on AI-adoption roadmaps, capability building, and organizational policy support to close the AI adoption gap and drive innovation and productivity in today’s workplaces.

Orchestration in Product-Service System (PSS) Ecosystems: The Role of Digital Technologies

TIM
Lucas Gabriel De Paula Silveira (FGV EAESP)
The digital revolution has brought advanced technologies that are transforming how physical, digital, and social domains connect. These technologies have become central to the evolution of Product-Service Systems (PSS), particularly in the transition to Digital PSS, enabling personalized, innovative solutions and driving the integration of services into traditional manufacturing models, specially through digital servitization. Existing literature in digital servitization often focuses on firm-level or dyadic relationships, overlooking the ecosystem-level interactions. Despite significant advancements, there is an opportunity in understanding how digital technologies facilitate the orchestration of digital servitization ecosystems. Thus, this research aims to investigate the role of digital technologies in the orchestration process of digital servitization ecosystems. Drawing on three qualitative cases of digital servitization ecosystems, we show that digital technologies shape orchestration not only by enabling service offerings but by becoming coordination infrastructure. Across cases, platform architectures, governance mechanisms, and service delivery routines jointly stabilized participation, managed openness–control tensions, and supported scaling through data foundations, documentation, and learning loops. Case differences reveal equifinal pathways: internal platformization and control, partner-centric scaling with citizen development, and customer-interface discipline via roadmaps and knowledge-centered support. We contribute a framework linking digital capabilities to ecosystem governance and to the servitization paradox.

Product Market Competition and Industrial Intelligence: Evidence from Chinese Manufacturing Firms

TIM
Manman Tu (South China Normal University); Xueling Yan (Sichuan University); LEYANG WANG (South China Normal University); Shule Yu (East China Normal University); Jialin Huang (South China Normal University)
Why do firms adopt industrial intelligence? Challenging the conventional view that focuses on labor cost savings, this study argues that product market competition is a primary driver. Using a dataset of Chinese listed manufacturing firms from 2011 to 2019, we find that intensified product market competition significantly propels firms to adopt industrial intelligence, as proxied by robot exposure. Further analysis indicates that such adoption serves as a strategic response, for it fosters product innovation and increases product market share. This effect is particularly pronounced for firms with lower financing constraints and greater supply chain stability. Our study contributes to the literature by identifying industrial intelligence not merely as a cost-cutting device, but as a strategic imperative for survival and growth in competitive markets.

The Fragility of Agility: Integration Complexity and Architectural Exploration

TIM
Xiaochuan Ma (Zhejiang University); RUCHEN SHAO (Zhejiang University); Yongyi Shou (Zhejiang University); YU WEI
The digital transformation of manufacturing has spawned a "Modularity Illusion": the belief that software-defined architectures allow firms to expand their product portfolios at near-zero marginal cost. We challenge this assumption by identifying a countervailing force—Systemic Integration Complexity. We argue that while digital tools lower the cost of designing new platform roots (Architectural Exploration), they exponentially increase the cost of validating their physical inter-dependencies. Leveraging a census-level dataset of the Chinese automotive industry (2015-2025) constructed from regulatory "Root Codes," we document a robust inverted U-shaped relationship between architectural exploration intensity and firm growth. While moderate exploration creates option value for market segmentation, excessive architectural proliferation causes "traffic jams" in the engineering pipeline, directly evidenced by significantly prolonged Time-to-Market Latency. Crucially, we uncover a phenomenon we term the "Fragility of Agility." While Entrants are unburdened by legacy assets, their performance suffers a cliff-like drop once complexity exceeds a critical threshold. In contrast, Incumbents exhibit a flatter, resilient trajectory, suggesting that their institutionalized routines act as necessary "Organizational Buffers" against systemic entropy. Paradoxically, we identify a "Resource Trap": abundant financial slack exacerbates the integration burden by fueling undisciplined, "premature scaling." Our findings reshape the "Attacker's Advantage" narrative, demonstrating that in complex engineered systems, the primary constraint on sustainable growth is not physical scale or financial resources, but the Architectural Governance Capability to integrate divergent platforms.
