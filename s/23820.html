From Job Insecurity to Divergent Behavioral Responses: The Role of Workplace Anxiety

OB
WENG Qingxiong (University of Science and Technology of China); Mingzhi Xie (University of Science and Technology of China); Lixin Chen (University of Science and Technology of China)
Grounded in appraisal theory of emotion, this study investigates how job insecurity triggers distinct employee behavioral responses through workplace anxiety. Drawing on three-wave survey data from 321 Chinese employees, we develop and test a moderated mediation model linking job insecurity, workplace anxiety, and two forms of coping behavior: problem-focused (help-seeking behaviors) and emotion-focused (cyberloafing) responses. The results reveal that job insecurity significantly increases workplace anxiety, which, in turn, mediates opposite behavioral tendencies. Specifically, anxiety undermines employees’ propensity to seek help while promoting subtle withdrawal behaviors such as cyberloafing. Moreover, individual regulatory focus moderates these indirect effects: employees with a promotion focus are better able to sustain proactive coping under anxiety, whereas those with a prevention focus are more likely to engage in avoidance-oriented behaviors. This research advances the literature on job insecurity by clarifying the emotional mechanism underlying divergent coping strategies, extending the behavioral outcomes to hidden withdrawal behaviors, and identifying motivational orientations as key boundary conditions.
