University Technology Transfer and Industrial Upgrading: Dual Pathways and Capacities

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Author: Tingwei Zhao Wuhan University of Technology

Session overview

This study investigates the systemic mechanisms through which University Technology Transfer (UTT) drives Regional Industrial Upgrading (RIU). While existing literature acknowledges UTT's positive externalities, the specific transmission channels remain underexplored. Leveraging a panel dataset of 30 Chinese provinces from 2013 to 2022, we construct a "Dual-Pathway, Dual-Moderation" framework. We posit that UTT promotes industrial upgrading through two distinct mediators: (1) a "Factor Reconfiguration Effect" (optimizing labor and capital allocation) and (2) a "Technological Breakthrough Effect" (fostering radical innovation). Furthermore, we integrate the concept of ambidextrous innovation capacity to examine boundary conditions, proposing that Regional Absorptive Capacity moderates the factor pathway, while Firm Innovation Capability moderates the technological pathway. Empirical results from fixed-effects models and mediation analyses confirm that UTT significantly accelerates RIU through both channels. Crucially, the moderation analysis reveals a structural matching effect: regional absorptive capacity amplifies the impact of factor reconfiguration, whereas firm-level capabilities are critical for realizing technological breakthroughs. These findings offer a nuanced policy roadmap for optimizing the university-industry innovation interface in emerging economies.
