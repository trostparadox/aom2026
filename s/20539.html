Unequal Returns to Visibility: Why Some Successful Women Are Promoted and Others Aren’t

Author: jiayin tu Tsinghua University

Session overview

Why do some women secure promotion after successfully completing high-visibility tasks while others do not? Addressing this persistent puzzle of within-gender variance, we move beyond static models of disadvantage to propose a dynamic signaling framework. We theorize that for women, initial success is often a contested achievement requiring subsequent "interpretive labor" to secure advancement. We introduce three novel, dynamic constructs— incremental cost gradient, signal time density, and maternal resilience display—that moderate the efficacy of the initial performance signal. We hypothesize that these strategic post-task behaviors accelerate promotion speed by enhancing managers' perception of promotability. We want to test our model using a multi-method design: a three-wave longitudinal field study of 500 female employees and a randomized scenario experiment with 320 managers. This study contributes to career and signaling literatures by shifting focus from performance events to the temporal stream of agentic signals women use to navigate the "leaky pipeline".
