Session overview

Management research has become increasingly global, yet much of its theoretical and methodological infrastructure remains implicitly calibrated to Western institutional, cultural, and data environments. Scholars working in non-Western contexts, such as Asia, Africa, and Latin America, face distinctive challenges in theorizing, data access, measurement, and publication, but these challenges are rarely addressed in collective, experience-based formats. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to create a vibrant and inclusive forum for scholars who conduct, or aspire to conduct, research in non-Western settings. The workshop brings together six expert researchers with extensive experience across diverse non-Western contexts to share practical and intellectual lessons on (1) theorizing from context rather than merely controlling for it, (2) designing credible empirical strategies under data and institutional constraints, (3) accessing and validating archival, administrative, network, and historical data, and (4) positioning non-Western research for publication in top-tier journals. Rather than treating non-Western settings as peripheral sites of replication, the PDW highlights how they can serve as engines of theory development, revealing new organizational mechanisms, institutional dynamics, and strategic logics. The PDW combines short panel presentations, plenary discussion, and interactive roundtables in which participants can receive targeted feedback on their own projects. By emphasizing shared craft knowledge, design reasoning, and publication strategies, the workshop helps participants translate contextual complexity into theoretically meaningful and methodologically credible research. In doing so, it supports the Academy’s commitment to globally inclusive scholarship and advances ENT, IM, OMT, ONE, RM, SIM, and STR divisions’ efforts to move beyond Western-centric assumptions in the study of organizations and strategy.
