Conducting the Twin Megatrends: MNE Orchestration Capabilities for Global Digital Sustainability

IM
Claire Kilpatrick (Queen's University Belfast); Kieran Michael Conroy (Queen's University Belfast)
Multinational enterprises (MNEs) face mounting pressure to adopt advanced technologies and generate innovative solutions for increasingly intractable sustainability challenges. Although prior research acknowledges the intertwined nature of digital transformation and sustainability, the capabilities enabling MNEs to harness global digital sustainability (GDS) remain insufficiently understood. Using qualitative case studies, we explore how MNEs build the orchestration capabilities required to pursue GDS. Our findings show that firms advance digital sustainability through flagship initiatives: strategic, transformative efforts that combine multiple technologies to scale high-impact solutions to complex sustainability problems. Despite their considerable value creation, such initiatives are asset-intensive and destabilizing, placing significant strain on existing MNE resources, structures and networks. Building on dynamic capabilities research, we explicate that advancing these initiatives requires MNEs to construct orchestration capabilities. We theorize orchestration as a higher-order meta-capability constituted by a nested set of interrelated sub-capabilities involving recombination, reconfiguration and synchronization. Specifically, we unpack how these sub-capabilities are enacted through distinct orchestral activities that enable MNEs to continually synthesize and amplify internal operations, harmonize ecosystem partners and sequence the pace of GDS initiatives with rapidly evolving conditions. Our findings offer practical insights into how firms leverage synergies arising from the intersection of digital sustainability.

International Business and Grand Challenges: From Empirical Context to Theoretical Integration (WITHDRAWN)

IM
Lisa F. Canova (University of Geneva); Tina C. Ambos (University of Geneva)
Grand challenges (GC) - such as climate change, global health, and social inequality - are systemic problems that demand not only a thematic inclusion, but a new theoretical lens on how organizations can address GC. Their complexity and transnational scope make GC highly relevant to international business (IB), a field with long-standing expertise in cross-border coordination, institutional diversity, and global-local interactions. While IB studies have increasingly included GC themes in their empirical scope, the field has been slower than other management disciplines to integrate its theoretical foundations with GC research and, thus, expand its analytical scope. This article positions GC as an emerging research arena with four defining dimensions – temporal scale, spatial scale, actor-logic diversity, and modes of response – and shows how these dimensions open entry points for IB theory to connect with and advance theorizing on GC. We offer a forward-looking research agenda with four theoretical reorientations to guide IB scholarship under GC conditions. Our conclusion is that deeper engagement with GC offers IB not only greater relevance but also a pathway to refine and extend its theoretical foundations.

Social Welfare Deficiency and Informal Entrepreneurship: A Safety-First Perspective

IM
Mengli Zhao (Wuhan University); Jinxin Liu (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Elizabeth Marie Moore (Northeastern University)
Informal entrepreneurship remains a persistent phenomenon across both emerging and advanced economies, yet existing explanations rooted in economic rationality—emphasizing cost–benefit trade-offs, institutional voids, and regulatory burdens—offer limited insight into why informality endures even where formal infrastructures improve. We address this gap by advancing a safety-first perspective that conceptualizes informal entrepreneurship as a fallback strategy under institutional vulnerability. We theorize basic social welfare as a foundational social institution that mitigates livelihood risk, arguing that its deficiency increases the likelihood of informal entrepreneurial entry. This effect is amplified when ventures are founded by solo entrepreneurs, and further conditioned by national-level informal (uncertainty avoidance) and formal (investment landscape) institutions. Using a multilevel dataset of 14,636 entrepreneurs across 44 countries, our multilevel logistic models strongly support these predictions, reinforced by extensive robustness checks. Overall, our study contributes to international entrepreneurship and comparative institutional theory by offering a more socially embedded account of entrepreneurial entry under constraint.

Geopolitical Risk and Corporate Social Responsibility Orchestration: A Domestic Stakeholder View

IM
Zhenkun Wei (Renmin University of China); Huimin Zhou (Central South University); Zixiao Zhu (Renmin University of China)
While existing literature has predominantly focused on the consequences of geopolitical risk globally, there is still a lack of insights into how geopolitical risk travels back and influence the domestic market. We propose that geopolitical risk shifts the audience legitimacy evaluations and the competitive environment in domestic market, which intensifies the pressure on obtaining legitimacy and competitive advantages. Building on optimal distinctiveness theory, this study investigates the effect of geopolitical risk on corporate social responsibility (CSR) scope conformity and CSR emphasis differentiation from an orchestration-based view. Using data from 2013 to 2023 and conducting a difference-in-differences design by the first U.S.-China trade war in 2018, this study finds that geopolitical risk decreases CSR scope conformity but increases CSR emphasis differentiation. Furthermore, nationalism strengthens the decreasing effect on CSR scope conformity, while analyst attention strengthens the increasing effect on CSR emphasis differentiation, revealing the mechanisms of legitimacy compensation and stakeholder importance reconfiguration. CSR emphasis differentiation makes the firm perform more stable in the capital market, indicating that CSR orchestration is valuable for navigating geopolitical risk.
