Session overview

Actors and entities in organizational life, including organizations, products, and even market categories, are evaluated through multiple, distinct classification systems that simultaneously operate along cognitive, regulatory, and normative dimensions. This PDW explores how organizations navigate these systems and manage tensions when different classification schemes impose conflicting demands. While existing research has extensively examined category spanning within single systems, we lack understanding of how entities strategically position themselves across multiple systems, particularly when heterogeneous audiences apply different evaluation schemes. This gap is especially consequential for entrepreneurship, where new ventures must construct meaning for novel offerings while also seeking regulatory approval and building normative legitimacy. By bringing together scholars from diverse theoretical and methodological perspectives, this PDW aims to establish a research agenda that recognizes multiple categorization as a qualitatively distinct phenomenon requiring examination of the interactive, multi-level, and sometimes contradictory relationships between classification systems. Through our distinguished panelists’ input and interactive roundtable discussions, we seek to generate new insights into audience heterogeneity and evaluation, strategic categorization, cultural entrepreneurship, and institutional dynamics that shape organizational outcomes in complex categorical environments. We expect participants to engage across different streams of categorization research, generate new research questions through collaborative discussion, and build connections across theoretical and methodological traditions.
