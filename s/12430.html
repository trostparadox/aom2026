Session overview

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has gained prominence in management over the last two decades due to its unique ability to simulate interactions among heterogeneous, interdependent agents within complex systems. Despite its versatility and potential to complement other research methods, ABM remains underutilized. Skepticism in research, review, and publication processes stems in part from limited methods training for scholars, journal editors, and reviewers. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) addresses these gaps by bringing together a panel of experts to discuss ABM’s strengths, limitations, and practical applications. The session includes two parts: Part 1 features a panel discussion and open Q&A with ABM experts, and Part 2 provides participants an opportunity to engage in small group discussions with panelists on specialized topics within ABM, such as triangulating research methods, applying systems thinking, and designing ABM studies for organizational contexts. This interactive format allows participants to gain tailored insights and practical advice for advancing their ABM research. Unlike hands-on coding workshops, this PDW focuses on how ABM is designed, evaluated, triangulated, and reviewed in organizational research, addressing a key barrier to broader adoption.
