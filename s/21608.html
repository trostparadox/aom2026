Gatekeepers or Game-Changers? Intermediaries and Beneficiary Empowerment in MSPs

Author: Clara Emefa Asempapa Ca' Foscari University of Venice

Session overview

Multistakeholder partnerships are collaborative mechanisms for addressing complex grand societal challenges, with intermediation playing a vital role in facilitating coordination across diverse actors. However, how intermediaries influence the empowerment of intended beneficiaries remains underexplored and theoretically fragmented. This structured literature review analysed 54 peer-reviewed articles and identified dominant themes, critiques, and emerging directions in the field. Findings reveal that while intermediaries are often seen as neutral facilitators, their roles are deeply embedded in power dynamics that can either enhance or hinder inclusive governance. Key gaps include limited empirical tracking of empowerment outcomes, confusion between participation and empowerment, and a strong geographical bias toward the Global North. The review proposes four transformative research directions: politicising intermediation, centring beneficiary agency, adopting temporal frameworks of empowerment, and exploring alternative governance mechanisms. Reframing intermediation enables a more critical and inclusive understanding of how MSPs can genuinely contribute to equitable and sustainable development.
