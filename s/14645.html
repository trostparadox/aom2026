Session overview

Corporate–startup collaborations (CSCs) are reshaping how firms innovate. Startups bring speed, creativity, and novel technologies; corporations contribute to market and product development resources. Despite their proliferation, CSCs remain inherently complex, with challenges stemming from power asymmetries, divergent organizational goals, and lack of clear performance evaluation metrics. At the same time, scholarly work on CSCs has evolved in silos, focusing separately on startup experiences, corporate strategies, or intermediary roles. This PDW brings these perspectives together through the co-development of a multi-actor impact assessment framework that captures how value is created, constrained, and distributed across CSCs. The workshop is structured around parallel roundtables representing three stakeholder viewpoints—startups (e.g., collaboration pathways and common pitfalls), corporates (e.g., startup selection criteria and integration barriers), and intermediaries (e.g., orchestration and balancing conflicting demands). After synthesizing insights across these perspectives, participants will jointly identify key value creation mechanisms, outcomes, and challenges. The resulting framework will provide a shared conceptual tool to advance theory (e.g., beyond dyadic views) and practice (e.g., better program design and support initiatives). The PDW convenes scholars working on startup scaling strategies, corporate venturing, interorganizational alliances, and entrepreneurial support organizations. Participation in this PDW is open to all conference attendees, with no pre-registration required. Participants who wish to receive feedback and discuss their ongoing research on corporate–startup collaborations are invited to submit an extended abstract to Elie Saad (elie.saad@umu.se) prior to the meeting.
