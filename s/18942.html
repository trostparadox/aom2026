Migrant Entrepreneurs in Entrepreneurial Ecosystems: A Systematic Review

Authors: Runshuang Vivian Tang University of Technology Sydney; Pavlina Jasovska UNSW Sydney; Najmeh Hassanli University of Technology Sydney

Session overview

Migrant entrepreneurs account for around 17 percent of self-employment across OECD countries and have contributed nearly four million jobs over the past decade, yet this economically vital population remains substantially under-theorized. Entrepreneurial ecosystems support venture development through relational ties and institutional resources, but access to these resources typically depends on social embeddedness and cultural capital that many migrants cannot readily draw on and must instead construct within unfamiliar host contexts. Despite the evident intersection of these phenomena, scholarship on migrant entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial ecosystems has developed along parallel trajectories, yielding limited understanding of how migration status conditions ecosystem participation and resource access. This systematic literature review synthesizes 43 peer-reviewed studies (2017–2026) examining the intersection of migrant entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial ecosystems. We develop a three-dimensional analytical framework organizing findings across micro-level (migrant entrepreneur characteristics), meso-level (ecosystem stakeholder interactions), and macro-level (institutional contexts) dimensions. Our thematic analysis identifies six interconnected themes: institutional barriers, network access, resource acquisition, legitimacy building, capability development, and strategic adaptation. We further identify critical research gaps including limited longitudinal evidence, geographic concentration in Western economies, undertheorized intersectional effects, and insufficient outcome measurement. Our synthesis contributes an integrative conceptual framework bridging these parallel literatures while specifying a prioritized research agenda advancing theoretical understanding and informing ecosystem policy interventions targeting migrant entrepreneurs.
