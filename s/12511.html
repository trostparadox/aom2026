A Double-Edge Sword Effect of Leader-Member Exchange on Employee Voice Quality

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

Authors: Shiqi Xiao Rutgers Business School; Junchao Li Rutgers Business School; Mengyi Zhang Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Session overview

Research on employee voice has started to emphasize the study of the quality of employees’ voice. Beyond encouraging employees to speak up, we need to better understand the factors that influence the quality of employees’ voiced suggestions and ideas. Drawing on the informational benefits and, at the same time, the relational burden of a high-quality leader-member exchange, this research aims to shed light on the dual implications of leader-member exchange on the dimensions of employee voice quality. We propose that, on the one hand, employees with a high leader-member exchange will gain greater clarity about team structure (i.e., informational benefits), which helps increase the desirability and feasibility of their voice. On the other hand, employees with a high leader-member exchange will engage in more perspective-taking from the leader's perspective, which results in the incrementality of their voice (i.e., focusing on minor changes rather than proposing disruptive suggestions). A leader-member dyadic sample of 379 participants found that LMX increases voice desirability and feasibility through employees’ clarity about team structure. Contrary to our expectation, LMX did not influence voice incrementality through perspective-taking. This research contributes to voice research by shedding light on the enablers and constraints that a high-quality leader-member exchange imposes on employees’ voice quality.
