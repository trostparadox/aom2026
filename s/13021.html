Session overview

Organisation theory provides mature accounts of how firms coordinate judgment under uncertainty, allocate decision rights, and secure legitimacy. These frameworks assume human actors occupy the seat of consequential judgment. When algorithmic systems participate in decisions across strategy, finance, and risk, this assumption no longer holds. This workshop introduces Authentic Intelligence as a construct naming what AI-native organisations require beyond accuracy: decisions that command authority (recognised as binding), sustain legitimacy (defensible under scrutiny), and maintain trust (accepted as sound). The construct becomes salient when AI participates in decisions that are consequential, contested, and institutionally visible. Drawing on 50 executive interviews and 408 survey responses, we surface persistent misalignment between strategic intent and governance capacity. Participants engage in a four-stage Authentic Intelligence Diagnostic Cycle: diagnosing authority distribution, auditing legitimacy mechanisms, stress-testing trust resilience, and designing governance repairs. The PDW generates reusable research artefacts, including a completed diagnostic canvas, a research question bank, and starter propositions, enabling participants to seed cumulative theorising around Authentic Intelligence within OMT.
