Scaling Green Hydrogen: Drivers, Barriers, and Collaboration Configurations in Germany

ONE
Andrea Capogrosso (Technical University of Munich); Valentin Tiepel (Technical University of Munich)
Green hydrogen is widely seen as a key option for decarbonizing Germany’s hard-to-abate industries, yet evidence on what enables or constrains domestic electrolysis projects remains fragmented across techno-economic, systems, policy, and acceptance research streams. We systematically review 50 peer-reviewed studies on green-hydrogen production in Germany and, using bibliometric clustering (VOSviewer) and structured content analysis with vote counting, integrate adoption evidence across streams. We organize the literature into five recurring clusters and synthesize six cross-cutting driver-barrier domains. Here we show that the evidence base is dominated by economic viability and market/infrastructure feasibility, together accounting for 45% of all coded adoption factors, while societal-perception constraints receive comparatively limited attention despite their potential to become binding in implementation. We further examine a focused subset of 35 studies on inter-organizational coordination and identify three recurring collaboration configurations: spatial supply-chain arrangements, multi-actor governance formats (e.g., regional cluster/valley initiatives), and infrastructure-/offtake-oriented coordination mechanisms that connect supply and demand and support bankability. Together, the resulting adoption heatmap and collaboration typology offer an integrated framework to pinpoint bottlenecks and align coordination choices for project development and scale-up in Germany and comparable industrial-transition contexts.

Navigating Divergence: The Response of QFIIs to Local-Global ESG Rating Divergence in China

ONE
Juzhang Feng (South China Normal University); ChenYi Wei (South China Normal University); Yunyue Zhou (South China Normal University)
This study examines how Qualified Foreign Institutional Investors (QFIIs) respond to divergence between local and global environmental-social-and-governance (ESG) ratings assigned to firms, addressing the strategic dilemma between conservative and aggressive investment. Integrating insights from prospect theory and signaling theory, we investigate the effect of ESG rating divergence on QFII investment decisions. Using panel data from Chinese A-share firms between 2011 and 2023, we find that greater divergence in ESG ratings significantly increases both the likelihood and the scale of QFII investment. This effect is asymmetric and occurs primarily when local ESG ratings are more favorable than global ratings. Further analysis indicates that the reduction of information asymmetry serves as the underlying mechanism. In addition, the relationship is strengthened by higher corporate risk-taking and weakened by higher industry concentration. Our findings help resolve previous strategic dilemma, showing how foreign institutional investors actively interpret ESG rating divergences as opportunities through information processing. The study contributes to the expanding literature on sustainable strategic investment in emerging markets.

Stakeholders, Scales, and Expertise in Social-Ecological Challenges: The Case of Water in Spain

ONE
Daniel Arenas; Francois Herve Collet (ESADE Business School); Lucie Baudoin (ESCP Business School)
To cope adequately with social-ecological challenges such as local impacts of climate disruption, biodiversity erosion or depletion of natural resources, stakeholder participation is widely recognized as crucial in governance processes. This article contends that such participation hinges on two often-overlooked inter-related dimensions: the cross-scale characteristics of these challenges and the plurality and evolution of expertise surrounding them. Through a longitudinal analysis of Spain’s water governance spanning over a century, we identify three phases (the last one with two branches) that demonstrate the dynamic interdependence between stakeholders, scale, and expertise—revealing how shifts in one dimension lead to changes in the others. Building on this analysis, we propose a conceptual model that elucidates these interactions and their implications for perceptions of social-ecological challenges and decision-making. Our findings underscore that the participation of a more diverse set of stakeholders in the collaborative governance of such challenges goes hand in hand with a pluralistic view of expertise and a cross-scale perspective—one that transcends traditional administrative and disciplinary boundaries and is adaptive to the complexity of these challenges.

Impacts of Environmental Technology Patents on GHG Emissions: Evidence from OECD Countries

ONE
Chi-Lin Yang (University of Taipei)
Global warming is an important issue worldwide, and countries must reduce their greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. This study investigates the impact of environmental technology patents and socioeconomic variables on GHG emissions across 38 OECD countries from 2005 to 2020. The findings indicate that environmental technology patents have a significantly positive impact on emissions due to rebound effects. In terms of energy consumption, the adoption of renewable energy can significantly reduce emissions; however, the use of fossil fuels is positively and significantly related to emissions. In the economic field, the growth of GDP and population leads to increased emissions. However, R&D expenditure, female educational attainment, and unemployment rates are negatively associated with emissions. R&D improvements in sustainable technologies, enhanced female education, and increased climate awareness and behavioral change can indirectly decrease emissions. Overall, the results demonstrate that technological innovation cannot achieve meaningful decarbonization without supportive regulatory frameworks and broader socioeconomic measures. Policy implications include mitigating rebound effects through targeted regulation, accelerating the adoption of renewable energy, phasing out fossil fuels, expanding investments in low-carbon R&D, and strengthening human capital, particularly through education for women. These insights offer valuable guidance for OECD countries to achieve the SDGs with zero-carbon emissions.
