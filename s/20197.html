Designing Algorithmic Performance Evaluation

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Author: Pingxuan Chen University of Leeds

Session overview

Algorithmic performance evaluation (APE) is rapidly diffusing beyond platform work into conventional organizations, where it is used to monitor behavior, score performance, and inform high-stakes decisions. Yet APE is not a single practice: employees encounter distinct design configurations that vary in transparency, monitoring intrusiveness, and opportunities for human review. Building on the job demands-resources (JD-R) model and organizational justice theory, we develop a design-oriented framework explaining when APE functions as a hindrance demand that triggers threat appraisals and strain, and when it functions as an enabling resource that supports challenge appraisals and constructive engagement. We distinguish purpose transparency (clarity about why metrics are collected and how they align with goals) from process transparency (clarity about how data are transformed into scores), and argue that these transparency dimensions have asymmetric effects: purpose transparency primarily reduces uncertainty and supports challenge appraisal, whereas extensive process transparency can create cognitive load and heighten threat appraisal when monitoring is highly intrusive. We further theorize that human-in-the-loop contestability (voice, appeal, and supervisor review) strengthens procedural and interpersonal justice, buffering threat appraisals and enhancing trust in the evaluation system. To test these claims, we propose a two-study design: (1) a multi-wave field survey of employees subject to APE, measuring perceived design features, challenge-threat appraisals, well-being, and behavioral intentions; and (2) a vignette-based conjoint experiment that estimates employees’ preferences across APE design attributes (purpose transparency, process transparency, intrusiveness, and contestability) and their downstream fairness, stress, and acceptance judgments. This research contributes by shifting the algorithmic management debate from “algorithm versus human” to “which configuration works for whom,” offering actionable guidance for HR leaders designing APE systems that promote learning and engagement while minimizing strain.
