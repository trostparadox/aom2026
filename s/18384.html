Reducing Information Asymmetry of Expected Higher Pay: Pay Transparency and Employee Creativity

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Huaiqian Zhu Renmin University of China; Yu Zhou Renmin University of China

Session overview

Pay transparency has become an increasingly prevalent organizational practice, yet its implications for employee creativity remain theoretically and empirically underdeveloped. This study examines how pay transparency influences employee creativity from an informational and motivational perspective. Integrating information asymmetry theory and expectancy theory, we propose that pay transparency motivates employees to engage in diverse information searching, which subsequently enhances creativity. We further argue that this process is contingent on pay for individual performance. Using a two-wave, multi-source field survey and a real-organization simulation, the findings demonstrate a moderated mediation model linking pay transparency to employee creativity through diverse information searching. The findings enrich pay transparency literature, pay-creativity literature and expectancy theory.
