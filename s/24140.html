The Political Economy of Sustainability-Reporting Standardization

CMS
Issam Benhayoun (Moulay Ismail University)
The consolidation of sustainability reporting into globally harmonised standards, led by the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), marks a profound reconfiguration of accounting’s role in governance. Framed as a technical response to demands for comparability and transparency, this process embeds social and ecological concerns within market-oriented calculative logics. This paper offers a critical–interpretive synthesis of sustainability-reporting scholarship published between 2011 and 2025, examining global standardisation through the analytical vantage point of emerging economies, where its power effects and epistemic consequences are most sharply revealed. Drawing on 31 peer-reviewed studies, the review shows a field dominated by institutional and legitimacy theories, methodologically reliant on audit-oriented quantification, and epistemically skewed toward Northern contexts. Sustainability reporting thus increasingly appears as an auditable artefact rather than a site of democratic accountability, marginalising alternative moral, social, and ecological understandings of responsibility. The paper develops a Political Economy of Sustainability Reporting framework that integrates institutional, legitimacy, and postcolonial perspectives, reframing standardisation as an exercise of epistemic power that restructures accountability and narrows the political imagination of accounting.
