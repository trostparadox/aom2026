Fuel or Poison? How Attribution Shapes Helping Behavior's Impact on Motivation and Job Crafting

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Authors: Ejun Jang Seoul National University; Jin Nam Choi Seoul National University

Session overview

Helping behavior is often assumed to motivate employees and encourage proactive work behaviors, yet recipients do not always respond positively to help. Drawing on attribution theory, this study examines how employees’ interpretations of why they receive help shape its motivational consequences. We propose that helping behavior enhances achievement motivation when it is attributed to altruistic motives but loses its motivational value when attributed to instrumental motives. Achievement motivation, in turn, promotes proactive job redesign in the form of task and relational crafting, while reducing job inertia. Using two-wave, multi-source data from employee-supervisor dyads in Korean organizations, multilevel analyses show that instrumental attribution weakens, whereas altruistic attribution strengthens, the positive relationship between received help and achievement motivation. Moreover, the indirect effects of helping behavior on job crafting and inertia via achievement motivation are contingent on attribution. These findings highlight attribution as a critical psychological filter that explains when helping behavior fosters proactive motivation versus passivity.
