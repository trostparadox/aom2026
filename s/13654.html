Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) tools become embedded in students’ academic work, traditional written assignments increasingly fail to provide reliable evidence of student learning. AI-generated writing represents growing challenges for assurance of learning, fair assessment, and academic integrity. In response, many faculty are exploring oral assignments and oral exams, yet remain concerned about consistency, fairness, scalability, and instructor workload (Bayley, Maclean, and Weidner, 2024). This PDW positions AI-enabled oral assessment, such as oral exams, structured role-plays, and graded team discussions, as a developmental, rigorous, and adaptive alternative to written work in management education. Research on dialogic and interactive learning demonstrates that oral and discussion-based activities promote deeper cognitive engagement, reasoning-in-action, and communicative competence by requiring students to articulate, defend, and adapt ideas in real time (Alexander, 2008; Mercer, 2000; Chi, 2009). Meta-analytic evidence further confirms that interactive pedagogies outperform text-dominated approaches in producing durable learning outcomes (Freeman et al., 2014). Research on AI-enabled learning analytics positions AI as a mechanism for making oral assessment fairer, more transparent, and more instructionally useful, all without replacing instructor judgment (George & Wooden, 2023). Drawing on our experience in undergraduate and graduate OB and Leadership courses, this workshop will coach faculty on designing and running AI-assisted oral exercises and assessments that ensure student learning and preserve instructor control while leveraging AI capabilities. Participants will review existing AI-enabled tools, experience a mock oral exam, analyze and assess oral performance using AACSB Assurance of Learning aligned rubrics, and redesign a “traditional” written assignment into an oral format.
