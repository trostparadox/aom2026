AI Adoption in Small Businesses: Founder Cognition and Persistent Partial Adoption

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6
ENT

People

Author: Meghana Sridhar Arizona State University

Session overview

AI adoption theories derive from large organizations with formalized structures. This multi-method study examines how 70 small business founders adopt and use AI under resource constraints. Drawing on surveys and interviews, we document three patterns challenging linear adoption models: (1) persistent partial adoption as stable equilibrium, not transitional phase; (2) ceremonial adoption driven by legitimacy-seeking despite private skepticism; (3) cognitive resistance where AI violates work philosophies, not capability. We theorize that founder cognition acts as both adoption mechanism and organizational context in SMBs, creating non-linear, identity-driven patterns. Findings extend entrepreneurial cognition theory to technology adoption, challenge linear diffusion models, and offer practical insights for SMB support programs and AI vendors.
