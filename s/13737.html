Session overview

Impression management is central to how individuals navigate workplace relationships, yet well-intentioned self-presentational efforts often fail or backfire. This symposium brings together four papers that advance understanding of how impression management is interpreted, misinterpreted, and evaluated in organizational contexts. Collectively, the papers illuminate how impressions hinge on strategic signals, contextual cues, and asymmetric beliefs between actors and observers. Across diverse organizational settings, including supervisor–employee relationships, client-facing interactions, and early-stage relationship formation, the papers show that impressions are dependent not only on what individuals say or do, but also on what they omit, and on how situational cues alter the meaning of self-presentational behaviors. Drawing on multiple methodological approaches, including natural language processing, field surveys, and controlled experiments—and leveraging data from real-world online profiles and organizational contexts—this symposium offers a multifaceted examination of impression management in the workplace.
