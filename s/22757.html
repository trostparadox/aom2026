Positive Workplace Deviance as Communication: An Integrative Review and Solve-Signal-Serve Framework

OB
John Razzouk (University of North Carolina at Greensboro); Madelynn R. D. Stackhouse (University of North Carolina at Greensboro)
Although positive deviance - intentional behaviors that depart from the norms of a referent group in honorable ways has gained traction in organizational research, existing studies lack a unified theoretical framework for understanding its forms and underlying motivations. Scholars have emphasized moral or organizational imperatives, while overlooking another vital pathway: the communicative role of positive deviance as a deliberate signal. This article makes three key contributions to the literature. First, we systematically review peer-reviewed scholarship on positive, constructive, and creative deviance, and integrate insights from adjacent literatures on voice, whistleblowing, and organizational citizenship behavior. Second, drawing on signaling theory, dramaturgical perspectives, and motivational perspectives, we develop a triadic Solve-Signal-Serve dynamic model of positive deviance, that includes motivational mechanisms and processual unfolding processes. The triadic model sees positive deviance as a form of performance and focal mirror designed to convey meaning, within a framework that explains how employees engage in positive deviance to - solve problems, serve hypernorm or stakeholder values, or signal dissent from a referent-norm - often in combination. Third, we resolve incompatibilities between multiple conceptualizations of positive forms of deviance within the workplace deviance stream. This opens fertile ground for our future research agenda particularly exploring positive deviance as communicative in intention, action, and interpretation.

Unintended Ethical Consequences of Workplace Fun: Positive Emotions and Optimism in Driving UPB

OB
Muhammad Tufail (Macquarie University); Naseeb Dar (IESE Business School); Rahman Ullah
This research aims to investigate the influence of workplace fun as a potential antecedent of unethical pro-organizational behavior. It assesses whether positive work experiences can motivate employees to take actions that benefit the organization but contravene established ethical standards. To test this proposition, we collected data from various organizations, focusing on measures of workplace fun, positive emotional states, optimism, and unethical pro-organizational behavior. The study employs structural equation modeling to evaluate the hypothesized mediating and moderating relationships. Findings indicate that workplace fun fosters unethical pro-organizational behavior by enhancing positive emotions. Additionally, optimism moderates this relationship, with highly optimistic employees exhibiting a diminished emotional response to workplace enjoyment. Theoretically, this study demonstrates that positive organizational practices may, under certain circumstances, inadvertently promote unethical conducts. It emphasizes positive emotions as a critical mediating factor and identifies optimism as a boundary condition influencing this process. Practically, organizational managers should foster a positive work environment while implementing ethical safeguards to mitigate the risk of unintended pro-organizational misconduct.

The Nexus between CSR and Sustainability Performance in Hospitality: A Moderated Mediation Analysis

OB
Fahad Alam (Tongji University); Ahmad Nisar (Wuhan University)
This study examines the impact of corporate social responsibility (CSR) on sustainability performance dimensions in the hospitality sector by proposing a moderated mediation model grounded in the resource-based view (RBV) theory. Specifically, the model investigates (i) the direct effect of CSR on sustainability-oriented commitment, sustainable operational efficiency, and sustainable competitive advantage, (ii) the mediating role of sustainable human resource management (SHRM), and (iii) the moderating role of organizational cynicism in the CSR-SHRM relationship. Data were collected from 141 hotels in China using a time-lagged survey design, and the model was tested with Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). Results from the analysis reveal that CSR positively and significantly influences all three sustainability performance dimensions and SHRM. SHRM was found to partially mediate the CSR-sustainability performance relationship, while organizational cynicism negatively moderates the CSR-SHRM link, indicating that employee skepticism weakens the effectiveness of CSR in fostering SHRM practices. These findings advance theoretical understanding of the mechanisms through which CSR translates into sustainability outcomes and emphasize the importance of addressing employee perceptions. Practical implications highlight the need for hotel managers to embed CSR into HRM systems and to mitigate cynicism through transparent and authentic CSR initiatives in order to achieve long-term sustainability and competitive advantage.

The Direct and Indirect Effects of Corporate Social Responsibility Authenticity on Whistleblowing

OB
FACUNDO GARCIA PEREYRA (IQS School of Management - Universitat Ramon Llull); Monica Riviere Rogoz (ISC Paris Business School); Youssef Souak (INSEEC School of Business and Economics)
Drawing on Social Information Processing Theory and Social Exchange Theory, this study aims to examine the extent to which employees’ perceptions of corporate social responsibility (CSR) authenticity encourage internal whistleblowing. Specifically, we first assess whether employees who perceive their organizations’ CSR initiatives as authentic are more likely to engage in internal whistleblowing. Additionally, we test whether affective commitment and workplace well-being mediate this relationship. Finally, we examine whether these effects vary across four Western countries (i.e., France, Italy, Spain, and the United States). Across two studies (one cross-sectional field study and one experimental vignette study), we demonstrate that CSR authenticity positively influences employees’ intentions to engage in internal whistleblowing across all countries examined. Furthermore, workplace well-being positively mediates this relationship in all four countries. However, our results indicate that affective commitment does not significantly mediate the relationship between CSR authenticity and internal whistleblowing in Italy and Spain.
