Session overview

This symposium explores how evaluative authority shapes social evaluations in an era of increasing institutional fragmentation. As traditional sources of evaluative authority – such as governments, universities, and mainstream media – lose credibility, newer actors, including platforms, rating agencies, and activist networks gain influence over how what is collectively valued is both understood and assessed. These shifts signal a broader redistribution of who is recognized as having the right to judge what counts as valuable, why, and for whom. Bringing together leading and emerging scholars, the symposium examines how changes in evaluative authority influence the emergence, stabilization, and transformation of key social evaluations, including legitimacy, reputation, status, authenticity, stigma, and trust. Through dialogue across theoretical and methodological approaches, participants will reflect on these evolving foundations for social evaluation research and identify new directions for understanding evaluative authority in management and organization studies.
