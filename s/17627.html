A Contingent Social Exchange Model of Leader Curiosity

Authors: Haeseen Park Bowling Green State University; Kyoungjo (Jo) Oh The University of Connecticut; Wonbin Sohn University of Hawaii at Mänoa; Hye Joon Park The Pennsylvania State University; Kwanghyun Kim Korea University; Yuqing(Alice) Liu The University of Connecticut

Session overview

Curiosity is increasingly recognized as a valuable trait in today’s dynamic and uncertain work environments. Yet, we still know surprisingly little about how a leader’s curiosity shapes follower outcomes. In this study, we draw on social exchange theory and cognitive load theory to explore the relational and contextual processes involved. Specifically, we propose a moderated mediation model in which leader curiosity enhances the quality of leader-member exchange (LMX), which in turn boosts follower task performance and proactive behavior. However, this positive effect depends on task complexity: when tasks become more complex, the beneficial link between leader curiosity and LMX weakens, reducing its overall impact on followers. We tested our hypotheses using time-lagged, multisource survey data from 232 leader-follower dyads. The results support our model, showing that leader curiosity is positively related to LMX quality, which mediates its effects on follower outcomes. Importantly, these indirect effects are less pronounced when task complexity is high. By framing leader curiosity as a relational signal within leader-follower exchanges, this research contributes to the curiosity literature, extends LMX theory with a novel antecedent and boundary condition, and offers a more nuanced understanding of when curiosity-driven leadership truly matters.
