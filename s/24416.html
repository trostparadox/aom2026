Influence of Leading Organization in Tourism Destinations for Creating Shared Value

STR
Miwa Nishinaka (Kagawa University)
High-quality tourism destination development requires leadership grounded in values. This study empirically investigated residents’ engagement, shared values, and the intention to sustain the areas as a destination, drawing on Stakeholder Theory and the Creating Shared Value (CSV) framework. A mediation analysis was performed to assess the influence of a leading regional organization that has transformed the Setouchi Islands into high-quality tourist destinations. Data from 1,124 resident respondents were analyzed. Results indicate that on islands where the organization’s influence is strong, shared values partially mediate the relationship between residents’ engagement and their intention to sustain the destination; engagement alone suffices to generate sustainable intentions, irrespective of the statistical significance of shared values. Conversely, on islands with weak organizational influence, shared values fully mediate this relationship, implying that sustainable intentions arise only when residents perceive shared values positively. These findings demonstrate that the presence of a leading organization fosters shared values, thereby enhancing residents’ engagement and intention to sustain tourism development. This study thus provides empirical support for CSV as a strategic tool in tourism destination management and underscores the pivotal role of leading organizations in cultivating shared values and qualitative tourism outcomes.
