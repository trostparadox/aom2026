Session overview

Nationalism—an ideology that prioritizes the interests, identity, and sovereignty of the nation-state—has profound implications for corporations. Some firms find themselves constrained by protectionist policies, immigration restrictions, and geopolitical rivalries, while others actively leverage nationalism to build brand identity, undermine foreign competitors, or rally support from domestic stakeholders. Although nationalism has long been studied at the state and individual levels, the research on its operation at the organizational level has been fragmented. Recently the framework of organizational nationalism has been proposed to systematically investigate how corporations and nonprofit organizations can adopt, enact, and propagate nationalist values, symbols, and narratives through their discourses, strategies, and interactions with the broader sociopolitical environment. This lens positions organizations as intermediaries that both shape and are shaped by individuals’ national identity and the state’s nationalism policies. Drawing on insights from five subject-matter experts, this PDW seeks to advance and integrate this nascent literature by exploring foundational questions about the conceptual boundaries, diverse manifestations, underlying mechanisms, and organizational consequences of nationalism. Through interactive discussions and collaborative breakout sessions, participants will gain fresh perspectives and practical guidance for studying nationalism in a global environment marked by rising geopolitical tensions and resurgent national identities.
