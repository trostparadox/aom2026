Compliance Creep: Negative Spaces of Regulation in Generative AI Inter-Organizational Relationships (WITHDRAWN)

HCM
Edoardo Trincanato (Not Associated); Emidia Vagnoni (University of Ferrara); Nicola Franco
Generative AI (GAI) cooperation increasingly takes the form of long-term inter-organizational relationships (IORs) with vendors, platform providers, and public agencies. A core strategic challenge becomes allocating control rights at runtime—who can detect emerging risks and who can legitimately stop, roll back or reconfigure the systems. In regulated settings, organizations often assume that standards, contracts, and regulatory compliance will safeguard performance and safety. Yet many collaborations can drift under compliance: while remaining formally aligned, they may face rising safety exposure and cooperation frictions as intervention becomes slow or infeasible. We address this paradox by asking how the design of human–GAI decision boundaries in AI-focused IORs undermines runtime controllability. Drawing abductively on a qualitative documentary multi-case of 64 GAI collaborations in public hospitals, we introduce negative spaces of regulation: compliance-produced pockets within dense regulatory and contractual architectures where runtime sensing and legitimate, actionable stop/rollback rights do not overlap. We show how such negative spaces widen signal-to-stop distance and, under tight workflow coupling and incremental system updates, heighten assurance drift exposure, a growing gap between ex ante safety assurances and evolving risk conditions. We identify five recurring failure modes through which negative spaces translate into drift and derive design principles for restoring runtime authority alignment as GAI IORs evolve. Our final process model clarifies when formally compliant collaborations become operationally fragile and offers actionable guidance for governing runtime AI safety in regulated, multi-actor innovation collaboration.
