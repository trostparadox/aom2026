Consulting for Integrity in SMEs: An Intervention Case Study of Agile Anti-Corruption

MC
Jocelyne Boulos Eid
How do management consulting interventions help small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) build anti-corruption capabilities when formal institutions are weak and informal pressures are strong? Drawing on Responsible Management-as-Practice (RMAP) (Gherardi & Laasch, 2022), agility theory (Wang & Ahmed, 2007; Motwani & Katatria, 2024), and management consulting scholarship on knowledge co-production (Kipping & Clark, 2012), this study conceptualizes consulting for integrity as a practice-based governance mechanism. Empirically, we analyze a longitudinal intervention conducted in a Lebanese family-owned exchange house, Cedrus Exchange, using the Socio-Economic Approach to Management (SEAM) (Savall & Zardet, 2008). Based on interviews, focus groups, documentary materials, and SEAM diagnostics (HoriVert analysis and hidden-cost assessment), we trace how consulting tools and participatory routines produced four interrelated governance mechanisms: distributed ethical leadership, compliance sense making, proactive risk vigilance, and employee moral agency. We contribute to management consulting research by showing how integrity-oriented consulting creates durable capabilities rather than delivering checklist compliance; to RMAP by specifying how responsibility is enacted through consulting-enabled routines; and to corruption research by identifying micro-mechanisms of resistance in institutional voids. The study offers implications for consultants designing ethics and compliance interventions for SMEs in fragile contexts.
