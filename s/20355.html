Redeploying Data for Competitive Advantage

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Author: Wendy Bradley

Session overview

Traditional antitrust theories, developed in the context of industrial-era firms, emphasize price-based measures and industry concentration to assess market power. However, many digital industries are zero-price markets, where these metrics fail to identify anti-competitive behavior. This paper proposes that data consolidation, or the accumulation and redeployment of data across markets, is a more relevant measure of market power in the digital economy. Drawing on resource redeployment theory, this paper proposes a theoretical model in which firms with large, redeployable data portfolios gain competitive advantage, enabling market expansion without triggering anti-trust regulation. Unlike traditional resources, data is intangible, scalable at near-zero cost, inconsistently valued, and often unobservable. These features make it uniquely difficult to regulate under current antitrust law. The paper proposes a set of propositions for measuring data-driven market power and argue that asymmetries in data ownership contribute to enduring dominance of a few, technology firms in digital industries. These insights call for a reorientation of antitrust scrutiny toward data-centric indicators of competitive harm.
