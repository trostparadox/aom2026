How AI Washing Undermines Firm Value? A Stakeholder Decision-Making Perspective

ODC
Xingying Wu
As AI emerges as a critical indicator of competitiveness, firms’ AI washing (AIWASH) behavior, where firms’ AI-related actions fall short of their claims, is becoming increasingly prevalent. However, research on its economic consequences and underlying mechanisms remains limited. Using a sample of Chinese firms from 2007 to 2024, this study investigates how AIWASH affects firm value by shaping the decisions of core stakeholders. Our findings reveal that AIWASH exerts a significant negative impact on firm value. This result holds across several endogeneity tests and robustness checks, including two-stage least squares regression, Heckman test, propensity score matching, and alternative measurement approaches. Moreover, this effect is more pronounced among high-tech firms, those operating in highly dynamic technological environments, and firms with a higher proportion of managers possessing AI backgrounds. Mechanism analyses indicate that the main effect operates through four channels: the loss of technical employees, increased cost of debt, weakened customer stability, and diminished investor confidence. Overall, this study provides novel evidence on the economic costs of AIWASH, offering insights for regulating firms’ AI-related disclosure and conduct and for promoting the healthy development of the technology industry.
