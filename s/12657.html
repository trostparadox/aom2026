Session overview

Organizations increasingly operate in environments shaped not only by market forces but also by diverse non-market stakeholders. Actors such as NGOs, lobbying firms, social movements and local communities hold expectations, exert influence, and control resources that can support or challenge firms. Their actions shape access to cooperation, legitimacy, and approvals, affecting organizational behavior and long-term value creation. To pursue their goals, non-market stakeholders deploy tactics ranging from public campaigns and media mobilization to legal actions and negotiation. These activities impose reputational, operational, and regulatory pressures and influence how other audiences evaluate firms. In response, firms increasingly adopt strategies to build more favorable non-market environments or address societal concerns. Notwithstanding these advancements, we still need a better understanding of how firms navigate strategic interactions with non-market stakeholders, which mechanisms foster constructive engagement, and how these relationships shape outcomes across market and non-market domains. As societal scrutiny intensifies and stakeholder demands grow, understanding these dynamics is crucial for explaining contemporary firm behavior and performance. This PDW focuses on how organizations strategically engage with non-market stakeholders, such as NGOs, social movements, and regulatory actors. The PDW will consist of two parts. In the first part, which is open to all registered AOM attendees, leading scholars will present their research and engage in a panel discussion on strategic interactions with non-market stakeholders, highlighting key mechanisms, theoretical perspectives, and emerging research opportunities. The second part of the session will be more interactive and will involve developmental feedback on participants’ research ideas. Participants interested in this segment are invited to submit a one-page extended abstract in advance. Due to time constraints, a limited number of submissions will be selected for feedback by the panelists. Further details on the submission process and organization of the interactive segment will be shared on the AOM STR Discussion Board.
