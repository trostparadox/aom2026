Why Kyoto Creates Century-Old Firms: A Quantitative Comparative Analysis of City-Based Ecosystems

Authors: Akira Nakashima; Yukihiro HAYASHI; Yoshimasa Uematsu Hitotsubashi University; Masaki Toyoda Hitotsubashi University; Kazuhito Isomura Chuo University

Session overview

This study aims to investigate why Kyoto has the world’s highest concentration of century-old firms—defined as firms founded more than 100 years ago per city-level firm population—and to examine the structural mechanisms that enable long-term corporate survival at the city level. Building on our qualitative research presented at AOM 2025, the 2026 project develops quantitative, international comparative, and historical analyses to theorize the “Kyoto Model” as a city-based ecosystem. Our 2025 study emphasized that management philosophy, craftsmanship, and community ties play important roles in sustaining long-established companies. However, it did not sufficiently explore the systemic factors that allow Kyoto to continuously produce long-lived firms. To address this research gap, we construct a quantitative framework that compares Kyoto with other enduring industrial regions such as Tuscany, Bavaria, and Silicon Valley. Using survival analysis, network analysis, and publicly available regional statistics—including firm age distributions, exit rates, industrial structures, and research linkages—we analyze how city-based ecosystems influence firm longevity. We also conduct field research and interviews with long-established Kyoto firms to clarify how theoretically designed concepts of ecosystems are embedded in actual business practices. This mixed-method approach uncovers how Kyoto’s cultural institutions, inter-firm collaboration norms, and regionally embedded knowledge systems interact to support sustainable business models. Thus, the study contributes to OMT by theorizing city-based ecosystems as meso-level infrastructure for firm longevity and by proposing a comparative model of how cities cultivate long-term corporate resilience.
