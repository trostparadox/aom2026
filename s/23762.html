Quiet Ones – Adapting to Increased Repression Through the Visual Images in Authoritarian Context

ENT
Denis Borodin (emlyon Business School)
Entrepreneurs in authoritarian contexts face acute resource scarcity and heightened risks when communicating with external audiences, yet we know little about how they strategically use multimodal communication to secure the resources needed for survival and resilience. This paper asks: how do entrepreneurs in creative industries create and communicate images and texts to solicit resources under intensified political repression? Drawing on an inductive multiple-case study of six founder-led ventures in Russia’s creative industries (streetwear and accessories, podcasting, and animation) during the post-2022 crackdown. Findings conceptualize intensified repression as political adversity marked by three resource-depleting features: polarization, authoritarianism, and identity crisis, that constrain financial, emotional, cognitive, and relational capacities. Entrepreneurs respond through three distinct multimodal communication strategies: signaling, repositioning, and manipulation. Across strategies, entrepreneurs build an externally oriented resilience capability - emotion-ideational connecting, through which they achieve emotional and ideational alignment with key audience segments, mobilizing “greater-than-expected” emotional, relational, and at times financial support despite severe communicative constraints. The paper advances research on entrepreneurial communication by shifting attention beyond investor-focused signaling in stable environments, extends resilience scholarship by theorizing capability development as relational and interactional, and enriches authoritarian entrepreneurship research by detailing how founders creatively manage visibility, legitimacy, and identity under political threat.
