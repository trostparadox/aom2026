The Impact of Board Gender Composition on Female Managers’ Motivation to Lead

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Shora Moteabbed George Mason University; Shiva Taghavi NEOMA Business School; Maria Kakarika Durham University Business School

Session overview

The underrepresentation of women in upper-level management has been a persistent concern in the management literature, with a substantial body of research demonstrating that gender diversity in top management teams is positively associated with firm-level outcomes. However, the micro-level consequences of such diversity for female employees—particularly female managers—remain insufficiently understood. Across two studies, we examine how awareness of women serving on boards of directors influences female managers’ gender–leader identity and affective motivation to lead. Results from Study 1 indicate that a higher proportion of women on boards is associated with lower affective motivation to lead among female middle managers. This relationship is mediated by increased gender–leader identity conflict, suggesting that exposure to senior women may activate identity-based tensions rather than straightforward role-modeling effects. In Study 2, we further investigate the psychological mechanism underlying this effect by examining the emotion of awe experienced when female managers gain deeper awareness of female board members through reading their biographies. Findings show that exposure to directors’ biographies heightens feelings of awe, which in turn increases gender–leader identity conflict and subsequently reduces affective motivation to lead. Together, these findings highlight a paradoxical effect of female representation at the top of organizations. We plan conducting future studies that will examine whether presenting richer narratives of female directors’ career trajectories can foster identification and transform senior women into positive role models, thereby reducing identity conflict and enhancing motivation to lead for female middle managers.
