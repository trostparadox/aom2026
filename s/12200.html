Session overview

Management education increasingly seeks to address complex societal and organizational challenges that no single sector can solve alone. In response, educators are designing programmes that bring together policymakers, academics, industry leaders, entrepreneurs, and civil-society actors. Yet while such multistakeholder programmes are proliferating, they remain under-theorised and under-designed as objects of management education, as existing frameworks largely privilege single-actor learning models and offer limited guidance on aligning multiple stakeholders and educational objectives (Starkey & Madan, 2001; Hodgkinson & Starkey, 2011).This PDW is structured as a Co-Design and Simulation Studio that treats multistakeholder programmes as educational systems rather than collections of modules. Drawing on anonymised design artefacts from an existing international, multi-actor programme, participants examine the tensions inherent in designing education across institutional boundaries, including learning versus legitimacy, academic rigour versus action, openness versus governance, and innovation versus viability. Through guided deconstruction and collaborative design, participants work in small groups to prototype a multistakeholder learning programme aligned with explicit learning objectives, stakeholder roles, assessment logic, and sustainability constraints. Facilitated peer critique and synthesis connect participants’ designs to contemporary debates in management education on impact, relevance, and system-level learning. Participants leave with a transferable programme design canvas applicable across higher education, executive education, and policy-facing initiatives. This is a highly interactive co-design studio with limited capacity. Participants will work in small groups to prototype a multistakeholder learning programme and are expected to actively engage throughout. No prior preparation is required; however, participants may benefit from briefly reflecting on a programme or initiative they are currently designing or involved in. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
