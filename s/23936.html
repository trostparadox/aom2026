Uncovering the Effect of Gamification Stressors on Presenteeism: The Buffering Roles of Justices

TIM
Furong Jia (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Yan Pan (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Jie Yu (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Hock Hai Teo (National University of Singapore)
Gamification has been widely adopted by e-commerce platforms to increase user presence, particularly during high-intensity promotional events such as shopping festivals. However, increased presence does not necessarily reflect high-quality engagement. Users may continue participating despite experiencing psychological strain, a phenomenon conceptualized as presenteeism. Although gamified mechanisms are generally assumed to enhance engagement, limited research has examined presenteeism as an ambiguous outcome of gamification. Drawing on dual-process theory and equity theory, this study theorizes how gamification-induced stress operates through social overload and cognitive overload, and how these stress experiences shape users’ presenteeism intentions. We further conceptualize procedural and distributive justice as context-sensitive fairness mechanisms that condition how users cope with overload under varying levels of time pressure. Using a two-wave study in a gamified e-commerce context, we find that social overload increase users’ presenteeism intentions. Moreover, we show that time pressure dynamically reweights the effectiveness of justice mechanisms: distributive justice plays a stronger buffering role under high time pressure, whereas procedural justice becomes more salient under low time pressure. By introducing presenteeism as a critical outcome of gamification-induced stress and demonstrating the dynamic role of justice under temporal constraints, this study advances research on gamification, technostress, and fairness in digital platforms. The findings also offer practical insights for designing gamified systems that balance engagement intensity with users’ psychological sustainability.
