More Than Meets the Meme: Symbolic Exaptation and the Repurposing of Pepe the Frog

TIM
Rémi Le Goff (Montpellier Business School); Fakher Omezzine (Montpellier Business School)
This study examines how cultural artifacts can generate novelty through shifts in meaning rather than function. Drawing on a historical case study of Pepe the Frog, we trace how the same symbol was repeatedly reinterpreted across new contexts and communities. The analysis identifies four mechanisms of symbolic repurposing: discursive recontextualization, hybridization, commercialization, and reappropriation. We also show how socio-technical infrastructures condition these processes by shaping which meanings stabilize, fragment, or are monetized. The findings highlight both the fragility and the power of symbols, which can escape their originators yet mobilize collective action and create economic value. By theorizing symbolic exaptation, the paper broadens understandings of innovation beyond functional recombination and contributes to research on symbolic management, cultural entrepreneurship, and digital infrastructures.
