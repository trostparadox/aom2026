Gatekeeping Algorithms and Epistemic Harms: Candidate Adaptation, Resistance, and Refusal- AI Hiring

Authors: Damion Purfey Rennes School of Business; Carole Anna Denise Bonanni Rennes School of Business

Session overview

AI-enabled hiring systems have become key gateways to employment, yet their design often reproduces bias and exclusion, particularly for marginalized candidates. Fairness is frequently treated as a purely technical feature of applicant tracking systems (ATS), requiring candidates to navigate opaque and unaccountable algorithms. Drawing on interviews with 12 participants, this paper explores how intersectional, gender-diverse candidates experience and behave in ATS pre-screening through an epistemic injustice lens. Our contributions are threefold: empirically, we highlight candidate actions as justice-seeking practices; theoretically, we extend epistemic injustice scholarship through a finer-grained analysis of adaptation, resistance, and refusal; and managerially, we suggest reforms—including feedback loops, human review, and co-design—to identify participatory elements that enhance fairness in AI-mediated recruitment. Keywords: Epistemic injustice, ATS, Intersectionality, Adaptation strategies, Resistance, Refusal
