Session overview

MNEs increasingly must strategize in a fractured environment marked by overlapping and conflicting stakeholder demands and institutional pressures. Rising geopolitical rivalry and populist politics intensified regulatory inconsistency and operational uncertainty. This panel convenes scholars to advance understanding of MNE responses to such fragmentation. Panelists will discuss (1) how trade and tariff policy affect firm strategy; (2) how geopolitical resource dependence drives supply-chain reconfiguration, including friend-shoring and decoupling dynamics; (3) ways by which MNEs can exploit the political resources of their home countries; and (4) to what degree the academic and public discussion on de-globalization aligns with recent data.
