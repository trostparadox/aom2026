Response Expectations as a Driver of Techno-Invasion and Work-Life Balance Erosion

Session overview

Digital connectivity promises flexibility, yet many organizations simultaneously cultivate response expectations that employees remain reachable during nonwork time. Drawing on conservation of resources (COR) theory, we conceptualize response expectations as a normative digital demand, distinct from technology use per se, that depletes employees’ resources by fostering techno-invasion, defined as technology-enabled intrusion of work into private life. We propose and test a moderated mediation model in which techno-invasion represents a central mechanism linking response expectations to work–life balance satisfaction. In addition, we theorize resilience as a personal resource that shapes employees’ capacity to recover from, rather than prevent, techno-invasion, thereby attenuating, but not eliminating, its negative consequences for work–life balance. Using survey data from 1,534 employees, we find support for a conditional indirect effect whereby response expectations are associated with lower work–life balance satisfaction via techno-invasion, and this indirect effect is weaker at higher levels of resilience. These findings advance research on digital work by distinguishing response expectations from technology use, positioning techno-invasion as a key explanatory mechanism linking availability norms to employee well-being, and clarifying the role of resilience as a downstream coping resource in contexts of chronic digital intrusion.
