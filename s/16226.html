Session overview

Robust social network research requires high-quality, contextually valid data. However, methods such as name generators – asking for distinct types of relationships – and types of interviews differ in suitability across diverse settings. As relational meanings and interaction norms vary, researchers must adapt their methods to local contexts and remain mindful of ethical considerations when conducting their study. While context-specific theorizing has gained increasing attention in management and entrepreneurship research (e.g., Baker & Welter, 2020; Wickert et al., 2024), this PDW shifts the focus to the methodological foundations that make such theorizing possible. The PDW brings together international scholars working on organizational, managerial, and entrepreneurial networks to examine four core methodological challenges: (1) identifying context-specific types of ties, (2) adapting qualitative and quantitative methods to diverse settings, (3) leveraging innovative and multimethod data sources, and (4) addressing the ethical complexities inherent in network data collection. Building on and extending the successful AOM 2025 panel symposium on social networks in non-Western settings, the PDW places particular, though not exclusive, emphasis on data collection in under-researched contexts (e.g., non-Western or extreme contexts), where commonly used methods, often rooted in Western research traditions, may not translate well without appropriate contextual adaptation. In addition to a keynote by a distinguished speaker and brief presentations by the facilitators, the session will include interactive roundtable discussions organized around the four core themes. These discussions will provide participants with opportunities to engage directly with experts and peers, share challenges and experiences from their own research, and explore concrete strategies for advancing context-sensitive, methodologically rigorous, and ethically responsible social network research. No registration is required for this PDW. The PDW offers participants the opportunity to discuss both their own experiences and early-stage ideas in roundtable discussions.
