Where Attention Goes, How Help Flows: Temporal Focus, Job Crafting, and Helping in the Workplace

OB
Yating Gao (China University of Geosciences Beijing); Jiang Xu (Beijing Sport University); Yizhen Feng (Inner Mongolia Normal University)
Employees vary not only in how much they help others at work, but also in whether their help empowers recipients to develop capabilities or provides immediate, non-empowering solutions. Drawing on self-regulation theory and conservation of resources theory, we propose that employees’ temporal focus —attention to the past, present, or future—shapes distinct job crafting strategies that, in turn, influence the form of help they provide. Temporal focus directs self-regulatory priorities and resource allocation, channeling employees toward task, relational, or cognitive crafting, each with distinct implications for helping behavior. Using multi-wave survey data, we find that past focus increases task and relational crafting but reduces cognitive crafting; present focus decreases task crafting and increases cognitive crafting; and future focus decreases task crafting and increases cognitive crafting, with neither present nor future focus affecting relational crafting. Task crafting is negatively related to both empowering and non-empowering help, relational crafting positively predicts empowering help only, and cognitive crafting positively predicts both forms of helping. Mediation analyses indicate that job crafting explains the indirect effects of temporal focus on helping behavior. Implications for theory and practice are discussed.
