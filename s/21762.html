Session overview

Firms are increasingly operating in institutional environments characterized by political volatility, regulatory uncertainty, and shifting social expectations. This symposium brings together five papers that offer an integrated, multi-level perspective on how firms navigate institutional change through political connections, influence strategies, disclosure choices, and internal adaptation. Spanning democratic and authoritarian contexts, formal and informal institutions, and external and internal organizational processes, the papers show how institutional change reshapes the risks, incentives, and effectiveness of non-market strategies and organizational responses. Together, this symposium advances a unified framework that bridges non-market strategy and organizational theory to explain how firms strategically respond to persistent institutional instability.
