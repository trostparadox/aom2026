Intermittent Discontinuance in Generative AI Systems: A Conservation of Resources Perspective on Use

CTO
Maria Akhtar (Zhengzhou University)
This research is based on the theory of Conservation of Resources (COR), where the antecedents of intermittent discontinuance in users of generative artificial intelligence (AI) systems are studied with the focus on user exhaustion and contextual determinants. The multi-method research design was employed including Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM), Importance- Performance Map Analysis (IPMA), and fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA). This combination method allowed the linear and configurational determinants of intermittent discontinuance to be assessed simultaneously. PLS-SEM results revealed that algorithm bias, misinformation and privacy concerns significantly increase usage exhaustion, which in turn promotes intermittent discontinuance. Conversely, switching costs, subjective norm, and irreplaceability enhance switching exhaustion, exerting a more complex influence on disengagement rather than a direct negative effect. Moderation analysis showed that perceived usefulness attenuates the impact of both types of exhaustion on discontinuance. IPMA highlighted the high relevance but suboptimal performance of exhaustion-related factors, suggesting opportunities to improve user experience. The fsQCA further identified algorithm bias and irreplaceability as core conditions in pathways leading to intermittent discontinuance, emphasizing non-linear and configurational dynamics. The findings indicate that the generative AI platforms must plan to decrease the use exhaustion, enhance perceived usefulness and manage the algorithmic bias and irreplaceability to maintain the user attention. The practitioners can be advised to adopt the multifaceted approach in the treatment of the user exhaustion and discontinuance. The existing literature is largely concerned with adoption and sustained use, the study is innovative and conducts the first study to examine passive disengagement behaviors. The study is a valuable addition to theoretical and methodological insights by explaining the mechanisms of intermittent discontinuance in terms of a resource-balance approach and demonstrating the synergistic value of linear and configurational analytical approaches.
