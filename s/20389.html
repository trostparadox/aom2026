Informational Justice in Bad News Conversations: A Double-Edged Sword?

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Authors: Taylor A Carroll University of Waterloo; Ramona Bobocel University of Waterloo

Session overview

Employees react negatively to receiving bad news at work; for instance, they may withdraw from their job. It is recommended in the literature that managers enact informational justice when delivering bad news, as it has been shown to alleviate negative reactions. Drawing on fairness theory and procedural justice research, we argue that informational justice is a double-edged sword in bad news conversations: it reduces organization-blame but increases self-blame, which produces opposing indirect effects on employees’ withdrawal. Across three studies using diverse methodologies, we found evidence of these opposing indirect effects. Importantly, self-efficacy attenuated the indirect effect via self-blame. Our research highlights the importance of promoting employees’ self-efficacy in addition to being informationally just in bad news conversations.
