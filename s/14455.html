Session overview

Transformative entrepreneurial learning is necessary if we are to change learners’ mindset at a deep cognitive level, e.g., the progress from the mindset of a novice in the direction of the mindset of an expert. This makes it imperative for us to understand what causes this and why it happens and how to implement that. In this symposium, we explore the impacts of short-term immersion learning using the globally successful Startup Weekend program. Startup Weekend participants report that it is an intense, often emotionally-charged experience, making this an ideal domain to assess physiological and cognitive impacts. Immersion experiences offer a long history of success at rapidly facilitating deep cognitive changes (e.g., Hamari, et al. 2016). Education theory provides the constructivistic model where learners not only acquire new knowledge content but also change how learners structure that knowledge. These deeper knowledge structures reflect cognitive phenomena such as scripts, schema, maps, etc. that rest upon deep anchoring assumptions that usually lie beneath conscious thought (Krueger 2007; Kaffka & Krueger 2019). Research tells us that it is difficult, if not impossible, for a learner to approach the mindset of an expert by only acquiring content knowledge; how they structure that knowledge matters even more. They get there via critical developmental experiences that change deep anchoring assumptions underneath their knowledge structures (McCall, et al. 1988; Krueger 2007). Do short-term/high-intensity programs provide those experiences? And how might we tell?
