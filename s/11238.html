Session overview

Because of the ever-increasing prevalence of the remote/virtual workforce and workers’ strong demands for locational flexibility, organizations are facing unprecedented pressures to embrace the remote/virtual workforce and navigate paths to manage this workforce successfully. This symposium aims to address such organizational needs by shedding new light on workplace characteristics that help the remote/virtual workforce flourish. By doing so, we seek to shift the extant literature’s focus on beneficial and detrimental implications of remote/virtual work to organization-, team-, and individual-level characteristics which improve remote/virtual workers’ attitudes, performance, and well-being. Specifically, utilizing diverse methodological approaches (e.g., multi-level study, scale development, meta-analysis, multi-wave survey study), the papers collectively study formal remote work policies (Hong & Lee study), perceived organizational support for virtual work (Keating et al. study), coworker support (Ponnapalli et al. study), virtual teams’ input, process, and emergent states (Greco et al. study), and monitoring and availability expectation (Ferreira & Golden study) as predictors of different remote/virtual worker outcomes (e.g., job satisfaction, loneliness, virtual team performance, and psychological safety). Together, the papers suggest that organizations’ thoughtful design of their workplaces allows for the creation of a flourishing remote/virtual workforce.
