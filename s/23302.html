Conceptualizing the Digital Solopreneur: A New Entrepreneurial Figure in Digital Environments

ENT
Camille Coulon (Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University)
A growing number of individuals create, operate, and sustain entrepreneurial activity entirely on their own through digital infrastructures. Existing scholarship has examined aspects of this phenomenon—freelancing, self-employment, platform work, content creation—but no framework conceptualises it as a distinct entrepreneurial configuration. This paper addresses this gap by developing the concept of the digital solopreneur. We define a digital solopreneur as an individual who creates, operates, and stabilises economic activity entirely alone, relying structurally on digital infrastructures. Building on abductive engagement with empirical research, we develop a framework explaining how digital solopreneurship is organised through three interdependent dimensions: organisational minimalism, sociotechnical embeddedness, and identity-based organising. The paper makes two contributions. First, it defines the digital solopreneur and clarifies what configurations are included or excluded. Second, it develops a framework showing how individuals sustain entrepreneurial activity alone—through the interplay of minimalism, embeddedness, and identity. This extends sociomaterial perspectives on digital entrepreneurship to the individual level.

Digital Entrepreneurship and Sustainability: The Role of Digital Dynamic Capabilities in Startups

ENT
Irene Gea Labella (University of Almería); Belén Payán-Sánchez (University of Almería); Miguel Pérez Valls (University of Almería); Eva Carmona-Moreno
The entrepreneurial landscape is being reshaped by rapid digital transformation and growing environmental and social pressures. While digital technologies offer significant potential for startups to contribute to sustainable development, the mechanisms through which the embeddedness of digital technologies translate into sustainable entrepreneurial outcomes remain underexplored. Drawing on dynamic capability theory, this study examines how digitalization in startups might foster sustainable entrepreneurship through the possession of digital capabilities. We develop and test a mediating model in which digital dynamic capabilities link the degree of digitalization to sustainable entrepreneurial initiatives. Using survey data from 145 Spanish digital startups, we find that digital products and services are positively associated with sustainable entrepreneurship, and that this relationship operates exclusively through the possession of digital dynamic capabilities. The findings demonstrate that digitalization alone is insufficient to foster sustainable entrepreneurial outcomes. Instead, sustainability emerges when startups possess the digital capabilities required to strategically deploy digital technologies for environmental and social value creation. This study offers important theoretical and practical implications for advancing digital entrepreneurship research toward sustainability.

Putting the "I" in Entrepreneurship: Digital Entrepreneurship & the Individualist Founder Identity

ENT
Katie Apker (Cornell University)
Digital technologies have fundamentally transformed entrepreneurship by enabling new forms of venture creation and identity expression. However, existing founder identity theory largely preceded the digital era and has not adequately addressed how digital affordances enable new types of entrepreneurial identities. This study extends founder identity theory through qualitative analysis of 61 entrepreneurs using a gender-balanced sample to examine founder identity configurations within the modern digital era and their relationship to strategic behaviors and venture outcomes. The analysis reveals the emergence of a previously unrecognized founder identity type - the "Individualist" - characterized by personal-level self-representation, self-expression as primary motivation, and focus on an idealized self rather than market opportunities. Digital affordances enable this "digitally-enabled individualism," which is strongly associated with dampened venture outcomes. The findings challenge core assumptions about universal opportunity orientation in entrepreneurship theory.

Technology Perceptions to Digital Entrepreneurial Intention: A Comparative Contextual Analysis

ENT
Md Farid Talukder (McNeese State University); Muhammad Rofiqul Islam (Texas A&M International University); Leonel Prieto (Texas A&M International University); Md. Rabiul Islam (Bangladesh Islami University); Md. Asaduzzaman; S.M. Sayem (Bangladesh University of Professionals); Md Fahad Sheikh (University Putra Malaysia); Md Al-Amin Talukder (Bangladesh Islami University)
The purpose of this study is to examine two distinct populations—students and employees—to assess how perceptions of technology usefulness and ease of use influence individuals’ risk-taking propensity and their intentions to engage in digital entrepreneurship. This paper comprises two studies. Data was collected from two distinct populations—students and employees—operating in resource-constrained and resource-affluent countries. Study 1 utilized 329 responses collected from university students in Bangladesh, while Study 2 employed 202 responses collected from employees in the USA to test the proposed hypotheses using PLS-SEM. Drawing on the technology acceptance model (TAM) and the theory of planned behavior (TPB), this study provides a solid theoretical framework to justify the proposed relationships. Across both technologically and economically less advanced and more advanced countries, perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use significantly influence individuals’ digital entrepreneurial intentions. Risk-taking propensity partially mediates the relationship between perceived usefulness and digital entrepreneurial intentions in the technologically less advanced country. In contrast, it fully mediates this relationship in technologically advanced countries. This study attempts to jointly examine the perspectives of students and employees in technologically and economically less advanced and more advanced situations, thereby providing a more balanced and realistic organizational perspective, as well as more robust and consistent findings.

When Vulnerability Backfires: Audience Sophistication and Digital Entrepreneurial Signaling

ENT
Vahid J. Sadeghi (Aston University); Razieh Sadraei (Aston University)
Recent entrepreneurship research suggests that vulnerability signaling enhances authenticity and elicits stakeholder support. Yet digital entrepreneurial platforms routinely expose founders to heterogeneous audiences whose evaluative frameworks remain poorly understood. This study examines when and why vulnerability disclosures succeed or fail in digital entrepreneurial communities by integrating signaling theory with audience heterogeneity perspectives. Using computational text analysis of 29,620 entrepreneurial posts and 1.9 million comments from Reddit communities (2015–2024), we distinguish between emotional vulnerability (psychological distress, fear, self-doubt) and functional vulnerability (operational failure, uncertainty, resource constraints). We further theorize how audience cognitive complexity and emotional intelligence condition responses to these disclosures. Results reveal a striking asymmetry: emotional vulnerability increases community support, whereas functional vulnerability consistently reduces it. More unexpectedly, higher levels of cognitive complexity and emotional intelligence systematically reduce supportive responses to both vulnerability types. These findings challenge core assumptions in signaling theory that costly, honest disclosures uniformly enhance credibility. We introduce a dimension prioritization mechanism, showing that vulnerability signals simultaneously activate authenticity and competence evaluations, with audience sophistication shifting attention toward dimensions where vulnerability signals weakness. By demonstrating how heterogeneous audiences fragment evaluation processes, this study reconceptualizes digital entrepreneurial platforms as environments where authenticity-based signaling becomes strategically fragile rather than universally beneficial. The paper contributes to theories of signaling, entrepreneurial communication, and digital entrepreneurship by identifying boundary conditions under which vulnerability undermines, rather than mobilizes, support.
