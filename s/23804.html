When and How Does Green Psychological Climate Lead to Environmental Citizens?

OB
Eunji Huh (University of Wisconsin-Green Bay); Eun-Suk Lee (Chung-Ang University)
Departing from prior studies suggesting that green psychological climate has pro-environmental outcomes, this study explores a boundary condition in which a positive green psychological climate leads to feelings of fatigue associated with pro-environmental issues, which we refer to as environmental citizenship fatigue. Drawing upon person–organization fit theory, we propose that employees’ perception of organizational greening (i.e., green psychological climate), if not aligned with their personal characteristics regarding green issues (i.e., commitment to the environment), triggers environmental citizenship fatigue via a strengthened belief that pro-environmental behaviors are inconvenient. Results from two studies—an experimental study based on data collected from 365 employees in the United States and a two-wave survey study based on data collected from 337 employees in South Korea—provide support for our predictions. By revealing the possible detrimental outcomes of green psychological climate, this study offers the novel insight that even the well-intentioned pursuit of organizational greening may lead to ironic consequences if the complexity of employee psychology regarding environmental issues is not fully considered.
