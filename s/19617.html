Session overview

Public procurement accounts for a significant share of OECD economies, yet its potential as a strategic lever for innovation remains underexplored, particularly at the regional level. This study investigates how Italian public authorities influence environmental innovation in manufacturing SMEs through Green Public Procurement (GPP). Drawing on a two-wave survey of 3,500 SMEs and detailed territorial data from the Green Tenders Observatory and the Italian National Anti-Corruption Authority, we analyze how both the quantity (number of contracting authorities) and quality (experience and capability in applying Minimum Environmental Criteria, CAM) of local procurement institutions shape firms’ exposure to GPP and their subsequent innovation outcomes. Our two-stage empirical design first models the likelihood of encountering CAM and then estimates the effect of CAM on environmental innovation, considering territorial conditions as moderating factors. Results reveal that SMEs located in territories with more numerous and virtuous contracting authorities are significantly more likely to engage with CAM and convert this exposure into environmental innovation. By integrating public management, innovation studies, and place-based theory, our findings highlight how public authorities act as catalysts of localized, sustainable innovation, providing both theoretical and policy insights into the strategic role of procurement in promoting regional development.
