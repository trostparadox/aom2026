How Employee Wellbeing Evolves During a Cross-Organizational AI Implementation Program in SMEs

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1

Authors: Sara Luisa Kappelhoff Technical University of Munich; Nadja Mirjam Born

Session overview

In this field study, we examine changes in employee wellbeing during a five-month artificial intelligence (AI) training and implementation program in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Despite increasing research attention to AI implementation in SMEs and the relevance of employee wellbeing for business performance, it remains unknown how employee wellbeing develops during AI implementation in SMEs. Therefore, we repeatedly collected wellbeing data from 235 employees during an AI training and implementation program. Based on the job demands-resources model, we examine how structured AI implementation influences job demands and job resources, affecting job stress and work engagement over time. Surprisingly, initial patterns in the data indicate a decrease in job demand and job stress after the beginning of the AI trainings. However, work engagement seems to remain mostly constant. We contribute to the literature on the socio-technical aspects of AI implementation in SMEs and employee wellbeing by offering dynamic insights into the employee experience of a structured AI training and implementation program. Our findings can guide the design of effective AI adoption programs in SMEs that support employee wellbeing.
