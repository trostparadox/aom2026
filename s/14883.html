Leader Vulnerability as a Core Capacity in Authentic Leadership

Authors: Jae Young Lee University of Georgia; Shinhee Jeong The University of Texas at Tyler

Session overview

Authentic leadership has gained sustained attention in both scholarship and practice in an era of uncertainty and complexity. However, recent scholarships have questioned its conceptual clarity, particularly its emphasis on internal states and its limited explanation of how authenticity is enacted relationally. Addressing these concerns, this study proposes a conceptual model that foregrounds leader vulnerability as a core leadership capacity. The model distinguishes between self-referential authenticity (self-awareness and internalized moral perspective) and relational authenticity (balanced processing and relational transparency) and conceptualizes leader vulnerability as the mechanism that translates intrapersonal authenticity into interpersonal enactment. Furthermore, leaders’ authenticity is not only expressed but also recursively reshaped through follower responses and organizational feedback, thereby recalibrating leaders’ self-referential perspectives. By conceptualizing authentic leadership as a dynamic process, this study advances authentic leadership theory toward a process-oriented understanding of leadership enactment.
