Breaking Free: How Organizations Escape Self-Reinforcing Absenteeism–Performance Dynamics

OB
Thomas Reibel (RWTH Aachen University); Susan Stead; David Antons (University of Bonn)
Absenteeism pervades organizations, yet its dynamic impact on organizational performance and competitive advantage remains underexplored. Prior work focuses on individual-level absenteeism or treats organizational-level absenteeism as a dependent metric, rather than examining how absenteeism and performance co-evolve over time. Building on the resource-based view (RBV) and conservation of resources (COR) theory, we examine how absenteeism influences organizational performance and, reciprocally, how performance shapes subsequent absenteeism. Employing latent change score modeling on longitudinal data from 127 acute hospitals in the English National Health Service (April 2013–March 2020), we uncover a significant bidirectional relationship: Performance deficits predict rising absenteeism rates, while elevated absenteeism deteriorates performance. These effects vary by workforce consensus on resource availability, which attenuates the performance-absenteeism spiral. Our study makes three theoretical contributions. First, we establish a theoretical basis for examining the competitive consequences of absenteeism. Second, we challenge RBV’s unidirectional logic by showing that performance pressures accelerate resource decay, locking underperformers into self-reinforcing decline. Third, we extend COR by identifying personal resource consensus as a collective boundary condition shaping how organizational stressors spread. Organizations should develop structural capabilities to sustain performance amid fluctuations in absenteeism and cultivate a shared understanding among employees of available resources and realistically attainable goals.

Cultural Diversity as a Buffer Against Membership Change: The Role of Transactive Memory

OB
Yongkang Yang (Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University); Anne Nederveen Pieterse (Erasmus University Rotterdam); Jia Li (University of Copenhagen)
Membership change is a pervasive feature of modern teamwork, often disrupting coordination and impairing performance. We investigate whether team cultural diversity mitigates these negative effects. Across two experiments using an emergency simulation, we examine how diverse and homogeneous teams respond to the membership change. Study 1 (N = 97 teams) demonstrated that while homogeneous teams suffered a significant performance decline following membership change, culturally diverse teams maintained their performance levels. Study 2 (N = 67 teams) found that diverse teams invested in developing a more accurate objective transactive memory which facilitated the effective integration of newcomers. These findings suggest that the transactive memory necessitated by cultural diversity provides a cognitive infrastructure that helps teams adapt to membership dynamics.

Scaling Without Freezing: Standardization and Adaptation Tension During Organizational Expansion

OB
Xiameng Fan (Tsinghua University); Chen Zhang (Tsinghua University); Laijie Yang (Tsinghua University); Wenchen Lin (Capital University of Economics and Business); Zhaodong Qiu (NEOMA Business School)
Research on routine dynamics assumes that variation in routine enactment is inherent, enabling organizations to balance stability and flexibility even under high levels of standardization. We challenge this assumption by highlight the cross-level tension between organizational imperatives and frontline work demands under dynamic work environments such as organizational expansion. Drawing on an emic perspective of routine enactment, we develop a model to address on how to avoid the pitfall of the tension between standardization and adaptation in employees' work during organizational expansion. We gain theoretical insights from qualitative fieldwork and test our conceptual model through a weekly study of 88 frontline workers (across 7 weeks) in an organization undergoing expansion. Results suggest that weekly standardization in the form of targeted policing rather than generic emphasis induces rigidity about standards, which in turn undermines performance. Critically, employee’s awareness of expansion needs buffered the negative effects by reframing current standards as provisional and temporarily-appropriate guidelines.

Organizational Identity Dynamics in Corporate Pivoting

OB
Louisa Maria Levetzow (University of St. Gallen); Janine Crivelli (University of St. Gallen); Joakim Wincent (University of St. Gallen)
Corporate pivots can force rapid, top-down redefinitions of ‘who we are’ before a future identity can be credibly enacted. Yet organizational identity research has paid less attention to the emotional dynamics through which members live such imposed transitions. In a qualitative single-case study, we examine how organizational members experience and respond to an organization-wide identity transition during a major corporate pivot in a long-established legacy organization entering an unfamiliar industry. We find that the pivot destabilized the organizational identity through legacy identity inertia, credibility strain and authenticity work, and future uncertainty. These conditions generated collective identity grief, which captures how organizational members mourn the fading legacy identity and, unevenly, begin to rebuild shared meaning, ultimately shaping patterns of re-engagement with the new identity, provisional compliance while withholding commitment to the new direction, or withdrawal and exit. Leaders influence these dynamics by mediating the organizational identity transition through sensegiving and facilitation of identity enactment, which enable future-focused sensemaking among organizational members. We develop a process model of organizational identity dynamics in corporate pivoting and advance organizational identity theory by theorizing collective identity grief as the core mechanism through which organizational members process the rapid loss of a valued legacy identity while orienting toward an imposed future identity.
