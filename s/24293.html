Size or Performance: How Business Group Affiliation Shapes Aspiration Metrics in Problemistic Search

STR
V VEERA BALAJI (Indian Institute of Management Tiruchirappalli); Manikandan Karayambadi Srinivasan
This study examines a critical but overlooked aspect of problemistic search: the choice of aspiration metric. Current research expects firms to prioritise performance goals over size goals. We contend that this goal hierarchy is not universal and shaped by organisational structure. Specifically, we investigate how business group affiliation—a multi-level structure involving both affiliate management and a group center—influences firm’s choice of aspiration metric. Drawing from attention-based view and research on problemistic search and business groups, we theorise that business group affiliation activates opportunity-seeking and simplicity-seeking behaviors that shift attentional focus toward size goals, whereas standalone firms experience certainty-seeking behavior that prioritizes performance. We test these arguments using a 20-year longitudinal sample of firms in India. Our findings provide robust evidence that the choice of aspiration metric is context-dependent. Furthermore, we show dominant coalition dynamics in multilevel organisations shape attention allocation and influence problemistic search. Finally, we advance research on problemistic search in business groups by shifting focus from search outcomes to the foundational choice of the aspiration metric itself.
