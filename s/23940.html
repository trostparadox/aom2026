Effects of Chinese Government Demonstration Projects on Key Emerging Technologies Diffusion

TIM
HAN YUMENG (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); SUN PENGFEI; Chunhui Yuan (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications)
Contemporary China's industrial policies increasingly seek to direct technical change toward strategic frontiers. Yet, the translation of this strategic intent into mass diffusion is often hindered by severe market rigidities. Consequently, the micro-mechanisms by which state interventions overcome these diffusion barriers remain under-explored. In this context, demonstration projects serve as a critical institutional bridge connecting frontier breakthroughs with broad-based diffusion. Our study employs a staggered difference-in-differences (DID) design using China's "Smart Manufacturing Pilot Demonstration Projects" as a quasi-natural experiment. We define "Key Emerging Technologies" (KETs) as those associated with China's "Strategic Emerging Industries"—a state-prioritized agenda designated as the primary engine for future growth—and identify them using the official government IPC concordance table. We find robust evidence that demonstration policies significantly accelerate the diffusion of external KETs among manufacturing firms. Mechanism analysis reveals that these projects function as an institutional bridge, reducing diffusion frictions through risk mitigation, cost regulation, and supply chain coordination. Furthermore, moderation analysis highlights that the effect is reinforced by robust social networks and intellectual property protection, while exhibiting bidirectional heterogeneity based on patent width and depth. This study provides micro-level evidence on how state-led interventions can successfully jump-start industrial upgrading by governing the frictions of technological transition.
