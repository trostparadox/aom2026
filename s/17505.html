The Effects of Internal Labour Markets on Turnover Behavior Among IT Employees in the IT Sector

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Author: Limor Kessler Ladelsky Bar-Ilan University

Session overview

Turnover behaviour has substantial negative consequences in the high-technology (high-tech) sector, including the loss of organisational knowledge, patents, skilled human capital, and information technology talent. This study examines whether Internal Labour Markets (ILMs) in high-tech firms influence turnover behaviour (actual turnover) among high-tech employees beyond the effects of a novel constellation of explanatory variables. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate ILMs together with key antecedents of turnover behaviour during a pandemic or crisis, under conditions of shelter-in-place and lockdown. Data were collected from an international high-tech firm in Israel during the third COVID-19 lockdown. All participants were software engineers or developers. Logistic regression analyses and moderated mediation tests were conducted using PROCESS. Construct validity and discriminant validity were assessed through confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) using Mplus. The findings indicate that ILMs significantly reduce turnover intention, while the direct effect of ILMs on turnover behaviour is only preliminarily significant. However, the indirect effect of ILMs on turnover behaviour through turnover intention is significant. Furthermore, feeling moderates the relationship between turnover intention and turnover behaviour, and positive affect (PA) mediates the partially moderated effect of ILMs on turnover intention. The measurements and findings are discussed in light of the existing literature. Theoretical and practical implications are outlined, and directions for future research are proposed.
