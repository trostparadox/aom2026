State-Conferred Certification and Overseas Market Expansion

IM
Qian Xu (College of Science & Technology Ningbo University); Yapu Zhao; Guozhang Xu (Chizhou University)
Amid rising de-globalization and geopolitical tensions, Chinese firms face increasing legitimacy barriers when expanding into overseas markets. While prior research has emphasized the resource-based effects of industrial policies, we know relatively little about whether and how government certification can mitigate firms’ legitimacy liabilities abroad. Drawing on institutional theory, we examine the Manufacturing Single Champion policy—a state-conferred certification program in China—to investigate its impact on firms’ overseas market expansion, measured by the establishment of overseas subsidiaries. Using a multi-period difference-in-differences design on a panel of Chinese listed manufacturing firms from 2012 to 2023, we find that firms receiving the Single Champion designation significantly expand their overseas subsidiary networks. Further analyses reveal that this effect is stronger when firms’ top executives possess overseas experience, indicating enhanced capability and confidence to leverage certification signals in foreign markets. In addition, the positive effect is amplified in more competitive industries, suggesting that competitive pressure motivates firms to translate legitimacy gains into overseas expansion. Robustness checks using event-study analyses, propensity score matching, and placebo tests support our findings. This study contributes to the literature on firm internationalization and industrial policy by highlighting the legitimacy-enhancing role of state certification in shaping firms’ overseas market strategies, particularly under conditions of de-globalization.
