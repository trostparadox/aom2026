Digital Decoupling and Green Technology Innovation: Does “More Words Than Deeds” Matter?

TIM
Siyu Shao (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Ling Zhang; Jianjun Yang (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
Digital transformation (DT) has been viewed as a valuable approach to driving green technology innovation (GTI). However, companies may engage in opportunistic behavior by making more symbolic disclosures rather than actual actions regarding DT strategy—referred to as digital decoupling. This phenomenon complicates our understanding of the relationship between DT and GTI. Based on panel data from A-share listed companies in China, this study finds that: digital decoupling positively influences GTI; the promotional impact of digital decoupling is weakened when firms have high supply chain concentration, and strengthened when managers possess green experience. Additionally, digital decoupling facilitates GTI by leveraging positive external feedback from disclosures rather than capability enhancement from actual digital actions. Lastly, the impact of digital decoupling on GTI is reflected in quantity rather than quality.
