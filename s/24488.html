When Strong Governance Backfires: Multiple Large Shareholders and the Rise of Innovation Washing

STR
Yixin Xie (Zhejiang University); jiaying cao (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Innovation attracts resources and legitimacy, yet its opacity allows firms to signal innovativeness while cutting real investment—a practice we call innovation-washing. Building on stakeholder governance and multi-principal monitoring, we theorize that multiple large shareholders (MLS) can unintentionally intensify innovation-washing. When monitoring becomes excessive, shareholders’ low tolerance for uncertain R&D heightens short-term discipline and encourages firms to substitute low-cost symbolic signals for substantive innovation. We further argue that external evaluators can magnify this dynamic by rewarding innovation signals without fully verifying their substance. Using 9,515 firm-year observations of Chinese high-tech enterprises from 2016–2021, we find that MLS is associated with a significantly higher likelihood of innovation-washing, operationalized as abnormal reductions in R&D. Counterintuitively, this relationship strengthens under higher media attention and more favorable ESG ratings, suggesting that scrutiny and reputational endorsements may intensify incentives to “look innovative” while retrenching investment. Our findings advance strategic governance research by identifying a monitoring paradox: ownership structures intended to discipline management may instead fuel symbolic behavior when innovation is highly uncertain and difficult to measure.
