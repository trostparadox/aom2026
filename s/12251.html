Session overview

The dominant organizational learning models focus on cognitive and knowledge considerations (Jarvenpaa & Välikangas, 2025). This year’s PDW – the second in the series of “What’s Next for Organizational Learning Theories” – will debate and discuss whether and how future learning theories should consider additional bases of learning in organizations that expand beyond shared meanings and knowing- related practices. How might emotion, affective engagement, or even humor be incorporated into learning? Do the learning theories need to have a greater consideration of gossip, help seeking and help giving, foolishness, and jester-like truth-speaking and doubting acts? What roles do physical presence play, if anything? As intelligent technologies make inroads into organizations, are cognitive, promptable, elements enough as a basis for organizational learning? This PDW uses the metaphor of tango (Bamberger, 2009) as a backdrop in discussing the need for human superpowers. It will continue the exploration of What’s Next in organizational learning in the midst of the profound technological changes happening in organizations. But rather than focusing on digital technologies and their immediate potential, this time we seek to explore and cherish the capacities of people, the employees and managers of an organization. In this PDW we ask: What are human superpowers in organizational learning and what difference, if any, do they make? Such superpowers may include being emotionally present, the ability to break out of a rhythm of activities, and the capacity to exercise doubt. There are certainly other superpowers as well.
