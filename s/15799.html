Quixote or Panza? Character Development and the Hermeneutic Turn in AI Ethics

Authors: Joel Adams Washington and Lee University; Agrim Sachdeva The University of Arizona

Session overview

Organizations are embedding AI copilots into consequential practices—employee development, performance evaluation, hiring, and strategic planning. These systems promise consistency but become quiet governors of what counts as "good work." As normative targets become coordination rules, they attract optimization pressure, triggering Goodhart's Law: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. In AI systems, this appears as reward hacking—satisfying metrics while undermining the goods they represent. We argue these failures stem from an architectural gap: lack of telos—the plural developmental ends toward which humans grow through practice. Consider two users: one optimizes for visionary thinking and risk-taking (Quixote), the other for operational excellence and consistency (Panza). What constitutes good judgment differs fundamentally between them. A system pushing both toward the same profile—even one scoring well in aggregate—undermines excellence for at least one. This is not a training data problem; it's architectural incompatibility between universalist optimization and plural goods. We propose a hermeneutic turn: treat ethical AI as interpretation and support of telos-in-practice rather than optimization toward universal goods. We distinguish deep constraints (safety, rights, non-deception) from positive guidance, which should be personalized and developmental. We present seven design principles for telos-driven AI, including iterative elicitation, growth-oriented feedback, exception-as-signal interpretation, and anti-delegation safeguards. This repositions core constructs in human-AI ecologies research: prediction error becomes diagnostic of successful personalization rather than system failure; behavioral variance signals character discovery rather than coordination defect. Our framework suggests research agendas on telos negotiation, normative drift governance, and how AI delegation reshapes judgment and accountability in organizations.
