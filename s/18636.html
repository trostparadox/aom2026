Session overview

In 2017, Vaswani and colleagues disrupted the field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) by introducing new ideas about how AI processes language. Their work led to the development of novel AI systems – known as generative AI (GAI) – that can receive user inputs (prompts) and generate seemingly coherent outputs, including text, images, and music. These technologies have since had significant impacts on individuals, organizations, and society, including academia. The use of GAI in research is highly problematic for several reasons. First, GAI hallucinates. Second, it is trained on data that is inherently biased, often derived from internet sources. Third, reliance on GAI can lead to what we call scholarly deskilling. Finally, GAI may influence the essential intellectual contributions – ideas and creativity – that are traditionally considered the exclusive domain of human researchers. This PDW aims to examine the extent to which GAI should (or should not) be embedded in different stages of the research process. Today, GAI can perform a literature review, analyze data, formulate research questions and hypotheses, and even draft an entire paper. However, the topic remains contentious, and publishers' and journals’ policies are in constant flux. To address these issues, we will convene a panel of major publishers who will take questions from editors and authors about the evolving nature of GAI policies. We will also explore the tensions that exist between publishers and editors regarding these policies.
