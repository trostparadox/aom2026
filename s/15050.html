Session overview

#15050 From Policing to Pedagogy: Redesigning Assignments to Improve Learning in the Age of Generative AI Sponsors: Primary - Management Education and Development Potential - Management Consulting ABSTRACT Generative AI has rapidly become embedded in how students complete academic work, often prioritizing speed and output over learning. In response, many faculty have attempted to prohibit AI use and rely on detection-based enforcement, only to find these approaches ineffective, demoralizing, and misaligned with how students actually engage with coursework. For some instructors, this has undermined confidence in assessment and raised deeper questions about the sustainability and purpose of teaching. This PDW begins from that lived faculty experience and reframes the challenge not as widespread student misconduct, but as a mismatch between assignment design and the contemporary context in which students are attempting to learn. The workshop introduces a design-based instructional framework that helps faculty move from policing student work to redesigning assignments that actively support learning in AI-enabled environments. Drawing on Bloom’s Taxonomy, McGuire’s Study Cycle, and structured peer evaluation, the framework positions generative AI as a cognitive scaffold when paired with requirements that make thinking visible through student explanation and justification. Rather than attempting to determine authorship, faculty can redirect efforts toward evaluating student learning based on students’ structured demonstrations of reasoning, judgment, and synthesis. Participants will examine why policing strategies fail pedagogically and engage in a guided design sprint to redesign an existing assignment from their own teaching context. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
