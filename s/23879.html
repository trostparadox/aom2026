How Coworkers’ AI Crafting Triggers AI Learning and AI Use Undermining: A Comparison Perspective

OB
Zhaoyue Sun (Shandong University); Fengyu Zhai (Shandong University)
People frequently engaged in AI crafting to proactively adapt the changes brought by organizational AI adoption. While previous studies have reported that employees’ AI crafting benefit their job, we still know little about how employees perceived their coworkers’ AI crafting. Drawing on social comparison theory, we develop an integrative model to examine the interpersonal consequences of AI crafting. We propose that employees’ AI crafting may trigger two different emotions (i.e., envy and inspiration) in their coworkers, thus result in their subsequent work behaviors (i.e., AI use undermining and AI learning). Moreover, we also consider the contingent conditions of goal interdependence (i.e., competitive goal interdependence and cooperative interdependence). Using a two-wave study of 252 employees, we find that coworkers’ AI crafting promotes employees’ AI use undermining (via envy) and AI learning (via inspiration). Specifically, cooperative goal interdependence strengthens the positive effects of coworkers’ AI crafting on employees’ AI learning via envy. Our study reveals a double-edged sword effect of coworkers’ AI crafting, advancing our understanding on AI crafting.
