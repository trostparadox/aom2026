Session overview

Organizations increasingly expect graduates to translate AI capabilities into credible managerial decisions, yet business schools often struggle to teach AI in ways that integrate technical reasoning, organizational judgment, and cross-functional collaboration. This Professional Development Workshop presents a replicable, interdisciplinary course design that combines Research-Based Learning (RBL) and Team-Based Learning (TBL) to develop AI/GenAI competence across mixed STEM and non-STEM cohorts. The design guides teams through an end-to-end pathway—from working with real datasets and building interpretable models, to articulating a business case and governance considerations, to using generative AI via prompt-engineering roles that structure human–AI collaboration rather than replacing human judgment. The workshop is organized as a hands-on “design studio.” Participants will (1) map learning objectives to an integrated sequence of technical and managerial deliverables; (2) apply team-formation and role-allocation templates that mitigate knowledge asymmetries and participation imbalances; and (3) adapt assignment scaffolds and grading rubrics that evaluate both the quality of analytical artifacts (e.g., model validity, explainability) and the quality of managerial reasoning (e.g., contextual fit, risk awareness, KPI governance). Participants will also discuss common implementation challenges, such as overreliance on AI outputs, superficial business narratives, and uneven workload distribution, and identify concrete interventions (structured critique prompts, verification checkpoints, peer assessment, and reflective documentation) to improve learning outcomes.
