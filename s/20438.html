Session overview

Effectuation and uncertainty are foundational concepts in entrepreneurship research and education, yet educators consistently report a gap between students’ conceptual understanding of these ideas and their ability to enact them in practice. While effectuation and uncertainty are relatively easy to teach about, they remain difficult to learn through experience. This Professional Development Workshop addresses a core pedagogical challenge for entrepreneurship education: how to design learning environments in which uncertainty is not merely discussed, but genuinely experienced in ways that support effectual action. Building on effectuation theory (Sarasvathy), classic distinctions between risk and uncertainty (Knight), and learning perspectives such as threshold concepts and liminality (Meyer & Land), the workshop argues that effectuation is difficult to learn because it presupposes sustained engagement with uncertainty—conditions rarely maintained in educational settings. We draw on comparative insights from multiple play-based and gamified learning designs used across cohorts to illustrate recurring learning patterns when students are required to act without predictive closure. After a brief conceptual framing, participants will engage in facilitated small-group discussions to share pedagogical tools, simulations, games, and assignments they use to expose students to entrepreneurial uncertainty. At least 30 minutes of the session are devoted to structured peer discussion. The workshop aims to advance entrepreneurship education by building a community of educators focused on designing experiences—rather than explanations—that enable effectual learning under uncertainty.
