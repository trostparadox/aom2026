Invisible Pressure: The Potential Pitfalls of Pay Transparency for High-Paid Employees

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Yanshu Ji Nanjing University; Yuchuan Liu Nanjing University

Session overview

Although pay transparency is increasingly championed as a panacea for organizational inequity, the visibility of compensation may impose unintended psychological costs on high-paid employees. Drawing on the psychological safety theory, we challenge the prevailing focus on lower-paid employees’ reactions by examining how transparency transforms compensation from a private reward into a socially evaluative spotlight. We propose that for high-paid employees, the public nature of their compensation triggers anticipations of social envy, legitimacy challenges, and interpersonal backlash, thereby undermining their psychological safety. This erosion of safety, in turn, manifests in diminished job satisfaction and heightened turnover intentions. Results from two studies (a field survey and an experimental study) demonstrate that the detrimental effects of transparency on psychological safety and subsequent job attitudes are significant for high-paid employees. By unveiling an interpersonal threat pathway, this research extends pay transparency literature beyond justice frameworks and highlights the divergent psychological consequences of transparency across the pay hierarchy.
