How Could Emerging-Market Firms Navigate Institutional Deficits in Reverse Cross-Border M&As?

IM
Sijia Yu (Renmin University of China); Yuchen Yang
Overseas mergers and acquisitions (M&As) constitute a vital strategic avenue for firms from emerging economies. Focusing on Chinese firms’ inclination towards reverse cross-border M&As, this study investigates whether and how directors’ and officers’ liability insurance (D&O insurance) assists firms in alleviating institutional deficiencies during cross-border transactions. Using Chinese listed firms from 2011 to 2019, we find that firms covered by D&O insurance are significantly more likely to initiate cross-border M&As. To strengthen causal identification, we exploit the Sino-U.S. trade war as an exogenous shock, and our findings remain robust across a series of sensitivity tests. Mechanism analyses indicate two channels: active risk-taking and improved investor protection. Further analysis shows that the effect of D&O insurance is amplified in settings with more pronounced institutional deficits, i.e. when acquirers operate in weaker domestic legal environments or targets operate in stronger legal jurisdictions. These results suggest that D&O insurance not only enhances firms’ overall capacity for active risk-taking, but also acts as a complement to inadequate domestic investor protection. Collectively, we highlight the important role of D&O insurance in facilitating emerging-market firms’ strategic oversea decisions, providing evidence on how financial instruments can function as effective complements to legal infrastructure in weak institutional environments.
