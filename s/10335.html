Session overview

While organizational scholars and workplace leaders alike are increasingly recognizing the importance of employee well-being (for a review, see: Gabriel et al., 2022), the study of well-being at work—and, how organizations treat well-being at work—is often reactive, rather than proactive. Such a focus is surprising, as employees are not passive actors in their work lives; rather, they are agents that can, and do, craft their roles and environments to better serve their needs (e.g., Wrzesniewski & Dutton, 2001) and support recovery from work stress (e.g., Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007). Thus, to better understand well-being and the ways that employees proactively navigate this experience, we must bring the employee and their actions into the conversation. By doing so, we can better understand how employees at work can decide what they are, and are not, willing to do in order to work well and live well. To advance this conversation, the current symposium organizes four papers that highlight the role of employee behavior in crafting situations for well-being across various contexts including situations of workplace mistreatment and anxiety, motherhood, and the job search process itself.
