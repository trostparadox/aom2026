Session overview

Many critics claim that management education quietly passes on values which influence students’ ideas about managerial life and achievement (Bennis and O’Toole, 2005; Ghoshal, 2005). One often-cited example is related to neoliberal meritocracy, which proposes a belief that effort, hard work and talent alone guarantee equal opportunities and outcomes (Eisenman, Foroughi, and Foster, 2024). If this goes unchecked, it can operate as a ‘hidden curriculum’ that students may accept as normal. They might see inequality as deserved or fail to notice structural barriers that others face (Dar and Faria, 2025; Clarke, Hurst and Tomlinson, 2024). Yet, it is not straightforward to challenge meritocracy in the classroom. Developing critical thinking, or in this sense, critical reflexivity, in students is often subtle, unpredictable and emotional (Dyer and Hurd, 2016). Unlike previously noted in the literature, students are not passive recipients of ideas (Al-Zahrani, 2015). Through discussion, they question, adjust, strengthen, or even reject what they hear (Steele, 2025; Blumer, 1980). This professional development workshop (PDW) offers educators a practical, research-based approach for ‘unhiding’ and challenging meritocracy and fostering inclusive classrooms. Participants will be given the opportunity to use a narrative negotiation approach. They will look for cues, notice disruptions, apply sensemaking and develop narratives from this process. The goals of the PDW are to: (1) identify common meritocracy cues in curricula, (2) experiment with inclusive teaching strategies that invite open conversation yet protect classroom trust, and (3) guide each participant through designing a teaching plan using prompts and reflection exercises suited to their context. Each attendee will leave with a usable toolkit for teaching and classroom practice, as well as connecting with peers for ongoing support. Participant evaluations are invaluable. They help us understand what attendees find most beneficial, identify opportunities to strengthen future PDWs, and demonstrate the impact and value of the PDW program to the Academy.
