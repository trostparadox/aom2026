Session overview

CEO sociopolitical activism has become an increasingly visible feature of contemporary organizational life, as executives face growing pressure to speak out on contentious social and political issues. While prior research has generated important insights into the consequences of CEO activism, it has paid comparatively less attention to how issue characteristics shape the decision to engage in the first place. In particular, issue contentiousness has often been treated as a background condition or as a property of activism itself, rather than as a central antecedent that CEOs interpret when deciding whether, when, and how to speak out. This panel symposium rethinks issue contentiousness as a core driver of CEO sociopolitical engagement. Bringing together leading scholars in strategic leadership, nonmarket strategy, organization theory, and political sociology, the panel advances a dynamic rather than static perspective on contentiousness. Panelists examine what issue contentiousness captures conceptually, how it differs from related constructs such as polarization, and why these distinctions matter for understanding stakeholder reactions. They then explore how CEOs interpret contentiousness and related environmental cues when assessing engagement opportunities, how these interpretations evolve over time on the same issue, and why CEO activism may intensify, recede, or disappear even when public division remains high. The symposium further extends the conversation beyond individual executives by considering how different organizational forms condition engagement with contested issues and shape broader societal impact. By conceptualizing issue contentiousness as a salient, evolving signal that executives interpret within complex stakeholder environments, the panel moves CEO activism research beyond static treatments of issue characteristics. In doing so, it offers new theoretical insights into executive decision-making under societal division and identifies promising directions for future research on leadership, nonmarket strategy, and the role of business in society.
