Morphogenesis in AI and Organisations:The Role of AI Developers’ Agency in Structural Transformation

CMS
Vaishalini Singh Jamwal (Indian Institute of Management Kashipur); Venkataraman Anuratha
Within the increasingly VUCA landscape of contemporary technologies, Artificial Intelligence has remained the focus of scholarly attention given its transformative potential and unprecedented scalability. Socio technical scholarship has given increasing attention to the interplay of technology and social contexts within which they are designed, however, it remains fragmented in explaining how these socio-technical dynamics unfold over time particularly as AI becomes embedded in everyday organisational activities. Drawing on Archer’s morphogenetic approach of Critical Realism, in this paper, we draw on 46 in-depth interviews of AI developers to explore the temporally sequenced assembling of agency and its role in mobilising broader structural changes in the context of AI development. Our findings reveal paradoxical happiness and anxiety, defensive and contingent alignment and systemic fragility as factors driving AI developers’ reflexive judgements thus forming their agency. Drawing on analytical dualism, we conceptualise our findings into the morphogenetic framework to explain the temporal co-constitution of agency and structure. Our framework reveals amplified prognosis, heightened agentive actions, and increased agentic learning proactiveness as temporally sequenced outcomes of the morphogenetic cycle. Our framework accounts for broader structural elaboration and genesis of a new morphogenetic sequence through interplay of agency and structure. We contribute to existing scholarship in organization studies that examine technology and organizing by elaborating the processual and temporal morphogenetic sequence that accounts for broader structural transformation.
