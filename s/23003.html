Future Making in Designing Organizations: Handling the Future in Strategic Activities

SAP
Vera Kralova (Prague University of Economics and Business); Pavel Kral (Prague University of Economics and Business)
Strategic activities are inherently future-oriented, yet organization design research has largely approached the future through formal structures and planned change, overlooking how practitioners experience and enact the future in their everyday design activities. Drawing on the future making perspective, we explore how practitioners bring the future into being through design activities aimed at transforming existing organizational situations into future, preferred ones. Based on a multiple-case study of four organizations undergoing structural redesign, we analyzed how responsible practitioners engage with the uncertain future as a problematic temporal category while designing their organizations. We aimed to explain how practitioners engage with uncertainty and enact organizational futures through their actions. Our findings identified five interrelated practitioners' temporal aspects of future making temporal dimensions that shape design activity, which are future prospection, perception of interdependencies, consistency toward the future, embracing persistency, and handling the future. Further theorizing process enables revealing two distinct future-making moves through which practitioners enact the future of organizations: protective moves and audacious moves. Our study brings a practice perspective as a way to study organization design, respectively designing organizations as with a focus on the role of the future and related temporal aspects within related design activities.

Beyond Project Boundaries: Making a Desirable Future Actionable

SAP
Sarah Stanske (University of Bremen); Laura Fey (Leuphana University of Lüneburg); Pauline Charlotte Reinecke (Leuphana University of Lüneburg)
We study how projects enable actors to construct actionable futures that extend beyond formal temporal boundaries. Analyzing a youth participation initiative, we show how actors continued future-making across three project cycles despite temporary funding. Findings show that an actionable future unfolded through four sequential yet recursive phases: initiating, enacting, sustaining, and redefining a desirable future. We offer two contributions pertaining to future-making through projects. First, we depict projects as designated sites of hyper-projectivity where emancipatory inquiries based on values could emerge. Second, we depict project boundaries as more permeable than conventionally suggested, allowing the occurrence of future-making beyond project phases.

Orchestrating Future-Making in Innovation Ecosystems: Learning from the NOI Techpark Case

SAP
Silvia Sanasi (University of Bergamo); Emanuela Rondi (Politecnico di Milano); Alfredo De Massis; Federico Paolo Zasa (Politecnico di Milano)
Traditional innovation approaches often focus on short-term outcomes, falling short of addressing the distant future. Drawing on the revelatory case of NOI Techpark – a science and technology park acting as orchestrator across diverse ecosystem actors – and its Alpha Innovation approach, we identify five key practices for collaborative future-making in innovation ecosystems: involving future interpreters, distributed sensemaking, designing around future users, creating ecosystem-level visions, and setting open-ended decision-making principles. Through these practices, NOI orchestrates the future-making efforts of multiple ecosystem actors, using future-making practices as a platform for collaboration rather than forecasting tools. The study contributes to management theory and practice, advancing the understanding of future-making as an ecosystem-level endeavor and offering actionable managerial takeaways for orchestrating collaborative future-making efforts.

Temporal Navigation: How Organizations Work with Multiple Futures (WITHDRAWN)

SAP
Jennifer Sloan (University College London)
Algorithmic tools promise to make the future calculable, yet organizations inhabit multiple futures simultaneously, with some amenable to prediction and others resistant to control. How do organizations keep these multiple futures in play during strategizing? Through a four-year ethnography of a construction firm's analytics transformation, I identify four metaphorical futures that actors construct and navigate: future as foresight (predictable and controllable), future as luck (unpredictable and uncontrollable), future as vision (unpredictable but shapeable), and future as constraint (predictable but uncontrollable). I introduce temporal navigation—the process by which actors shift among metaphorical futures, reconfiguring how the future becomes knowable and actionable—and show how it enabled organizational members to move fluidly among all four orientations rather than resolving them into coherent accounts or separating them into distinct domains. Two mechanism pairs accomplished this movement: calculating ? intuiting governed how futures became knowable; scripting ? improvising governed how they became actionable. What distinguished effective strategizing less determined by algorithmic sophistication than by the capacity to complement foresight with alternative orientations when its assumptions failed. These findings shift the theoretical problem from achieving temporal coherence to maintaining access across futures, while offering a temporal account of why algorithmic initiatives often underdeliver.
