Feeling Ambivalent During Decision Making: How Emotional Ambivalence Improves Decision Quality

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Jaewon Shin Yonsei University; Jeyoung An Yonsei University; Myeong-gu Seo University of Maryland College Park

Session overview

While research has examined how positive or negative feelings influence decision performance by facilitating decisions or inducing bias, the role of emotional ambivalence in the decision-making process has remained elusive. Emotional ambivalence, which refers to the simultaneous experience of both positive and negative emotions toward an object, can enhance decision quality by broadening perspectives, neutralizing affect-driven and motivational biases. Using an internet-based 20-day stock investment simulation combined with an experience sampling methodology, we found that ambivalence is positively related to day-level decision performance. Notably, the relationship remains robust even after controlling for alternative explanations, including positive and negative activation, affective dominant states, affective intensity, neutrality, as well as cognitive components such as expectancy judgments, goal level, and goal commitment. These findings contribute to the ambivalence and decision-making literatures by demonstrating that emotional ambivalence represents a distinctive affective state that enhances decision quality beyond affective or cognitive mechanisms.
