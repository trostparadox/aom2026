Loyal Servant or Moral Citizen? Employee Traditionality and Unethical Pro-Supervisor Behavior

OB
Pengcheng Zhang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Shou Gao (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Huili YE (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Meiqin Jiang (Huazhong Agricultural University)
While employees with high Chinese traditionality are often viewed as moral actors, this study challenges this monolithic view by proposing a double-edged sword effect. By integrating role theory with trait activation theory, we argue that empowering leadership determines whether traditionality inhibits or promotes unethical pro-supervisor behavior (UPSB) by activating distinct role identities. A scenario experiment (Study 1, N = 150) and a multi-wave field survey (Study 2, N = 321) support our model. The results reveal that employees’ traditionality has an indirect positive effect on UPSB via leader-face maintenance and an indirect negative effect on UPSB via work moralization. Furthermore, when empowering leadership is high, employees’ traditionality is more likely to stimulate the path of leader-face maintenance and thus encourage subsequent UPSB. However, when empowering leadership is low, employees’ traditionality is more likely to stimulate the path of work moralization and inhibit subsequent UPSB. We discuss the theoretical and practical implications of these findings.
