Session overview

U.S. federal government agencies, including organizations responsible for regulating and providing healthcare as well as protecting public health, have been undergoing disruptive processes of major restructuring. These changes-- including downsizing and redefinitions of core functions-- clearly pose immediate and long-term threats to federal government organizations. However, the upheaval may eventually also create productive opportunities for change. In our Professional Development Workshop (PDW), we explore how previous and future organizational research findings could contribute to rebuilding governmental organizations in the healthcare sector, ranging from departments to agencies and programs. The PDW seeks to raise participants’ awareness of the current threats and opportunities, identify new research directions and foster future collaboration with practitioners. Our PDW team invites participants to explore how various streams of organizational research could inform efforts to rebuild federal healthcare agencies or programs. Team members will open the discussion by presenting research anchored in distinct conceptual frameworks that could guide future rebuilding efforts, including implementation science and restoring trust in organizations. Team members will engage participants in lively breakout group discussions about the streams of organizational research that could inform and guide efforts to rebuild federal agencies or programs. Participants will also explore how organizational research could contribute to assessments of past damages, analyses of current grassroot efforts to fill gaps in federal government functions, and preparations for future implementation of innovations. The PDW thereby enables participants to explore how current and future organizational studies could guide practitioners-- and researchers-- when they seek to rebuild federal healthcare agencies.
