Session overview

Recent investigations have underscored the significance that play has for education, especially when it comes to ‘execution’ sciences like management, wherein immersion is a prerequisite for a successful project. One such strong execution science in Strategic Management, wherein involvement and immersion are imperatives; thus, play could be a helpful tool for administering Strategy Education. This PDW proposes translating ancient Indian board games into portable, pedagogical designs for strategy studies. We use three anchors, each representing a distinct strategic logic: Pallanguzhi (planning with moving constriants, resource allocation and ‘portfolio logic’, path dependence, competititive intelligence with partial information, conversion efficiency) and Aadu Puli Attam (asymmetric advantage, two- phased strategy and timing, coordination and positioning, calculated losses, threat credibility and signalling), and Dahdi (early placement & late play, strategy myopia, opportunity cost, shaping rival choice architecture, defensive advantage, commitment traps, bounded rationality, strategy-as-heuristics). The workshop is hands-on and design-oriented. Participants will (1) experience short, facilitated play segments; (2) map observed behaviors to core strategy concepts (e.g., positioning, real options, escalation, and stakeholder trade- offs); and (3) build a game-to-learning design blueprint that can be deployed in undergraduate, MBA, and executive classrooms. The design logic draws on experiential learning and the simulation/gaming literature, emphasizing debriefing as the hinge between “play” and durable conceptual learning.
