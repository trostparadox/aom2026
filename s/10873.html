Connecting the Dots: Systems Thinking Practices in Entrepreneurship

Authors: Viktoria Luise Unger LMU Munich School of Management; Jonah Zankl The University of Queensland

Session overview

This study explores how entrepreneurs enact systems thinking. While it is often assumed that market reforms occur in isolation, scholars increasingly emphasize the role of systems-level action in addressing complex challenges. Despite systems thinking being associated with the creation of sustained impact, research has largely left the question of how systems thinking is practiced underexplored. Drawing on an inductive, multi-case study of 30 impact entrepreneurs in Germany, we examine the practices, tools, and frameworks that constitute systems thinking enactment. Our findings highlight how entrepreneurs: (1) “connect the dots” to identify high-leverage intervention points; (2) visualize futures that bridge temporal domains; and (3) develop indicators of systems change that can guide and evaluate their impact. Our study contributes to understanding how systems thinking is practiced and embodied in entrepreneurial action when dealing with complex challenges.
