Driven by the Market or State? How Attention Structures Shape Corporate Digitalization

SAP
Li Li (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Chengli Shu (Xi'an Jiaotong University)
Digitalization is an important corporate-wide issue that requires strategic attention from the company. Such attention is, in turn, influenced by the corporation’s attention rules, which can determine what answers are conceived and selected to solve the digitalization issue. Drawing on the attention-based view, this study investigates how two key types of rules—market-driven rules (as represented by market power) and state-driven rules (as represented by political ties)—significantly influence the selection of digitalization answers. In addition to rules, we explore how other dimensions of attention structure, such as structural position (as represented by customer concentration), resources (as represented by knowledge dispersion), and players (as represented by R&D talent misallocation), function as moderators in the answer selection and issue-solving processes. We test our hypotheses by relying on a dataset of public companies from the Chinese stock markets, and it consists of 12,404 firm-year observations spanning from 2008 to 2021. Based on a series of robust data analyses, we find that market power has a positive impact while political ties have a negative impact on digitalization, and these effects are attenuated by the selected attention structure factors such as customer concentration, R&D talent misallocation, and knowledge dispersion. This study contributes to the literature by establishing an attention-based view of firm digitalization, which shows how attention rules directly and jointly with other attention structures impact answer selections to solve digitalization issues.
