Status Dynamics in Helping and Leadership Emergence in Global Workplaces

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

Authors: Marie-Catherine Mignault ESCP Business School; Minna Marinova Paunova ESCP Business School

Session overview

Leadership emergence is a negotiated social process in which individuals claim influence and others grant it through everyday interactions. We propose that helping exchanges—who seeks help, who provides it, and whether help is reciprocated—are a central micro-foundation of informal leadership legitimacy because both giving and seeking help shape competence and trust inferences. Yet helping is rarely status-neutral: dyadic status differences shape the costs of requesting help, the expectations to comply, and whether helping is rewarded with recognition. We develop and test a dyadic status perspective on helping and leadership emergence using complementary designs. Our focal test uses repeated speed-networking interactions (˜1,500 dyads) to estimate actor, partner, and relationship effects of demographic status (e.g., ethnicity and childhood socioeconomic status) on help seeking, refusal, and perceived leadership. We complement this with planned interactive studies that capture behavioral helping and emergent leadership in dyads and four-person groups, and with motivating organizational survey evidence with an embedded vignette (n˜700) that supports a national-status lens (HDI). Across studies, Actor-Partner Interdependence Models and Social Relations Models quantify how status alignment/mismatch predicts helping asymmetries and when helping exchanges translate into leader granting.
