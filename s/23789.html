Mapping Employer Image Research: Intellectual Structure via Bibliometrics and Topic Modeling

HR
Yeye Li (Hainan University); tianyu zhou (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Lijing Zhao
Amid intensifying talent competition and accelerating digitalization of labor markets, employer image has become a core construct linking organizations to current and prospective employees. Yet the literature remains fragmented across disciplinary traditions, levels of analysis, and methodological approaches, limiting cumulative knowledge. We synthesize the intellectual structure and thematic evolution of employer image research by integrating bibliometric mapping with Structural Topic Modeling (STM). Using 252 journal articles indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection, we identify the field’s knowledge foundations, delineate major thematic clusters, and trace how topics shift over time. The analysis reveals a distinctly interdisciplinary and increasingly cross-national research landscape. Substantively, the dominant themes span (a) attraction and job pursuit among potential applicants, (b) internal branding and employer brand management, (c) corporate social responsibility and reputational signaling, (d) employee attitudes and behaviors, (e) industry-level differentiation, and (f) the governance of online employee reviews and digital employer reputation. Building on these findings, we articulate an agenda for future research that emphasizes multi-source and multi-platform data integration, methodological triangulation (e.g., combining topic models with causal inference and measurement validation), and multi-level theorizing that connects external signaling with internal employee experience. Overall, this review provides a theory-relevant map of a rapidly expanding domain and offers a methodological template for generating cumulative insights from large-scale scholarly corpora.
