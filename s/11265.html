Organizational Issues in South African Health Clinics: Language incongruence and Patient Adaptations

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Michael Luma Ekema California State University-Long Beach; Azad Patel California State University-Long Beach

Session overview

Background: The current study examines how patients adapt their behavior to access healthcare services in linguistically diverse settings consisting of language incongruence between patients and healthcare providers. Method: 10 isiXhosa speaking individuals from the townships of Langa and Khayelitsha in South Africa’s Western Cape Province participated in semi-structured interviews where they shared their experiences seeking care at clinics where the staff primarily spoke Afrikaans or English. Interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed, and analyzed using deductive content analysis. Results: Language incongruence negatively impacted healthcare access. Patient adaptations included: Pre-sunrise travel, additional meal preparation, and a focus on staff professionalism. In terms of facilitating patient inclusion in the healthcare system, the utility of these adaptations was evident but limited. Discussion/Conclusions: Researchers and policy makers should study larger samples and non-isiXhosa populations to understand the circumstances when adaptations are most/least useful. Such information could inform future investments in the healthcare sector.
