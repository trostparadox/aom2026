Enabling the Role Hybridization of Doctor-Managers Through Participative Budgeting

HCM
Domenico Raucci; Manuela Paolini; Nicoletta Cera (University of Porto); Fausto Di Vincenzo
Assigning budgetary responsibilities to physicians leading operational units in public hospitals, alongside their clinical duties, has given rise to the role of ‘doctor-managers’, who are middle managers potentially experiencing role ambiguity issues. Drawing on literature on participative budgeting (PB), this study investigates how intra-organizational collaboration patterns within budgeting practices may support doctor-managers’ in clarifying role expectations and better addressing their budgetary responsibilities, thereby acting as an enabling mechanism for role hybridization. Using data from doctor-managers working in Italian public hospitals, the findings revealed that the budgeting exchange with top management and controllers activates positive cognitive and motivational functions, fostering a more effective integration of managerial and clinical roles. This research contributes to advancing psychology-based budgeting studies in public hospitals by examining individual-level drivers of healthcare middle managers’ involvement in the budgeting process.
