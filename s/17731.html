“Reading the Room”: How Marginalized Employees Manage Identity

Session overview

How do marginalized employees decide when to manifest, suppress, or strategically manage their identities at work? Research has long emphasized the benefits of identity expression, yet employees from marginalized backgrounds continue to suppress aspects of who they are in everyday organizational life. This symposium advances a more nuanced understanding of identity management by examining identity management strategies shaped by organizational cues, interpersonal dynamics, and broader socio-political contexts. Across five papers, presenters investigate when and how identity management strategies could carry psychological, relational, and career consequences. Topics include organizational signals of value and threat, cultural identity hobby disclosure in informal interactions, gender-based invisible cognitive labor, the trade-offs of minorities using whitened names, and the impact of DEI backlash on social class disclosure. Collectively, the symposium highlights how marginalized employees actively “read the room” to navigate competing demands for authenticity, safety, and belonging, offering new insights for creating workplaces that support inclusion without placing the burden on minority individuals alone.
