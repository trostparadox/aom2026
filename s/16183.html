Why Some Firms Seldom Invest in Corporate Social Responsibility: A Real Options Perspective

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 2
ONE

People

Authors: Yalan Xu South China University of Technology; YI YING The Chinese University of Hong Kong; David Ahlstrom The Chinese University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Drawing on real options theory, this study examines why many firms remain reluctant to engage in CSR, even as CSR is increasingly viewed as a strategic investment that generates option value by preserving flexibility under uncertainty. We argue that CSR investment decisions are contingent upon two uncertainty-related factors: the awareness of environment-based uncertainty, and the capability to manage resource-based uncertainties. These factors condition whether firms translate motivation into actual CSR commitment. Leveraging the introduction of China’s first CSR standards in 2015, we employ difference-in-differences design using a large longitudinal sample of 3,347 publicly listed firms from 2010 to 2020. The results provide preliminary support for our hypotheses, showing that while the external shock creates a strong motivation for CSR engagement, firms with limited uncertainty awareness and resource-management capability remain hesitant to invest in CSR.
