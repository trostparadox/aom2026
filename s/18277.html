Session overview

Only 3% of business decision-making research addresses high-volatility contexts, despite 25% of humanity operating in geopolitical conflict zones (Zaman et al., 2025; UN, 2024). Though most management theories assume institutional stability as a baseline, a significant portion of the global population operates under persistent volatility, economic, ecological, social, geopolitical, and technological disruptions. This Professional Development Workshop challenges stability-optimized assumptions in entrepreneurship and international management by examining how innovation emerges when constraints are not temporary obstacles but enduring conditions. Drawing on four IDRC-funded cases from Mexico, Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay, the workshop introduces Regenerative Intelligence—a decision architecture in which constraints function as generative forces rather than deficits to be overcome. Using a comparative design lens, we show how Global South innovators move from improvisational crisis response toward systematic regenerative frameworks, patterns that are often invisible or misclassified in dominant Western models. The workshop combines short case provocations with an interactive design sprint in which participants work in small groups to architect innovation responses to high-volatility scenarios (e.g., hyperinflation, ecological collapse, institutional voids). Participants will leave with analytical tools to identify epistemological blind spots in their own research, test the completeness of existing theories across volatility contexts, and generate new research questions grounded in underrepresented environments. This PDW positions constraint-led innovation not as resilience despite adversity, but as a source of intelligence critical to advancing entrepreneurship, international management, and social impact research. This workshop is designed for scholars who suspect their theories work well in stable contexts but break down under sustained volatility.
