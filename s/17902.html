Session overview

This PDW develops scholars’ and educators’ ability to study, and teach, how AI is reshaping entrepreneurial judgment in opportunity evaluation, experimentation, and funding decisions. Rather than treating generative AI as a back-office efficiency tool, we focus on AI as a partner in judgment that can alter how decision-makers search for signals, form confidence, manage disagreement, and allocate scarce resources under uncertainty. Recent work on AI in private markets highlights a key paradox: the promise of “more data, better decisions” can be dangerously incomplete in domains with slow feedback cycles and strategically curated disclosures, exactly the conditions that characterize early-stage venture evaluation and fundraising. In such settings, AI can increase fluency and apparent clarity faster than it improves truth, making polished narratives easier to produce and harder to interrogate. Participants will take part in an experiment: a live, structured early-stage venture evaluation exercise in small groups, comparing decisions made with AI-generated screening support versus human-only evaluation. This shared experience makes the mechanisms of AI-augmented judgment visible and creates a common basis for multi-way discussion across entrepreneurship, entrepreneurial finance, strategy, and technology/innovation domains. A moderated panel then synthesizes what the exercise reveals about (a) opportunity evaluation and screening, (b) experimentation and learning, and (c) funding decisions. The session is explicitly oriented toward actionable takeaways. Participants will leave with: (1) hands-on tools to leverage AI as partner in judgement while assessing entrepreneurial ventures, (2) a research “design menu” of testable questions and feasible empirical approaches (experiments, field designs, and measurement ideas) for studying human–AI decision processes in entrepreneurship and finance; and (3) ready-to-use teaching components (classroom exercise prompts, discussion questions, and assessment rubrics) for integrating AI-augmented judgment into entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial finance courses.
