Author: Tom Elwood Culham

Session overview

This paper proposes a Spiritual Research Paradigm (SRP) for management scholarship and education, addressing limitations of traditional scientific approaches that privilege reductionism, materialism, and third-person epistemologies. Drawing on Pierre Hadot’s conception of philosophy as a way of life and Michel Foucault’s “technologies of the self,” SRP integrates ancient and modern practices of intentional self-formation with contemporary research rigor. The framework is informed by Iain McGilchrist’s hemispheric model of attention, which distinguishes two modes of consciousness—left hemisphere (analytic, abstract, control-oriented) and right hemisphere (context-sensitive, relational, holistic)—and Edward O. Wilson’s cultural diagnosis of humanity’s imbalance between inner capacities and outer technologies. SRP operationalizes this integration through attentional protocols, dialogical formats, and artifact-based documentation, sequencing inquiry as Whole ? Part ? Whole: experiential grounding, analytic modeling, and reintegration. Applications span leadership development, organizational change, and management education, supported by virtue-oriented rubrics and mixed-method designs. By restoring parity between conceptual and experiential ontologies, SRP offers a rigorous yet humane paradigm that cultivates ethical discernment, dialogical competence, and practical wisdom. This contribution advances methodological pluralism within the MSR domain and provides actionable pathways for bridging theory and practice in complex organizational contexts.
