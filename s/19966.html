Using Large Language Models as Pseudo-Subject Matter Experts to Generate Situational Judgment Tests

Author: Christopher Huynh Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University

Session overview

This study presents a scalable methodological approach for generating Situational Judgment Test (SJT) items by integrating the Critical Incidents Technique (CIT) with large language models (LLMs). Drawing on Flanagan’s (1954) CIT framework, we demonstrate how ChatGPT can function as a pseudo–subject matter expert (SME) to simulate critical incident interview responses across diverse domains. Using a structured, Python-implemented pipeline, the approach first generates a heterogeneous pool of simulated SME roles spanning organizational levels and contexts. These pseudo-SMEs then respond to CIT interview prompts, producing detailed antecedent–behavior–consequence (ABC) descriptions of positive and negative incidents. The resulting critical incidents are systematically transformed into SJT scenarios aligned with Goal Orientation constructs, with response options mapped onto theoretically grounded dimensions of each construct. To enable scale, the ChatGPT API automates item generation across the workcontext, producing hundreds of scenarios in structured JSON format for downstream validation and analysis. Exploratory factor analysis and parallel analysis suggests the AI item generation process maintains the intended conceptual structure of goal orientation. This approach illustrates how LLMs can augment traditional qualitative methods to efficiently generate large, theory-driven SJT item pools while preserving conceptual rigor. The method contributes to research on assessment development by offering a replicable pipeline that combines qualitative theory, computational scalability, and validity considerations.
