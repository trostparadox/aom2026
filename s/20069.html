Session overview

Hope is increasingly recognized as a critical resource for individuals and organizations navigating societal issues. While scholarship has traditionally examined hope through psychological, philosophical, and political lenses, organizational research has yet to fully explore how hope is constructed, sustained, and mobilized within and across organizations. This study addresses that gap by investigating hope as an organized, actionable practice embedded in systemic change efforts. Philanthropic foundations with their long-term horizons, flexible capital, and convening power, offer a compelling empirical setting for examining how hope is cultivated and operationalized to shape transformative societal futures. Drawing on systems theory, which emphasizes complexity, interdependence, and nonlinearity, the study conceptualizes hope as a collective capacity to envision and step into emerging possibilities amid uncertainty. Using an inside-out methodology, the research combines interviews, observations, and archival data from six European foundations engaged in systemic change, supported by insights from a broader Community of Practice of philanthropic foundations. The study seeks to unveil how mechanisms for funding systems change function as sites for the manifestation of hope, how relational work fosters shared purpose and agency, and how foundations navigate tensions between ambition and practice. Ultimately, this article aims to advance organizational scholarship by theorizing hope as a vital force in enabling coordinated action and sustaining long-term transformative efforts.
