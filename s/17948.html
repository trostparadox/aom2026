AI as a Pedagogical Amplifier: How an AI-Enhanced Icebreaker Shapes Engagement and Communication.

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: Marina McCarthy University of Miami; Don Donelson University of Miami

Session overview

Icebreakers are commonly used in management education to promote early engagement and interaction, yet their effectiveness varies widely, particularly in large or technology-mediated classrooms. At the same time, artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly incorporated into educational settings, raising questions about how it shapes engagement during early-course activities. This study examines the effects of an AI-enhanced icebreaker on student engagement and communication in an undergraduate management course. Using an experimental design, course sections were assigned to either an AI-based or traditional version of the same icebreaker activity. Student engagement and communication were measured immediately before and after the activity. Regression analyses indicate that AI did not uniformly increase engagement. Instead, a significant interaction emerged such that communication was a stronger predictor of engagement in the AI-enhanced icebreaker than in the traditional version. We test whether artificial intelligence (AI) can function as a pedagogical amplifier by enhancing, rather than replacing, interpersonal dynamics during early-course icebreaker activities. In a between-sections experiment (n = 114) conducted during the first week of an undergraduate management course, sections experienced either an AI-enhanced or a traditional version of the same icebreaker. Because icebreakers are widely used yet yield uneven results, especially at scale, clarifying how AI shapes engagement in these early activities is practically important. Student engagement and communication were measured immediately before and after the activity. Regression analyses showed no uniform main effect of AI on engagement. Instead, a significant interaction emerged: communication predicted engagement more strongly in the AI-enhanced icebreaker than in the traditional version. When communication quality was high, engagement was higher in the AI condition; when communication quality was low, engagement did not differ meaningfully across conditions. These findings support AI as a pedagogical amplifier by strengthening the relationship between communication and engagement rather than replacing interpersonal interaction. The study contributes to management education research by offering empirical evidence on how AI integration shapes engagement dynamics in early-course experiential activities.
