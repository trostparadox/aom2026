Understand and Mitigate the Impact of Abusive Supervision on Employees' Self-Concept

ODC
Feirong ("Jackie") Yuan (University of Houston-Victoria)
The purpose of this paper is to use Karpman’s seminal work of Drama Triangle (1968) to explain the negative impact abusive supervision can exert on different areas of employees’ self-concept and, with that, to inform coping strategies for individuals and organizations. Specifically, we draw from Karpman’s framework to show how the three ego states of an abusive supervisor (i.e., as a “persecutor”, a “rescuer”, and a “victim”) can negatively influence several distinct aspects of an employee’s self-concept. We then identify mitigating strategies that both individual employees and organizational policymakers can adopt to lessen these negative influences. The theorization in this paper shows that: (1) abusive supervision can impact different aspects of an employee’ self-concept; (2) employees can use different strategies to cope with its impact in different areas; and (3) organizations can set up programs to support employees’ coping in these areas.
