Session overview

Computational Theory Construction (CTC) employs computational tools, such as social network analysis, sequence analysis, text analysis, and agent-based simulations, to uncover patterns in social systems and develop theories from them. Within the organizational and information systems fields, this approach is also referred to as "computationally intensive theory construction." Recent advancements in computational methods, including the adoption of large language models and Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), have expanded the potential of CTC. As these developments continue, a growing community of scholars is actively shaping and advancing this research genre. This PDW is a follow-up to the previous AOM PDWs on “Expanding and Extending the Community for Computational Theory Construction” in 2025, “Towards a Community of Computational Theory Construction Scholars” in 2024, “Towards a Craft of Computational Theory Construction” in 2023, “Computational Methods for Theory Building: Practices & Struggles” in 2022, and “The Rise of Computational Methods for Theory Development” in 2021. In these prior workshops, we focused on establishing the legitimacy of this new genre of research and providing a collaborative forum to help participants. As computational techniques continue to evolve, this follow-up workshop is dedicated to introducing advanced computational approaches to theory construction while articulating, discussing, and addressing the challenges that arise from their use. Building on the traditions of prior CTC PDWs, the workshop aims to connect computational and qualitative researchers, share insights, and support junior scholars in developing rigorous CTC projects. Part I of the PDW – the keynotes and panel discussion are open to all AOM registrants. Part II – roundtable discussions are only for participants who would like to receive feedback on their ongoing or planned projects and have been accepted to attend the roundtables. For those who are interested in participating in roundtables, please submit a short vignette (max 2 pages, PDF format) to ctcpdw21@gmail.com by July 15. The organizers will send out the notification of acceptance in mid-July. Your vignette should cover the following aspects. • Brief introduction of the research project • Your use of computational techniques in combination with other methods, such as qualitative approaches, for theory construction. • What struggles have you faced in understanding, using, or considering using computational methods in your theory development work? Those who have not conducted any such research but plan to do so can submit short descriptions of their intended work, focusing on their planned combination of manual and computational techniques or related questions. These descriptions should focus on the CTC practices you plan to adopt and related challenges. We look forward to seeing you and engaging in fruitful discussions during the PDW!
