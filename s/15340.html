Session overview

Many large-scale change initiatives struggle not because strategies are poorly designed, but because insufficient attention is paid to how change unfolds once plans meet organizational reality. In complex institutional contexts, momentum rarely follows linear implementation. Instead, it emerges through the coordination of small actions across organizational boundaries, ongoing feedback, and the renegotiation of commitments under uncertainty. This PDW draws on anonymised evidence from an international, multi-region, cross-sector change programme in which coalitions of policymakers, academics, entrepreneurs, corporate leaders, investors, and civil-society actors co-designed innovation action plans and then began translating them into practice. The setting provides a rare longitudinal view of what happens when formal objectives encounter distributed authority, professional identities, resource constraints, and competing time horizons. Using artefacts from later stages of the change process (including draft action plans, stress-testing feedback, and revised commitments), the workshop examines how modest adjustments (such as redefining ownership, narrowing scope, or sequencing actions) compound over time and give rise to emergent change. Through short simulations and structured group exercises, participants identify micro-actions that create traction, trace how these ripple across relationships and routines, and analyse why some actions amplify momentum while others dissipate. Facilitated reflection connects experience to ODC scholarship on emergence, distributed agency, and cross-organizational change, equipping participants with process-oriented lenses for studying and working with change as it actually unfolds. This is a highly interactive workshop. Participants will engage in simulations, small-group work, and structured reflection throughout the session. No prior preparation is required, but participants may wish to reflect on a change initiative they have experienced, particularly where outcomes diverged from initial plans.
