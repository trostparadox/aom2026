Antecedents of the Shift From Stock Options to Restricted Stock in CEO Compensation

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
STR

People

Authors: Shuaijun Zhang Georgia Gwinnett College; Erin Hou Lamar Academy; Alice Xi

Session overview

Drawing on agency theory and managerial power perspective, this study examines how CEO duality, board independence, and institutional ownership influence the likelihood that firms transition from stock options to restricted stock in CEO compensation. By analyzing a longitudinal panel of 943 U.S. publicly listed firms between 2002 and 2021, we find that CEO duality significantly reduces the likelihood of switching, suggesting that powerful CEOs resist compensation structures that reduce upside potential. Second, board independence increases the probability of switching, consistent with stronger monitoring and long-term incentive alignment. Third, institutional ownership exhibits a strong positive association with the switch, indicating that external monitoring pressures favor restricted stock over risk-inducing options. These results remain robust to extensive controls and a Heckman selection model addressing potential sample selection concerns. Overall, the study reframes executive compensation design as a dynamic governance outcome shaped by power and monitoring. The findings contribute to research on executive pay, corporate governance, and strategic control by explaining when and why firms replace option-based incentives with ownership-based compensation.
