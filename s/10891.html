Session overview

While quantitative metrics are essential for corporate accountability, reliance on data alone often fails to foster stakeholder engagement, creating a "communication gap." This study explores the strategic role of narrative as a complementary tool to metrics in sustainability communication. Drawing on in-depth case studies of women entrepreneurs in the circular economy in Japan and Denmark, we analyze how they leverage storytelling to overcome barriers and build legitimacy. The findings reveal that these entrepreneurs use narrative for sensemaking, transforming abstract sustainability concepts into tangible daily actions ("One carton," "Every grain counts"), while anchoring their stories in objective frameworks like the SDGs. We argue that authentic communication requires "complementarity": metrics provide the evidence, while narrative provides the context and purpose. This study offers practical implications for how organizations can integrate narrative strategies to bridge the divide between technical disclosure and genuine stakeholder trust.
