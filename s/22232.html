Session overview

Throughout millennia, at first through oral traditions and then written word, stories have connected people and societies through shared understandings of the human experience. For human development at all ages and stages, stories are a powerful means to organize and transmit information, bring abstract principles to life, and concretize the particulars of experience in a time and place for all people and cultures as universal human experience. The purpose of this PDW is to collectively explore storytelling as a pedagogical resource to enhance classroom teaching and learning as well as honor it as a shared emotional/spiritual experience. Mental phenomena are just as real as physical phenomena. Teachers and students learn and grow through sharing of ideas and beliefs, images and feelings, passions and dreams as they are all a part of our uniquely embodied and collective minds. Facilitators will introduce a story from the Sufi tradition around the theme “identity formation and transformation.” Participants will reflect and share personal thoughts, associated memories, and emotional reactions to the story. Then, in small groups, participants will select a photograph from Life Magazine archives to explore psychological projections onto the image as a way of understanding more deeply one’s identity associations and values through stories regarding varieties of human experiences. Specifically: 1) What inspired your selection of the image? 2) Narrate a story about the image. 3) What insights about your own selfhood does the image elicit? 4) How do you make meaning of emerging feelings and thoughts as related to your personal/social identity?
