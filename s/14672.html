Drivers of Business Undergraduates’ Learning Engagement: A Motivational Process Perspective

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: Chung-Kai Huang National Taipei University of Business; CHUNYU LIN National Taipei University

Session overview

This study explores how teaching models and students’ self-efficacy jointly shape learning engagement in Taiwanese undergraduate business education. Special attention was paid to the motivational mechanisms and learning conditions that explain when and why pedagogical efforts are effective. Grounded in self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 2013) and social cognitive theory (Bandura, 1986; 2000), the study develops and empirically tests a moderated mediation framework in which intrinsic motivation serves as a central psychological mechanism that connects teaching models and self-efficacy to learning engagement. Under this mechanism, the value of learning tasks was also found to condition the strength of both direct and indirect relationships (Gan et al., 2023). By integrating instructional design factors with individual motivational beliefs, this study responds to the ongoing calls in management education research for theory-driven investigations that move beyond surface-level examination regarding teaching practices and student learning outcomes in and out of business classrooms. In the past decade, business and management education in Taiwan has increasingly emphasized meaningful learning experiences, intrinsic motivation, and active student engagement (Chen, 2022). Rather than relying primarily on passive knowledge transmission, this pedagogical shift reflects broader global trends toward learner-centered and constructivist approaches (Alam, 2023; Mir, 2025). Business and management educators and researchers have put more effort into understanding the learning needs and preferences of Generation Z students, who tend to value autonomy, relevance, interaction, and purpose in their educational experiences (Cain et al,,2022; Mantha & Krishna, 2024). With the growing adoption of experiential, reflective, and technology-supported teaching models, business and management education has become more dynamic and participatory (Lin et al., 2020). However, despite the urgent call for implementing innovative teaching approaches, empirical evidence explaining how these models influence students’ motivation and engagement and under what conditions they are effective remains under-researched. Existing research studies that focus on instructional design have continued to call for examining the unpacking of psychological processes in teaching and learning of business and management education (Huang & Lin, 2017; Lin & Huang, 2020; Tran, 2024). Through which teaching models exert their pedagogical impact and how they account for individual differences among undergraduate business students on the learning outcomes (Mohsin, 2024). Prior research suggests that self-efficacy plays a crucial role in shaping students’ motivation and engagement in the learning environment they are exposed to (Alemayehu & Chen, 2023). Based on social cognitive theory, self-efficacy reflects an individual’s belief in their capacity to overcome challenges and perform needed tasks. Students with higher self-efficacy are more inclined to approach learning tasks with greater confidence, manage difficulties more effectively, and experience positive affect during their learning trajectory (Zhai & Li, 2025). Echoing self-determination theory, such experiences suggest that intrinsic motivation may develop when learners perceive that their needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are adequately supported (Scogin et al., 2023). According to the aforementioned viewpoints, self-efficacy can be viewed as a critical antecedent of intrinsic motivation under the conditions that students who feel capable are more likely to find learning activities interesting, enjoyable, and satisfying. Intrinsic motivation, in turn, has been identified as another predictor of sustained effort that promotes quality learning engagement. Despite this theoretical alignment, management education studies have suggested that self-efficacy, intrinsic motivation, and engagement as components of an integrated motivational process. In addition, teaching models can be treated as direct drivers that may operate through students’ internal motivational states. Teaching models that emphasize interaction, reflection, collaboration, and learner autonomy are theorized to support students’ psychological needs for cultivating their autonomy and competence, thereby stirring up intrinsic motivation. Unlike compelling engagement through external instructional control or mandate pressure, such models could create motivational conditions that encourage students to engage more willingly and meaningfully. Subsequently, this study aims to examine whether intrinsic motivation mediates the relationships between self-efficacy and learning engagement, as well as between teaching models and learning engagement. On top of these mediating mechanisms, this study highlights the importance of learning value as a critical boundary condition. Learning value captures the extent to which students perceive learning as relevant, worthwhile, and meaningful as opposed to the quest for immediate performance outcomes. Students may differ in how they value learning itself and these differences may further shape whether pedagogical efforts translate into genuine engagement. Students who perceive learning tasks with high value are more likely to internalize learning goals. By responding positively to motivational cues, they are more able to sustain engagement even in the face of challenges. Consequently, learning value is expected to strengthen the effects of self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation on learning engagement. Also, it could amplify the indirect effects of self-efficacy and teaching models through intrinsic motivation. This study, by incorporating learning value as a moderator, examines the common ground between general business and management education by offering a more nuanced explanation of students’ engagement responses. To empirically test the proposed moderated mediation model, data were collected from 135 undergraduate Taiwanese students through a survey. These students are those who enrolled in workplace communication and sustainability courses at a vocational-tracked university. These courses were selected because they incorporated a range of teaching models, including interactive discussions, group-based projects, reflective assignments, and real-world applications, that provide a suitable context for examining variation in instructional design and student experiences. Validated measurement scales were used to assess the following constructs: teaching models, self-efficacy, intrinsic motivation, learning value, and learning engagement. We employed AMOS software for structural equation modeling to evaluate the measurement model and test the hypothesized mediation effects. PROCESS macro analyses were used to examine moderation and moderated mediation relationships. The analytical strategy enabled a rigorous examination of the proposed theoretical framework while accounting for the complexity of motivational processes. The findings provide both theoretical and practical support for the proposed model. Intrinsic motivation fully mediates the relationships between self-efficacy and learning engagement and between teaching models and learning engagement. This relationship indicated that both individual beliefs and instructional design influence engagement through motivational pathways rather than through direct effects. Furthermore, learning value significantly moderates the relationships between self-efficacy and learning engagement and between intrinsic motivation and learning engagement. These relationships are strangely demonstrated among students who perceive learning as highly valuable. Learning value strengthens the indirect effects of both self-efficacy and teaching models on learning engagement through intrinsic motivation, confirming the presence of moderated mediation. These results highlight the role of intrinsic motivation as a psychological mechanism that links pedagogical practices and personal beliefs to engagement. The importance of students’ value orientations in shaping the effectiveness of instructional interventions was highlighted simultaneously. This study has several pedagogical implications that are particularly insightful in the context of vocational undergraduate business and management education. Vocational universities in Taiwan typically recruit students with diverse backgrounds, practice-oriented goals, and prior learning experiences that emphasize skill acquisition over theory-driven inquiry. Vocational students face challenges related to academic self-efficacy, learning confidence, and sustained engagement in traditional lecture-based classrooms. The findings suggest that instructors and school administrators should devote more effort to providing structured pedagogical support, engaging in authentic tasks, and emphasizing the value of meaningful learning. Design-thinking-based teaching approaches, workshop-oriented training sessions, and field trips to industry, along with field trips to business organizations and keynote presentations featuring industry experts, can be considered to address these challenges by promoting intrinsic motivation and engagement. As Taiwan continues to bridge the gap between the supply and demand of higher business and management education and workforce development, developing motivationally supportive teaching models and learning modules will be a long-term commitment in vocational undergraduate contexts.
