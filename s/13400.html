When Learning Organizes Work: Rethinking Differentiation through Sociomaterial Interaction

Authors: Ana Milan Aleksic Miric University of Belgrade; Katarina Božic University of East Sarajevo; Anja Stevanovic University of Belgrade, Faculty of Economics and Business

Session overview

Classical and sociomaterial perspectives on organizational change share a human-centered epistemology: learning, adaptation, and change are enacted by people, while materiality provides relatively stable affordances. Yet contemporary sociotechnical systems increasingly learn from use, accumulate statistical priors, and modify their own affordances over time. Through an illustrative vignette of AI-assisted radiology, we show how human clinicians and learning systems engage in reciprocal adaptation, producing workflow reconfiguration and organizational redesign without formal change interventions. We term this condition learning materiality, in which material artifacts possess memory, update parameters, and alter future sociomaterial arrangements. Under such conditions, organizational change becomes an emergent consequence of interacting learning systems rather than the outcome of top-down restructuring or continuous sensemaking alone. We articulate implications for organizational change theory, organizational design, and governance by highlighting how learning materiality redistributes epistemic authority, coordination, and responsibility within complex organizations.
