Session overview

This symposium examines how entrepreneurship unfolds among individuals and communities facing persistent economic, social, geographic, and institutional marginalization, and argues that centering these contexts can revise core assumptions in entrepreneurship and organizational theory. Bringing together four studies across diverse empirical settings, the symposium highlights how structural constraints shape resource access, legitimacy, and venture strategy for micro-entrepreneurs, women and racial minorities, founders from lower social-class origins, and justice-involved entrepreneurs. The papers introduce new mechanisms and methods: LLM-assisted mechanism discovery to explain gender profit gaps in microenterprises; evidence that elite private school pedigree advantages founders in early VC access; mixed-method and experimental evidence showing that “centering” incarceration stigma in venture narratives can improve investor evaluation; and an RCT demonstrating that structured family consultative voice can shift investment and profit dynamics in micro-enterprises.
