Session overview

Decentralization in organizations is gaining attention as a research stream due to its alignment with contemporary challenges, including skepticism toward managerial hierarchy, demands for greater transparency and inclusion, and the need for agility amid macro-level disruptions. Yet despite this momentum, the discourse on decentralization remains fragmented. Competing definitions, heterogeneous empirical settings, and diverging ideological commitments have made it difficult to establish clear theoretical foundations or cumulate insights across studies. Recent scholarship cautions that decentralization risks becoming a management fad or a repackaging of long-standing organizational ideas, even as empirical work highlights significant opportunities for theory development and engagement with contemporary challenges. This PDW seeks to advance the research by moving beyond foundational and ideological debates toward a more coherent and actionable research agenda. Drawing on scholars across organizational theory, strategy, change, innovation, and design, the session emphasizes conceptual refinement, key empirical opportunities, and the practical challenges shaping decentralization efforts. Building on prior AOM caucuses and symposia, this fourth edition shifts the focus from mapping existing work to specifying concrete pathways for theory building and empirical research. Through panel insights, audience-engaged discussion, and roundtables for participant-submitted work, the PDW aims to catalyze collaboration and advance high-impact research and practice.
