Creating Something from Nothing: How Actors Construct Rural Entrepreneurial Ecosystems

ENT
Yuehua Xu (Shandong University); Yike Sui (Shandong University); Yimin Wang (Shandong University)
Rural areas constitute a unique institutional field. Existing literature predominantly focuses on entrepreneurial ecosystems in urban or developed regions, leaving a gap in the in-depth exploration of improving business environments and establishing entrepreneurial ecosystems in rural areas characterized by resource constraints and institutional voids. Adopting an exploratory single-case study method, this study dissects the transformation mechanism of Deng Guang Village from a natural village into an entrepreneurial ecosystem characterized by superimposed commercial ecologies. The findings reveal that the construction of a rural entrepreneurial ecosystem is a non-linear evolutionary process driven by endogenous institutional entrepreneurs. In the first stage, institutional entrepreneurs primarily “reshape field operational rules” to activate dormant resources, thereby achieving a focal breakthrough in the collective economy. In the second stage, these entrepreneurs “orchestrate field symbiotic order” to integrate and coordinate multiple stakeholders—including enterprises and farming households—for collaborative co-creation, ultimately facilitating the emergence of the rural entrepreneurial ecosystem. This study highlights that in rural contexts plagued by an insufficient supply of formal institutions, the institutional entrepreneurial actions of micro-level actors constitute the core driving force initiating and propelling the construction of entrepreneurial ecosystems.
