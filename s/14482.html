Beyond the Norm: Noble Entrepreneurship Through the Lenses of Physical and Virtue

Authors: Navarat Sachayansrisakul; Nattawat Ponnara

Session overview

In today’s global economy, business decision-making is often dominated by materialism, consumerism, and competitive pressures, resulting in an imbalance between profit and purpose. This study proposes a reorientation of entrepreneurship grounded in the Buddhist law of karma, which emphasizes that actions rooted in ethical and moral responsibility generate enduring outcomes for both individuals and enterprises. Drawing on Buddhism as an educational system rather than a comparative belief framework, the study reframes entrepreneurship as a process of ethical education and human cultivation. The research introduces the Ladder of Noble Enterprise, a five-step assessment model that explains how ventures progress toward sustainability: Start-up (commitment and self-belief), Survival (effort without structure), Strong (discipline and responsibility), Stability (virtuous consistency as a way of life), and Sustainability (wisdom-led leadership and compassion). The model operates across two interconnected dimensions—the physical development of the enterprise and the virtuous development of the entrepreneur—illustrating how managerial, moral, and spiritual growth reinforce one another. The study addresses two research questions: (1) how ventures transition physically across the five stages toward sustainability, and (2) how entrepreneurs evolve from specialists to managers and ultimately noble entrepreneurs, guided by Buddhist teachings on offering, discipline, and wisdom. Using a qualitative, interpretive methodology, data were collected through semi-structured interviews and case studies involving 22 Buddhist entrepreneurs in Thailand and Australia. Findings indicate that most participants were positioned within the Survival and Strong stages, demonstrating skill and discipline but limited progression toward sustainability. To support advancement, the study develops the Noble Canvas, a reflective self-assessment tool derived from the Ladder model. The findings suggest that sustainability is both a moral and strategic necessity: enterprises that fail ethically cannot sustain over time. The study contributes to entrepreneurship and management scholarship by integrating Buddhist philosophy with sustainability discourse and offers a practical framework for educators, policymakers, and entrepreneurs seeking to cultivate virtuous and sustainable enterprise.
