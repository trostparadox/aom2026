Session overview

Worldwide, organizations have reevaluated their roles and economic activities in response to urgent societal challenges. In this context, technological advances have led to numerous innovations and entrepreneurial ways to capture economic value (including technology commercialization, new business models, startups, and new markets), while also addressing social needs and creating public value. Using a multidisciplinary approach—covering entrepreneurship, innovation management, sustainability, public administration, and institutional theory—, this Professional Development Workshop (PDW) provides an engaging debate into how “technological advances,” “entrepreneurial opportunities,” and “public/social value” have encouraged the rise of various “prosocial agents” (entrepreneurs, producers, inventors, changemakers) across organizations (public, private, non-profit) and institutional contexts (developed, developing, emergent), transforming entrepreneurial activities, traditional technological processes, organizational management, workplace setups, business models, performance metrics, and ecosystem interactions. This PDW aims to be interactive, engaging, and outcome-oriented. Following a brief introduction to prosocial technology by the organizers and Idea Sparks—featuring provocative questions from senior academics and editors of four top-tier journals—the session extends beyond typical roundtables by including Action Labs facilitated by early-career scholars, promoting tangible results. By the end of this PDW, we hope to foster an emerging international community of scholars dedicated to public-good technology and entrepreneurship.
