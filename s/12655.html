Plaintiff vs. Defendant: The Divergent Innovation Consequences of Patent Litigation

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 5
TIM

People

Authors: Ao Shen Xidian University; Patrick Peng Wang Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University; Chenguang Hu Beijing Normal-Hong Kong Baptist University

Session overview

In the context of increasing technological advancements, patent disputes and litigations are becoming more and more prevalent. Intellectual property protection has thus become a crucial factor for companies to foster innovation and maintain competitiveness. Whether the involvement of patent litigation will stimulate and suppress subsequent innovation remains a significant issue that needs further academic exploration. This study, based on the perspective of innovation motivation, examines the complex effects of patent litigation on corporate innovation behavior. Although both plaintiff and defendant companies will increase their innovation investments after the litigation to address corresponding challenges, their innovation strategies differ significantly as a result of differentiated motivations: plaintiff companies are more inclined to pursue substantial innovation, while defendant companies focus more on strategic innovation. Based on a unique sample of technology-based small and medium-sized enterprises in China, the theoretical framework is largely supported. The study not only contributes to corporate litigation literature by distinguishing between the plaintiff and the defendant, but also provides actionable solutions for intellectual property management in complex market environments.
