Pixels to People: The Role of Digital Leadership in Social Identity Evolution

MOC
Sana Mumtaz (University of Niagara Falls, Canada); Sinnathamby Sritharakumar (University of Niagara Falls, Canada)
The integration of emerging technological tools has enabled organizations across various industries to transform their work processes into digital ones. While this literature offers a holistic understanding of the conducive outcomes of digital leadership on organizational work efficiency, a lack of understanding exists regarding the individual-level change experiences of employees while working in a digital work environment. Hence, this research has been integrated within higher education institutions. In this regard, a process model has been proposed to suggest how digital leadership enhances employee voice behaviour, particularly when digital maturity is high in organizations, and is likely to lead to enhanced psychological safety among employees. Moreover, perceptions of psychological safety facilitate high digital resilience and encourage employees to embrace positive social identity changes with time. While the developed model may be applied and tested in diverse contexts to understand the social identity change experiences of employees, its application may be particularly useful for higher educational institutions to understand in-depth how social identity changes are reflected through changes in digital engagement, socio-psychological well-being and educational innovation of academicians.
