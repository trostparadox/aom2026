Session overview

Green innovation is central to addressing climate change and other grand challenges, yet existing theories of innovation only partially explain how green technologies emerge, scale, and persist in settings characterized by contested responsibility, intertemporal trade-offs, and heightened exposure to non-market scrutiny. This symposium brings together four papers that examine the behavioral, organizational, and institutional foundations of green innovation across the innovation lifecycle—from early-stage capital allocation and regulator–firm information exchange, to enforcement-triggered technological problem solving and the persistence of green technologies after invention. Collectively, the papers advance research in Strategic Management, Organizations and the Natural Environment, and Technology and Innovation Management by showing how market and non-market actors jointly shape firms’ green innovation choices and trajectories, and clarifying when general theories of innovation travel to green innovation contexts and where distinct theoretical lenses are needed.
