Session overview

Organizations are increasingly sites where grand challenges such as climate change, inequality, and technological ethics are contested and negotiated. Across mission-driven and profit-seeking settings alike, insider social change agents (ISCAs) mobilize efforts to advance social purposes beyond symbolic commitments. Although foundational research on tempered radicals demonstrates how these actors achieve local “small wins” within organizations, and recent studies document the expanding diversity and roles of ISCAs within various organizational contexts, key questions remain. This symposium positions organizational design as both a context for insider social change and a consequence of sustained ISCA activity. The five presentations investigate how organizational structures, control systems, and coordination mechanisms facilitate, constrain, or direct insider activism; how ongoing ISCA efforts drive the emergence of new governance arrangements, hybrid organizational forms, and democratic organizing practices; and the conditions under which local initiatives produce enduring, organization-wide change. Employing ethnographic, mixed-methods, and longitudinal comparative methods, the presentations reveal the reciprocal relationship between organizational design and insider-driven social change across diverse contexts, including cooperatives, universities, technology firms, corporations, and multinational enterprises.
