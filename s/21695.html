The Dark Side of Emotional Intelligence: Perceived Dirty Work, Metastereotypes and Workplace Bullying

Authors: Sodiq Olanrewaju Babatunde University of Cincinnati; Ifeoluwa Tobi Popoola Murray State University; Babatunde Musiliu Abina Nottingham Trent University; Scott Dust University of Cincinnati

Session overview

We unpack the link between perceived dirty work (PDW) and workplace bullying (WB) through the lens of Minority Stress Theory (MST). Typically, when working in stigmatized occupations, tendency to view one's work as ‘dirty’ reflects a belief that others consider the job as low-status and not deserving of respect. This consequently results in metastereotypes (MS), or belief that other people hold negative views about one’s profession. Like any other form of stereotype, MS serves as a stressor with the immediate appraisal fostering (vulnerability to) workplace bullying. We hypothesized that PDW is positively related to WB (H1), and MS positively linked to MB (H2) while mediating the PDW-WB relationship (H3). Moreover, we predicted that emotional intelligence (EI) would strengthen both the direct effect of PDW on employee’s MST (H4a) and would indirectly influence WB positively (H4b). Therefore, revealing the dark side of emotional intelligence in the workplace. Controlling for participants’ age, gender, and education, we tested our theoretical model using data from 234 full-time employees in WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic) nations of the UK and U.S. Result support all hypotheses and findings reveal the paradoxical nature of EI in stigmatized contexts. Offering theoretical and practical contributions for managers and administrators to manage workplace stress and bullying. Keywords: Emotional intelligence, Metastereotype, Minority stress theory, Perceived dirty work, Workplace bullying.
