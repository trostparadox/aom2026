A Latent Profile Analysis of Upward Communication and Its Impact on Performance Evaluations

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

People

Authors: Junchao Li Rutgers Business School; Mengyi Zhang Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Jingzhou Pan Tianjin University; Shuhua Sun Tulane University

Session overview

This paper employs a latent profile analysis approach to investigate the patterns of employees’ upward communications towards their supervisors and the implications of these patterns on employees’ performance evaluations. Applying a 2 (improvement-oriented vs. positive-oriented) by 2 (self-focused vs. leader-focused) taxonomy, we focused on four types of upward communications: (a) feedback seeking (improvement-oriented, self-focused), (b) constructive voice (improvement-oriented, leader-focused), (c) upward capitalization (positive, self-focused), and (d) upward ingratiation (positive, leader-focused). Across two studies (N = 887 and N = 788), we identified five communication profiles. Longitudinal analyses revealed that substantive communication (voice, advice-seeking, capitalization) predicts higher performance evaluations, but with diminishing marginal returns. Counterintuitively, high ingratiation combined with high substantive communication backfires—employees who engaged selectively (high substantive, low ingratiation) received significantly better performance evaluations than those who combined high substantive communication with high ingratiation. These findings challenge assumptions about the benefits of ingratiation and highlight that the importance of communication effectiveness depends on the holistic pattern of behaviors rather than individual dimensions in isolation.
