Leaders’ Virtual Visibility in Global Virtual Teams: Exploring the Role of Identity Leadership

Author: Omaima Hajjami Louisiana State University

Session overview

Team identification is recognized as a key driver of individual and collective performance. In traditional, co-located teams, team identification emerges through sustained face-to-face interactions among members. However, in global virtual teams (GVTs), geographical dispersion and reliance on virtual communication make the development of team identification more complex. While leadership is recognized as a facilitator of team identification, existing research has predominantly focused on traditional teams, leaving a gap in understanding its role within GVTs. Therefore, the purpose of this study will be to explore how global virtual teams’ leaders and members perceive the role of leadership in strengthening team identification. This study will employ a qualitative appreciative inquiry design using semi-structured interviews to answer the following research question: 1. In what ways do global virtual teams’ members and leaders perceive identity leadership as influencing team identification? I will collect the data, transcribe, and analyze it using NVivo following a multi-cycle coding process (open, pattern, and axial coding) to identify overarching themes that answer the research questions. Findings of this study will address theoretical, empirical, methodological, and practical gaps centered around the development of identity leadership in GVTs.
