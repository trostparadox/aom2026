Impact of Visual & Print Media Representations of Neurodiverse Individuals on Career Aspirations

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Authors: Amy E. Hurley-Hanson Chapman University; Cristina Marie Giannantonio Chapman University; Yeeun Jeon; Ashley Nicole Scott Chapman University

Session overview

This paper examines how visual and print media representations of neurodivergent individuals may potentially influence the career aspirations of neurodivergent individuals through the formation of image norms. Giannantonio and Hurley-Hanson (2006) suggest that an image norm is the belief that individuals must present an image consistent with occupational, organizational, or industry standards to be hired or promoted. It is suggested that image norms may influence the career aspirations of neurodivergent individuals as a result of the jobs held by neurodivergent characters in both print and visual media. A comprehensive search was conducted to identify neurodivergent individuals portrayed in print (e.g. books) and visual (e.g. movies and television shows) media. A variety of neurodiversity diagnoses were included in the search (e.g. ADHD, autism, dyslexia, Tourette’s, etc.). The material was analyzed to identify the characters’ current occupational job category, and when possible, information on their career trajectory. Additional data collected included job level, meaning leadership or managerial responsibilities, whether the job involved working alone or with others, and demographic data on age, gender, and race. Fifty six representations of neurodiversity in print and visual media were identified. The implications of these findings on the career aspirations of neurodivergent individuals are discussed.
