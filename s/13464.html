Session overview

Strategy-as-Practice (SAP) research has developed into a vibrant and diverse scholarly community, characterized by methodological richness and strong engagement with empirical phenomena of strategizing and organizing. At the same time, scholars—particularly doctoral students and early-career researchers —might struggle with transforming promising SAP ideas into publishable journal articles. Due to the empirical, theoretical, and methodological diversity of SAP research, experienced scholars from divisions such as OMT, STR and ONE may find positioning contributions, articulating theoretical value, and translating rich empirical material into practice-theoretical arguments challenging as well. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is designed to support scholars in addressing these challenges through a developmental format. The workshop will consist of two parts. First, the workshop will include an introductory panel with experienced SAP scholars, who will reflect on their own publication trajectories, writing practices, and key turning points in developing successful research papers. This part is open to everybody. Second, the workshop will host roundtable discussions led by experienced mentors and will focus on discussing and developing participants submitted extended abstracts. By integrating insights from experienced scholars with developmental mentoring and discussion, the PDW seeks to demystify the publication process in SAP research, strengthen participants’ manuscripts, and foster a supportive scholarly community. The workshop is particularly suited for doctoral students and early-career researchers but also offers value for more experienced scholars seeking to refine their writing and positioning strategies within the SAP literature. Dear Attendees, If you are interested in receiving feedback for your Strategy-as-Practice (SAP) related publications and drafts, please consider applying for the second part of the Paper Development Workshop. While the first part of the workshop is open to everybody, the second part transitions into multiple roundtable sessions. In these roundtables, accepted applicants will have the opportunity to discuss their work in progress and receive developmental feedback from experienced SAP scholars and mentors. Submissions can range from early-stage manuscripts to papers that are currently under revision. If you wish to apply, please submit an extended abstract of your manuscript. Please ensure your submission adheres to the following guidelines: Word Count: Extended abstracts should not exceed 2000 words. Required Content: Submissions must outline your research questions, theoretical framing (incl. its relation to SAP literature), empirical material and findings if available, and your intended contributions. Current Status: Please indicate the current status of your paper (e.g., under review, intended submission).
