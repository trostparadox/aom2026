Navigating Multiple Institutional Logics in the Space Economy: A Comparative Case Study

ODC
Matthew Ayamga (Wageningen University and Research); Antonio Messeni Petruzzelli (Polytechnic of Bari); Umberto Panniello
Why do dual-segment space companies develop unique organizational capabilities to manage institutional complexity? While research on institutional logics primarily examines how firms manage competing demands through decoupling or symbolic compliance, less is known about how companies operating across both upstream and downstream segments of the space economy respond when faced with conflicting logics across jurisdictions. Using a comparative qualitative case study of four leading dual-segment space companies—Northrop Grumman, Maxar Technologies in the United States (US), Airbus Defence and Space, and Thales Alenia Space in the European Union (EU)—we examine how these organizations respond to institutional demands arising from four dominant logics: national security, commercial innovation/market, scientific exploration, and sustainability. To our surprise, and contrary to common assumptions, we find that these companies do not simply balance competing logics—they develop distinct capability configurations that either connect logics (e.g., coalition management, boundary-spanning roles) or shield them (e.g., digital engineering, modular platforms). Based on this insight, we propose a capability-based framework for institutional complexity in the space economy, advancing understanding of how dual-segment space companies adapt dynamically to their geopolitical and regulatory contexts. We further discuss how these findings advance theories on institutional logics, organizational capabilities, and embeddedness.
