Session overview

Municipalities are increasingly becoming the primary sites where emerging technologies are reshaping governance, service delivery, and social outcomes. While the “smart city” agenda promised efficiency and optimization, it has also raised concerns related to inequality, surveillance, fragmentation, and limited societal impact. These tensions are especially salient in municipalities of the Global South, where institutional capacity, social inequality, and rapid urbanization coexist with accelerated technological adoption now permitting rural adoption. This symposium aims to advance an integrated research and practice agenda on digital-driven regional transformations, with a specific focus on human-centered governance models for smart and inclusive municipalities. Building on emerging frameworks such as human-centered smart cities, governance intelligence, and strategic maturity models. The symposium brings together scholars, journal editors, and practitioners to: (1) identify core research questions at the intersection of technology, municipal governance, and societal impact; (2) discuss methodological and theoretical approaches suitable for studying AI in municipalities; (3) translate research insights into implications for teaching, policy design, and practice. Through interactive sessions, participants will co-develop future research directions, reflect on publication strategies, and examine real-world governance challenges from municipalities in Latin America and beyond.
