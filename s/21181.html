Cultivating Professional Skepticism in Strategic Foresight for Management Consultants

Session overview

Management consulting education faces a critical gap: students learn frameworks and methodologies but rarely develop the professional skepticism required to question client narratives, surface hidden assumptions, or challenge stakeholder biases. Without this competency, graduates become "narrative parrots" - practitioners who replicate dominant frameworks without interrogating their assumptions or unintended consequences. This gap becomes acute when addressing uncertain futures, where students must ask: Whose future are we building? Drawing from presenter’s background as a chartered accountant and on over 250 real-life student consulting engagements spanning fifteen years and, this workshop challenges the need to cultivate professional skepticism through foresight-infused experiential learning. We reveal six predictable failure modes when skepticism is absent - from parroting client narratives to deferring uncritically to authority - and show how integrating foresight practices disrupts these patterns. Techniques such as tension mapping, causal layered analysis, and scenario planning force students to examine opposing perspectives, surface unstated assumptions, and identify which futures are systematically excluded. The session features an interactive case with guided discussion from the presenters who possess decades of management consulting experiences as pracademics, attendees will gain insights into embedding professional skepticism and foresight into consulting pedagogy. Our goal is to move consulting education beyond teaching students what to think toward teaching how to think critically, ethically, future-oriented, and with independent, professional judgment.
