Session overview

The purpose of this symposium is to discuss the growing prominence of organizational purpose and stakeholder theory and asks whether these ideas have truly moved from the margins to the mainstream of management thought and practice. Building on stakeholder theory’s foundational notion that firms exist to create value for multiple stakeholders, this symposium will explore different perspectives from strategy, corporate governance, and economics. Panelists address unresolved questions about the stakeholder theory and corporate purpose, its evolution over time, and its implications for value creation and performance. Contributions examine how organizational capabilities and resource-based theory intersect with stakeholder theory, how stakeholder governance can address collective action problems, and how economic debates continue to challenge stakeholder-oriented conceptions of the firm. Drawing on empirical evidence, governance models, and real-world corporate practices, the symposium highlights tensions between shareholder primacy and stakeholder value creation. By bringing together leading scholars, the panel aims to advance theoretical integration, clarify conceptual ambiguities, and identify promising directions for future research on corporate purpose and the relationship between business and society.
