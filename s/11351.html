Reconstructing Agency and Communion in Human–AI Collaboration: A Triadic Perspective

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

People

Authors: Mengyang Jia Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Ningyu Tang Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes embedded in work, human–AI collaboration reshapes employees’ psychological and social experiences. Moving beyond dyadic perspectives that treat AI as a perceived social target, our study introduces a triadic social-cognition framework that considers the self, coworkers, and AI as interrelated actors to investigate a deeper question: How does collaborating with AI reconstruct employees’ own lived experiences of agency and communion? Through in-depth interviews with 25 employees across diverse roles, we identify three core mechanisms: agentic offloading (reframing self-perception, advancing work progress, and reorienting learning), relational reorganization (redirecting communication and reinterpreting social ties), and AI sensemaking (redefining AI’s role, evaluating AI’s capabilities and boundaries, and managing dependence and affective experience). Together, we contribute a triadic social-cognition framework that conceptualizes AI as a socially consequential third party in workplace, and fundamentally reframe agency and communion as dynamic, human-centered outcomes of collaboration, rather than static attributes of AI, thereby providing a more integrated, human-centric understanding of human-AI collaboration at work.
