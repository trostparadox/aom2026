Session overview

During last year's workshop sessions, we studied the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in teaching and changes in the teaching process. Now, one year later, discussions about business education and the application of AI as a teaching tool are still relevant, especially given the pace of changes in business influenced by the adoption of AI technologies. Although the speed of AI adoption in education has increased, recent studies and feedback reveal some negative consequences of AI use, including increased cognitive load, decreased intrinsic motivation, and decreased confidence in the quality of one’s own work (Robinson, 2024; Liu et al., 2025). The phenomenon described by practitioners as 'workslop' describes the outcome of AI processes that appear, in some respects, to be the product of real, critical thinking and analysis, but are, in fact, devoid of such thinking and analysis. This has raised serious concerns for both consultants and managers (Niederhoffer et al., 2025). This workshop aims to develop the ability to scrutinize AI-generated outputs and differentiate the good from the bad, focusing on professional judgment, quality assessment, and ethics. Participants will engage in activities that allow them to create teaching strategies for their discipline that integrate the realities of consulting in an AI-enabled world.
