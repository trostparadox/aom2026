Session overview

As work environments become more dynamic and uncertain, individuals increasingly engage in job and role changes, whether proactively or reactively, as a means of sustaining employability and advancing their careers. Rather than unfolding as seamless “boundaryless” moves, job mobility is often constrained, competitive, and fraught with adjustment challenges. Access to desirable mobility opportunities is often limited both inside and outside organizations, even for highly capable individuals. Moreover, securing a move does not guarantee success. Early newcomer turnover remains a persistent organizational challenge. Even those who move internally can experience challenges and failures. Therefore, job mobility is much more than a move, rather a complex and often rocky process involving significant physical, psychological, behavioral, and relational adjustment. In response to these challenges, research on job mobility has increasingly shifted from asking whether individuals move to examining how they experience and navigate work role transitions. The goal of this symposium is to contribute to this dialogue by bringing together four papers that examine critical, yet distinct, obstacles to succeeding across different forms of job mobility: from newcomer entry to lateral and upward internal movement. Drawing on diverse theoretical perspectives and methodological approaches, these papers illuminate the obstacles, the mechanisms, and practices that shape success or failure in work role transitions. The symposium will conclude with insights from our distinguished discussant, JR Keller, whose expertise in internal labor markets and job mobility will help synthesize these contributions and stimulate engaging discussion with the audience.
