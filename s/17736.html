The Timing of Ties: The Imprinting Effect of Historical Context on Interfirm Relationships

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4
OMT

People

Authors: Yi Ren Montclair State University; Huy Nguyen Montclair State University; Young Hoon (Gabriel) Jung Montclair State University; Zhu Zhu Montclair State University

Session overview

Why do firms facing similar contemporary conditions exhibit different levels of internationalization? Building on imprinting theory, we develop a relational imprinting explanation in which institutional logics at the moment an interfirm dyadic tie is formed become embedded as durable relational norms that persist even after the environment changes. We test this argument in the historical context of South Korea which had went through three distinct eras. Consistent with our theorizing, we find that banks whose initial chaebol relationships were formed under the state-logic era maintain a broader foreign presence than banks whose initial ties were formed under either community or market logics. We further theorize how factors such as CEO orientation and state ownership condition the strength of relational imprinting. Our study advances imprinting research and also the embeddedness literature by applying a longitudinal lens to examine initial ties’ enduring effects in binding firms’ strategic actions.
