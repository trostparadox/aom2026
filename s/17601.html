Star Performance in Nonprofits: Heavy-Tailed Distributions and Institutional-Entrepreneurial Drivers

Authors: Weiyu Dai Iowa State University; Andreas Schwab Iowa State University; Kaushik Gala University of Kansas

Session overview

Recent empirical entrepreneurship research suggests that performance distributions are frequently heavy-tailed, indicating that a few “star entrepreneurs” drive performance. The related empirical research, however, has focused nearly exclusively on for-profit ventures and largely ignored non-profit ventures. However, performance is relevant for both types of organizations, despite the mission-driven orientation of non-profit organizations, who face greater constraints on the use of their generated revenues. Consequently, this study applies methodological advances in analyzing star performers to nonprofit organizations. We focus on six U.S. healthcare sectors to explore the prevalence and impact of star performance in non-profit settings. The empirical evidence shows that nonprofit revenues are systematically heavy-tailed, with a small number of organizations capturing disproportionate shares of sector revenues. This distributional structure calls for going beyond traditional performance research that assumes normal distributions and focuses exclusively on average performance improvements. Instead, we argue for a more explicit research focus on star performers, i.e., the tails of performance distributions. We also demonstrate how entrepreneurial and institutional perspectives may plausibly explain star performance. The reported findings extend emerging star performance research to non-profit ventures.
