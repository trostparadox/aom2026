Delaying the Cut: Successor Limbo in Family Business Succession

Authors: Marina Vujovic University of Montenegro; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro

Session overview

Family business succession research has emphasized succession intentions and planning, yet in many family firms the pivotal “cut”, a binding decision that allocates leadership and/or ownership, remains persistently deferred. We examine how such deferred cuts are produced and sustained and how they shape successors’ trajectories. Anchored in the nondecision perspective (Bachrach & Baratz, 1963) and strategic ambiguity (Eisenberg, 1984), and complemented by socioemotional wealth (SEW) motives for control and harmony and temporal orientations in succession (Gómez-Mejía et al., 2007; Magrelli et al., 2022), we develop the concept of successor limbo. Successor limbo is a prolonged liminal state in which a next-generation member performs de facto successor work and carries high responsibility, yet lacks de jure authorization, recognition, and/or ownership due to deferred succession decisions (Beech, 2011). Based on 15 semi-structured interviews with second-generation daughters in Montenegrin family firms (1,065 minutes, ~110,000 words), an informative case for gendered inclusion (Constantinidis et al., 2022), our abductive analysis shows that incumbents’ nondecisions are sustained through recurring ambiguity scripts (“later,” “we’ll see,” “let me think”) that preserve short-term harmony and discretion while generating persistent role ambiguity for successors. Over time, successor limbo was described as intensifying conflict, exhaustion, and disengagement or exit, while shifting invisible operational and relational work onto daughters. We theorize delayed succession as power through nondecision, articulate successor limbo as a distinct process outcome of postponed cuts, and show how SEW-protective deferral can accumulate hidden costs that undermine continuity.
