Session overview

Digitalization and new technologies are transforming not only where organizing happens but also how spaces, places, and organizing are enacted through human, material, and digital elements. Rather than treating the digital and the physical as separate domains, contemporary organizing unfolds simultaneously – part digital, part embodied; part local, part remote – requiring us to theorize the affordances and frictions of each and the deliberate choices organizations make to combine them. Even though there is a lot of work on the role that digital infrastructures have on organizing, and there is no shortage of research that features rich descriptions of space and place throughout the organizational literature, to date, there remains a need for more in-depth, theory-led empirical investigations into the interactions between spatial and digital domains and their consequences. This PDW aims to explore the organization of space and place in the wake of digitalization, and, generally, inspire future research on the role of space and place in a digitalized world. This PDW will include a discussion and comparison of topics (what is being studied in terms of space and place in the wake of digitalization), spatial theories (which conceptual tools do researchers draw on in studying space, including a distinction between space and place), empirical sites, and methodologies (which methods do researchers draw on in studying space in a digitalized world). After the panelists’ presentations, this PDW will allow participants to discuss their research with experts on the topic.
