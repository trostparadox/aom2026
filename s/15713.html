From ESG Ambition to Project Reality: Identifying Capability Gaps Using IPA

Authors: Bojana Savic Faculty of Technical Sciences; Danijela Ciric Lalic Faculty of Technical Sciences

Session overview

Projects increasingly serve as the primary organizational arenas through which environmental, social, and governance (ESG) strategies are implemented. While ESG principles are widely recognized as important, organizations often struggle to translate these expectations into consistent project-level practices. This study examines whether and where gaps exist between the perceived importance and actual performance of ESG-related project management competencies. Using survey data from over 700 project professionals across multiple industries and organizational contexts, the study applies Importance– Performance Analysis (IPA) to assess environmental, social, and governance competencies. The findings reveal systematic and statistically significant gaps across all ESG dimensions. Governance-related competencies exhibit the largest discrepancies, followed by environmental and social competencies. IPA mapping identifies a set of ESG competencies located in the “concentrate here” quadrant, indicating priority capability gaps that may hinder effective sustainability-oriented project delivery. By reframing ESG implementation challenges as capability gaps rather than compliance failures, the study provides a diagnostic perspective with direct relevance for management consulting. The results support targeted capability development, project governance interventions, and advisory services aimed at strengthening organizations’ capacity to operationalize ESG strategies through projects.
