Session overview

The rapid diffusion of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in organizations raises a foundational question for management scholarship: Does GenAI primarily enhance human development, or does it inadvertently constrain it? This symposium advances a developmental perspective by conceptualizing GenAI as either a “developmental scaffold” that supports human judgment, learning, emotion regulation, and adaptation at work, or a “cognitive crutch” that biases or constrains human functioning through mechanisms such as overreliance or cognitive offloading. Across five complementary papers, the symposium examines GenAI’s paradoxical developmental consequences across four core domains of organizational life, including (1) managerial judgment and comprehension, (2) skill acquisition and learning, (3) emotion regulation in interpersonally demanding work, and (4) adaptive responses to technological threat—while also revealing how cultural tendencies embedded in GenAI shape these developmental processes. Using diverse AI-enabled methods, the studies identify psychological mechanisms and boundary conditions—such as user experience, individual differences, task structure, temporal framing, and linguistic–cultural context—that determine when GenAI amplifies versus hinders human capability. By integrating insights from organizational behavior, managerial and organizational cognition, cultural psychology, and learning theory, this symposium reframes GenAI not simply as a productivity tool, but as a developmental technology whose design and use can either cultivate or constrain human potential. In doing so, the symposium offers a unifying framework for understanding GenAI’s paradoxical developmental effects at work and provides actionable guidance for organizations seeking to harness AI to promote sustainable human development rather than cognitive dependency.
