Unpacking the Gender Gap in AI Use: Exploring Drivers and Performance Impacts

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

People

Authors: Emma Graupner Rosenheim Technical University of Applied Sciences; Carolin Fleischmann Rosenheim Technical University of Applied Sciences; Peter Wilson Cardon University of Southern California

Session overview

Despite the productivity benefits of AI, gender disparities in use risk deepening inequalities. Drawing on gender theory, gender is conceptualized as a social system shaping technology interaction rather than innate ability. Using data from 713 business students across 11 countries (7-week consulting project), this study measures UTAUT predictors, AI use frequency, and objectively assessed performance outcomes. Path differences emerged despite nonsignificant overall moderation: Performance expectancy predicts AI use for all genders but is significantly stronger for those identifying as male. Effort expectancy marginally influences use of those identifying as female, while social influence discourages use, suggesting contextual norms may deter adoption. Importantly, increased AI use enhances performance for those identifying as male only, indicating unequal benefits from use. These findings underscore that equitable access alone does not ensure equitable outcomes. Interventions fostering AI confidence, literacy, and supportive social environments are essential to narrow the gender gap in both adoption and value realization. The study highlights gender as a factor in AI adoption and performance impact.
