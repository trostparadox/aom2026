Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop introduces participants to the New World Game, an AI-enabled, collaborative learning platform designed to support hands-on engagement with complex social and environmental problems articulated through the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. The workshop focuses on how instructors can use the platform to design short, in-class exercises tailored to the courses they teach, rather than adopting pre-scripted simulations or generic AI activities. Rather than treating AI or simulation tools as value-neutral or as substitutes for human judgment, the workshop emphasizes the role of instructional framing, facilitation, and reflection in shaping learning outcomes. Participants are introduced to the core features of the New World Game through a live demonstration on the platform. Faculty then work individually or in small groups to design a short, course-specific, in-class exercise that can be implemented immediately in their own courses. The session demonstrates how the platform can surface system-level interdependencies, trade-offs, unintended consequences, power dynamics, and who benefits, who bears costs, and who is affected by different strategic choices. The workshop highlights how the New World Game can align with course learning outcomes in classes that ask students to identify complex problems, analyze root causes, consider stakeholder implications, and propose viable solutions. By the end of the workshop, participants will leave with a concrete in-class exercise and a clearer understanding of how AI-enabled platforms can support systems thinking as a disciplined approach to analysis and responsible decision-making in the classroom.
