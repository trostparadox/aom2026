Is God in the Classroom? Spirituality's Role in Entrepreneurship Education

Authors: Antonieta Alcorta Rosales University of Vechta; Jantje Halberstadt University of Vechta; Jana-Michaela Timm NBS Northern Business School - University of Applied Sciences

Session overview

Grand societal challenges require forms of entrepreneurship oriented toward systemic change rather than narrow profit maximization. Building on prior work conceptualizing a form of entrepreneurship that aims for societal transformation, we focus on one understudied attribute highlighted in that work: “spiritual connectedness”, both relevant and underexplored. Spirituality is controversial in higher education, often conflated with religion and viewed as non-scientific or exclusionary. Furthermore, it is often treated as private, religious, or out of place in business education, yet it may shape how entrepreneurs develop purpose, resilience, and ethical commitment—particularly when they seek to create impact and transformation based on sustainability values. This poster examines how entrepreneurship educators interpret and handle spirituality in the classroom. Using an exploratory qualitative design, we analyze 25+ semi-structured interviews with entrepreneurship educators across Argentina, Germany, and Thailand. We investigate educators’ interpretations of spiritual connectedness in entrepreneurship education, its perceived implications for pedagogy, and its association with educators’ role orientations (Athena– Prometheus–Asclepius). Preliminary patterns suggest a spectrum from explicit integration to deliberate exclusion, with many educators describing “spirituality-adjacent” approaches under alternative labels (e.g., reflection, mindfulness, values, purpose). The study contributes an empirically grounded map of positions and practices that can inform entrepreneurship education aimed at transformation and flourishing.
