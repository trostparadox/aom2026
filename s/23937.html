Pioneer-Follower Innovation Dynamics: Bayesian Learning on Signaling, Spillovers, and Competition

TIM
Lijun Wang (Capital University of Economics and Business); Qiang Liu; Shengxia Xu (Capital University of Economics and Business); Jiarong Zhou (Capital University of Economics and Business)
In the AI era, the business environment is increasingly hypercompetitive, making firms’ innovation more dependent on competitors’ reactions. We develop a Bayesian learning model to examine how small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) respond to innovation by pioneers and how their innovation contributes to industry-level value-chain upgrading—an underexplored topic. Using a panel of 1,607 innovative SMEs in China (2013–2023), we show that follower innovation is affected by pioneer innovation disclosure through two positive channels: a signaling effect that raises consumers’ quality expectations and a spillover effect that reduces followers’ innovation costs and uncertainty. These mechanisms offset the competitive effect that would otherwise deter follower innovation. The effects vary with the industry information environment, firms’ innovation bases, and financing conditions. More importantly, while follower innovation boosts short-term firm performance, it also supports long-term value-chain upgrading at the industry level.
