Algorithmic Organizations: Reimagining Scope and Structure in an AI World

MC
RAJ MOHAN (Indian Institute of Management Indore)
This paper explores a novel conceptualization of contemporary organizations through the metaphor "organizations as algorithms”. As such, it examines how enterprises are evolving into dynamic, self-optimizing entities that continuously learn, adapt, and reconfigure based on data-driven insights thus impacting their scope and structure. As artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly permeates organizational boundaries, fundamentally it alters the traditional sources of competitive advantage, necessitating the development of new managerial capabilities. This study thus investigates how digitalization enables unprecedented forms of value creation that transcend conventional organizational boundaries while simultaneously presenting novel challenges for scope optimization. The paper contributes to the organizational theory literature by examining the transformation of organizational scope decisions in the digital context and analyses how technological advancements reshape organizational architecture and design paradigms. Finally, the work advances both theoretical understanding and practical application of organizational design principles in the digital age, offering valuable insights for scholars and practitioners.

How AI is Redefining Consultants Work: A Task-Based Perspective on Resource Orchestration

MC
Christian Johannes Pfaff; Jeremy Zwiegelaar (Oxford Brookes University)
This study examines how the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping task execution, resource allocation, and efficiency gains in neo–Professional Service Firms (neo-PSFs), with a particular focus on management consulting. We develop a novel framework for classifying consulting work that builds on the taxonomy of professional tasks proposed by Shao et al. (2025) and integrates literature specific to the consulting context. The resulting framework offers a comprehensive categorization of consulting task types and contributes to a more nuanced understanding of how work is organized and managed in this professional domain. Drawing on Resource Orchestration Theory (ROT), the study investigates how AI influences the structuring, bundling, and leveraging of human and technological resources within neo-PSFs. Empirically, the analysis is based on semi-structured expert interviews conducted across multiple career stages and organizational contexts within AI-enabled consulting firms. By comparing conventional and AI-assisted modes of task execution, the study assesses time savings, efficiency gains, and implications for prevailing staffing and turnover models. The findings extend existing ROT-based perspectives by incorporating AI-assisted task reallocation as a central orchestration mechanism. In doing so, the study advances theoretical understanding of how AI transforms professional work while also offering actionable insights for balancing technological integration with the preservation of professional identity and standards in neo-PSFs.

Perception Before Adoption : Artificial Intelligence Through Employees’ Eyes

MC
Manvi Kalra (International Management Institute)
This study aims to redirect adoption of artificial intelligence in organizations from just a discrete decision to an appraisal-based, perception-centred process whose interpretation leads to the behavioral intention of adoption, it also aims to conceptualize employees perception of AI at work. Drawing from literature on artificial intelligence, employee’s perspective of artificial intelligence and adoption of artificial intelligence, this paper formulates propositions and suggests a model for employees perception of AI in organizations. For a coherent theoretical explanation, the integration of Artificial Intelligence Device Use and Acceptance framework along with Cognitive Appraisal Theory is used as it explains how employees perception emerges, emotions get involved and shapes employees willingness to use the AI-enabled systems. This paper argues that adoption is not merely a technological response or an organizational mandate but how employees perceive AI at work, employee responses to AI-enabled systems emerge not only from technological features, but from cognitive appraisals and their ability to cope with the systems. The paper offers a foundation for future empirical studies and a novel theoretical contribution by linking cognitive and affective responses to employees perception of AI. This opens a new perspective on willingness to adopt AI, moving beyond technical features and resistance to a better understanding of employee-AI interaction in usage of AI-enabled systems in organizations. Artificial intelligence is not adopted first, it is perceived by employees first and through perception the consequences for work unfold.

Axiological Displacement in Management: Research Value and Organizational Practice in the AI Era

MC
Caroline Gans Combe (INSEEC Grande École)
This paper introduces the concept of axiological displacement to examine how artificial intelligence fundamentally transforms what counts as valuable in management sciences research and organizational practice. Drawing on epistemological analysis and contemporary business evidence, I argue that AI systems do not merely optimize existing value frameworks but recursively reshape the criteria by which research quality, data validity, and actionable knowledge are evaluated. This transformation carries profound implications for companies deploying AI: organizations that fail to understand axiological displacement risk, incur making strategic decisions based on algorithmically distorted metrics, communicating through channels that impede knowledge retention, and losing the capacity to perceive their own organizational reality accurately. To address these challenges, I propose a dual framework integrating two complementary methodological traditions that, together, illuminate the full knowledge cycle organizations must govern. The logical connection between these traditions is as follows: organizational knowledge processes involve both an input dimension, concerning the quality of data upon which decisions depend, and an output dimension, concerning the effectiveness with which insights reach and influence decision-makers. Oskar Morgenstern's foundational work on the accuracy of economic data addresses the input dimension, identifying how measurement, reporting, aggregation, and conceptual errors contaminate the data that feed AI systems. His framework reveals that AI cannot transcend the limitations of its inputs; rather, it amplifies errors while obscuring their presence. Armand Morgensztern's beta coefficient for media memorization addresses the output dimension, quantifying how different communication channels vary dramatically in their capacity to create lasting cognitive impressions. His research demonstrates that even accurate insights may fail to influence behavior if communicated through low-memorization channels, a concern magnified as AI increasingly mediates organizational communication. Together, these frameworks reveal why AI investments so frequently fail to generate expected returns: companies attend to algorithmic sophistication while neglecting the data quality problems Morgenstern identified and the communication effectiveness challenges Morgensztern quantified. The paper concludes that organizations cannot capture value from AI without governing the complete knowledge cycle from data collection to decision implementation.

Innovativity as a Diagnostic Lens in Digital Transformation: A Qualitative Socio-Economic Analysis.

MC
Andres Davila (ESCE); Charlotte DAVILA VALDIVIEZO
This paper proposes a qualitative-dominant diagnostic framework to examine innovativity during digital transformation processes. Drawing on exploratory data collected from seven large organizations engaged in a corporate digital transformation training program, the study investigates how employees’ professional characteristics relate to discursive orientations toward innovativity. Inspired by qualimetric principles, the research adopts a diagnostic design grounded in qualitative interpretive analysis and identifies recurrent discursive patterns. Rather than explaining performance outcomes, the study aims to surface diagnostic signals that inform assessments of organizational readiness for digital transformation, while explicitly acknowledging its exploratory and pre-implementation scope.
