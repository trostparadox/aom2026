Session overview

Intelligent technologies and new ways of working are fundamentally reshaping how work is accessed, organized, evaluated, and experienced (Howcroft & Taylor, 2023; Whittaker et al., 2019). Artificial intelligence, algorithmic platforms, remote and hybrid work arrangements are redefining opportunity structures, evaluative criteria, and social relations at work, often in ways that remain underexamined in organizational research (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021; Duggan et al., 2020). For people with disabilities, these transformations are particularly consequential. On the one hand, emerging technologies and flexible work arrangements may alleviate long-standing barriers related to physical access, rigid temporal structures, and embodied norms of the “ideal worker” that have historically shaped who is seen as able to work and advance (Stone & Colella, 1996; Colella & Bruyère, 2011). On the other hand, technological mediated workplace may generate new forms of exclusion through algorithmic evaluation, skill requirements, and expectations of constant availability or self-optimization, reflecting how norms of ability and performance become embedded in technological systems. Inclusion in the future of work therefore hinges not only on access to technology, but on how organizational norms, evaluative logics, and social relations are designed and governed in technology-embedded work contexts (McAlpine et al., 2025). This panel symposium brings together scholars interested in organizational behavior, inclusion, and social issues in management; to examine how emerging technologies and new ways of working are reshaping work, social relations, and career for people with disabilities. By using disability as an analytical lens, the symposium invites participants to rethink core assumptions about work design, evaluation, and inclusion in the future of work, while identifying promising directions for organizational research on inclusive futures of work. Therefore, the purpose of this panel symposium is to create an agenda-setting scholarly conversation on how emerging technologies and new ways of working are reshaping work and organizing with and for people with disabilities. Rather than presenting finished empirical papers, the panel is designed to foster critical dialogue around unresolved questions, conceptual tensions, and promising directions for future management research.
