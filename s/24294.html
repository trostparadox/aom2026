Conceptualizing Innovation in the Data Economy Era: An Integrative Review on Data-Driven Innovation

TIM
Catherine Geneviève Morin (Laval University)
Data-driven innovation has gained attention as a new and improved innovation approach, with research across various disciplines examining how analytics convert data into valuable resources for innovation. This has led to the fragmentation and discipline-specificity of knowledge about data-driven innovation. Additionally, data-driven innovation remains confined to scientific discourse, and its successful implementation in firms is relatively uncommon, despite research that offers insights into some of its drivers. Therefore, this study aims to consolidate how DDI is conceptualized within existing academic literature. It adopts a knowledge-based perspective to explain how data are transformed into valuable knowledge assets that foster innovation. An integrative review methodology is employed, with the selection of 71 articles following a systematic literature review process. The findings highlight the characteristics of knowledge management activities that drive innovation through a data-driven approach, as well as the factors that influence them. By critically analyzing and synthesizing the literature, this paper introduces a new DDI framework that contributes to innovation research in the data economy and promotes its interdisciplinary nature. The framework depicts DDI as a data-driven process that remains dependent on both practical and experiential knowledge, emphasizing the role of data alongside human contributions and tacit knowledge. Accordingly, it provides an analysis of specific characteristics of DDI that distinguish it from a traditional innovation process. This work also contributes to the knowledge management literature by examining the essence of knowledge assets in the era of the data economy.
