Synthetic User Simulations: An AI Framework for Experiential Learning in Management

Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: Ester Pescio Hult International Business School; Andrea Ridi Hult International Business School

Session overview

Experiential learning is widely recognized as central to management education, particularly for developing skills such as negotiation, decision making, and stakeholder management. However, traditional experiential approaches, including role plays and live simulations, often face constraints related to scalability, consistency, and opportunities for repeated practice. This poster introduces a pedagogically grounded framework for synthetic user simulations: interactive, AI orchestrated learning environments in which students actively engage with multiple synthetic stakeholders in complex managerial scenarios. The framework is designed to support student learning outcomes and the development of managerial competencies by enabling structured, repeatable experiential practice. It specifies six interrelated design components that guide how synthetic stakeholders are defined, coordinated, and engaged, ensuring realism while keeping instructional goals central. Rather than automating case analysis, the simulations require students to articulate arguments, make decisions under uncertainty, respond to competing perspectives, and reflect on the consequences of their choices. Two illustrative applications demonstrate the framework’s versatility: a venture capital negotiation simulation used in entrepreneurship education and a B2B contract negotiation simulation adapted for strategy and operations courses. Planned evaluation examines engagement, reflective writing, and expert assessments, highlighting student centered experiential learning for management education.
