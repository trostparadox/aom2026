Splinternet, Brussels Effect, Sovereign Shield: The New Institutional Landscape for Digital Firms

IM
Maximilian Stallkamp (East Carolina University)
Recent research has emphasized that digital firms are shaped and constrained by institutions, yet this literature remains largely focused on host-country environments. This paper broadens that perspective by theorizing three institutional forces that structure the digital economy. The first is national fragmentation, often described as the Splinternet, which reflects governments’ efforts to assert regulatory control over digital markets. The second is supranational diffusion, captured by the Brussels Effect, a concept drawn from law and political science that explains how powerful jurisdictions extend their regulatory standards globally. The third is the Sovereign Shield, a new mechanism introduced in this paper that describes how home-country governments project authority outward to protect domestic digital firms from foreign regulation. We extend the CSA–FSA framework to explain how these forces reconfigure the balance between location-bound and transferable firm-specific advantages and develop a typology of strategic responses that highlights firms’ capacity to adapt, harmonize, or contest institutional pressures across markets. This framework advances international business theory by integrating host, supranational, and home-country institutions to explain how digital multinationals compete in a fragmented and politicized global environment.
