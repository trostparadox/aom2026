Inner Form, Outer Form: AI, Meaning, and the Rise of the Post-Intentional Organization

OMT
Othmar Manfred Lehner (Hanken School of Economics)
This paper argues that AI-mediated organizing, especially through large language models and adaptive learning systems, produces linguistic coherence without the intentional acts of meaning that once grounded organizational understanding. Drawing on Gustav Shpet’s collectivist phenomenology, the analysis shows how AI systems generate the outer form of meaning, well-formed expressions and classifications, while the inner form, the lived and collectively sustained act that binds expression to a shared world, recedes. The resulting configuration, the post-intentional organization, remains operationally coherent even as its justificatory, intersubjective, and ethical foundations thin. To diagnose this shift, the paper develops five phenomenological lenses, intentionality, rationality, intersubjectivity, form, and ethics/epistemics, that trace how AI infrastructures replace constitutive acts of sense with statistical correlations and procedural adequacy. The argument then turns to governance and introduces an intervention grammar, encapsulation, category formation and visibility regimes, and recognition-repair, that identifies where intentionality can re-enter hybrid human–AI settings. In reframing AI not as a technological tool but as an ontological displacement of collective sense, the paper offers organization theory a way to study meaning once part of its formation has migrated into code. Keywords: collective consciousness; AI and organization; phenomenology; sensemaking; algorithmic intersubjectivity; post-intentional organization.
