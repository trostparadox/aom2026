The Curvilinear Effects of Team Friendship Network Density on Newcomer Inclusion and Socialization

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Authors: Yue Pan The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Jingfeng Yin The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Betty Zhou Southern Methodist University; Wu Liu The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Rui Guo

Session overview

In an era of increasing project-based team, newcomers need to navigate frequent transitions into new team environments. While research emphasizes newcomers’ proactive relationship building, we extend this agentic view by highlighting a structural paradox: the dense connections within may also hinder newcomers’ relationship development. Integrating sociocentric and egocentric network perspectives, we theorize that team friendship network density has an inverted U-shaped relationship with newcomers’ friendship ties with incumbents. We further propose that newcomer friendship ties enable newcomer inclusion, which in turn relates to higher innovative performance and lower turnover. Multi-wave, multi-source data from 75 project teams in a railway construction company in China largely support our model. Results showed a curvilinear effect of team friendship network density on newcomer centrality, and that newcomer’s friendship ties mediated the indirect effects of density on newcomer inclusion and innovative performance. This study advances the network-based perspective on socialization, revealing how team network structures can either facilitate or constrain newcomer inclusion, with implications for managing socialization and supporting newcomer adjustment in dynamic teams.
