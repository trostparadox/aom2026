Session overview

Thirty years after the landmark 1995 AOM symposium that introduced jazz improvisation as a metaphor for organizing—an event later memorialized in a special issue of Organization Science—scholars’ interest in the relationship between music and management has grown substantially. Yet music’s role in organization studies remains unevenly understood and, at times, undervalued. Building on renewed theoretical and empirical momentum, this panel brings together leading scholars who integrate music into their research—as context, metaphor, theory, or methodological inspiration—to explore how music can both inform and be informed by management and organization theory. We develop a “give and take” framework to clarify the two principal avenues through which music enriches organizational scholarship. First, music-as-a-context leverages the unique features of musical organizations and creative industries to surface puzzles, boundary conditions, and insights that extend existing theories. Second, music-as-a-theory harnesses concepts from music theory—such as harmony, rhythm, timing, dissonance, and improvisation—to recalibrate foundational assumptions in strategy, organizational cognition, identity, and stakeholder theory. Despite emerging examples in recent AMJ, ASQ, and AMR publications, this second avenue remains significantly underdeveloped. Our symposium convenes an interdisciplinary panel—including scholars of strategy, cognition, creativity, institutional theory, and stakeholder theory, several of whom are professional musicians—to examine how these two avenues can stimulate new theoretical advances. Through a blend of performance, reflection, and scholarly dialogue, we aim to deepen the Academy’s engagement with music as both an empirical setting and a theoretical lens. This symposium expands the long-standing conversation between music and management, offering fresh directions for OMT, STR, and MOC scholars interested in creativity, temporality, meaning-making, coordination, and anticipatory decision-making.
