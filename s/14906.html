Session overview

Operations and supply chain management (OSCM) scholars regularly confront persistent, interdependent tensions, including efficiency–flexibility, lean– innovation, stability–change, automation–augmentation, and resilience–sustainability. While paradox theory has been widely applied in general management literature, its adoption in OSCM offers unique opportunities to both apply and extend the theory. Importantly, OSCM contexts raise critical questions for paradox theory: tensions considered paradoxical at the organizational level may not appear paradoxical at the operational or supply chain level, and vice versa. Moreover, OSCM research emphasizes processes, temporal unfolding, and system-level interdependencies across networks of actors—features less central in much of the existing paradox literature. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) will discuss paradox theory in OSCM context, exploring which operational and supply chain tensions constitute true paradoxes, and examine how OSCM perspectives can refine and extend the theory. Participants will engage in an integrated panel discussion followed by structured roundtable discussions focused on specific OSCM paradoxes. The PDW aims to stimulate cross-disciplinary dialogue, generate research questions, theoretical insights, and potential collaboration opportunities, while providing guidance on incorporating paradox theory into OSCM research.
