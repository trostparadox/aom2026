How Transparency Shapes Digital Government Responsiveness? A Perspective of Neo-Institutional Theory

SIM
Qihui Fan (Tianjin University); Xiaofei Zhang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Feng Guo (Tianjin University)
Government responsiveness is a central manifestation of external legitimacy in digital governance, yet how formal and informal institutional pressures interact to shape such responsiveness remains insufficiently understood. Drawing on neo-institutional theory, this study conceptualizes responsiveness as a legitimacy-oriented construct shaped by the interplay of coercive transparency policy and multifaceted informal institutional pressures. Using 3,256 city-year observations from China’s largest nationwide forum, the study shows that government transparency significantly increases both the quantity and quality of responses. Different layers of informal pressures exert distinct influences: public normative pressure (vertical pressure) amplifies these effects, whereas peer mimetic pressure (horizontal pressure) strengthens only transparency’s impact on response quality. These findings underscore that institutional pressures shape digital government responsiveness within public administration in unequal ways, with coercive transparency policies serving as the primary driver of responsiveness prioritization, while informal pressures exert a more contingent and variable influence.
