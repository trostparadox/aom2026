Can Accelerators Level the Playing Field for Female Social Entrepreneurs?

ENT
Juelin Yin (Sun Yat-Sen University); Xinyu Yang (Sun Yat-Sen University)
Social impact accelerators (SIAs) aim to assist social startups in overcoming fundraising challenges while generating positive social impact alongside financial returns. However, little is known about whether SIAs address gender inequality in fundraising within social entrepreneurship. Drawing on benevolent sexism and signaling theories, we theorize that SIAs may inadvertently exacerbate the gender funding gap. Using a global sample of 5,540 social startups accepted into SIA programs, we find that female-founded firms receive significantly less equity financing than male-founded counterparts. This gap narrows when women are the primary beneficiaries of the venture’s social mission, but widens for startups with prior equity financing experience and those in seed-stage accelerators. This study highlights the distinct dynamics shaping investors’ gender bias in social entrepreneurship, offering new insights into the relationship between SIA participation and equity funding outcomes. It challenges assumptions about SIAs’ inclusivity and offers actionable guidance for designing and operating SIAs to better support female social entrepreneurs.
