Session overview

This session examines how women navigate male-dominated industries and occupations, including technology, logistics, entrepreneurship, and finance. The papers reveal how organizational norms, embodied expectations, silence, and informal barriers shape women's experiences and advancement in settings where masculinity remains the default. The session offers practical insight into the challenges of creating more equitable cultures in sectors marked by strong gendered assumptions.

Papers (5)

Burned Out and Boxed In: Gendered Constraints on Voice and Exit in China’s Tech Industry

DEI
Duanyi Yang (Cornell University); Tingting Zhang (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign)
We examine how gender shapes workers’ responses to overwork in China’s tech sector. Drawing on 69 interviews, we show voice is rare for both men and women, but they use distinct mechanisms to remain silent. Men rely on internalized moral silence and status-protective defensive silence to avoid signaling weakness. Women use internalized relational silence and reputation-protective defensive silence to avoid backlash. Exit is also gendered: men are “boxed in” by breadwinner expectations, while women more often exit or downshift. The paper extends the exit-voice-loyalty model by theorizing how gender roles and workplace gendered cultures shape silence and response options.

Equality at Work: Organisational Truths, Leadership, and Women in Logistics

DEI
Wiktoria Alicja Westen (University of Porto)
Despite increasing organisational commitment to diversity and inclusion, gender inequality in leadership persists in logistics. Drawing on feminist poststructuralism and Foucault’s notion of regimes of truth, this article treats equality not as an outcome to be measured but as a discursive accomplishment produced through authorised narratives, institutional validation, and everyday organisational practice. Empirically, the analysis draws on qualitative material comprising professional social media posts, publicly available interviews, and semi-structured interviews with women working in the logistics sector. The findings reveal a disjuncture between highly visible organisational narratives of progress and the persistence of informal power relations that continue to shape women’s leadership experiences. While women leaders are frequently positioned as symbols of inclusion and organisational success, their authority remains contingent, relational tensions are rendered unsayable, and responsibility for navigating inequality is individualised. Under conditions of symbolic inclusion without structural security, heightened legitimacy pressures and queen bee dynamics become intelligible rather than exceptional. The article contributes to feminist scholarship on leadership by shifting attention from the presence of equality discourse to the conditions under which organisational truths about inclusion are produced, circulated, and sustained.

Beyond Technical Skill: Leadership Strategies of Women Entrepreneurs in Technology

DEI
Monica Amadio (Stockton University)
Leadership strategies employed by high-achieving women entrepreneurs operating in technology-related sectors were analyzed, revealing a novel framework. Drawing on leadership and entrepreneurship literature and connecting to theoretical frameworks, this study presents qualitative evidence from a purposive sample of semi-structured interviews with six cisgender women entrepreneurs in highly male-dominated competitive markets. These strategies are examined as adaptive responses to gendered structural constraints, including exclusion from capital, legitimacy, and authority in male-dominated technology markets. Leadership traits, organizational practices, and strategic approaches to team development and culture were analyzed. Findings illuminate how these entrepreneurs construct mentorship networks, implement selective hiring practices emphasizing technical expertise and diversity, and use bricolage to transfer innovations across industries. This research contributes to scholarship on gendered leadership by articulating the strategic mechanisms underlying organizational success in technology-driven contexts and by identifying new pathways for future research on structural and strategic supports for women entrepreneurs.

Role of Bodies in Women Leadership

DEI
Nyla Aleem Ansari (Institute of Business Administration)
There is a growing body of research that argues that gender not only bifurcates male and female leadership styles but also discriminates against bodies of women and men. I present a qualitative study, using Gioia method (2013) to analyze how women leaders choose their attire for a strategic expression of their credibility and confidence to advance in a male dominated work environment. Based on interviews with 20 Pakistani women leaders in financial institutions, I propose three typologies of dress and display by senior women theorized as body strategies to achieve their professional independence. Findings suggest that women leaders’ choices of their attire become a battleground between religious modesty, culture and self-expression (Bartky 1988). Key words: Bodies, gender, women leaders, workplace, attire.
