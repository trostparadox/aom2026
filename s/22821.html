Board Capital, Government Subsidies, Negative Media Coverage: Governance Insights on Greenwashing

SIM
Yongjun Mo (Tongji University); Junaid Aftab (Tongji University)
Corporate sustainability has gained global prominence as firms increasingly integrate environmental, social, and governance (ESG) principles into strategic decision-making. Yet, the persistence of ESG greenwashing, where companies exaggerate or misrepresent their sustainability performance, poses significant challenges for investors, regulators, and stakeholders. While prior research has largely emphasized external drivers of this phenomenon, such as stakeholder pressure and regulatory oversight, less attention has been paid to the internal governance mechanisms that shape corporate ESG credibility. Grounded in agency theory, this study highlights the crucial monitoring role of boards of directors, particularly how their human and social capital influence the authenticity of ESG practices. Furthermore, it considers the broader institutional context by examining how government incentives and media attention interact with board governance to shape sustainability outcomes. By integrating insights from corporate governance, sustainability reporting, and institutional theory, the study advances understanding of how internal oversight and external pressures jointly determine the integrity of ESG disclosure. The findings contribute to ongoing discussions on improving board effectiveness, refining sustainability governance, and promoting transparency in corporate ESG practices, issues of growing relevance in emerging markets amid heightened global scrutiny of greenwashing.

Similar Controversy, Similar ESG Strategies?

SIM
Linda Du (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics Zhejiang College); Zhe Li (Shanghai University of Finance and Economics); Jianfei Sun (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); Qigui Zhu
This study investigates the Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) of China’s A-share listed firms that operate in the industrial sector often viewed as divergent from social norms and thus are often termed as controversial by literature. We find that similar to their counterparts in the U.S., these controversial firms in China also have better ESG than their peer firms. While controversial firms in the U.S. predominantly excel in the social category, those firms in China score higher in both environmental and social categories. More interestingly, within the social category, controversial firms in the U.S. focus on the employee subcategory to attain better performance, but those firms in China exhibit higher scores in the product subcategory.

Selective CSI coverage: The Role of Prior CSI, Geopolitical Adversaries, and CSR History

SIM
Hyo Eun Sarina Son (Not Associated); He Soung Ahn (Myongji University)
As information about corporate social irresponsibility (CSI) is typically not voluntarily disclosed to the public, stakeholders must rely on information intermediaries such as the media to learn of corporate misconduct. This paper investigates the drives of selective CSI coverage, focusing on whether repeated CSI incidents and the involvement of geopolitical adversaries as key newsworthiness cues. In addition, firms' CSR history as the boundary conditions of CSI coverage is examined. Utilizing a sample of 446 CSI incidents related to data breaches involving 44 US publicly-listed firms between 2016 and 2021, we find empirical evidence that repeated CSI and geopolitical adversaries are newsworthiness cues that increase CSI coverage, while CSR history weakens the extent of media attention of geopolitical adversary-linked CSI.

Learning from 30 Years of Sustainability Research in Business Literature to Move Forward

SIM
Robert Sroufe (Chatham University); Nagaraj Sivasubramaniam (Duquesne University); Mark Geiger (Duquesne University)
This systematic review examines the evolution of definitions and theories of sustainability in the business management literature from 1996 to 2024. Drawing from a sample of 474 peer-reviewed empirical papers published in major journals, this study synthesizes the theoretical trajectories, methodological approaches, and evolution of the sustainability paradigm in business management research. The analysis highlights the dynamic conceptualizations of sustainability, shifts in theoretical foci, and the diversity of approaches used to investigate the constructs and paradigm.
