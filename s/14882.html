Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop is designed for a moment that feels different from earlier periods of whole-system work. Consultants and educators are now teaching in environments shaped by heightened complexity, polarization, and uncertainty, where confidence in expert-driven answers has diminished and no single role or organization can define the way forward. In this context, the challenge is not only how to lead or facilitate whole-system change, but how to teach others to do it. While whole-system approaches have a long history in organization development and consulting, much of that work has focused on methods, events, or the role of skilled facilitators. This workshop takes a different focus. It centers on Whole-System Development as a teaching challenge: how consultants and educators help people build the capacity to work across boundaries, engage difference productively, and take shared responsibility for improving their own systems over time. Participants engage in a structured learning experience built around a realistic, cross-boundary challenge. Through reflection and dialogue, they explore how design choices, who is involved, what questions are asked, how differences are surfaced, and how shared purpose is articulated, so they can shape what becomes possible. The workshop draws on experiential and adult learning traditions. This is not a Future Search training or a method-focused session. Whole-System Development is presented as a way of thinking about change that prioritizes learning, shared responsibility, and adaptability. Participants leave with clearer language for teaching whole-system change and practical ideas they can adapt for their own teaching and consulting contexts.
