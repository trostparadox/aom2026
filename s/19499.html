Entrepreneurial Mindsets and Health Innovation Intentions Among Asian Youth

Authors: Forhad Hossain Sophia University; Ashir Ahmed Kyushu University

Session overview

Entrepreneurship supports sustainable development by encouraging innovation, creating jobs, and advancing social progress, especially in emerging economies. With the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in mind, youth-led healthcare entrepreneurship can address ongoing problems with access, fairness, and service delivery. Asia accounts for over 60% of the world’s youth and is a key region for studying entrepreneurial intentions in health innovation. This study examines the mindsets, abilities, and challenges faced by Asian youth involved in or interested in healthcare innovation. We used a mixed-method approach and collected data from an online survey of 455 people ages 18 to 30 in 7 Asian countries. We measured entrepreneurial attitudes with a five-item Entrepreneurial Mindset Index (EMI; a = 0.88) and included open-ended questions about barriers, support needs, and preferred areas for innovation. The results show a strong overall entrepreneurial orientation (M=4.34/5), especially in interest in solving healthcare problems and in the belief that young people can improve health systems. However, social and institutional support was weaker. Principal component analysis found two main factors: innovation drive and social orientation. Cluster analysis identified three groups of youth with different levels of readiness for entrepreneurship. Qualitative results point to funding shortage, limited mentorship, and weak infrastructure as the main barriers, with strong demand for networking, training, and ecosystem support. These findings add to research on entrepreneurship by showing the untapped potential of Asian youth in health innovation and offer guidance for policies to build a more inclusive and sustainable entrepreneurial ecosystem.
