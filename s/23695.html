Exploring the Impact of Authoritarian Leadership on Career Outcomes

CAR
zizheng LI (Nottingham University Business School China); Eric Adom Asante (University of Nottingham Ningbo China)
Drawing on the social cognitive career theory, this study explored the impact of authoritarian leadership on employees’ career outcomes. Specifically, we propose that authoritarian leadership will enhance employees’ interaction avoidance with their leaders. This we argue will in turn decrease their proactive career behaviors and increase their job insecurity. We further suggest that these indirect effects are weaker when leader competence is higher. Based on a sample consisting of 304 employees nested into 62 supervisory teams, authoritarian leadership decreases subordinates’ motivation for proactive career behaviors and increases their perception of job insecurity via interaction avoidance. Furthermore, we found that leader competence moderates the positive relationship between authoritarian leadership and interaction avoidance as well as its indirect effect on proactive career behavior and job insecurity. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.
