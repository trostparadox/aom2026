Digital Nativeness, Academic Boredom, and Perceived Usefulness in Technology-Enhanced Learning

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

People

Authors: Yun-Sin Chuang National Yunlin University of Science and Technology; Shang-Ping Lin

Session overview

Understanding what influences students' expectations is crucial for successful implementation of technology-enhanced learning, which is becoming more and more integrated into higher education. Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the study examines how digital nativeness and academic boredom jointly influence perceived usefulness of technology-enhanced learning. Using survey data from 108 third-year university students across multiple disciplines, hierarchical regression analyses were conducted with perceived usefulness as the outcome variable, digital nativeness and academic boredom as focal predictors, and ICT competence as a control variable. Results indicate that both digital nativeness (ß = .34, p < .001) and academic boredom (ß = .28, p < .01) are positively associated with perceived usefulness, collectively explaining 42% of the variance. Moreover, digital nativeness moderates the relationship between academic boredom and perceived usefulness (ß = .19, p < .05), such that students high in digital nativeness who experience boredom report particularly strong perceptions of usefulness toward technology-enhanced learning. By integrating generational characteristics and affective states into TAM, this study extends technology acceptance research and offers empirically grounded implications for designing learning environments that are not only technologically accessible but also pedagogically engaging and emotionally responsive.
