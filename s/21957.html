Empathy by Algorithm: Fairness, Customer Satisfaction, and Loyalty in AI-Mediated Service Encounters

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Author: Luciana Simion Claremont Graduate University

Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly replaces or augments human frontline employees in customer service, organizations face a fundamental question: can algorithmic agents convey empathy in ways that preserve perceptions of fairness, customer satisfaction, and long-term loyalty, particularly in emotionally charged service encounters? This paper presents a multi-study experimental investigation of AI-expressed empathy and its downstream consequences for consumer evaluations in AI-mediated service delivery and recovery contexts. Grounded in organizational justice theory, fairness heuristic theory, relationship marketing, and the Technology Acceptance Model, the research develops and tests a moderated mediation framework in which perceived fairness and customer satisfaction function as key psychological mechanisms linking AI-expressed empathy to loyalty intentions. Across two vignette-based experimental studies, the paper examines how varying levels of AI-expressed empathy shape interactional and procedural fairness perceptions, and how these effects depend on critical boundary conditions, including service encounter type (delivery vs. recovery), service outcome valence (favorable, neutral, unfavorable), trust in AI, technology acceptance, and brand trust. The proposed model predicts that AI-expressed empathy enhances fairness perceptions, which in turn increase satisfaction and loyalty; however, these effects are attenuated in high-stakes service recovery contexts and among consumers with low trust in AI or low technology acceptance. By extending justice-based frameworks to algorithmic service agents, this research contributes to theory by clarifying when and for whom empathic AI can function as a relational signal rather than a purely instrumental interface. Practically, the findings offer guidance for organizations designing emotionally intelligent AI systems, allocating human versus automated service roles, and managing reputational risk in AI-enabled customer experiences.
