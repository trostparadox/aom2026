Session overview

As geopolitical rivalry intensifies and the world order undergoes structural transformation, firms operating in Latin America confront a strategic dilemma with real performance consequences: align with a major power to secure preferential market access, institutional anchors, and stakeholder endorsement or maintain strategic autonomy to preserve partnership optionality and insulation from bloc-driven frictions. This Oxford-style debate panel moves beyond country-level diplomacy to interrogate the firm-level mechanisms through which alignment decisions reshape competitive positioning. We theorize four pathways linking geopolitical posture to firm outcomes. First, capability upgrading: alignment channels knowledge search toward bloc-affiliated partners, potentially accelerating domain-specific learning while constraining the network diversity that sustains long-run upgrading. Second, alliance formation: heightened animosity in rival dyads elevates transaction costs and reduces cross-border partnership feasibility, implying that alignment forecloses options with opposing-bloc firms. Third, profitability and internationalization: alignment may unlock preferential financing, governance templates, and dispute-resolution infrastructure inaccessible to non-aligned firms. Fourth, reputation and legitimacy: alignment enhances credibility with stakeholders in affiliated markets while risking legitimacy discounts, regulatory burdens, or demand-side penalties elsewhere. By juxtaposing “align” versus “do not align” arguments and specifying when each logic dominates, the panel offers a contingent, mechanism-based framework for understanding how geopolitical alignment reshapes firm capabilities, networks, and performance in Latin America. This interactive panel also aims to invite scholars and practitioners to surface overlooked moderators and identify strategic imperatives for navigating an increasingly contested competitive landscape.
