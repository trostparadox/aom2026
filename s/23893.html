Unpacking the Negative Effects of Public Supervisor Positive Feedback on Employee Task Performance

OB
Yinxu Zhou (Renmin University of China)
Existing research on supervisor positive feedback has predominantly revealed its benefits in facilitating employee task performance. However, the way in which such feedback is delivered—whether publicly or privately—is critical in shaping recipients’ reactions but has received comparatively little scholarly attention, thereby resulting in an incomplete understanding of supervisor positive feedback. To enrich the conventional belief in the favorable nature of supervisor positive feedback, our research investigates how and when public (versus private) supervisor positive feedback impairs employee task performance. Drawing upon self-presentation perspective, we propose that public (versus private) supervisor positive feedback evokes employees’ image preservation motives, especially when they possess higher rather than lower levels of public self-consciousness, consequently reducing their propensity to seek feedback from others. Such reduction in feedback seeking behavior, we argue, can further exert a negative impact on employee task performance. Results of a pre-registered experimental study from 214 U.S. full-time workers and a pre-registered two-wave, multi-source survey study from 113 employees in a Chinese manufacturing company support our hypotheses. Our research contributes to the feedback literature and offers practical implications to enhance the effectiveness of supervisor feedback delivery.
