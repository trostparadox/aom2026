Symmetry-Based Theory Building: An Effective Research Strategy in and from the Global South

OMT
Xin Li
Inspired by the history of theoretical physics—where symmetry has served as a guiding principle for discovering fundamental laws—this paper develops a symmetry-based approach to theory building in management and organization studies. It advances three key ideas: (1) symmetry, as a heuristic for conceptual parity, legitimizes opposites, seeing them being complementary; (2) complementarity anticipates asymmetry as the opposite of symmetry itself, which recognizes that organizational life often reveals systematic imbalances and encourages scholars to theorize their causes and consequences; and (3) symmetry and asymmetry coexist in a recursive and paradoxical process of symmetry breaking and asymmetry reduction, offering a framework for understanding how imbalance is generated and redressed through strategic, cognitive, or technological interventions. The paper explores the implications of these ideas for constructing more inclusive, context-sensitive, and dynamic theories. It argues that symmetry-based theorizing facilitates the legitimate integration of indigenous perspectives from the Global South into global scholarship, serving as an effective strategy for decolonizing social science. Furthermore, it positions symmetry-based theorizing as a pathway toward generating potentially universal knowledge—one that embeds epistemic pluralism into the architecture of theory itself, rather than relying on post hoc cross-cultural validation. Through conceptual development and illustrative examples, the paper demonstrates how symmetry can function as both a lens and a stance for advancing management and organization studies going forward.
