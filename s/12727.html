Session overview

The goal of this PDW is to discuss the central role that process thinking/theory and process-focused theoretical argumentation play in developing impactful prescription-oriented OM scholarship. To foster an understanding of why process thinking and process-focused theoretical argumentation is at the core of such inquiry, we must equip scholars with a clear understanding of what exactly process thinking/theory and associated arguments entail (Part 1). Once established, a discussion can be had around what state attributes of operational processes, broadly established in practice and in academic discourse, though often overlooked in contemporary theoretical argumentation, are integral to describing and sustaining the dynamics of processes (Part 2). That, then, permits a consideration of operational levers (e.g., design choices), many of which are also well established in practice, available as process interventions (i.e. controlled shocks or fundamental process restructurings) capable of shifting the dynamics of processes and their state attributes in the direction of managerial intentions (Part 3). In combination, contextually-relevant process thinking, an understanding of process state attributes, and the impact that operational design choices may have at specific points of a process in question provide theoretically grounding for process arguments that are at once nuanced and ostensibly prescriptive. The hope of this PDW is that individuals emerge with an appreciation of how to use such tactics in their own research.
