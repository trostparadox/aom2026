Session overview

This symposium integrates cutting-edge research examining cultural tightness through an explicitly interactional lens, exploring how cultural contexts shape organizational realities and how organizational practices influence broader cultural patterns. Four papers address critical questions using diverse methodological approaches across multiple countries. The first presentation challenges the assumption that tight cultures reduce deviance, demonstrating that individuals in tight cultures engage in more rule-breaking in weak situations due to heightened psychological reactance. The second distinguishes between public and private norm tightness across Chinese provinces, revealing different socioeconomic correlates. The third examines how needs-supplies overfit shapes territorial behaviors differently under tight versus loose organizational cultures. The final presentation explores how cultural tightness-looseness moderates the relationship between trait authenticity and leadership emergence. Together, these presentations advance a more integrated, dynamic understanding of cultural tightness that recognizes organizations as both products and producers of cultural contexts, with implications for cross-cultural management and organizational change.
