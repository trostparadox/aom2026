Session overview

Generative AI systems are increasingly used across the research lifecycle, from literature reviews and data analysis to writing and peer review. While existing discussions emphasize ethical issues such as transparency, bias, and accuracy, less attention has been paid to the deeper trade-offs these systems introduce for researchers’ critical discernment, reflexivity, and sense-making practices. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) examines these spiritual and epistemic trade-offs by integrating perspectives from AI ethics, research methodology, and spirituality. Drawing on the trustworthy AI framework alongside Vedantic perspectives on knowledge, knowing, and the knower (e.g., the K-Model of spirituality), the workshop explores how reliance on generative AI may reshape researchers’ relationships with inquiry, agency, and responsibility. Through short conceptual inputs and guided interactive exercises, participants will (1) Gain a fundamental understanding of the trustworthy AI framework (2) critically map where generative AI enters their own research processes, (3) identify methodological and spiritual trade-offs involved in delegating, sensory,cognitive and interpretive tasks to AI systems, (4) develop reflective questions and practices for more conscious and responsible use of generative AI in research and (5) evaluate the trustworthy AI requirements for generative AI systems from a spiritual lens. The PDW is particularly relevant for doctoral students and faculty interested in research methods, AI ethics, and spirituality in management scholarship.
