Mitigation Without Adaptation? Organizational and Policy Constraints on SME Climate Resilience

Authors: Paraskevi (Evita) Psoni Athens University of Economics and Business; Ioannis Thanos Athens University of Economics and Business

Session overview

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) are increasingly exposed to climate-related disruptions, yet their capacity to build climate resilience remains uneven and insufficiently understood. Prior research has largely emphasized mitigation strategies and large-firm responses, leaving limited insight into how SMEs experience and operationalize climate adaptation within real-world policy environments. This study examines how organizational and institutional factors jointly shape SME climate resilience, with particular attention to the imbalance between mitigation-led policy frameworks and firms’ adaptation needs. Drawing on qualitative evidence from five focus groups with 21 stakeholders, including SME owners, business representatives, and institutional actors, we explore perceived barriers, enabling mechanisms, and practical interpretations of resilience in a Mediterranean context highly exposed to climate risks. Using thematic analysis, we identify three key findings. First, SMEs frequently equate mitigation investments with resilience, creating a fragile and often misleading sense of preparedness. Second, organizational constraints, such as limited financial resources, lack of technical expertise, and short-term planning horizons, are compounded by fragmented and misaligned policy mixes that under-support adaptation. Third, practitioners conceptualize resilience in operational terms (e.g., recovery time, business continuity, and adaptive flexibility), revealing a mismatch between policy indicators and firm-level realities. This study contributes to research by reframing climate resilience as a multilevel organizational and policy challenge rather than a purely strategic or technological issue. The findings highlight the limitations of mitigation-centric approaches and inform the design of more coherent, capacity-building policies to support SME climate adaptation and resilience.
