From ESG as Values to ESG as Infrastructure: Commensuration and Reactivity in SMEs

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 7
SIM

People

Authors: Maiko Kodama Meiji University; Miho Murashima Rikkyo University; Muhammad Amru Yazid Meiji University

Session overview

ESG requirements are rapidly moving from listed corporations into supply chains, yet SMEs often experience ESG less as a value-led initiative than as a practical condition for being readable to buyers, financiers, and auditors. Building on research on quantification and management control, we conceptualize ESG as an external measurement infrastructure that can be internalized as a management control package. Using seven semi-structured interviews with ESG and supply-chain actors in Malaysia’s oil and gas services and equipment (OGSE) SME ecosystem, we develop an abductive, mechanism-based account of this translation. We identify four linked mechanisms. First, commensuration turns heterogeneous practices into comparable data, expanding “data work” (definitions, boundaries, baselines) and increasing formalization. Second, reactivity redirects effort toward checklist-fit and document-fit as firms redesign routines to produce defensible evidence. Third, measurability bias reallocates scarce resources toward what is easiest to verify, often privileging environmental and governance artifacts over harder-to-evidence social impacts. Fourth, divergence across templates and ratings destabilizes evaluation criteria, amplifying workload and encouraging short-term compliance optimization. We contribute a process model that reframes ESG diffusion in SMEs as a management control design problem, not simply an adoption or disclosure problem, and we highlight practical control design that can reduce burden while keeping ESG connected to learning and substantive improvement.
