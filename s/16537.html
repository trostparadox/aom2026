Session overview

This symposium examines how markets are socialized through organizational practices that embed pro-social goals—such as cooperation, democratic participation, and collective responsibility—within economic activity. Challenging the assumption that markets are inherently competitive and individualistic, the symposium focuses on how organizations actively reconstruct market relations through values, institutions, actors, and non-market modes of coordination. Drawing on four empirical studies from Japan, the symposium conceptualizes market socialization as a multi-level process of organizing. The presentations analyze how socialization unfolds through value-based legitimation within firms, actor-level dynamics of psychological ownership, institutionalized systems of allocation grounded in social reciprocity, and collective organizing under crisis conditions where market mechanisms and formal institutions fail. Together, the symposium advances a practice-based understanding of how markets are reshaped through social relations, highlighting both the possibilities and tensions inherent in pro-social and democratic forms of organizing.
