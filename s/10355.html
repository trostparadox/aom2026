Session overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly mediating organizations’ interactions with those who affect or depend on them, yet we have limited understanding of how this changes who receives attention, how claims are evaluated, and who is held responsible. Research on AI in management has largely focused on internal adoption and augmentation, while stakeholder theory has paid little attention to AI-mediated relationships. This symposium brings these literatures together to ask a foundational question: Who counts in the age of AI? The four presentations examine how artificial intelligence reshapes stakeholder relations by rethinking who qualifies as a stakeholder, how non-traditional stakeholders can gain voice through AI-enabled representation, how artificial agents compete with humans for organizational attention, and how managers experience responsibility and justice when required to enact opaque algorithmic judgments. Together, they invite renewed debate on stakeholder theory and AI in management by showing how pervasive automation reshapes organizational relationships and strategies, attention allocation, and accountability in practice.
