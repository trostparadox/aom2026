Artificial Intelligence Fluency, Cultural Distance, and Expatriate Success: A Conceptual Framework

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3

People

Author: Oludare Oladimeji Fadare The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley

Session overview

Expatriate assignments are critical to multinational enterprises’ (MNEs) global strategies, yet expatriate underperformance and early return remain persistent challenges. Although prior research has identified cultural intelligence, language proficiency, and organizational support as key drivers of expatriate adjustment and success, comparatively little attention has been paid to how digital capabilities shape expatriate experiences in contemporary organizations. At the same time, expatriates increasingly operate in AI-enabled work environments while navigating varying degrees of cultural distance between home and host countries. Drawing on human capital theory and sensemaking theory, this conceptual paper develops a framework explaining how expatriates’ AI fluency influences expatriate adjustment and, in turn, expatriate success. We further theorize that cultural distance moderates this relationship by shaping the degree of ambiguity and interpretive effort required during international assignments. By positioning expatriate adjustment as a central mediating mechanism and cultural distance as a contingent boundary condition, this framework extends expatriate research into the digital era. In doing so, it advances a more nuanced understanding of how individual digital capabilities interact with contextual distance to shape expatriate success in AI-enabled global work environments.
