Board Involvement with Artificial Intelligence: A Typology for Corporate Strategy and Governance

STR
Hellen Rodrigues (FEI University Centre); Roberto Carlos Bernardes
Contemporary corporate governance operates in a landscape marked by profound uncertainty, shaped by climate change, geopolitical challenges, and the disruptive emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Board members face a dual obligation to oversee AI as a central component of corporate strategy while integrating it to enhance their own decision-making and oversight functions. The literature lacks an explanation of how these challenges reshape board characteristics. This study addresses this gap by examining the theoretical functional roles of boards and empirical cost-based governance configurations from the perspective of the Awareness-Motivation-Capability (AMC) framework. The proposed typology delineates six distinct board configurations, providing insight into how boards engage in strategy and AI augmentation. The findings help clarify the characteristics a board can exhibit, whether proactive, resistant, or symbolic, in relation to AI integration.
