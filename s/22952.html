Support as a Catalyst: Examining POS as a Mediator of Post-Merger Identification

ODC
Mohini Yadav; Mahima Thakur (University of Delhi); Innat Adil
Amidst the accelerating trend of M&As, cultivating employee identification with the newly formed organisation has become a necessity. This empirical study examines four key organisational factors that influence post-merger identification (PMI): transformational leadership (TL), perception of continuity (POC), satisfaction with HR initiatives (SWHR), and perceived organisational support (POS). The primary objective is to examine the mediating role of POS in shaping PMI. Drawing on data collected from 412 employees involved in ten cross-border M&A cases within the Delhi-NCR region over the past five years, the study employs a robust structural equation modelling (SEM) approach to test the proposed relationships. The findings offer critical insights into the psychological mechanisms underpinning employee integration during M&A transitions, underscoring POS as a central lever through which organisations can strengthen identification in post-merger contexts. The study offers valuable implications for M&A strategists, HR professionals, and organisational psychologists aiming to enhance employee alignment and stability during periods of transformative change.

Unpacking the Multilevel Effects of Organizational Change: The Moderating Role of Culture

ODC
Yurina Lee (Yonsei University); Boram Do (Yonsei University)
Organizational change is a recurring reality in today's dynamic business environment, impacting employee well-being and intentions. While existing research emphasizes the disruptive nature of change, a limited understanding remains of how this relates to behavioral outcomes like turnover intention, particularly considering the moderating role of organizational culture. By drawing on the Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) model, we examine how burnout mediates the impact of organizational change on turnover intention. More importantly we introduce organizational culture, focusing on clan and hierarchical cultures, as crucial moderators in shaping these relationships. Findings from a multi-level path analysis underscore the intricate interplay between organizational change, burnout, and turnover intention, emphasizing the central roles of clan and hierarchical cultures. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of the complexities surrounding organizational change and its implications for employee outcomes, highlighting the need for culture-sensitive approaches in managing transitions.

Technostress, Workload, and Emotional Exhaustion: The Role of Supervisory Coaching

ODC
Leila Messarra (Lebanese American University); Grace K. Dagher (Lebanese American University); Diditta Rizk (Lebanese American University); Mazen Malaeb (Loughborough University)
Emotional Exhaustion is increasingly prevalent in the workplaces, driven by increasing workload and technostress. This state of emotional exhaustion has been linked to various forms of employee withdrawal, both physical (e.g., absenteeism) and psychological (e.g., disengagement). Despite its growing significance, there is limited research exploring how emotional exhaustion translates into these withdrawal behaviors. Moreover, the role of supervisory coaching in mitigating these effects remain underexplored in organizational settings. This study investigates the relationship between emotional exhaustion and employee withdrawal behaviors, and examines whether supervisory coaching can moderate this relationship. Data was collected using online survey. The findings reveal that both technostress and workload significantly contribute to emotional exhaustion, which in turn increases withdrawal behaviors. Importantly, supervisory coaching was found to moderate the relationship between emotional exhaustion and physical withdrawal, though it had no significant effect on psychological withdrawal. These results highlight the value of supervisory coaching as a targeted intervention to reduce the physical manifestations of employee withdrawal. This study provides actionable insights for scholars, organizational leaders and Human Resource professionals aiming to enhance employee well-being and retention.

When Supportive Systems Fail: Emotional Labor and Stress in Veterinary Medicine

ODC
Gershon L Alaluf (Pepperdine University); Kevin S. Groves (Pepperdine University)
Organization development and change research often emphasizes resilience and psychological safety as core resources for sustaining well-being in demanding work systems. However, evidence from emotionally intensive professions suggests that supportive systems can fail under conditions of sustained emotional labor. Drawing on conservation of resources theory and work design perspectives, this conceptual paper advances a model in which emotional labor operates as a structural condition that weakens the stress-buffering effects of resilience and psychological safety while amplifying vulnerability arising from baseline psychological distress. Using veterinary medicine as a theoretically informative context undergoing disruptive organizational and professional change, we illustrate how work systems may remain superficially supportive yet produce elevated work-related stress and increased intention to leave the profession. This paper contributes to organization development and change scholarship by clarifying when supportive systems become unsustainable and by offering research propositions that highlight how durable change requires redesigning the emotional architecture of work rather than relying solely on individual or cultural supports. We conclude with a discussion of future research directions, pathways for testing the conceptual model, and practical implications for veterinary practice, education, and work systems.

Human Resource 5.0 and Organizational Development Effectiveness, the Mediating Role of HR Efficiency

ODC
Ahmad Alnsour (Princess Sumaya University for Technology)
This research aims to study how implementing Human Resource 5.0 in Jordanian commercial banks impacts Organizational Development (OD) effectiveness. The research model was developed after thoroughly reviewing the existing literature on the topic to answer three key questions and test five hypotheses. HR Managers and Employees from commercial banks throughout Jordan were the population of this research. This research collected 171 responses using a census method, and then the data was analyzed using AMOS and SPSS software. The research results revealed that Human Resource 5.0 has a notably positive impact on Organizational Development effectiveness through Human Resource Efficiency. The research concludes that implementing HR 5.0 has a positive impact on HR efficiency, therefore positively affecting organizational development effectiveness. This includes implementing it in strategic HR planning, recruiting, training and development, and performance management. HR 5.0 assesses organizations in analyzing the large amount of data, which allows HR professionals to make accurate decisions within an efficient timeframe and resources. HR 5.0 also frees up time for HR professionals by automating routine tasks, allowing professionals to focus more on their strategic role. The study presents solutions to increase OD effectiveness through increasing HR efficiency, and it can benefit decision-makers in Jordanian commercial banks by emphasizing the effect of implementing HR 5.0 and how this implementation will make HR activities more efficient and OD more effective. It is expected that leaders in these organizations will value HR 5.0 and adapt it in their organizations.
