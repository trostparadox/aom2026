Session overview

Attracting and retaining high quality human capital is both pivotal and systematically difficult for startups. Startups are typically resource constrained, operate under high market and technological uncertainty and often lack credibility as attractive employers. From job seekers’ viewpoint, working for a startup also entails considerable uncertainty regarding future career paths, the redeployability of skills, and often lower pay (Hietaniemi et al., 2024; Sorenson et al., 2021). Yet the entrepreneurial labor market is a distinct one in which individual self-selected for reasons such as preference for autonomy, risk and intellectual challenges (Nyström, 2021; Roach & Sauermann, 2015), the nonpecuniary benefits associated with startups (Roach & Sauermann, 2024) and differences in ability (Honore & Ganco, 2023; Kim, 2018). More recently, a growing body of literature has begun to investigate specific hiring strategies, including signaling through founders’ human capital, affiliation-based hiring, the communication of a social mission and firm-driven search (Kim & Pergler, 2025; Rocha & Brymer, 2025; Rocha & Grilli, 2024; van Balen & Taracki, 2024). Despite these advances, our understanding of how startup hiring unfolds and when particular strategies are effective remains limited. First, there is substantial heterogeneity among startups (e.g., high tech vs. service oriented; VC-backed vs. non-VC backed; academic spin-offs vs. non-spin-offs; commercial oriented vs. social-mission oriented) and startup founders (e.g., first-time founders vs. serial founders), which likely make some more salient and attractive to job seekers than others. Second, the heterogeneity among job seekers is also substantial concerning their perceived ability and preference (e.g., for autonomy, risk or social impact). Since these characteristics are imperfectly observable, they complicate startups’ efforts to target and attract suitable candidates. Third, beyond the dyad of startups and job seekers, local labor dynamics such as the presence and characteristics of surrounding incumbent employers may also shape startup hiring outcomes by affecting both the supply and competition for entrepreneurial talent. Against this backdrop, this presenter symposium is organized around three guiding questions: (1) How does heterogeneity among startups (e.g., organizational attributes, founder characteristics) shape the effectiveness of different hiring strategies? (2) How do differences in job seekers (e.g., human capital, social preferences, beliefs of organizational attributes) influence their sorting into different startups? (3) How do local labor market conditions and the presence of incumbent firms shape startups’ ability to hire talent?
