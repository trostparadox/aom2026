Cross-Border Recruitment, Retention, and Organizational Practices in Asia

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Author: Aashlesha Marathe University of Tsukuba

Session overview

This project studies cross-border recruitment in Asia through three perspectives, namely recruitment agencies, employing organizations, and employees. It explores how retention can be improved through adaptation across the recruitment-to-employment process. Japan, along with South Korea, Taiwan, and Singapore, faces persistent labor shortages. These countries have responded by revising visa policies and expanding cross-border recruitment across Asia. Despite increased labor migration, organizational adaptation remains limited. Foreign employees are treated as labor resources, while their emotional and social needs remain marginal to organizational decision-making. This results in high attrition and creates high costs for firms that invest in the candidates’ recruitment, relocation, language training, and skill development. Recruiters play an important role as HR intermediaries. They shape person-organization fit, frame expectations, and influence early employment experiences. However, their role remains understudied in labor migration and retention. The poster presents empirical findings from interviews with recruiters, fieldwork at recruitment events, and interviews with Indian individuals who joined Japanese companies as new graduate employees. It also presents an analysis of where misalignment in expectations occurs. Findings show that recruitment in Japan prioritizes language compatibility over technical skill alignment. Companies rarely adopt inclusive approaches that extend beyond language training to broader organizational adaptability. The study suggests that recruiters could contribute to improving retention by encouraging organizational adaptation through promoting adaptive, technology-supported communication practices that support longer-term employment outcomes. While findings focus on the hiring of Indian graduates in Japanese companies, the discussion extends to broader cross-border recruitment practices across Asian and global contexts.
