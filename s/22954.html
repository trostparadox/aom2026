Understanding Ecosystem Renewal Across Industry Boundaries: A Hybrid Organizing Perspective

ODC
Yi Sun; Hanning Shi (University of Cambridge); Yongjiang Shi (University of Cambridge)
This study investigates how ecosystems renew across industry boundaries through the lens of hybrid organizing. Although cross sector expansion between the circular economy (CE) and the creative industry (CI) is increasingly common, limited research explains how firms integrate distinct institutional logics during the renewal phase of ecosystem evolution. Drawing on 17 years of longitudinal data, we adopt an extreme single case design centered on Spring Pool Glass, a Taiwanese firm that transitioned from a CE oriented recycler to a CI driven innovator. The findings reveal a multi-level process of cross industry ecosystem renewal comprising narrative bridging, offering design, and network expansion. At the narrative level, CE and CI logics are integrated through aesthetic, symbolic, cultural, and experiential elements that establish cross-industry legitimacy. At the product level, offering design functions as an experimental space where institutional values are translated into tangible offerings. At the network level, partner selection increasingly emphasizes shared value propositions and complementary expertise for value co-creation. This study extends hybrid organizing to the ecosystem level, advances ecosystem renewal research by clarifying cross-industry evolution dynamics, and offers practical insights for CE firms repositioning in CI markets.

Tsunami Change: Building Proactive Capacity Under Extreme Disruption

ODC
Steven H. Cady (Bowling Green State University); Donald D. Warrick (University of Colorado-Colorado Springs)
Organizations increasingly face extreme, compounding disruptions that overwhelm conventional approaches to change. Drawing on tsunami science, this article introduces Tsunami Change as a distinct form of large-scale disruption in human systems and proposes an eight-phase framework that explains how such change unfolds. Using a theory-building, cross-disciplinary approach, the framework is illustrated through a qualitative analysis of publicly available archival data on the COVID-19 pandemic. Rather than treating the pandemic as a bounded case, it serves as a naturalistic context for examining the dynamics of Tsunami Change across levels and over time. Building on the framework, the article introduces proactive capacity as a systemic, human-centered capability that shapes whether organizations respond reactively or navigate disruption with resilience, learning, and renewal. Implications for translational research and professional practice are discussed across Organization Development and Change (ODC), Change Management, and Crisis Management. The article invites future research on how organizations anticipate, absorb, and learn from sustained disruption in an increasingly volatile world.

Innovation Barriers and Sustainability Innovation Performance: The Role of Entrepreneurial Bricolage

ODC
Tzujung Wu (National Taipei University of Nursing and Health Sciences); Chih-Cheng Lin
Innovation is widely recognized as a critical capability for enterprises to achieve sustainable development and competitive advantage. However, firms frequently encounter multifaceted obstacles—including market, financial, and technical challenges—throughout the innovation process. Drawing upon the framework of Resource Orchestration Theory (ROT), this study develops and tests an analytical model of "Innovation Barriers–Entrepreneurial Bricolage– Sustainable Innovation Performance." Specifically, it explores the impact of innovation barriers on entrepreneurial bricolage behaviors and the mediating role of entrepreneurial bricolage in the relationship between innovation barriers and sustainable innovation performance. Adopting an empirical research design, this study analyzes data from 300 manufacturing firms sourced from the Taiwan Corporate Innovation Survey conducted between 2020 and 2025. The results indicate that innovation barriers significantly influence entrepreneurial bricolage, which, in turn, fully mediates the relationship between innovation barriers and sustainable innovation performance. Furthermore, the findings suggest that while innovation barriers are often perceived as impediments, they can also serve as catalysts for entrepreneurial bricolage. Such barriers prompt firms to integrate internal and external resources to overcome challenges, ultimately realizing sustainable innovation performance.

Situated Transcending: Leveraging Institutional Translation for Organizational Transformation

ODC
Dastan Khussainov (SDU University); Anjan Ghosh (Nazarbayev University); Arijit Chatterjee (ESSEC Business School)
Institutional translation is intended to produce organizational and societal improvements, yet how it can extend beyond its original aims remains understudied. Addressing this gap, we examine how organizations strategically leverage translation to drive large-scale transformation. In this paper, we report the findings of an ethnographic study of a Central Asian university that used a double degree program to reshape its activities and culture. Through inductive theorization based on a process study, we offer situated transcending as a way of linking translation with organizational transformation. Our research makes three key contributions. We demonstrate how translation can be leveraged beyond its original goals to support holistic organizational development and change. Second, we highlight the role of situatedness in enabling actors to link translation with transformation. Third, we show how experimentation helps organizations identify, influence, and enact structures and functions that support this process. Our study offers practical implications for policymakers and organizational leaders, particularly in transition economies, by illustrating how translation models such as global collaborations can drive strategic change.
