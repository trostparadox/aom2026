Making Sense of Followership: How Virtuality Impacts Social Construction of the Follower Role

Authors: Gloria Calabrese Sapienza University of Rome; Patrick Coyle La Salle University; Madison Hintze La Salle University

Session overview

The present study employed a qualitative effort to examine how virtuality influences the social construction of the follower role. Specifically, we aim to address how follower roles are constructed and interpreted in virtual environments compared to traditional face-to-face settings. Data collected using semi-structured interviews across 24 full-time working adults (n=12 fully in-person, n=12 fully remote) revealed important similarities between how key themes were contextualized and operationalized across both formats. Reliability, communication, and autonomy were key themes regarding follower role construction, however, trust building, modality, and how autonomy is earned differed. Sensemaking processes, inferencing, and stability were key themes to discern context, however, the source of cues, inference mechanisms, and temporal logic of alignment differed.
