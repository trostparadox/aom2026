Session overview

Idea management systems are increasingly used by organizations and universities to stimulate innovation, creativity, and problem solving. These systems— such as hackathons, innovation challenges, and idea competitions—are inherently coopetitive: participants collaborate intensively within teams while simultaneously competing against others for recognition, rewards, and implementation opportunities. Despite their widespread adoption, there is limited guidance on how coopetition can be deliberately designed and managed to improve innovation results rather than create counterproductive tension. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) focuses on coopetition in idea management, drawing on practical experience from hackathons organized at Riga Technical University. The workshop conceptualizes hackathons as temporary idea management systems in which cooperation and competition coexist across multiple stages, including idea generation, development, evaluation, and selection. Using this experience as an anchoring case, the PDW illustrates how specific design choices—such as team composition, mentoring arrangements, evaluation criteria, and incentive structures—shape idea quality, participant engagement, and learning outcomes. The workshop adopts a paradox perspective, emphasizing that cooperation and competition should not be treated as mutually exclusive but as complementary forces that, when carefully balanced, can enhance innovation performance. Participants will engage in interactive activities, including a hackathon-inspired simulation and small-group design exercises, to experience and analyze coopetition dynamics firsthand. This PDW is intended for doctoral students, early-career scholars, and experienced researchers interested in innovation management, entrepreneurship, strategy, and organizational design. Participants will leave with a clearer conceptual understanding of coopetition in idea management, concrete research questions and methodological approaches, and practical tools for designing innovation processes that boost results through competitive collaboration.
