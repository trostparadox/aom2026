The Design of Hybrid Work for Sustainable Well-Being: A Framework for Strategic HRM

HR
Roopa Nagori
Hybrid work has emerged as a dominant arrangement post-pandemic, yet organizations struggle to design sustainable models supporting employee well-being and engagement. This systematic literature review synthesizes 78 studies to develop an integrated framework identifying five critical factors: (1) space and work environment, (2) culture and leadership, (3) jobs and tasks allocation, (4) digital tools, and (5) digital well-being management. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources and Conservation of Resources theories, we position HR professionals as strategic architects of sustainable hybrid work systems. Our analysis reveals tensions between flexibility demands and coordination needs, highlighting HRM’s strategic role in balancing autonomy with effectiveness. We offer five evidence-based propositions addressing workspace design, leadership development, task allocation, digital collaboration, and well-being interventions. This research contributes to strategic HRM literature by theorizing how HR practices facilitate sustainable work arrangements aligning with UN Sustainable Development Goals while addressing contemporary engagement and well-being challenges in hybrid contexts.
