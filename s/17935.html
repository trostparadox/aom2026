Session overview

This symposium advances a process-oriented perspective that conceptualizes networks as socially embedded systems shaped by interpretation, emotion, identity, and social evaluation. Across diverse empirical contexts, the papers included in this symposium examine how actors’ perceptions of relationships, emotional responses to past and anticipated interactions, and others’ evaluations of network behavior shape outcomes such as mobilization, brokerage, and influence. A central theme of the symposium is that agency in networks is relational and contingent on social judgment. The included set of papers highlights networks as arenas of social evaluation in which efforts to activate ties or mobilize support are filtered through judgments of legitimacy, usefulness, and appropriateness. The symposium further explores how categorical identities (e.g., race and gender) and individual differences (e.g., emotional orientation, relational strategies, and positional experiences) systematically shape opportunities and constraints in networks. Collectively, the included papers in this symposium highlight the importance of defining networks as dynamic, emotionally charged, and socially embedded systems through which agency and inequality are continuously negotiated.
