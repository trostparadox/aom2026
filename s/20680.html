From Exaptation to Disruption: Contingencies and the Moderating Role of Technological Relatedness

Session overview

This study examines how technological adaptation influences disruptive innovation and the contingent role of technological relatedness. While adaptation is theorized to foster disruption by enabling functional shifts, its excessive use may impede knowledge integration. We hypothesize an inverted U-shaped relationship, driven by two latent mechanisms: adaptive novelty and adaptive mismatch. Furthermore, we propose that technological relatedness flattens this curve by simultaneously dampening both the positive and negative effects of adaptation. Using a large-scale panel dataset of USPTO patents in the electrical communication domain, our fixed-effects models support these hypotheses. The findings reveal that moderate levels of adaptation optimally promote disruption, and this effect is weaker when firms operate in technologically related domains. This research contributes to innovation theory by unpacking the nuanced mechanisms and boundary conditions under which adaptation translates into disruptive outcomes, enriching the literature on cross-domain knowledge recombination and technological evolution.
