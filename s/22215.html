When Servant Leadership Backfires: Examining Motivational Pathways to UPB

Session overview

Early research on servant leadership (SL) portrays it as an ethical and other-oriented leadership that foster prosocial behaviors while restraining unethical behavior. However, more recent studies provide evidence suggesting that its effects are more complex. Grounding our research on behavioral decision theory and social cognitive theory, we tested and found a paradoxical interaction between SL and unethical pro-organizational behavior (UBP). We suggest that the consequential effect of servant leadership on UPB is parallelly and simultaneously activates, through competing motivational paths, employees’ perceived accountability, identification with the organization, and career self-interest. SL may stimulate or restrain the engagement in UPB depending on the pathway. We also proposed and found moral identification as a boundary condition influencing this relationship. Our hypotheses were tested using two samples from business students working at a large Midwestern university located in the United States (N = 276; N = 291). Results support our theorization. In particular, we found that SL is positively related to UPB through career self-interest and organizational identification respectively while restricting the engagement in UPB through the presence of (greater) accountability (ACC). Overall, our findings provide a complex dynamic between SL and UPB, and the paradoxical nature (or dark side) of SL through various motivational mechanisms. In a nutshell, our research advances leadership, ethics, and accountability research by shedding light on the context dependent, non-linear nature of SL. Further offering insight into the important role of the adequate implementation of SL and motivational programs at work. Keywords: Accountability, Career self-interest, Organizational identification, Servant leadership, Unethical pro-organizational behavior (UPB)
