Expatriate Perceived Job Mobility Inhibits Cultural Intelligence-Driven Knowledge Acquisition

IM
Yu-Shan Hsu (Concordia University); Flora Chiang (CEIBS); Emmy van Esch (The University of Auckland); Thomas A. Birtch (University of Exeter)
Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) and Social Information Processing (SIP) theories, this study examines how expatriates’ cultural intelligence (CQ) influences host country nationals’ (HCNs) knowledge acquisition through expatriate helping behaviors. Analyzing data from 93 expatriate-HCN dyads, we find that CQ positively relates to HCN knowledge acquisition via helping behaviors but only when expatriates perceive low external job mobility. These findings challenge the assumption that CQ universally facilitates intercultural knowledge transfer, highlighting its contingent value based on career contexts. We discuss theoretical and practical implications for expatriate management, knowledge transfer, and the contextual activation of cross-cultural competence.
