The Role of Demand Side Perspective in Shaping Internationalization: Evidence from Movie Industry

IM
Evelyn Lin Peng (The University of Sydney); Chinmay Pattnaik (The University of Sydney); Yi Li (The University of Sydney)
This study introduces two dimensions of home market demand-side consumer feedback and their influence on internationalization: sentiment and culture-specificity. Consumer feedback and evaluation as reflection of market perception can be high or low in culture-specificity and positive or negative in sentiment, thereby being one of four broad categories in a two-by-two matrix. Building on the demand-side perspective, this framework of demand-side consumer feedback attributes helps explain a series of gradations on how home market demand-side consumer feedback influences the internationalization strategy of cultural product. This paper examines the internationalization strategies of 2,493 motion pictures produced in the United States between 2000 and 2019, for which domestic box office information, casting details, movie attributes, consumer review, and global distribution data are available. We scrape 196,470 reviews on IMDb and utilize neural topic modelling technique (BERTopic) to operationalize demand-side home market consumer feedback.

Rethinking Liability of Foreignness: Audiences, Category Ambiguity, and Foreign IPOs

IM
Jay Inghwee Chok (Westcliff University); HuiGuo Liu
This paper reconceptualizes the liability of foreignness (LOF) as an audience-side interpretive problem rooted in category ambiguity rather than a firm-side handicap. Using data on 147 Chinese firms listing in U.S. markets between 1990 and 2015 (Pre-Trump Era), we examine how foreign IPOs are evaluated by a common host-market audience of U.S. investors. Category ambiguity is conceptualized as arising from three structural properties of the categorical environment: subcategory thinness, fuzzy density, and contrast. We find that foreign IPOs originating from thinner subcategories raise higher IPO proceeds and that higher fuzzy density and greater contrast within the home-country category are also positively associated with proceeds. We further examine the role of endorsement cues and macroeconomic narratives, showing that their relevance for capital allocation varies with issuers’ categorical positioning. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of category structure in shaping how host-market audiences interpret and allocate capital to foreign IPOs.

Co-Branding in Multinational Enterprises: Partner Choice, Integration, and Home-Country Firm Risk

IM
Shao-Chi Chang (National Cheng Kung University); Thi Ha Trang (Lucy) Dang (National Chung Cheng University); Tran-Phuong-Uyen Dinh (National Cheng Kung University)
We examine co-branding as a risk-management strategy used by multinational enterprises (MNEs). Based on 476 firm-announcement observations, we show that co-branding significantly reduces market-based systematic and idiosyncratic risk, as evaluated by investors in the MNE’s home-country capital market. Conceptualizing co-branding as a form of non-equity international collaboration, we further investigate how partner selection and the degree of integration shape these risk outcomes. Contrary to conventional expectations, alliances with high-value global brand partners do not generate superior risk reduction. Instead, collaborations with lower-value brands are more effective in mitigating systematic risk. Moreover, lower-integration or symbolic co-branding strategies provide stronger risk protection for MNEs than highly integrated approaches, such as ingredient or composite branding. These findings highlight the role of co-branding in helping MNEs manage international uncertainty and provide guidance on alliance design and partner selection.

From Differentiation to Conformity: Impression-Oriented Communication During Cross-Border M&As

IM
Hidehiro (Hide) Ito (Florida International University); William Newburry (Florida International University)
Announcements of cross-border mergers and acquisitions (M&As) often trigger negative market reactions as well as political and societal opposition. Building on research showing that firms release positive but unrelated information to mitigate negative reactions to acquisition announcements, we examine how acquiring MNEs adjust the intensity of impression-oriented CSR communication on owned social media relative to salient competitors during the announcement-to-closing period. Drawing on differentiation–conformity theory and host-country collective sentiment dynamics, we argue that acquirers initially differentiate through more intensive CSR communication following the announcement but become more circumspect when communication tone is highly subjective, and that early-stage differentiation conditions subsequent late-stage communication. Using CSR-related Instagram posts linked to cross-border M&As between 2017 and 2024 and a matched-pair difference-in-differences design, we find consistent support for these arguments. Our study extends differentiation–conformity logic from for-profit strategy to the domain of nonmarket strategy and highlights temporal trade-offs in legitimacy management during cross-border M&As that have received limited attention in prior research.
