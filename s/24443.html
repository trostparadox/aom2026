Exploring the Predictors of Employee Inclusion in Hybrid Teams: A Machine Learning Approach

OB
Susanne Kerschbaumer (TU Wien); Stephan Alexander Boehm (University of St. Gallen); Martina Hartner-Tiefenthaler (TU Wien)
With hybrid work becoming the norm in many organizations, new challenges arise in fostering employee inclusion. While prior research has begun to examine inclusion in hybrid teams, large-scale quantitative evidence on the conditions that foster inclusion in these contexts remains limited. Drawing on a large employee sample from a German manufacturing company (N > 1,300) and a broad set of predictors, this study employs a machine-learning approach to examine the relative importance, non-linear effects, and interactions of factors shaping inclusion in hybrid work settings. Importantly, we conceptualize inclusion as comprising two distinct dimensions – belongingness and authenticity – and analyze them separately. We uncover novel factors influencing inclusion (e.g., mental models and rules for hybrid work), highlight the heightened importance of known predictors in the context of hybrid work (e.g., intrateam trust), and identify non-linear relationships and interacting predictors. By applying a machine-learning approach to the study of inclusion in hybrid teams, this research extends existing inclusion models to contemporary work contexts, demonstrates the value of distinguishing between belongingness and authenticity, and illustrates how methodological approaches capable of capturing non-linear and interactive effects can generate new insights and hypotheses for future research. Together, these findings inform both theory and practice by clarifying what matters for ensuring that employees feel included in modern, distributed workplaces.
