Session overview

This symposium examines how artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping disability inclusion in the workplace, with a particular focus on human resource (HR) practices and employer decision-making. The session brings together disability scholars and a leading disability advocacy organization to explore AI’s dual potential to dismantle and to reinforce barriers across the employment lifecycle for people with disabilities. Panelists analyze how AI-enabled tools can enhance accessibility, communication, and participation for employees with physical, sensory, cognitive, and neurodivergent disabilities. The session highlights emerging risks, including algorithmic discrimination in hiring and promotion, inaccessible HR technologies, privacy concerns, and the possibility that AI obscures or replaces legally required individualized accommodations. Speakers will outline HR and governance strategies to ensure that AI remains accessible, fair, and compliant, emphasizing inclusive procurement, disability participation in design and testing, human oversight of AI-mediated decisions, and robust AI accessibility and fairness governance. The session will include a presentation of longitudinal data on employer disability practices that links AI’s consequences for workers with disabilities to existing organizational commitments, arguing that AI is most likely to promote inclusion where strong disability practices and cultures are already in place. Collectively, the session advances a research agenda at the intersection of AI, HR, and disability, identifying questions and areas for future study.
