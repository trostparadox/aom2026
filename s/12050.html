Session overview

This poster examines how definitions and drivers of value creation and capture have evolved since Lepak, Smith, and Taylor (2007), expanding beyond economic outcomes to incorporate social and environmental dimensions. As entrepreneurs and organizations increasingly face pressure to address societal and ecological challenges alongside traditional profit objectives, understanding these evolving concepts becomes critical. Drawing on frameworks including stakeholder synergy, social bricolage, sustainability-oriented business models, and responsible governance, this poster explores mechanisms enabling organizations to balance competing priorities while scaling their impact. It explores key gaps in understanding how evolving definitions translate into actionable strategies, how organizations navigate tensions between economic profitability and social/environmental value generation, and what drives these conceptual shifts. By synthesizing recent theoretical developments and empirical insights, this review offers a comprehensive framework integrating beyond economic into social and environmental dimensions of value creation and capture. The poster contributes to entrepreneurship scholarship by advancing theoretical understanding of context-dependent value definitions, highlighting governance mechanisms for aligning stakeholder priorities, and providing actionable insights for social and sustainable entrepreneurship research. For practitioners, it offers guidance on aligning organizational practices with broader societal goals while maintaining competitive advantages. This work contributes to entrepreneurship literature by demonstrating how traditional value creation and capture concepts must adapt to contemporary grand challenges, fostering innovation pathways that simultaneously serve economic viability and societal welfare.
