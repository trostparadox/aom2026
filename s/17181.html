Session overview

This panel symposium challenges the long-standing tendency to portray entrepreneurs as disembodied, rational decision-makers, by foregrounding the bodily dimensions of the entrepreneurial experience. It explores for instance how entrepreneurs communicate with and through their bodies (e.g., in pitches), how the emotional ups and downs (e.g., fear of failure, exhaustion) affect opportunity pursuit and well-being over time, and how more "animalistic" behaviors invite us to rethink entrepreneurship beyond purely rational models. Bringing together Melissa Cardon, Charles Murnieks, Maia Young, and Johan Wiklund, we ask what an embodied lens adds to how we understand entrepreneurial journeys, and reflect on how to study entrepreneurs’ bodies in practice and on whose bodies are implicitly assumed to "fit" dominant entrepreneurial ideals.
