Incentive Salience and Meaning-Making: Reframing Gen Z Work Engagement in Indian Organizations

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Elham Malik VIT Vellore; Siddhartha Dubey; Anwesha Bose

Session overview

Generation Z employees are frequently described as disengaged, impatient, and difficult to retain. Such narratives often rely on surface-level stereotypes rather than systematic analysis of how digital conditioning, neuro-motivational processes, and organizational structures interact to shape contemporary work behavior. This study reframes disengagement not as a motivational deficit but as an outcome of incentive salience dynamics and shifting meaning-making orientations among Gen Z professionals in India. Drawing on incentive salience theory, which distinguishes between cue-driven “wanting” and experiential “liking”, the study explains how digitally mediated reward environments amplify short-term motivational activation while weakening sustained satisfaction and organizational attachment. Traditional engagement models, while valuable, fail to capture these neuro-behavioral dynamics and generational value shifts sufficiently. Using a qualitative-dominant mixed-methods design, data are collected through semi-structured interviews and structured questionnaires from Gen Z professionals across Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 Indian cities. Reflexive thematic analysis is employed to identify recurring patterns of engagement, reward sensitivity, burnout, boundary-setting, and value–work misalignment. Preliminary findings suggest that disengagement often represents adaptive resistance, boundary protection, and a search for meaning rather than apathy or low commitment. The study contributes theoretically by integrating neuroscience-informed motivation with organizational engagement scholarship and generational research. Practically, it offers guidance for redesigning reward systems, feedback cultures, and leadership practices to support sustainable engagement in digitally accelerated workplaces. The findings are particularly relevant for emerging economies where traditional organizational models intersect with digitally native workforces.
