Moral Meaning-Making and Career Agency in the Age of AI: Evidence from Women Professionals in Iraq

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1

People

Authors: Ying Chen University of Rhode Island; Helen Ezekiel University of Rhode Island

Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) tools become embedded in everyday work, professionals face growing moral ambiguity regarding appropriate and legitimate AI use. While existing research emphasizes responsible AI governance and organizational policies, little is known about how individuals interpret and navigate the moral meaning of AI use within their careers, particularly in non-Western contexts. This study examines how women professionals construct career agency ethically while working with AI tools in environments characterized by social scrutiny and limited formal guidance. Drawing on 40 in-depth interviews with women professionals across sectors in Iraq, we adopt an inductive, process-oriented qualitative approach to explore how AI use is morally evaluated and enacted over time. Our findings reveal a five-stage process through which participants move from uncertainty about AI’s legitimacy, to experiencing AI use stigma, engaging in moral legitimacy work, developing ethical upskilling practices, and ultimately cultivating reflective digital career agency. Central to this process is the tension between technological efficiency and fears of reputational harm, dependency, or moral judgment from peers and supervisors. The study makes three contributions. Theoretically, it introduces the concept of AI use stigma and advances a process model of moral meaning-making that links ethical evaluation to career agency. Empirically, it extends career research to a Global South context where gender norms and organizational hierarchies intensify moral scrutiny around technology use. Practically, the findings offer guidance for organizations and HR leaders on how clearer ethical norms, training, and psychological safety can support responsible AI adoption without undermining professional identity or career development.
