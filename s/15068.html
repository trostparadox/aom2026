A Two-Phase Model of Flow Experience Affecting Entrepreneurial Minds in an Active learning Program

Session overview

We investigate the relationship between flow experience and entrepreneurial minds by evaluating an intensive active-learning program. We propose two-phase model. Specifically, in the first phase, we hypothesize that the distance between challenge and skill (individual factor) and collective efficacy (team factor) serve as antecedents to flow experience. In the second phase, we hypothesize that this flow experience influences entrepreneurial self-efficacy and intention. We aim to clarify the validity of this two-phase model. Startup Weekend is an established three-day entrepreneurship program in which participants form provisional teams that mirror real entrepreneurial ones. As the program is rigidly designed, the environment at each event can easily be controlled. We surveyed 106 samples at four events in Japan. We verified the appropriateness of the two-phase model, in which flow experience acted as an effective mediating factor. In the first phase, we confirmed that a small distance between challenge and skill, as well as collective efficacy, had a positive influence on flow intensity. In the second phase, flow intensity showed a positive influence on entrepreneurial self-efficacy and intention. However, the effect of pre-entrepreneurial self-efficacy and intention was stronger than that of flow intensity. This suggests that an individual's initial entrepreneurial mind had a more significant impact on subsequent outcomes than the experience provided by the program. This is reasonable given the intensive program nature. Nevertheless, our study contributed to the understanding of the educational effects of intensive program and clarified the mechanism by which flow experience moderates the development of entrepreneurial minds.
