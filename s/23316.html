Double-Edged Gift? Technology Bottleneck Resolution and AI Startup Growth Potential

STR
Raghavan K V V (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Vijayaraghavan Venkataraman (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore)
Artificial Intelligence (AI), a versatile evolving technology, is widely viewed as a driver of firm growth and global economic impact. Firm growth literature recognizes technology’s versatility, but the differential effects of technology bottleneck resolution, a key constituent of technology evolution, remain underexplored. This study examines the differential effects of AI bottleneck resolution on AI firm growth potential. We argue that bottleneck resolution opens a new Class of Problems enhancing firm growth potential, while also differentially amplifying firms’ prior resource bases, leading to differences in growth potential. Using a sample of 1,240 US AI startups, we tested our theory with difference-in-differences design, matching and firm fixed- effects to find support for our theory. The study contributes to research on AI, technology evolution, and ecosystems

How Design Novelty Influences the Impact of Adaptive Runtime Intelligence on Product Performance?

STR
Ram Ranganathan (The University of Texas at Austin); Puneet Sachdeva (Indian Institute of Management Udaipur)
We examine how adaptive runtime intelligence (ARI) – an application of AI that enables real-time, autonomous adaptation of products – affects product performance in creative industries. Using sales data on 42,000+ video games, we find that ARI increases the likelihood of success, but contingent on product design novelty. ARI increases performance when deployed alongside familiar product designs but presents limited gains to highly novel designs where the coordination complexity associated with ARI implementation limits its benefits. Additional analyses show ARI increases user engagement and expands market reach; these effects also diminish with increasing novelty and under heightened coordination demands. Our findings underscore the importance of integrating ARI with the product’s design architecture and contribute to strategy research on AI-enabled product strategy, organizational coordination, and human–AI collaboration.

AI Stars and Human Talents: How Generative AI Shapes Audience Sorting in the Music Industry

STR
Fan Yang (Tsinghua University); Mingtao Xu (Tsinghua University)
The integration of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) into creative industries challenges conventional valuations of human capital. This study investigates how the revelation of AI-generated content created a sorting of audience in the music industry. Leveraging the natural experiment of a trending musician’s AI nature revelation, we employ a difference-in-differences strategy to estimate how audience engagement changes once they learn the act is AI-generated. Results reveal that disclosure triggers value for authenticity. Musicians in genres perceived as low in humanity experience a relative decline in engagement compared to those in high-humanity genres, with those more closely associated with AI suffering greater losses. Additionally, musicians with lower career capital are disproportionately affected. Our findings demonstrate AI's context-dependent impact and provide a framework for understanding evolving work structures in the arts.

When Do Communication Technologies Improve the Management of Innovation?

STR
Casidhe Horan Troyer (London Business School); Catherine Magelssen (University of South Carolina)
Managing geographically distributed R&D is a core organizational challenge for multinational enterprises (MNEs). Communication technologies (CTs) have long been viewed as important tools for addressing this challenge. Yet whether CT-enabled reductions in communication costs between management and R&D subsidiaries actually improve innovation outcomes has remained theoretically and empirically unresolved due to two factors. First, even the most advanced CTs are relatively limited in transmitting tacit knowledge, which is critical for key aspects of innovation management such as monitoring and knowledge-sharing. Second, CTs can enable tighter managerial control over the innovation process, potentially constraining the initiative and creativity that innovation requires. We theorize that whether a CT improves R&D subsidiary innovation depends on organizational conditions that mitigate these potential constraints. We test our theorized contingency framework using a unique dataset linking R&D subsidiaries to their managing entities, and exploit the staggered rollout of broadband as an exogenous shock to the introduction of a new CT. We find that broadband introduction increases R&D subsidiary innovation, but only when (1) the managing entity and R&D subsidiary co-invented prior to treatment, helping overcome the CT’s tacit knowledge limitations, and (2) the managing entity’s span of control is high, reducing the likelihood of increased managerial intervention. Supplemental analyses support the proposed mechanisms. These findings show that CTs alone are insufficient to improve innovation. Instead, organizational context shapes whether firms realize the benefits—and minimize the negative consequences—of technology-enabled reductions in communication frictions.

When Do Digital Tools Buffer Firms? Platform Dependence and Performance under Shocks

STR
Shih-Ching Lin (National Taiwan University)
Digital transformation is often assumed to enhance organizational resilience, yet it remains unclear when internal digital tools function as effective resource buffers—and when their value is constrained by external dependence. Drawing on Resource Dependence Theory (RDT), this study examines how internally embedded digital tools shape firm performance under conditions of exogenous shocks. Using monthly transactional data from 800 restaurant firms in Taiwan between 2020 and 2022, we analyze how the use of SaaS-based point-of-sale (POS) systems affects firm performance during the COVID-19 pandemic. We find that internal digitalization positively influences performance, and this effect is amplified as external shocks intensify. However, the buffering role of digital tools is significantly weakened when firms become increasingly dependent on external platforms or rely heavily on promotional concession strategies. These findings advance RDT by conceptualizing digital tools as conditional resource buffers whose effectiveness depends on evolving dependence structures. More broadly, the study contributes to strategy and organization research by showing that platform-mediated dependence can erode the strategic value of internal digital infrastructures. [Keywords] Resource Dependence Theory; Digital Transformation; Platform Dependence
