Session overview

This professional development workshop focuses on the effective integration of simulations into international business education to deepen student engagement, enhance experiential learning, and develop globally relevant managerial skills. Drawing on the experiences of faculty members at an international business school, the session examines how simulations can be used to model cross-border decision-making, global competition, and institutional complexity in undergraduate, MBA, and executive education settings. Presenters will share concrete examples of simulation-based teaching across international business topics, including global strategy, international finance, operations, and cross-cultural management. The workshop emphasizes how simulations can help students grapple with uncertainty, interdependence, and trade-offs that characterize real-world international business environments. Faculty will discuss both digital and live simulations, highlighting design choices related to learning objectives, assessment, and facilitation across diverse student cohorts. In addition to showcasing successful applications, the session addresses practical challenges such as aligning simulations with course outcomes, managing student preparation and debriefing, balancing competition and collaboration, and ensuring inclusive participation across cultural backgrounds. Participants will engage with frameworks for selecting, adapting, and evaluating simulations to fit different institutional contexts and class formats. By combining faculty experience with structured discussion, the workshop equips participants with actionable strategies for integrating simulations into international business curricula. Attendees will leave with a clearer understanding of how simulation-based pedagogy can strengthen international business education by fostering experiential learning, systems thinking, and global managerial competence.
