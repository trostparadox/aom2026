Regulatory Governance of Omnichannel Marketing Through Third-Party Interventions

CM
Tingyu Lu (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Ruoyi Wang (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Kaiwen Bai (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
Applying the essential logic of the institutional theory, this paper investigates the impact of channel multiplicity on horizontal conflict and vertical conflict and examines the moderating roles of legal and political entities’ third-party interventions. Taking the omnichannel retailing practices of Chinese manufacturers as the research context and manufacturers as the research perspective, this study constructs a research framework for omnichannel conflicts. The findings are as follows: first, channel multiplicity intensifies both horizontal and vertical conflicts. While horizontal conflict improves firm performance, vertical conflict diminishes it. Second, third-party interventions by legal entities weaken the positive impact of channel multiplicity on horizontal conflict but exacerbate its effect on vertical conflict. Third, third-party interventions by political entities aggravate the positive impact of channel multiplicity on horizontal conflict but mitigate its effect on vertical conflict. Furthermore, the study finds that third-party interventions by legal entities reduce both horizontal and vertical conflicts, whereas interventions by political entities tend to intensify both types of conflict. The article concludes by discussing the research findings in terms of their theoretical contributions, practical implications, and limitations.
