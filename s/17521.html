Session overview

This symposium advances our understanding of remote work as a complex organizational phenomenon with heterogeneous effects that depend on policy design, organizational context, and individual circumstances. The symposium brings together five papers that examine remote work from complementary perspectives, employing diverse methodological approaches and theoretical frameworks. The papers collectively investigate how remote work arrangements affect time allocation and household labor division, how employees' psychological contracts shape their responses to changing organizational policies, how work modality influences the formation of workplace relationships and social networks, and how remote work impacts gender pay differentials. By examining these questions through quantitative analyses of large-scale survey data, qualitative investigations of employee experiences, and experimental manipulations in controlled settings, this symposium provides a comprehensive and nuanced understanding of the opportunities and challenges presented by the remote work revolution. The findings have important implications for organizational policy, suggesting that the effects of remote work depend critically on how flexibility is structured, how policies are communicated and implemented, and how work is organized to support both productivity and equity.
