Session overview

Teams often experience fleeting moments of extraordinary interpersonal alignment in which interaction feels effortless and shared energy emerges— phenomena commonly labeled as “chemistry.” Despite their intuitive importance, organizational theory lacks a coherent account of how such momentary sparks arise, why they sometimes consolidate into durable relational climates, and why they often dissipate. Existing research tends to privilege either momentary interactional processes or relatively stable team states, leaving the microfoundations of both amplification and fragility under-theorized. In this extended abstract, we introduce the VIBE–VIBING framework, centered on two novel constructs: Vital Interpersonal Behavioral Energy (VIBE) and Vital Interpersonal Bonding in Navigating Groupwork (VIBING). VIBE captures momentary, co-experienced interpersonal alignment, whereas VIBING reflects an emergent team-level climate that develops when such moments recur. Drawing on affective events theory, interpersonal synchrony, psychological safety, relational coordination, and emergence theory, we theorize a recursive dynamic through which episodic alignment either amplifies into durable bonding or decays through fragility. By distinguishing episodic alignment from emergent relational climates, the framework reframes team bonding as a dynamic, temporally embedded process relevant to contemporary, fluid forms of work.
