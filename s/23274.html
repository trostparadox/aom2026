Aligning Capabilities for Technology Transfer and New Ventures Formation

ENT
André NANA (IDRAC Business School)
Entrepreneurial sourcing, understood as a collaborative and ecosystem-based preincubation process, plays a critical role in enabling technology transfer through new venture creation, yet remains weakly theorized regarding how heterogeneous ecosystem actors coordinate their contributions and expectations at early stages. Drawing on a co-specialization perspective, this study examines how Entrepreneurial Support Organizations (ESOs) and a Technology Transfer Office (TTO) orchestrate complementary and non-substitutable resources during entrepreneurial sourcing. Based on an abductive qualitative study conducted in the Occitanie region (France), involving 53 interviews and observations centered on a public technology incubator and its ecosystem partners, we apply the thematic analyze of actors’ contributions, roles, and reciprocal expectations across a quintuple helix structure. The findings theorize co-specialization as a commitment-based coordination mechanism through which scientific assets, entrepreneurial talent, market knowledge, legitimacy, and funding are progressively aligned during preincubation. The study advances ecosystem and technology transfer research by opening the black box of early-stage orchestration and offers actionable implications for intermediaries and policymakers.

The Role of Social Persuasion and Career Plateaus in Employee Transition to Entrepreneurship

ENT
Hui Jiang (University of Science and Technology of China); Xiujuan Wang (Nanjing Audit University); Lixin Chen (University of Science and Technology of China); Ziwei Peng (University of Science and Technology of China); Min Li (University of Science and Technology of China)
Prior entrepreneurial studies have primarily focused on the role of internal drivers and external disruptions in the transition from paid employment to entrepreneurship, yet the interpersonal influence of significant others is underexplored. Rooted in social cognitive career theory, we aim to examine whether and when entrepreneurial social persuasion (from significant others) links to employees’ entrepreneurial discovery behaviors through self-efficacy and investigate career plateaus as the crucial boundary condition. We test the conceptual model using a time-lagged three-wave survey with full-time employees (final sample=241) in the Chinese context. The results showed that entrepreneurial social persuasion motivates employees to engage in discovery behavior, and this relationship is partly mediated by entrepreneurial self-efficacy. More importantly, we found that the direct effect of social persuasion on discovery behavior is stronger for employees who experienced hierarchical plateaus, and their indirect effect via entrepreneurial self-efficacy is stronger for employees who encountered job content plateaus. This study has important theoretical and practical implications in entrepreneurial career transitions.

Breaking Up (or Not): Entrepreneurs’ Relational Work Under Stakeholder Tension

ENT
Vanessa Lind (Stockholm School of Economics); Syeda Nimra Batool (Royal Holloway - University of London); Petra Andries (Ghent University); Stefanie Bröring (Ruhr University Bochum)
New ventures rely on diverse stakeholders to access critical resources, knowledge, and legitimacy. However, as relationships evolve, misaligned goals, shifting priorities, and uncertainty about future value of the relationship often generate tensions that can strain collaborations. In this study, we explore how entrepreneurs navigate such tensions and why similar tensions can lead to different disengagement outcomes. Drawing on qualita-tive data from 106 stakeholder relationships across eight university spin-offs, we induc-tively develop a framework illustrating how tensions trigger relational work. Our findings reveal that not all tensions result in relationship dissolution. Entrepreneurs often seek to reposition strong stakeholder ties by redefining the collaboration rather than terminating it. In contrast, weak ties particularly those characterized by interpersonal or process ten-sions are more likely to be dissolved. This study advances research on entrepreneurial network by shifting attention from tie formation to tie management and dissolvement. We show that network evolution is not merely additive but involves deliberate tie recon-figuration as entrepreneurs address tensions in ongoing stakeholder relationships. By dif-ferentiating types of tensions and corresponding relational work by entrepreneurs, we un-cover the micro-level mechanisms at relationship level that drive network evolution in entrepreneurial contexts.

Navigating Shadows of Legitimacy: Social Network Perspective on Ethical Duality in Entrepreneurship (WITHDRAWN)

ENT
Sidhant Bansal (Bocconi University)
Entrepreneurial success often presents a paradoxical ethical duality: founders must appear legitimate and “do good,” yet may also feel pressure to “do whatever it takes” in uncertain and resource-constrained environments. This conceptual paper explores how entrepreneurs navigate the bright and dark sides of ethical conduct through the lens of their social networks. Drawing on social network theory and business ethics, we explore the constituent elements of network influence: the type of ties entrepreneurs build, the roles these ties play, the structural patterns of their network and the resulting ethical outcomes. We then introduce a theoretical framework in which an entrepreneur’s network characteristics: such as cohesive ties, diverse bridges, brokerage positions, and network dynamics, shape their legitimacy-building strategies along a continuum from principled (bright) to questionable (dark) behaviors. We propose that tightly knit, high-closure networks can create an “ethical shield” that conceals misconduct, whereas diverse and brokered networks introduce novel opportunities but also exposure to ethically ambiguous norms. High network churn may enable founders to adapt or justify moral compromises under pressure. By synthesizing insights on social capital, moral cognition, and paradox theory, we offer four propositions that illuminate the underexplored role of networks in entrepreneurial ethical decision-making. We argue that different combinations of these elements give rise to distinct entrepreneurial network topologies: including Ethical Legitimizers, Boundary Navigators, and Strategic Concealers, each representing a characteristic mode of balancing principled and expedient legitimacy-seeking behaviors. This work contributes to entrepreneurship and business ethics literature by shifting attention from individual cognition to the relational architectures that scaffold moral action, conceptualizing how social networks influence ethical outcomes, and aims to provides practical insights for entrepreneurs and stakeholders to foster integrity in venture development.

Unowned Resources: Relational Resources in Entrepreneurial Judgment Theory

ENT
Mark D. Packard (Florida Atlantic University); Enimehen Augusta Ulinmhen (Florida Atlantic University)
Entrepreneurial judgment theory (EJT) depicts entrepreneurship as the dynamic allocation of owned resources in productive activities. We challenge this narrow conception of resources, acknowledging as ‘resources’ unowned (and unownable) assets: social capital, legitimacy, brand identity, etc. Acknowledging these “relational resources” requires an expansion of the EJT to clarify how entrepreneurs exercise judgment across heterogeneous resource types. Building on the EJT’s foundations in the resource-based view and resource orchestration schol-arship, we theoretically distinguish the judgment-based governance of owned resources, gov-erned through property rights and managerial control, and relational resources such as social capital and legitimacy, that are socially conferred, recognition-based, and jointly controlled. We argue that these resource types impose distinct evaluative demands— whereas owned re-sources are governed by a logic of control and design, relational resources require a relational governance expressed in the building and management of access, alignment, negotiation, and sustaining. By articulating how these judgment logics differ and complement one another, we extend the EJT—as a theory of entrepreneurship and as an entrepreneurial theory of the firm—beyond its traditional focus on owned assets and incorporate the social embeddedness of entre-preneurial resource mobilization. Our framework highlights how entrepreneurs navigate both control-oriented and relation-oriented evaluations under uncertainty and provides a foundation for theorizing how heterogeneous resources jointly shape venture emergence, credibility, and growth. Keywords: Entrepreneurial judgment, resource-based view, resource orchestration, relational resources, legitimacy, social capital, uncertainty
