Session overview

International management research, and management research more broadly, increasingly depends on cross-border collaboration, access to geographically dispersed data and field sites, and engagement with scholarly communities spanning institutions and countries. However, collaboration networks remain hub-dominated and structurally unequal, with a small number of highly connected institutions capturing disproportionate visibility. At the same time, co-authorship has become the default mode of knowledge production, making network entry and collaboration capability central to early-career publishing trajectories. This PDW is for early-career researchers who aim to plan and execute research visits to leading business schools as a practical strategy for international network entry and capability building. The workshop provides actionable guidance on identifying and approaching hosts, articulating a credible value proposition as a visitor, and designing a mutually beneficial agenda anchored in concrete deliverables. The 120-minute session uses a peer-to-peer format designed for candid exchange among equals. It begins with a short introduction outlining a step-by-step “visit design” roadmap, continues with a panel of early-career researchers who have completed research visits and sustained collaborations afterward, and concludes with an off-podium open discussion with additional discussants. Participants will be able to discuss their own visit plans, anticipate common pitfalls, and leave with practical strategies to reduce network distance to influential research communities and sustain collaborations beyond the visit.
