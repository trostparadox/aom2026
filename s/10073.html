When Code Writes the Coder: Productivity, Creativity, and the Future of Vibe Coding

Authors: Leonard Boussioux University of Washington; Ziyi Zhao Florida International University

Session overview

Generative AI (GenAI) has lowered the technical barriers to software creation, enabling individuals with limited coding expertise to build functional artifacts through natural language, a practice known as "vibe coding." While this democratization promises speed and accessibility, it brings development to populations whose behaviors and success factors remain poorly understood. When novices can ship software through conversation alone, what human qualities enable successful outcomes, and when does ease of creation compromise quality or originality? This study examines how human-AI agency configurations shape creation processes and output quality in AI-assisted software development. Drawing on theories of delegation and agentic AI, we argue that quality depends not on AI capability alone, but on how humans enact, retain, or relinquish control as task demands evolve. We test this through three lab experiments with 85 participants using Lovable, a popular vibe coding platform: benchmark replication (recreating a target webpage), open-ended creation (building a promotional site from scratch), and improvement (enhancing the quality, accessibility, and design of an existing baseline). Across all studies, we capture fine-grained behavioral traces through think-aloud protocols combined with screen and audio recording. This multimodal process data reveals recurring patterns in how individuals delegate, reclaim, and recalibrate control over GenAI. We also analyze diversity in outputs and problem-solving approaches to understand how different agency configurations lead to varied creative pathways. By theorizing vibe coding as dynamic collaboration rather than efficiency gain, this research offers a process-based account of when and why AI-assisted creation succeeds.
