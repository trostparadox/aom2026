Session overview

The Doctorate of Business Administration (DBA) programmes across the world are becoming increasingly attractive, driven by the inspiration of experienced managers seeking to address complex real-world problems. However, the DBA represents a distinct epistemological model from the PhD one. While the PhD aims to train future academics on how to fill theoretical gaps, the DBA is a professional practice doctorate designed to generate "actionable knowledge" anchored in rigorous applied research. This PDW addresses a critical faculty development need: How to effectively supervise senior executives during their DBA journey. Drawing on recent literature on phenomenon-based theorizing and on rigorous applied research, this practical masterclass reframes the DBA output as research that identifies important business phenomena and provides managerial implications derived from actionable theories and concrete levers for action. First, participants will collectively reflect on the shift in supervision posture required for DBA theses. Second, an associate editor of the Academy of Management Perspective will discuss how to evaluate rigorous applied research, and participants will then evaluate DBA proposals. Third, a panel discussion with a dean of business school and two DBA programmes directors will address the implications for programme management, faculty engagement and business school impact. The session concludes by establishing a global Community of Practice for DBA supervision, directly addressing the MED division's priorities regarding learning pathways, faculty development, and the impact of management education. This PDW Masterclass on DBA supervision is restricted to faculty eligible to supervise doctoral research.
