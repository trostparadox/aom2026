Impact of Feedback and Gender on Entrepreneurial Intentions Among Business Plan Competitors

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Shetu Ranjan Biswas Washington State University; Amrita Lahiri Washington State University; Leah Sheppard Washington State University

Session overview

Drawing from the extant entrepreneurship literature and role congruity theory, this research posits that negative feedback by judges in a business plan competition will be associated with reduced entrepreneurial intentions (EI) and lower willingness to continue with the proposed business (WTC), and that this relationship will be moderated by the gender of the entrepreneur such that the relationship will be stronger for women compared to men. Results from the repeated cross-sectional data (N =212) from an undergraduate business plan competition at a US public university indicated that negative feedback significantly reduces EI but not WTC. In contrast to earlier research, we did not find support for the idea that gender moderates the relationship between negative feedback on EI and WTC, respectively.
