Session overview

Items in the rearview mirror can appear larger than they are—yet when one becomes the subject of public scrutiny, the experience can feel immediate, personal, and destabilizing. Increasingly, faculty across disciplines are encountering organized challenges to their courses, pedagogical choices, or scholarly focus from external groups, political organizations, media outlets, and internal stakeholders. Despite the growing frequency of such incidents, many faculty report feeling underprepared to respond strategically, institutionally, and professionally when their teaching comes under attack. This PDW offers a candid, practice-informed examination of how faculty can navigate course-related attacks while protecting academic integrity, professional standing, and personal well-being. Grounded in the facilitator’s firsthand experience of being targeted, the workshop moves beyond retrospective storytelling to focus on decision-making frameworks, institutional navigation, and actionable response strategies. Participants will be guided through a structured process that includes: Identifying early signals that a course or faculty member is at risk Clarifying decision points and trade-offs under conditions of uncertainty Framing and sequencing questions posed to university leadership (e.g., legal counsel, communications, provost-level administration) Leveraging expertise, including crisis communication professionals, civic leaders, and legal. Distinguishing between reputational risk, legal exposure, pedagogical compromise, and personal safety concerns. The PDW emphasizes agency over reactivity, helping faculty assess what actions to take, when to escalate, when to document, and when to pause. Attention will also be given to how identity, power, and positionality may shape both the likelihood and consequences of being targeted. Designed for faculty at all career stages—as well as department chairs, program directors, and academic leaders—this workshop equips participants with practical tools, reflective frameworks, and peer-informed insights to respond with clarity rather than panic when teaching comes under fire.
