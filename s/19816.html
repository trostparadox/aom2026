Discursive Competence and Consensus Building in Emergency Multi-Departmental Meetings

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 2

Authors: Yuan Zheng Tsinghua University; Tiancheng Cao; Xiaoli Lu

Session overview

This study examines how discursive interactions lead to effective consensus in cross-departmental coordination meetings. While existing research focuses on specific discourse strategies and their effects, it overlooks participants' differential competence in strategy use. We construct a measurement framework for discursive competence with two dimensions: issue-related expertise application and legitimacy construction. Using audio data from 8 simulated emergency management joint meetings, we employed large language models (DeepSeek-V3) to assist in text transcription, coding, and competence measurement. Drawing from systemic functional linguistics, we treat mutual reference between hierarchical levels—when participants mention or address other roles—as a key micro-element shaping discursive competence dynamics. Our findings reveal that vertical interaction quality, specifically the discursive competence enhancement triggered when subordinates reference superiors, is the critical factor distinguishing high and low consensus achievement. The legitimacy construction dimension is more readily influenced by reference behavior than the expertise application dimension and shows stronger correlation with consensus building. This research advances understanding of consensus formation mechanisms at the micro-interaction level and provides insights for optimizing cross-departmental coordination processes in emergency management contexts.
