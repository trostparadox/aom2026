Change Capacity Under Conditions of Multiple Simultaneous Changes

MC
Chase Reske (Western Michigan University)
Organizations rarely experience change as a single, bounded event. More often, leaders and consultants are expected to deliver results across portfolios of overlapping initiatives, for example, an enterprise system rollout alongside a restructuring, continuous improvement work, and a cultural shift. These efforts often occur simultaneously and draw on the same people, attention, and budget. Yet much of what we know about organizational change capacity (OCC) and readiness for change has been developed with more episodic, discrete change in mind. The literature offers limited guidance for situations where initiatives compete for the same resources and for employees’ sensemaking bandwidth. This doctoral consortium proposal outlines a two-phase dissertation design. Phase 1 is an integrated literature review that develops a multi-level, multi-temporal framework of change capacity under conditions of simultaneous change. Phase 2 is a multi-level, multi-case study of 2 to 3 organizations, each navigating at least three concurrent change initiatives. Data will include interviews across hierarchical levels, document analysis, and meeting observations, supplemented by brief pulse surveys to triangulate perceived readiness, change load, and psychological safety. Teaching Improvisation : Disputatio as a Method for Making Metarules Visible in Crisis

MC
Carole Bousquet (Management Consulting Division, AOM); Raphael DE VITTORIS (Clermont School of Business); Pierre El Haddad (MACH Consultants); Marc El Haddad (Case Western Reserve University)
This article examines how managers and learners develop improvisational competence under crisis conditions by engaging with, rather than abandoning, organizational metarules. Drawing on a qualitative analysis of a Disputatio-based Professional Development Workshop at the AOM 2024 Annual Meeting, we show how structured dialectical confrontation triggers argumentative improvisation, surfaces implicit metarules, and fosters collective reflexivity. Our findings reveal a recursive learning cycle in which metarules guide improvisation, improvisation generates experiential insights, and these insights subsequently reshape metarules. We develop three theoretical propositions clarifying when metarules accelerate or hinder improvisation and how reflexive dialogue enables their reconfiguration. Pedagogically, we introduce Disputatio as an epistemic method capable of cultivating paradoxical reasoning, cognitive decentering, and valuable improvisational learning. Practically, we propose ways organizations can engineer metarules, design minimal structures, and institutionalize reflexive rituals to strengthen crisis preparedness. The study advances improvisation theory and contributes a robust dialogical pedagogy for management education. Keywords: Disputatio; Management education; improvisation; metarules

A New Frontier of Openness: Governing Firm Participation in AI-Based Knowledge Ecosystems

MC
Manuel von Krosigk (ETH Zürich); Ann-Kristin Weiser (ETH Zürich); Georg von Krogh (ETH Zürich); Mathias Beck (University of Applied Sciences of the Grisons); Martin Wörter (ETH Zürich)
AI-enabled platforms increasingly rely on shared data to fuel continuous learning, intensifying the tension between openness and the protection of proprietary interests. Building on – and extending – Chesbrough’s open innovation perspective, we introduce the notion of AI-based knowledge ecosystems, understood as multi-firm, AI-enabled arenas in which participants co-create knowledge through shared data and ongoing model improvement, and examine how firms can contribute safely while preserving proprietary interests. Drawing on three illustrative ecosystem vignettes and a field study of an AI-enabled genomic analytics platform, we develop a governance framework for ecosystem orchestrators and a “safe contribution” guide for participating firms. The article explains how participation in AI-based knowledge ecosystems reshapes firm boundaries and reconfigures how firms create and capture value, extending the openness–appropriation tension at the heart of Chesbrough’s open innovation program in the context of continuously learning AI systems while offering actionable guidance.

Chaotic Crystallizations

MC
Raphael DE VITTORIS (Clermont School of Business); ELDA Nasho Ah-Pine (ESC Clermont Business School)
How do inter-organizational collectives emerge and coordinate action when no actor possesses sufficient authority, resources, or legitimacy to lead? This article develops a processual explanation of collective emergence in polycentric and turbulent contexts. Drawing on organizational theory, social movement studies, political science, and complexity theory, we introduce the concept of chaotic crystallization to explain how collective order arises without intentional design or prior organizational stabilization. Chaotic crystallization is theorized as a nonlinear mechanism through which (1) integrated polycentricity, (2) distributed feedback loops (including institutional responses and backfire effects) and (3) non-proportional amplification converge to produce (4) a bifurcation and (5) a temporary organizational attractor. This attractor stabilizes coordination through minimal organizational containers while remaining inherently fragile and reversible. Building on comparative insights from diverse empirical cases, we develop a formal process model linking initial conditions, dynamic mechanisms, and collective outcomes, and advance falsifiable theoretical propositions. We further show that institutions and state responses act as endogenous modulators shaping amplification, bifurcation, and post-crystallization trajectories. By conceptualizing collective order as emergent, temporary, and nonlinear, this article contributes to research on organizational emergence, polycentric governance, and collective action under conditions of uncertainty and turbulence.

Does the Public Project Formulation and Management System in Chile Create or Destroy Public Value?

MC
Edgar Rebolledo Toro (LICGP); Fredy Oyarzo (LICGP); Diego Manriquez-Robles (Rice University)
This paper is based on the results of a 31-month longitudinal study of municipalities in the La Araucanía region and the challenges they face in the formulation and management of public projects. The analysis is theoretically and methodologically grounded in management sciences. We employ a qualitative intervention research methodology with an epistemological foundation in generic constructivism. Within this framework, we pose the question: Does Chile’s public project formulation and management system create or destroy public value? To address this research question, the paper presents a contextual and theoretical analysis defining the concepts used in the methodological study, analyzes the findings, and offers improvement suggestions that can be applied to the formulation and management of public projects.
