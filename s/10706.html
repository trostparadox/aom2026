The Impact of a Life Crafting Intervention on Meaningfulness: A Randomized Controlled Study

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7
OB

People

Authors: Joé G. Leduc Concordia University; Kathleen Boies

Session overview

Meaning in work (MIW) and meaning in life (MIL) are widely recognized as central to employees’ well-being, yet relatively little is known about how to increase meaningfulness among workers. In this randomized controlled study, we tested the impact of a life crafting intervention on MIW and MIL among early-career workers and examined whether goal self-concordance, goal progress, and goal alignment moderated changes in meaningfulness over time. Workers (N = 171) were randomly assigned to a treatment group or a control group. The control group completed tasks with intervention-quality face validity. One-month postintervention, workers who completed the life crafting intervention displayed increases in MIL judgments and sense of purpose compared with the control group. Within the treatment group, changes in MIL judgments depended on workers’ degree of identified goal regulation and goal progress, such that higher levels of these moderators were associated with larger increases in MIL judgments. Across both groups, changes in MIW and MIL were moderated by changes in goal alignment, such that workers with greater increases in goal alignment showed larger increases in meaningfulness. Together, these findings highlight life crafting as an inexpensive and scalable way to increase MIL judgments and purpose among early-career workers.
