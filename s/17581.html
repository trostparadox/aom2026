Shared Ethical Leadership in Teams: A Network Approach

Authors: Christian Resick Drexel University; Mary Elizabeth Mawritz Drexel University; Madison Suzanne LaBella Marist College; Jason Kiker Drexel University; John Sutich Drexel University; Zainab Zaheer Drexel University

Session overview

Most extant scholarly work on ethical leadership has taken the common, top-down perspective of leadership research, suggesting that ethical leaders are moral managers who demonstrate ethics from a position of authority over their followers and therefore, have a substantial impact on their followers’ attitudes and behaviors. However, we suggest that ethical leadership can also function as a shared leadership construct. More specifically, we introduce the construct of shared ethical leadership to capture the emergence of a collective form of ethics-focused leadership among team members. Using a networks approach to leadership, we propose that the density of shared ethical leadership ties in teams represents a unique form of leadership that can have significant ramifications for how a team functions and works to achieve its goals. This paper discusses the construct’s nomological network and provides evidence supporting content validity, convergent validity, and discriminant validity.
