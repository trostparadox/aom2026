Fight or Flight: MNE Adaptation Strategy to Geopolitical Risk

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
IM

People

Authors: YI YING The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Xufei Ma The Chinese University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Geopolitical risk presents a growing challenge for multinational enterprises (MNEs), yet existing research often categorizes firm responses into two main strategies: exit or influence. This paper introduces a third, under-theorized response: operational adaptation, where firms adjust their subsidiary networks to sustain a viable presence under constraint. Using the U.S.-China trade war as an exogenous shock, I employ a difference-in-differences design to examine how Chinese MNEs adapt to the geopolitical risk. The findings reveal that firms in politically targeted industries were more likely to adapt, especially when facing significant environmental uncertainty or high host-country market dependence. By contrast, firms more connected to home-country government were less likely to adapt. These results highlight the constrained yet strategic nature of MNE adaptation under geopolitical risk, offering new insight into how firms reconfigure their subsidiary networks not for growth, but for persistence.
