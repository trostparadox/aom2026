Digital Technology Application, Digtal Innovation Performance, and Technological Diversity

TIM
Taoyong Su; Shuling liu (Tongji University); Yongheng Chen (Shanghai Normal University)
How digital technology application shapes innovation performance remains debated in the field of innovation management. Drawing on knowledge recombination theory, we investigate the nonlinear effects of application breadth and depth on digital innovation performance, using panel data from Chinese listed high-tech firms (2012–2023). The results reveal that both breadth and depth exhibit inverted U-shaped relationships with digital innovation performance, suggesting diminishing or even negative returns beyond a certain threshold. We further theorize and test the moderating role of firms’ technological diversity. The findings show that high technological diversity (encompassing both related and unrelated variety) differentiates the two relationships: it sharpens the inverted U-shape for application breadth by steepening the curve and advancing the turning point, while it flattens the curve for application depth, postponing the turning point. These insights contribute to digital innovation management research by clarifying the boundary conditions under which digital technology application shifts from being an enabler to a constraint of innovation, and they offer actionable insights for managing knowledge portfolios in digital transformation.

From Constraint to Catalyst: How Data Privacy Regulation Reshapes Firm Performance

TIM
Ke Rong (Tsinghua University); Fei Hao (University of International Business and Economics); Huiyi Litan (Renmin University of China)
In the digital economy, data have become a critical source of firm competitiveness, yet compliance with data privacy regulation imposes substantial costs on businesses. How firms should strategically respond to such regulation therefore constitutes an important research question. This paper develops a theoretical model to examine firms’ strategic responses to privacy regulation. The model predicts that stringent privacy regulation reduces firm revenue while potentially stimulating firm innovation as an unintended consequence. We then empirically examine the effects of data privacy protection policies on firm revenue and innovation using a difference-in-differences (DiD) design. Focusing on China’s “Double Lists” policy on personal information protection, we find that the policy has a significant negative impact on firm revenue but a positive effect on firm innovation. Moreover, the revenue effects are heterogeneous: product content maturity and firm market power mitigate the adverse impact of privacy regulation on firm revenue. Together, these findings provide novel evidence on the economic consequences of data privacy regulation and offer important implications for policymakers seeking to balance privacy protection with firm performance and innovation incentives.

It Takes Two to Tango: How Open Government Data Affords SMEs Digital Innovation

TIM
Ruiyu Bruce Liu (Nanjing University); Jichang Zhang (Shenzhen Polytechnic University); Tao Wang (emlyon business school); Ziqi Zhang (Nanjing University); Zhong Yang (Nanjing University)
Drawing on technological affordance theory, this paper investigates the impact of openness of open government data (OGD) on SMEs’ digital innovation. We argue that the salient signifier of OGD can trigger SMEs’ engagement and the actualization of a broader set of action possibilities. Specifically, greater openness of OGD can encourage SMEs to leverage the data access and undertake a greater amount and diversity of digital innovation. Furthermore, SMEs’ digital expertise and government dependence regulate how SMEs perceive action possibility differently. The main effects are weakened when SMEs have greater digital expertise, and magnified when SMEs depend more on governments. Using a unique manually-collected dataset combining the information on OGD from China between 2012 and 2024 and publicly listed SMEs, findings largely lend support to our theoretical argument. Our approach uncovers the role of OGD in bridging digital divide as an emerging digital infrastructure and explicates the mechanism of how to design affordance.
