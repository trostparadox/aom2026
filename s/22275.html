Session overview

As Generative AI matures, the technological frontier in education is shifting from passive Large Language Models (LLMs) to autonomous "AI Agents." While standard chatbots (e.g. ChatGPT or Copilot) require continuous prompting to function, AI Agents are goal-oriented systems capable of planning, retaining memory, and executing multi-step pedagogical tasks with minimal human intervention. This shift presents profound opportunities and risks for management education. This EdTech Application Session provides a technical and pedagogical framework for integrating AI Agents into the management curriculum. Moving beyond simple prompt engineering, this workshop demonstrates how to architect agents that serve as persistent role-play partners, Socratic tutors, or simulation adversaries. Participants will engage in a hands-on design sprint, utilising low-code tools to prototype an agent with specific "guardrails," knowledge bases, and behavioural personas tailored to their specific teaching needs (e.g., a Negotiation Opponent or a Strategy Consultant). Crucially, this session prioritises realism over hype. We will critically examine the limitations of current agentic frameworks, addressing hallucination risks, the costs of API dependency, and the ethical implications of anthropomorphising software in a learning environment. Participants will leave with a deployable "Agent Blueprint" and the technical know-how to configure environments that enhance, rather than replace, critical thinking. We will also reflect upon the future development of AI agents, as well as considerations on their potential impact on the HE sector. Participant evaluations are invaluable.
