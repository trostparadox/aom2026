Institutional Entrepreneurs, Coopetition Capabilities and International Market Rule-Making

Authors: Tomonari Komiyama; Kenji NAGASATO University of Hyogo; Yasuhiro EZAKI

Session overview

This study investigates why firms and nations that achieve technological superiority often fail to convert it into sustained international market leadership. Moving beyond conventional explanations based on product quality, cost, or innovation speed, the study emphasizes the institutional dimension of international market rule making—such as standards, regulations, and certification systems—as a decisive factor shaping market access and competitive positioning. This study conceptualizes “institutional entrepreneurs” as central actors who design and construct market rules while institutional entrepreneurs manage paradoxical tensions between competition and cooperation among heterogeneous stakeholders across multiple fields. Employing a mixed-methods design, the study integrates qualitative interviews and documentary analyses with quantitative panel data covering more than 1,000 Japanese firms, complemented by in-depth case studies. Through iterative analysis, the study identifies four interrelated coopetition capabilities that enable institutional entrepreneurs to lead international rule-making processes: (1) integrating long-term national strategic visions with short-term tactical maneuvering in negotiations; (2) sustaining continuous, high-frequency communication across multiple competitive and collaborative fields; (3) cognitively reframing advanced technologies as national strategic and international public goods; and (4) institutionalizing temporary international agreements into durable rule systems. The study proposes a new theoretical framework that positions these coopetition capabilities as necessary conditions for transforming technological advantage into institutional and market leadership. The study also advances institutional entrepreneurship theory and offers policy and managerial implications for national strategies aimed at securing dominance in international market rule making.
