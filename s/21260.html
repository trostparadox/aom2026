Partnership Configurations for the Achievement of Higher Levels of Maturity in Healthtech Startups

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM

Authors: Leandro Lima Santos University of São Paulo; Rafael Morais Pereira University of Sao Paulo; Felipe Mendes Borini University of Sao Paulo; Moacir De Miranda Oliveira

Session overview

This study investigates how distinct partnership configurations contribute to the advancement of healthtech startups across stages of business maturity. Specifically, it examines which combinations of partnerships increase the likelihood of reaching more advanced maturity stages, such as operation, traction, or scaling, compared to earlier stages such as ideation and validation. Based on a database with information from 3,005 Brazilian startups, we performed an empirical investigation using the Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA) technique to identify what are the partnership combinations that may promote the achievement of higher levels of business maturity in a sample of 181 healthtech startups. The results indicate that healthtech startup maturity is not determined by the presence of all potential actors, but by specific, coherent, and contingent partnership configurations aligned with distinct technological and institutional development trajectories. Rather than depending on a single or ideal ecosystem structure, mature healthtech startups are linked to different combinations of present and absent partnerships involving academia, government, corporations, hubs, and civil society.
