Session overview

As technologies become embedded in critical infrastructures, innovation is increasingly shaped by safety scrutiny and accountability. This symposium examines how “risk signals” and governance changes—hazard certifications, mandated disclosure of adverse events, legitimacy-conferring breakthroughs, NGO collaborations that transmit pressure through supply chains, and shifts in regulatory form—redirect firms’ real and inventive activity. Across five papers spanning chemicals, medical devices, biomedicine, green supply chains, and regulatory text for green technologies, we show that scrutiny operates as an information-and-accountability shock whose effects are heterogeneous, path-dependent, and networked. Rather than uniformly increasing or decreasing R&D, scrutiny primarily drives substitution, portfolio reallocation, and technological trajectory change, conditioned by organizational design (e.g., integration vs legal separation), legal institutions (liability and disclosure regimes), and interorganizational ties. Together, the symposium offers a unified framework for understanding the emerging governance microfoundations of innovation in high-stakes domains.
