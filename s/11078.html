Slow Thinking Fast: How AI Trumped Human Bias

Author: Tina Shah Paikeday Claremont Graduate University

Session overview

Cognitive bias in behavioral decision-making has been a persistent problem in high stakes social decisions, where fairness matters, but practical interventions such as unconscious bias training have failed. In the context of employment, perceived bias results in thousands of discrimination claims each year. This research introduces search theory to explain why skill proxies are used to inform unconscious decisions when the cost of search is high. Although a majority of recruiters have adopted artificial intelligence (AI) to increase search productivity, current empirical evidence demonstrates that algorithmic decision-making accelerates cognitive bias when biased data trains algorithms. However, the extant literature grounded in dual process theory explains that AI can instead overcome bias by activating slow, evidence-based decisions. The research also provides rare empirical evidence of a paradox that the positive relationship between biased AI and cognitive bias reverses to negative when AI is designed free of data and algorithmic bias. A lab-in-the field between subjects experiment was conducted with 52 recruiting experts who evaluated a total of 224 shortlisted candidates in board, executive and professional level searches. Initial results were analyzed using ANOVA to compare measures of cognitive bias produced by AI with inherent data and algorithmic bias and AI which was designed with debiasing principles. This research is critical in providing empirical evidence that AI can be used in practice as a novel bias interruptor in employment outcomes but also in other high stakes social decisions such as consumer lending and policing.
