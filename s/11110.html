Remote Managers’ Role Stressors and Supervisory Outcomes

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

People

Authors: Nanhee Kim University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign; YoungAh Park University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign; Jun-Yeob Kim University of Central Florida

Session overview

As remote work substantially alters traditional patterns of work and communication, it creates a new managerial context. However, existing remote work research has predominantly focused on employees, leaving limited understanding of the challenges managers face in leading remote workers and how they respond to these challenges. To address this gap, we first conceptualize and define remote managers’ role stressors (RMRS) and develop and validate its measure. Next, we draw upon transactional theory of stress to examine the effects of RMRS on three supervisory outcomes—micromanagement, attitude toward remote work, and intentions to allow remote work—via perceived control. Across two field survey studies (N1 = 225, N2 = 376), managers experiencing high RMRS reported lower perceived control over remote management, which in turn was associated with greater micromanagement, less favorable attitude toward remote work, and reduced intentions to allow subordinates’ remote work. Overall, these findings identify RMRS as a unique stressor for managers in remote work contexts and elucidate the cognitive mechanism underlying supervisory behaviors and attitudes.
