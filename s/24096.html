The Price of Virtue: Populism and the Market Backlash Against ESG

IM
Nikolaus Niebuhr (WU Vienna); Thomas Lindner (Copenhagen Business School)
Populism has emerged as a central force reshaping interactions between business, governments, and society. While environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices are widely regarded as beneficial for MNEs, their meaning and market valuation may shift under populism. Drawing on Social Identity Theory (SIT), we examine how populist contexts alter investor responses to ESG engagement by reframing corporate behavior through identity-based narratives. Using an event-study design, we analyze over 50,000 firm-election observations from more than 15,000 firms around national elections in nine advanced democracies between 2007 and 2022. We integrate ESG ratings, populism measures, and firm-level financial, internationalization, and macroeconomic data. Our results reveal an ESG backlash under populism: firms with stronger ESG profiles experience more negative abnormal returns as populist rhetoric intensifies. We find that populist ideology plays a limited role, whereas a firm’s degree of internationalization conditions the strength of the ESG backlash. Overall, our study answers the call for greater integration of Social Psychological theory into International Management research by showing how populist identity politics reshape the social meaning and valuation of ESG.
