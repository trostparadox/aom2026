Session overview

In academia, the definition of “success” is often narrowly focused on publications, grants, and career progression. Yet, many academics struggle with well-being, work-life balance, and a sustainable sense of purpose. Using the results of a recent study (Academic Career Sustainability: Psychological Foundations of Career Satisfaction) this interactive, 90-minute workshop invites academic participants to reflect on their own definitions of success, explore personal values, and prototype alternative future pathways. Grounded in Design Thinking (Burnett & Evans, 2016), Career Construction Theory (Savickas, 2013), and Positive Psychology (Seligman, 2011), the session offers practical tools for personal reflection and peer exchange—fostering agency, meaning, and flourishing in academic careers. Using these tools, participants can address dissatisfaction in academia and understand their frustration is not primarily a deficit of motivation, resilience, or individual coping capacity.
