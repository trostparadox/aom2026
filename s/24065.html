Additive Manufacturing-Enabled Relocations: Exploring the Impacts on Supply Chain Resilience

OSCM
Alessio Ronchini (Politecnico di Milano); Albachiara Boffelli (University of Bergamo); Lydia Bals (Mainz University of Applied Sciences); Hamid Moradlou (University of Warwick); Ronan McIvor (University of Ulster)
Based on 26 interviews with 14 informants, this study examines how Additive Manufacturing (AM) adoption influences relocation decisions, specifically reshoring and distributed manufacturing, and how these AM-enabled reconfigurations contribute to the development of supply chain (SC) resilience capabilities. Overall, the study contributes to the literature by: demonstrating empirically how AM adoption and relocation decisions jointly shape SC resilience; providing a detailed mapping of the resilience capabilities developed through AM-based SC redesign; and introducing a panarchy-based framework that explains how resilience evolves across organizational and SC levels in AM-enabled contexts. Moreover, the panarchy-based framework outlines how firms develop across adaptive cycle stages as AM triggers internal capability building, disruption response, and the eventual reorganization of supply chain structures.
