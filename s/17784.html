Session overview

Scholars increasingly examine why idea generators and evaluators often disagree about the value or usefulness of ideas, usually focusing on either ideator or evaluator biases or (Fuchs et al. 2019; Lazar et al. 2022; Mueller & Yin, 2021). However, bias need not occur for actors to arrive at different conclusions; creators and evaluators may have different goals, roles, norms, resources, requirements, and criteria that generate contrasting evaluations. Recognizing this, more papers—including those in this symposium—focus on the dynamics and differences between idea generators and evaluators without necessarily assessing bias or blame. This approach is constructive for novelty evaluation where bias can be difficult to assess—we may not know a priori whether a new idea is a bust or a blockbuster, whether rejection (or support) is wisdom or foolishness. But we can identify when creators and evaluators disagree and rigorously examine that disagreement’s causes and potential resolutions. The goal of this symposium is to provide insights into why and when generators and evaluators see differently (or less differently) and its implications for organizations, individuals, and theoretical advances. Specific topics include motive alignment, creator and manager roles, rule-breaking creators and rule-adhering evaluators, cognitive differences between creator and evaluator, and different criteria and meaning for output for creators vs. non-creators.
