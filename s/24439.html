From Required to Voluntary: Unpacking the Transformation Mechanism of Employee Green Behavior

OB
Jian Zhang (University of Science and Technology Beijing); Ruibing Fu (University of Science and Technology Beijing); Na Zhang (Beijing Information Science and Technology University)
Employee green behavior (EGB), a core component of organizational environmental sustainability, comprises required EGB—common, compliance-driven behaviors—and voluntary EGB, which is spontaneous and enduring. Although prior research has mainly examined the antecedents of voluntary behaviors, it has largely overlooked how required behavior transforms into voluntary behavior. Drawing on organismic integration theory within self-determination theory, this study develops a motivational internalization transformation model to investigate how required EGB promotes voluntary EGB through the internalization of green motivation, and further tests the moderating role of self-enhancement values. Using two multi-wave survey samples (Study 1: N = 629; Study 2: N = 148), the findings show that required EGB positively predicts voluntary EGB; the internalization of green motivation mediates this relationship; and self-enhancement values negatively moderate the effect of required behavior on motivational internalization, such that high self-enhancement values weaken this internalization process. This study reveals the transformation mechanism through which employee green behavior shifts from controlled to autonomous, extends the application of organismic integration theory to organizational green behavior, and offers theoretical and managerial insights for fostering employees’ transition from compliance to proactive engagement.
