Bad News, Different Minds: Effects of Human vs. AI Negative Feedback on Employee Role Performance

OB
Guanglei Zhang; Yanan Xu (Wuhan University of Technology); Ying Zhou (Wuhan University of Technology)
Negative performance feedback is a critical yet paradoxical managerial practice. Although intended to correct performance deviations, it often triggers adverse psychological reactions that undermine employee performance. With the growing use of artificial intelligence (AI) in performance management, negative feedback is increasingly delivered by AI systems alongside human managers, raising questions about whether feedback from different sources operates through distinct psychological mechanisms. Drawing on Feedback Intervention Theory (FIT) and adopting an attention-based perspective, this study develops a dual-path model to examine how negative performance feedback from human managers versus AI influences employees’ role performance. We propose that human-delivered negative feedback is more likely to activate meta processes characterized by self-focused attention, whereas AI-delivered negative feedback is more likely to activate task processes oriented toward task improvement. By extending FIT to human–AI interaction contexts, this research advances process-oriented feedback theory and offers practical insights for designing more effective AI-enabled performance management systems.
