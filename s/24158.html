Human Trust in AI: A Meta-Analysis Across Cultures, Systems, and Domains

CTO
qinpu dang (Peking University); Guiquan Li (Peking University)
Human trust in artificial intelligence (AI) shapes whether capable systems are adopted, questioned, or resisted, yet evidence on its drivers remains fragmented across cultures, AI types, and application domains. We conducted a preregistered meta-analysis of 216 studies (n = 102,116) from 39 countries to estimate correlations between trust in AI, its antecedents, and its consequences. Perceived usefulness (? = 0.663), ease of use (? = 0.618), performance expectancy (? = 0.625), and effort expectancy (? = 0.514) were positively associated with trust, whereas perceived risk (? = -0.475) was negatively associated with trust. Trust, in turn, was positively associated with behavioural intention (? = 0.673). Although heterogeneity and prediction intervals were substantial, the directions of these associations were stable. Moderator and meta-regression analyses show that culture, AI type, and application domain do not reverse which cues support or undermine trust; instead, they systematically reweight their importance. For example, interactional ease matters more for chatbots than for robots, and usefulness and performance cues are more diagnostic in financial contexts than in educational ones. Together, these findings empirically ground contextualised AI: trust is governed by a common set of cues whose diagnosticity varies with culture, system form, and domain-specific stakes, providing benchmarks for theory, design, and governance in specific settings.
