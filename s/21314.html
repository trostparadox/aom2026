Reopening the Concept of Flourishing at Work: A Grounded Theory Study of Employees’ Lived Experience

Authors: Rachna Verma Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur; Srinivasan Sekar Indian Institute of Technology, Madras; Susmita Mukhopadhyay

Session overview

Although flourishing at work has attracted increasing scholarly attention, its conceptualization remains fragmented and largely shaped by theoretical assumptions rather than by systematic engagement with employees’ lived experience. This study adopts a constructivist grounded theory approach to inductively examine how employees understand and experience flourishing at work. We conducted 38 in-depth interviews with full-time knowledge workers across diverse industries and age groups in India, supplemented by a focus group with industry leaders and academic experts. Data collection and analysis proceeded iteratively across three phases using constant comparison and theoretical sampling. Our analysis reveals that employees do not experience flourishing as a stable psychological state but as a dynamic process shaped by what they seek to realize through work and by how organizational arrangements enable or constrain these pursuits. Participants’ accounts highlight multiple, interdependent values and show how these are continuously prioritized, traded off, and renegotiated over time. By reconstructing flourishing from lived experience, this study offers a process-oriented and contextually grounded conceptualization that provides a stronger foundation for future theory development and measurement.
