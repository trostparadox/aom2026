Session overview

Three big shifts are reshaping how work gets done: algorithm-human interaction in tasks is deepening, social and collaborative skills matter more, and where work is done is changing. While scholars and practitioners recognize these lasting shifts, our evidence-based understanding of how organizations should respond is still emerging. This hackathon-style PDW is designed to catalyze new research on these shifts in organization design, careers, and work. We use a flipped format: four panelists open with research “seeds,” for example, a sharp question in search of an empirical strategy, a method looking for management applications, or a novel dataset with underexplored potential. Participants then work in small groups with the panelists to brainstorm, match questions to data and methods, and sketch concrete next steps for publishable projects. By the end of the session, participants will have sharpened research ideas, identified new potential collaborators, and produced concrete next steps--such as draft research designs or data-method pairings--for studying how algorithms, people skills, and the shifting geography of work are transforming organizations.
