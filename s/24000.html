A Quadrant Framework of Resource Reconfiguration for Organisational Resilience

STR
Thomas Oyetunde Oladele (Woxsen University); Solomon Jeresa; Michael Oyenuga; Bolaji Akeem JIMOH
Modern organisations face a rapidly growing number of disruptive disturbances that are threatening their existence. Even though the current scholarship acknowledges the significance of resource reconfiguration in organisational resilience, it is not well defined how organisations reconfigure resources, the ability requirements of specific reconfiguration processes and the contextual conditions that define their application. Based on the Competing Values Framework and the theory of dynamic capabilities, this paper suggests a typology of resource-reconfiguration mechanisms and forms a theory of organisational transitions in response to disruptive change. We propose four reconfiguration mechanisms: Internal Resource Recomposition (bricolage, virtualization, and circularity), External Resource Renewal (strategic substitution, mergers, and architectural change), and External Resource Leveraging (complementary alliances, incremental outsourcing), and Internal Resource Leveraging (optimization, continuous improvement). These mechanisms are grouped (along two dimensions) in resource source and reconfiguration degree, and most organisations are strongly hypothesized to function in an internal-leveraging mode, until the disruption demands adaptation to an alternative mechanism. This framework is developed through six propositions; (1) there are specific reconfiguration pathways associated with distinct dynamic capabilities; (2) the strength of disruption moderates the suitability and effectiveness of each pathway; (3) organisational learning mediates the relationship between transition execution and capability development; and (4) quadrant shifting is a meta-capability enabling high-quality resilience. The study advances resource-based theory by specifying distinct reconfiguration mechanisms, extends dynamic capabilities research by linking specific capabilities to transition paths, and contributes to resilience literature by identifying resource-level mechanisms through which resilience is achieved during disruptive change.
