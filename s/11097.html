Session overview

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) has shifted from a peripheral organizational activity to a central feature of how organizations address ethical, social, and environmental challenges (Wilburn & Wilburn, 2014; Miles, 2023). This shift is especially salient as Generation Z (Gen Z) becomes a significant segment of the global workforce, with projections indicating that this cohort will comprise approximately one-third of the working-age population by 2030 (BLS, 2023). Prior research characterizes Gen Z as values-driven and socially conscious, with heightened expectations that organizations demonstrate authentic social responsibility (Deloitte Global, 2025; Estanyol, 2024). Despite this alignment, Gen Z has been associated with comparatively lower levels of work engagement than older cohorts, creating a paradox with implications for organizational effectiveness and social sustainability (George et al., 2024; Harter, 2024). Although CSR research has examined outcomes such as legitimacy and employer attractiveness, employees are often treated as a homogeneous group, and engagement is rarely examined as an internal motivational state shaped by CSR practices (Thang et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2025; Yassin & Beckmann, 2024). This pilot qualitative study uses a grounded theory approach to examine the relationship between CSR and work engagement among Gen Z employees, conceptualizing CSR as an internal organizational mechanism through which employees interpret organizational values and social commitments (Bolman & Deal, 2021; Shastri & Saha, 2025).
