Roadmap Visuals as Temporal Objects to Convey a Multiplicity of Quantum Futures

OMT
Lauri Horelli (Aalto University)
Future-oriented communications play a central role in the entrepreneurial process, helping not only to convince audiences to lend their support but also, more broadly, to make abstract futures more concrete and to energize audiences to move toward them. Such communications, however, are complex, as they demand a balance between appealing to diverse audiences and avoiding commitments that cannot be fulfilled. This study examines the future-oriented communications of quantum computing ventures, with a particular focus on communicative efforts that use “roadmap visuals” as the centerpiece. By embracing a multimodal semiotic approach, I introduce the pivotal role of these visuals as robust yet open-ended temporal objects that enrich persuasion and allow flexibility in communicating a multiplicity of future orientations: representing time as trajectory, kairos, contingency and teleology. The study offers a fresh perspective on the ubiquitous practice of roadmap communication in organizational life, showing how roadmap visuals function as malleable temporal objects and how they enable entrepreneurial actors to achieve temporal flexibility in nascent fields. Further, I introduce methodological novelty by applying a social semiotic approach to the study of temporal work.
