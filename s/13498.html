Management Support as a Boundary Condition for Pronatalist Policy Effectiveness

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 6
SIM

People

Author: SinHui Chong Nanyang Technological University, Singapore

Session overview

Persistently low fertility rates constitute a major social issue for many developed cities, with far-reaching implications for population sustainability and labor markets. Governments have responded with pronatalist policies that are implemented largely through organizations, such as family-friendly HR benefits. Drawing on Singapore’s extended shared parental leave policy rolled out in 2025, this research examines how nationally-induced HR policy changes relate to employees’ responses and intentions to have children, and when such policies are more effective. When policy changes are introduced, employees anticipate how coworkers’ use of extended parental leave may affect workload distribution within the organization. We argue that employees’ expected workload increase following the policy change is negatively related to their perceived valence of the policy change, which in turn predicts lower intentions to have children in the future. We further theorize that corporate management support for the policy change serves as a critical boundary condition that buffers this negative indirect relationship. Survey data collected from 582 full-time employees in Singapore in the week following the policy rollout support our hypotheses. This research contributes to social issues in management scholarship by highlighting how management support enhances pronatalist policy effectiveness.
