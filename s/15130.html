The Impact of Mask Wearing on Emotional Labor: Intrapersonal and Interpersonal Mechanisms

Authors: Ya Liu Wuhan University; Lakshita Boora University of South Florida; Peishan Wu Wuhan University; Russell Eric Johnson Michigan State University; Brent Scott Michigan State University

Session overview

Wearing face masks has become routine in many service occupations, yet its implications for emotional labor remain underexplored. Drawing on emotion regulation theory, we investigate how mask wearing shapes the effects of surface acting on work engagement and downstream outcomes, and through what intrapersonal and interpersonal mechanisms these moderation effects unfold. Across two field studies, we found mask wearing amplifies the detrimental relationship between surface acting and work engagement and strengthens its indirect effects on job satisfaction and work withdrawal. Further, psychological distance emerges as a key mechanism exacerbating the negative effects of surface acting. Overall, our research extends emotional labor literature by highlighting how and why mask wearing alters the effectiveness and costs of emotion regulation in the workplace.
