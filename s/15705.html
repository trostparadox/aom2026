Platform Strategy for Industrial Markets: New Venture Formation and Growth in a Manufacturing Firm

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Authors: Karl S.R. Warner University of Glasgow; Magdalena Marczewska University of Warsaw

Session overview

This qualitative study examines new venture formation and growth in a manufacturing firm that designed, governed, and orchestrated a platform strategy during its startup period, offering insight into how platform strategy emerges across industrial multi-sided markets. While platform research has largely focused on consumer-facing ecosystems, far less is known about how platform strategy unfolds in industrial contexts characterized by complex supply chains, advanced manufacturing practices, and specialized complementors. Industry 4.0 technologies – recognized as the Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR) – have accelerated the digital transformation of manufacturing sectors, creating new opportunities for platform ventures. Drawing on an in-depth case study of a pioneering industrial platform, the research investigates how platform governance, global hub locations, legitimization networks, and hybrid entry modes shaped the new venture’s formation and global reach. The findings show that industrial platform strategy relies on hybrid governance mechanisms that combine digital interfaces, global partnerships, and non-traditional entry modes. The study offers implications for understanding platform strategy beyond consumer markets and for new ventures seeking to capitalize on Industry 4.0 platform development.
