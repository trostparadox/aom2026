Advancing Financial Literacy in Developing Economies: A Responsible GenAI Perspective

Authors: Faiq Defiandry Qatar University; Ramzi El-Haddadeh Qatar University

Session overview

Digital financial services have played a central role in expanding financial access and inclusion, particularly in developing economies. More recently, generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) has emerged as a promising tool for enhancing financial literacy by offering conversational and personalized financial guidance. At the same time, the growing influence of GenAI in financial decision-making raises concerns related to bias, transparency, and accountability, underscoring the need for a Responsible AI (RAI) perspective. Despite its rapid adoption, empirical evidence on how responsible GenAI contributes to financial literacy remains limited. Addressing this gap, this study examines how the integration of responsible GenAI in financial services shapes financial literacy outcomes in developing economies. Drawing on the Information Systems Success Model (ISSM) and Social Cognitive Theory (SCT), the study develops a conceptual framework linking GenAI system characteristics to financial literacy through usage, satisfaction, and financial self-efficacy. Information quality, system quality, and service quality, captured through accuracy and explainability, adaptability and natural language interaction, and autonomy and responsiveness, are proposed as key drivers of GenAI-enabled financial learning. Using a positivist, survey-based research design, the proposed model will be tested in the context digital financial services in Indonesia. By integrating technological, cognitive, and responsible AI considerations, this study will advance the understanding of how GenAI can support financial literacy in resource-constrained settings and offers practical insights for policymakers, financial institutions, and technology developers seeking to design responsible, scalable, and inclusive GenAI-driven financial education solutions.
