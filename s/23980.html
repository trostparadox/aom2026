Corporate Governance at a Crossroads: 50 Years of Multidisciplinary Research

STR
Maria Goranova (University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee); Cynthia E. Clark (Bentley University); Yanqiu Ren (University of Barcelona); Marti Sagarra (University of Barcelona)
Interest in corporate governance has accelerated significantly, building an increasingly complex discipline. The purpose of this paper is to provide an integrative multidisciplinary review of corporate governance research with particular focus on interrelated but theoretically inconsistent streams in the collective body of accumulated knowledge. We first map the burgeoning corporate governance literature using a structured bibliometric analysis that allows us to take a “big picture” view of corporate governance interdependencies and complexity. We then contrast the cumulative buildup of corporate governance research with the proliferation of new perspectives, theories, and approaches. Juxtaposing foundational and new governance perspectives allows us to surface four governance paradoxes: (i) paradox of agency; (ii) paradox of accountability, (iii) paradox of ownership, and (iv) paradox of firms. By identifying points of contention leading to corporate governance puzzles and paradoxes, we hope that our review will be pertinent for the future paradigmatic development of the field.
