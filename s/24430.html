Micro Foundations of Academic Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Capability

ENT
Marie Louise Blankesteijn (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
Universities invest heavily in entrepreneurship education and innovation infrastructure, yet these investments often fail to translate into institution-wide entrepreneurial capability. While prior research emphasizes ecosystem structures, less is known about how bottom-up sensemaking shapes ecosystem emergence in universities. Drawing on an inductive qualitative study of a North European university—including interviews, survey data, and ecosystem documents—we identify three sensemaking mechanisms that shape entrepreneurial capability: identity–archetype misalignment, competence-construal drift, and ecosystem visibility gaps. Together, these mechanisms show that entrepreneurial ecosystem capability is not simply a structural attribute but an emergent, multilevel process grounded in shared interpretation. By theorizing how identity, competence, and visibility interact, the study advances understanding of the micro foundations of entrepreneurial ecosystems in university contexts.
