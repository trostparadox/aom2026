When Experts Meet AI: How Domain Knowledge Shapes Cognitive Adaptation to Generative AI

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 2
CTO

People

Author: Xiaotian Dai Warwick Business Schoool

Session overview

Generative AI is transforming knowledge work, yet its effects on professionals with varying expertise levels remain paradoxically inconsistent—some studies suggest AI democratizes capabilities for novices while others indicate experts gain disproportionate advantages. This study extends Technocognitive Structuration Theory (TCS) by introducing domain expertise as a critical boundary condition shaping cognitive adaptation to AI. We theorize that experts, possessing elaborate cognitive schemas, tend toward exploitative adaptation strategies reflecting cognitive entrenchment, whereas novices with more flexible schemas engage in exploratory adaptation that facilitates schema accommodation. A laboratory experiment with 100-120 programming students will examine how expertise influences interaction strategies with AI coding assistants across tasks of varying complexity. Participants will complete programming tasks while their AI prompts are logged and classified as exploratory or exploitative. Performance will be assessed through expert blind ratings. Using moderated mediation analysis, we examine whether task complexity amplifies divergent adaptation pathways between expertise levels. This research contributes theoretically by reconciling contradictory findings about AI's distributional effects and practically by informing differentiated training programs, team composition strategies, and AI system design that accommodate heterogeneous user expertise.
