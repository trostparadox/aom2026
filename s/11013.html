One Leader, Two Dimensions: Benevolence and Authoritarianism in Paternalistic Leadership

Authors: Jane Park Florida International University; Chockalingam Viswesvaran Florida International University

Session overview

Leaders are increasingly expected to balance decisiveness with empathy – enforcing standards while supporting followers’ well-being. Paternalistic leadership (PL) has been proposed as a style that integrates these dual demands by combining authority with individualized care, yet ambiguity remains regarding its dimensional structure and distinction from related leadership constructs, particularly in Western contexts. The present study reexamines the dimensionality and construct boundaries of PL by focusing on its two core components: benevolence and authoritarianism. Using confirmatory factor analysis, four models were compared, varying in whether PL dimensions were collapsed or conflated with leader-member exchange (LMX) or abusive supervision. Data were collected from 475 undergraduate business students who evaluated leader behaviors during a simulated team-based task. Results support a multidimensional conceptualization of PL. A four-factor model distinguishing benevolence, authoritarianism, LMX, and abusive supervision demonstrated acceptable fit and significantly outperformed alternative models. These findings clarify PL’s measurement structure and establish its construct distinctiveness.
