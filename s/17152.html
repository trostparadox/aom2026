The Polarizing Effects of Corporate Sociopolitical Activism

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 6

Authors: Tiziana Schopper Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich; Lukas Vogelgsang Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich

Session overview

Corporate sociopolitical activism (CSA) - firms’ public engagement with divisive political issues - has become increasingly common. While prior research shows that CSA often provokes polarized stakeholder reactions, its broader societal implications remain underexplored. We theorize how CSA may contribute to affective polarization, defined as dislike and distrust between political groups independent of policy disagreement. Drawing on social identity theory, we argue that CSA functions as a salient identity cue that signals partisan alignment and activates intergroup dynamics, thereby increasing affective polarization. We further propose that CSA, which frequently occurs in response to salient sociopolitical events, can amplify event-driven polarization by clarifying group boundaries during moments of heightened political salience. By conceptualizing corporations as non-traditional yet influential sources of identity cues, this framework extends research on CSA beyond firm-level outcomes and contributes to theories of affective polarization by highlighting the role of market actors in shaping intergroup relations.
