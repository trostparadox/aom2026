Session overview

The endurance of institutions remains a central puzzle in institutional theory. While traditional accounts emphasize structural explanations, recent work highlights meaning as a potential source of stability. We build on this shift by examining how meaning dynamics contribute to institutional persistence. Empirically, we analyze long-standing public facilities in Vienna and trace how their purposes, expressed as public values, evolved over 150 years using a word-embedding model applied to archival administrative reports. We conceptualize these facilities as institutionalized entities combining structural form with ideational purpose. Our findings suggest that persistence is supported by purpose hybridity, in which multiple purposes coexist, and by purpose configurations that adjust to changing societal concerns while retaining recognizable ideational anchors. Rather than depending on a single enduring purpose, institutions endure by recombining and rebalancing purposes over time. This study advances ideational perspectives on institutional persistence and demonstrates the value of computational text analysis for uncovering meaning dynamics over extended periods.
