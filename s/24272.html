Profit Destruction vs Bidding War: How Corporates’ Preemptive Acquisitions of New Ventures Shape Kil

ENT
Alessandro Genovese (University of Palermo); Mariangela Piazza; Giovanni Perrone (University of Palermo)
The dominance of technological giants in the digital platform industry has raised significant concerns regarding “killer acquisition” strategies, which aim to terminate the development of innovations that threaten their market position. A critical side effect of these voracious early acquisitions is the phenomenon of “kill-zones”—industry sectors where corporate acquisition behavior discourages Venture Capital (VC) investments. While existing research highlights the negative influence of early acquisitions on VC funding, recent contributions suggest these effects may be context-specific. This study reconciles these contrasting perspectives by developing a game-theoretic model that analyzes how the presence of potential acquirers influences VC investment behavior. Our model predicts a U-shaped relationship between the number of acquirers in an industry and the number of VC investments, driven by the interplay between two distinct regimes: a "Profit Destruction" regime, where early-stage preemption depresses exit prices and creates a "kill-zone," and a "Bidding War" regime, where intense buyer competition for mature technologies drives exit valuations up. Focusing on entrepreneurial ventures within the “Computer and Data Processing Services” sector (SIC 737), we constructed a panel dataset covering 40 quarters from 2010 to 2019, involving 8,848 different VC firms. The results of our econometric analysis validate the model’s predictions, illustrating that the number of acquirers and market competitiveness significantly shape VCs’ investing behavior. This study advances the theoretical understanding of “kill-zones” by introducing nuanced insights into VC behavior under varying corporate acquisition scenarios and provides critical implications for industry practitioners and policymakers.
