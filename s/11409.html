Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being applied in human resource management (HRM) (Strohmeier, 2022). Some estimates indicate that 88 percent of companies are using AI-enabled HRM (McKinsey, 2025), and apply it to enhance recruiting, selection, training, performance management, and compensation. There is no one overarching definition of AI, but most researchers refer to it as a technology that enables computers and machines to simulate human learning, comprehension, problem solving, and creativity (e.g., Johnson et al., 2025). Despite the increased use of AI-enabled in HRM, there has been relatively little research on its impact on organizational and HRM processes (i.e., Johnson et al., 2025). As a result, organizations are implementing these new systems without the benefit of research, and some applications may result in unintended consequences (e.g., invasion of personal privacy, unfair discrimination). Thus, HRM and management information systems (MIS) scholars and practitioners need to develop a better understanding of how AI can improve the effectiveness of HRM, but also consider its unanticipated negative consequences before it is implemented in organizations. In view of the widespread application of AI-enabled HRM, the lack of research on its effectiveness and its unintended consequences, the primary purposes of the proposed symposium will be to present four papers on AI-enabled HRM including those on (1) selection, (2) performance management, (3) training, and (4) how AI will influence employee job performance. The presentations will also consider the unethical consequences (e.g., invasion of privacy, generation of misinformation), and offer directions for future research and practice on the application of AI in HRM. The Efficiency and Effectiveness of AI in HRM The use of AI in HRM is in its infancy, and there has been relatively little empirical research on the topic. However, existing research has shown that AI enhances the efficiency of HRM processes (e.g., streamlines processes, decreases costs) (Johnson et al., 2025) and increases employee job performance. However, the research has not shown that AI-enabled systems always increase the effectiveness of HRM processes. Thus, we consider how AI is transforming three HRM processes (e.g., selection, performance management, training,) and discuss how it will influence the job performance of employees. It merits emphasis that we do not provide a complete review of the research on any given topic, but provide examples of research results. AI and HRM Processes The application of AI has transformed a number of HRM processes including selection, training, and performance management. In selection, AI can streamline the initial screening process, conduct automatic interviews, and can be used in sophisticated simulations to assess applicants’ KSAOs, decision making, and problem solving. Further, AI has given organizations the ability to analyze applicants’ narratives to predict individuals’ personality (Campion & Campion, 2023). Although there are a number of benefits, there are also some unintended negative consequences. For example, the use of AI in selection may invade personal privacy, result in unfair discrimination, or generate misinformation about individuals that may discredit them in the hiring process. In the sections below, we consider the efficiency and effectiveness of AI-enabled selection, training, and performance management. AI-enabled Initial Screening (Selection) Many large organizations receive and manually screen hundreds of applications, which is an extremely time-consuming process. As a result, organizations started using keyword systems to scan applications in the 1990s (Mohamed et al., 2002). Today, they are using AI systems for initial screening, and results revealed that they are more effective at analyzing applications than keyword systems. One potential reason is that AI systems use semantic analysis to read and understand words, and can analyze applications at a much deeper level than keyword systems. AI-enabled Assessment (Selection) AI is changing employment assessments in a variety of ways. For example, AI has expanded assessments to include a much broader array of measures (e.g., job simulations, scenarios, games, situational judgment tests,) and use of natural language programming to review applicants writing samples to make inferences about their personality. Organizations are also using AI systems to analyze facial expressions and voice modality, and make inferences about cognitive ability and personality. Research has shown that voice analysis can predict personality, but facial expressions may not be predictive of applicants’ emotions or personality. AI-enabled Hiring Decisions Organizations are also using AI-enabled algorithms to make final hiring decisions. One reason for this is that vendors argue AI will be less biased than human decision makers (Tambe et al., 2019). However, research has shown that AI-enabled hiring decisions are just as susceptible to biases as humans. The primary reason is that AI algorithms are trained on past data sets so if biases exist in previous human systems, then the AI systems will also be biased. Thus, one negative consequence of using AI algorithms to make hiring decisions is they may bias those decisions. Additional research is needed on this topic. Al-enabled Training In recent years, organizations have started using AI-enabled systems to enhance the effectiveness of the training process (BasuMallick, 2019). AI has altered training in at least three major ways including the use of (a) personalized training, (b) virtual tutors, and (c) job simulations to learn and practice their skills in a lifelike environment (e.g., doctors practicing surgery). Research on personalized learning has found that tailoring training to an individuals’ needs and cognitive styles can increase a trainee’s motivation to learn, engagement, satisfaction, and overall learning (Shemshack & Spector, 2020). Further, studies have revealed that virtual tutors can analyze a trainee’s performance, deliver feedback, and improve the learning process (Shemshack & Spactor, 2020). Finally, research on training simulations has found that it can be used to provide continuous feedback and increase trainees’ learning and performance levels. AI-enabled Performance Management Performance management (PM) is considered one of the most challenging HRM processes because it is only conducted only once per year, nay not provide employees with accurate or complete feedback, and is plagued with rater biases (Varma et al., 2024). Given these problems, organizations have started using AI to improve PM processes. For example, AI helps organizations collect data about employee’s performance on a continuous basis and can gather feedback from multiple sources (Varma et al., 2024). This allows for a more accurate and holistic picture of an employee’s performance, and may minimize some types of rater biases (Varma et al., 2024). AI-enabled PM also uses chatbots to provide feedback to employees that is often perceived as less evaluative than human feedback (Stone et al., 2024). Influence of AI on Employee Job Performance AI is also expected to have a major impact on employee job performance by automating many routine tasks, freeing up time for strategic issues, providing personalized feedback, and enhancing decision making which should boost productivity levels (Zheng et al, 2024). AI can identify skills gaps, recommend customized training, and use virtual tutors as coaches to help employees close skill gaps (Johnson et al., 2025). AI can also serve as virtual assistants that enable employees to gather vast information, make better decisions, and problem solve (e.g., Perez-Pino et al., 2022). Taken together, these AI applications should reduce employees’ frustration with mundane tasks, foster engagement, and help them increase productivity. However, it is also expected that the use of AI in organizations will result in large numbers of job losses in the next decade, and this will result in job displacement or job shifting (George, 2024).
