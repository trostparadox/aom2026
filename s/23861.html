When Time Pressure Backfires: A Nonlinear Model of Time Pressure and Employee Time Theft

OB
Yixuan Jing (Shandong University)
Time pressure is a widely used managerial tactic intended to enhance time efficiency, yet its relationship with time theft remains equivocal. Drawing on Job Demands–Resources (JD–R) model, we argue that these mixed findings stem from the inherently nonlinear nature of time pressure. Our theoretical framework and empirical findings reveal that lower levels of time pressure reduce employee time theft through its decreasing curvilinear relationship with boredom, whereas higher levels of time pressure increase time theft through its increasing curvilinear relationship with depletion. In addition, moderate levels of time pressure suppress time theft by fostering work engagement, which follows an inverted U-shaped relationship with time pressure. Furthermore, we introduce job crafting as a critical boundary condition that moderates these nonlinear relationships. We test our hypotheses using a two-wave field survey of 218 employees. The results provide support for the proposed nonlinear and moderated indirect effects. This study contributes to the literature on time theft, time pressure, and JD–R model. Practical implications and study limitations are also discussed.
