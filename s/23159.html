Reframing the News: Modelling How Users Reconstruct Meaning Through Online Comments

CTO
Lumin Shao (University of Bristol Business School); Roberta Bernardi (University of Bristol); Jie Zhang (University of Bristol Business School)
Social media has become a central space for public issues, where news circulates through comment interactions and users actively reconstruct meaning. Building on framing research, this study explores how discursive reconstruction arises from interaction between news and users. We conceptualize reframing as an umbrella process through which users reconstruct meaning by altering frames and shifting topics, treated as distinct but related dimensions. Yet, reframing has mostly been understood as a static phenomenon rather than as a dynamic process through which the meaning of a news issue evolves over time. To address this limitation, we develop an agent-based model that formalizes how users attend to news, evaluate it against prior opinions, update interpretations, and generate comments. Using two datasets of online news comments (English and Chinese), we evaluate the model by comparing simulated and empirical distributions of frames and topics. The model consistently outperforms a baseline model across empirically grounded parameter ranges, with stronger performance for frames than for topics. The results imply more regular diffusion in frames, while topic dynamics are more contingent and context dependent. By separating frames and topics and modelling their joint evolution, we clarify how news issue meaning changes as it spread on social media.

How Are We Mobilized to Express on Social Media? Identity, Persuasion, and Social Norms

CTO
Yunzhou Zhong (University of Pennsylvania); Bingjie Deng (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University)
The Mobilizing Power of Identity-Based Persuasion in Digital Sustainability DiscourseDespite the abundance of expressive affordances on social media platforms, public commenting remains rare, with many users choosing to remain silent even when they agree with or are persuaded by content. This study examines how persuasion is translated or fails to be translated, into public expression by conceptualizing commenting as a high-cost, socially embedded form of digital voice. Drawing on social identity theory, information processing and persuasion theory, and normative social influence theory, we develop a process model linking identity relevance, cognitive certainty, and normative legitimacy to public commenting. Using a controlled experiment in the context of sustainability communication, we show that in-group identity framing increases users’ intention to comment by enhancing the perceived persuasiveness of the information, thereby strengthening cognitive certainty about one’s stance. However, persuasion alone is insufficient to generate visible expression: the translation of perceived persuasiveness into commenting intention is contingent on perceived social norms, such that users are more likely to express themselves publicly when commenting is perceived as socially expected and legitimate. By explaining when and how persuasion becomes publicly actionable, this study advances understanding of digital expression beyond content-centric accounts of engagement and offers actionable insights for the design and governance of social media platforms.

Contentpreneurship: An Integrative Review and Agenda of Entrepreneurship Through Content Creation

CTO
Nick Johnson (Oklahoma State University); Victoria Antin Yates (Oklahoma State University)
Entrepreneurship theory has long assumed that value lies in products or services separable from the entrepreneur, that opportunities are created or discovered through entrepreneurial action, and that entrepreneurs can control their resources and markets. However, the rise of digital platforms has enabled a form of entrepreneurship that challenges these assumptions. Drawing on an integrative review of 47 articles across various domains, we highlight that contentpreneurship – entrepreneurship through content creation – exhibits characteristics that constitute a distinct form of entrepreneurial activity. Our review posits that identity labor, social capital, and platform dependence are three themes in which contentpreneurship challenges current assumptions in entrepreneurship theory. Contentpreneurship is unique in that individuals are embedded in the value they create, realize opportunities through social capital, and operate on platforms with strict algorithms and governance. Furthermore, this review offers future research directions to establish contentpreneurship as an entrepreneurship theory-building domain rather than merely an empirical context for study.

Deepfake Governance Unveiled: How Society Learns to Cope with Emerging Technologies

CTO
Jingyao Li (Bentley University); Marco Marabelli (Bentley University); M Lynne Markus (Bentley University); Silvia Masiero (University of Oslo)
Governing emerging technologies such as the most recent applications of artificial intelligence (AI) poses enduring challenges, particularly in their early stages when societal implications and applications remain poorly understood. Despite this uncertainty, early-stage technology governance is critical, as technologies are most malleable when they first emerge. This study investigates how US society is addressing this issue through an in-depth analysis of deepfakes, which serves as a salient example of the complexities surrounding emerging technology governance. Using secondary qualitative data, we trace the evolution of deepfake governance in the US and examine how diverse stakeholders (policymakers, industry alliances, and public advocacy groups) advance competing governance approaches. Our findings reveal that these actors engage in dynamic and sometimes conflicting practices that collectively shape emerging technology governance. This study contributes to the literature of technology governance by developing a nuanced understanding of multi-actor dynamics concerning emerging technologies, and by identifying implications for generating effective and balanced regulatory frameworks.
