Women’s Career Success Under Patriarchy: Supervisor Support, Psychological Capital, Promotability

CAR
Farzana Ashraf (Curtin University - Perth); Raymond Trau (Macquarie University); Denise Mary Jepsen (Macquarie University)
Little is known about how organizational and personal resources interact to shape career success when structural and cultural barriers limit employee promotion. This study investigates how supervisor support, psychological capital (PsyCap) and promotability interact to influence women’s career satisfaction in patriarchal contexts. Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) and Job Demands–Resources (JD-R) theories, we conceptualize supervisor support as a job resource, PsyCap as a personal resource and promotability as an external career resource. We propose that supervisor support enhances PsyCap, which in turn increases career satisfaction, and that this mediating effect is stronger for employees perceived as more promotable by their supervisors. Using a temporal-lagged survey of 214 employee-supervisor dyads in the patriarchal context of Bangladesh, results indicate that supervisor support positively predicts career satisfaction through PsyCap. Moreover, promotability moderates the PsyCap–career satisfaction link, such that higher promotability amplifies the indirect effect of supervisor support on career satisfaction via PsyCap. These findings extend resource-based career theories and highlight the synergistic interplay of personal, job and external resources, providing insights for supporting women’s career advancement in contexts with structural and cultural barriers.
