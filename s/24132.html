Beyond the Firm: Extending the Attention-Based View to Distributed Governance

OMT
Daniel saldanha Guedes (Fundacao Dom Cabral); Wagner Alledo; Luiz Henrique Cardoso Cruz
Franchise networks offer a compelling empirical context for examining how attention operates in distributed governance systems. Although the Attention-Based View (ABV) has primarily focused on intraorganizational hierarchies, little is known about how attention is orchestrated across semi-autonomous units that share a brand and interdependent performance outcomes. This article theorizes attentional governance as the socio-technical arrangements through which distributed actors notice, interpret, prioritize, and respond to issues. We conceptualize franchise networks as attentional architectures composed of four interdependent components: strategic attentional focus, curation processes, allocation mechanisms, and buffering mechanisms for handling anomalies. A practical application of this framework is its ability to enable franchise executives to enhance coordination efficiency, ultimately improving system-level performance and innovation. Building on this framework, we develop a typology of attentional architectures - reactive, functional, curatorial, and intelligent - that demonstrates how differences in attentional orchestration produce distinct system-level outcomes. Our theorization extends the ABV by shifting the locus of analysis beyond the firm boundary and contributes to franchising research by reframing coordination as an attentional rather than contractual problem. We outline a research agenda to operationalize attentional governance, examine transitions among architectures, and assess their consequences for innovation, resilience, and coordination in distributed organizational systems. By positioning attention as a collective rather than individual cognitive resource, this article offers a novel perspective on strategic action in networked organizational forms.
