Layoffs in Founder-Led Firms: Job Loss Communication Under Entrepreneurial Constraints

Authors: Nita Chhinzer University of Guelph; Akierah Binns University of Guelph

Session overview

Founder-led layoffs have recently drawn intense public scrutiny regarding how job-loss messages are delivered, being criticized as cold, narcissistic, or performative. Yet research on layoffs, job insecurity, and downsizing is dominated by large-firm settings. Large firms benefit from formal HR systems, managerial specialization, organizational slack, and internal redeployment options. Different structural conditions shape how layoffs are planned and communicated in entrepreneurial ventures yet remain underexplored. Accordingly, this conceptual paper addresses this gap by theorizing job-loss communication in founder-led firms, where volatility is high, and constraints include limited managerial capacity, minimal slack, tight relational dynamics, and strong founder identity attachment to employees. Integrating insights from job loss, organizational justice, and managerial communication literatures, we develop a contextual framework that highlights founders’ competing multi-stakeholder responsibilities (organization, employees, and themselves). Following this, we propose responsible job loss practices tailored to entrepreneurial conditions and organized across three phases (pre-layoff, layoff delivery, and post-layoff). Key recommendations include recalibrating managerial distancing to preserve dignity while enabling difficult decisions, prioritizing narrative (rather than purely informational) communication to support sensemaking, and avoiding counterproductive strategies such as excessive positive reframing or blame shifting. We also explore survivor re-engagement practices to mitigate survivor syndrome and help with organizational recovery. Collectively, this paper contributes to an evidence-informed, founder-relevant approach to planning and communicating layoffs that aims to minimize avoidable harm across stakeholders while acknowledging the distinctive constraints of entrepreneurial ventures.
