From Passing to Learning: Redesigning University Assessment Around Skills

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: Cecilia Primogerio Austral University; Camila Del Carril; Lucas Petralli Austral University

Session overview

This paper presents an assessment redesign in higher education that replaces traditional exam-centered evaluation with a continuous, skill-based framework anchored in active learning and formative assessment. The model integrates real-time interactive quizzes, collaborative applied projects, and structured oral examinations, generating consistently high attendance and sustained engagement throughout the semester in both virtual and in-person formats. Artificial intelligence tools support the process by generating quiz variations, analyzing response patterns, and enabling rapid feedback cycles, while remaining discreetly embedded to preserve the centrality of human teaching and learning. The framework evaluates not only conceptual understanding but also attention, collaboration, creativity, resilience, and oral communication. These competencies are measured repeatedly through a combination of in-class activities, group work, and reflective assessments. Real-time quizzes function as both participation incentives and diagnostic tools, transforming attendance into a meaningful component of evaluation. Group projects simulate professional problem-solving environments, prompting students to apply theoretical knowledge to ambiguous challenges while developing teamwork and communication skills. Oral examinations serve as reflective checkpoints, allowing students to articulate reasoning, integrate insights, and demonstrate growth beyond what timed assessments can capture. Findings indicate a marked shift in classroom dynamics. Students begin to perceive each class not as a step toward a high-stakes final exam but as a genuine opportunity to learn. Motivation increases, academic dishonesty decreases, and the learning environment becomes more interactive, autonomous, and purpose-driven. Faculty reviewers observing student outputs noted significant improvement in analytical depth, creativity, and professional articulation compared to cohorts evaluated through conventional exams.
