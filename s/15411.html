From Corners to Cartels: How Trust and Uncertainty Drive Strategic Networking in Entrepreneurship

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4
ENT

People

Author: Dian de Ridder Tilburg University

Session overview

To survive and grow, entrepreneurs rely on networking strategies. Literature typically argues that the bigger the network, the better, even though we acknowledge that networking comes with relational uncertainties, such as opportunism. To better understand the boundary conditions of the tensions between network expansion and relational uncertainty, we investigate networking strategies in the context of criminal organizations and focus on how entrepreneurs strategically balance the trade-offs for survival and growth. We argue that entrepreneurs employ two complementary strategies: embedding locally to secure resources and trust in early stages, and pursuing agentic expansion to access novel resources when local ties are insufficient. These strategies are interrelated, as local embeddedness continues even during expansion, and eventually comprises the entrepreneur in his own network. We test our arguments using longitudinal social network data on criminal actors, capturing tie formation, embeddedness, and network acceptance over time. Our findings contribute to network theory by showing (1) relational uncertainty drives complementary networking strategies, (2) that survival strategies themselves create systemic fragility as trust is a bilateral mechanism for network integration, and (3) provide extreme boundary conditions to networking theory through data in the criminal context.
