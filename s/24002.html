Institutionalizing Cross-Sector Partnerships: Networks and Governance

STR
Kawalpreet Singh (National Institute of Technology Rourkela); Arvind Kumar (National Institute of Technology Rourkela)
Industrial markets increasingly rely on cross-sector partnerships to address complex economic, social, and environmental challenges, yet such collaborations often struggle with unclear governance arrangements, power asymmetries, and weak mechanisms for collective agreement. This conceptual paper introduces the notion of constitutional networks to explain how cross-sector partnerships can be deliberately governed to support sustained value creation. Constitutional networks are defined by explicitly articulated rights and entitlements, layered rule systems that integrate public regulation and private governance, clearly assigned roles and decision authority, and systematic consent processes. Drawing on interaction theory, network governance, and stakeholder research in industrial marketing, the study develops a five-stage process model of constitutionalization from problem framing to value realization. The paper further advances six testable propositions that link constitutional design to partnership outcomes under conditions of power asymmetry and institutional distance, thereby extending network theory by clarifying how governance structures can be negotiated and institutionalized to enable durable cross-sector collaboration.
