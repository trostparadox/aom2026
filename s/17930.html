Session overview

We live in an increasingly interdependent world- socially, emotionally, economically, physically, environmentally, biologically, and evolutionarily. As a result of interdependence, we need relationships to achieve our desired outcomes, especially in the context of growing complexity. However, relationships are at risk worldwide due to the rise of remote work, technologically mediated communication, economic inequality, and political polarization. In this workshop, we focus on two approaches: Social Network Analysis (SNA) and Relational Coordination (RC), which offer theories and methods for exploring relational challenges and solutions. SNA explains the broad structural complexities of networks among people or entities, with the potential for greater focus by organizational scholars on organizational networks. RC focuses on the communication and relationship ties that coordinate work from small teams to ecosystems, how these ties drive outcomes, and the design of organizational structures to support these ties. Although SNA and RC are two primary lenses and approaches used to assess relationships among people and organizations, they are rarely used together. Together, they offer the potential to better understand the complex multilevel relational dynamics within and across organizations. This panel symposium aims to advance conceptual and methodological understanding of how SNA and RC can be integrated to study relational processes linking organizational structures to outcomes. We will achieve this goal by convening leading scholars and practitioners to engage in a moderated, interactive discussion that examines theoretical foundations, methodological choices, and practical implications of integrating SNA and RC across research and practice.
