Playful Work Design and Job Performance: The Critical Role of AI Interaction Style

Authors: Ivana Predrag Perovic; Jovana Cabarkapa University of Montenegro; Nadja Laban; Kristina Vlaovic University of Montenegro; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro

Session overview

This study examines how playful work design (PWD), defined as employees proactively infusing fun into their tasks, relates to supervisor-rated job performance through task-oriented generative AI use and how this process depends on AI interaction preciseness. Drawing on Trait Activation Theory (TAT), we argue that PWD encourages experimentation with work tools such as generative AI. However, because generative AI is a high-variance work resource whose outputs depend on interaction quality and verification, increased use does not uniformly translate into higher performance. We test a moderated mediation model with PWD as the predictor, task-oriented generative AI use as the mediator, supervisor-rated job performance as the outcome, and AI interaction preciseness as a moderator, controlling for tenure. Multi-source data from 340 employee–supervisor dyads in Montenegro show that PWD is positively associated with task-oriented AI use. AI use is negatively related to performance when interaction preciseness is low, becomes non-significant at typical levels, and turns positive only at very high levels. These findings suggest that frequent AI use without sufficient interactional discipline may undermine performance, whereas highly precise interaction enables performance gains. We discuss contributions to the playful work design literature by identifying a technology-enabled pathway that can backfire under low-quality human–AI interaction and offer practical implications for pairing AI experimentation with training in precise interaction and verification.
