Curiosity as a Driver of Employability: The Mediating Role of Job Crafting in Global Careers

CAR
Fangfang Zhang (Curtin University - Perth); Sten Langmann (Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University); Shanghao Song (Capital University of Economics and Business); Pieter-Jan Bezemer (Edith Cowan University); Luke van der Laan (Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University)
In boundaryless and uncertain career contexts, employability increasingly depends on individuals’ motivation to engage with novelty and proactively shape their work. Drawing on curiosity theory and job crafting research, this study examines curiosity as a motivational driver of employability and job crafting as the behavioral mechanism through which curiosity translates into employability outcomes. We distinguish between embracing curiosity (openness to uncertainty) and stretching curiosity (drive for challenge and learning), and between job crafting towards strengths and job crafting towards development. Using a three-wave survey of 422 self-initiated expatriates working across more than 100 countries, we tested a mediated path model linking curiosity to perceived internal and external employability via job crafting. Results showed that embracing and stretching curiosity was positively related to both forms of job crafting. Developmental job crafting, in turn, was positively associated with both internal and external employability, whereas strengths-based job crafting was related only to internal employability. Mediation analyses revealed that stretching curiosity indirectly predicted both internal and external employability through strength-based and developmental job crafting, whereas indirect effects involving embracing curiosity were not supported. These findings highlight curiosity particularly its stretching dimension as a key motivational resource for employability, and identify developmental job crafting as a critical pathway through which individuals actively build employability in global careers.
