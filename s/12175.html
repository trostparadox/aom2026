Session overview

Corporate sociopolitical activism (SPA)—firms’ engagement with contested social and political issues—presents complex strategic and organizational challenges. Domestically, rising polarization forces companies to navigate difficult trade-offs: taking a public stance may appeal to one stakeholder group while alienating another. Internationally, multinational firms face even sharper tensions as they operate across markets with divergent norms, values, and political expectations; a stance welcomed in one country may trigger backlash in another. Geopolitical friction further amplifies these contradictory pressures, placing firms in the crosshairs of nationalistic, regulatory, and public scrutiny. Against this backdrop, important questions arise: Why do firms engage in SPA at all, given these risks? How do they craft and communicate their stances? Do corporate activism strategies differ between domestic and global contexts? And how do shifting institutional environments—such as domestic political climates or global geopolitical dynamics—shape the evolution of SPA? This PDW brings together leading scholars to explore these questions from both domestic and international perspectives. Our goal is to illuminate the complexities of corporate SPA, highlight promising research directions, and equip participants with insights for advancing scholarship on this timely and rapidly evolving phenomenon.
