Session overview

While Upper Echelons Theory (UET) has shaped more than four decades of scholarship on how top executive characteristics relate to strategic outcomes, key mechanisms through which top executives reason and align on strategic choices remain underspecified—particularly at the level of top management teams. Moreover, despite the considerable time many executives spend in executive education settings, the role of executive education in shaping (and revealing) executive cognition remains understudied, even as emerging scholarship suggests that executive cognition may be more transient than originally assumed. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) convenes scholars with both research and executive education backgrounds to critically assess the cognitive foundations of strategic choice by top executives and the role of executive education therein. Specifically, this PDW will position executive education as a distinctive empirical and conceptual vantage point for studying strategic leadership processes as they unfold in real time. In executive classrooms, scholars and educators can observe how executives construct, challenge, and align mental models; how they direct attention and interrogate assumptions through questioning; and how these processes unfold under conditions of complexity and uncertainty. Accordingly, the PDW brings UET-based scholarship and executive education into dialogue. Rather than adjudicating empirical findings, this PDW aims to identify how executive education can serve as a unique vehicle for theory building and empirical testing and how key tenets of UET can be used as a foundation for instruction. Participants will leave with future research questions, instructional ideas, and insights into challenging and extending standard assumptions of strategic leadership theory.
