Artificial Intelligence Leaders: Literature Review and Future Direction

OB
Jiaxi Chen (Nanjing University); Ru Li; Jin Qian (Nanjing University)
From simple text input and calculations to analyzing and interpreting complex data, the role of artificial intelligence (AI) in organizational management has gradually evolved from an auxiliary tool to an intelligent agent with leadership functions. By systematically reviewing 51 relevant literatures, this paper summarizes AI’s significant advantages in information processing, decision-making efficiency, procedure execution, and conflict mediation, while pointing out its shortcomings in emotional resonance, value judgment, and creative tasks. It analyzes the diverse power forms of AI leaders, including legitimate power, reward power, coercive power, expert power, referent power, and biopower, and further sorts out the dual impacts that AI may bring as a leader. Finally, this paper proposes that future research should focus on exploring the emergence mechanism, subsequent effects, and ethical issues of AI leadership, and attempt to construct a future-oriented human-machine leadership mechanism, so as to deepen the theoretical understanding of AI’s leadership role in organizations and provide guidance and reference for enterprises to rationally deploy AI leaders.

How and When Leader Anthropomorphizing AI Shapes Employee–AI Collaboration: An Identity Perspective

OB
Su Cui (Nanjing University); Longdong Wang (Nanjing University)
As artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly enters organizations as coworkers, it reshapes identity processes by redefining who employees are and who AI is within the workplace. Yet, how leaders shape this identity reconstruction and its implications for employee-AI collaboration remains largely unknown. Drawing on identity theory, this study examines how leader anthropomorphizing behavior toward AI (LABA) influences employee-AI collaboration through two identity pathways. We propose that LABA simultaneously triggers employee identity threat by undermining role meaning and subjectivity, while also enhancing perceived AI social presence by positioning AI as an agentive social actor. These opposing processes lead to divergent collaboration outcomes. Moreover, we identify employees’ self-concept clarity (SCC) as a key boundary condition. Employees with lower SCC are more susceptible to identity threat in response to LABA, whereas those with higher SCC are more likely to perceive stronger AI social presence. Evidence from a three-wave survey (N=416) and an online scenario experiment (N=164) supports our model. Overall, this research highlights LABA as a double-edged leadership practice that reshapes both employee identity and AI identity, advancing understanding of identity dynamics in AI-enabled workplaces.

Translating Symbol into Action: How Leaders’ AI Symbolization Drives Employee AI Helping

OB
Xiaorui Zeng (Chongqing University); Cuilian Zhang (Chongqing University); Xinhui Qin (Chongqing University); Wei Yan (Chongqing University)
As AI integrates deeply into organizations, employee AI helping become critical for AI implementation. Yet its antecedents and mechanisms remain understudied, especially leader’s role. Based on sensemaking theory, this study examines how leaders’ AI symbolization influences employee AI helping, with work reflection and perceived AI usefulness as serial mediators, and learning goal orientation as a moderator. To test the theoretical model, we conducted a three-wave survey and collected data from 357 full-time employees. Results showed: (1) Leaders’ AI symbolization positively affects employee AI helping; (2) Work reflection and perceived usefulness with AI sequentially mediate this link; (3) Learning goal orientation moderates the serial mediation model, strengthening the positive indirect effect of leaders’ AI symbolization on employee AI helping through work reflection and perceived usefulness of AI. This study enriches AI and organizational behavior literature, and offers practical guidance for stimulating AI helping behavior.

Understanding How Organizational AI Adoption Influences Voice Behavior

OB
Jeeyoon Jeong (NUCB Business School, Nagoya University of Commerce & Business)
Does organizational AI adoption enhance or suppress employee voice? Drawing on Conservation of Resources theory, we propose that AI adoption enhances voice behavior through job engagement, but only when leader-member exchange (LMX) quality is high. We argue that LMX functions as a resource passageway determining whether employees can translate technological resources into psychological engagement and subsequent voice behavior. Using three-wave time-lagged data from 491 South Korean employees, we find support for our moderated mediation model. AI adoption positively relates to job engagement, which in turn promotes voice behavior. Critically, this indirect effect is significant only under high LMX conditions. Employees with low-quality leader relationships fail to benefit from AI adoption. Our findings challenge the assumption that AI universally affects employees and highlight leadership as a critical boundary condition. We contribute to AI adoption research by demonstrating that technological and relational resources must align for positive employee outcomes.
