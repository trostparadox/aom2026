Session overview

Global immersion courses and the International Residential remain one of the most powerful forms of experiential learning in MBA and Executive MBA programs, yet many schools struggle to move from travel and exposure to intentional learning design and reliable assessment. This hands-on workshop provides participants with a step-by-step method to design a high-impact international residential that measurably develops global leadership competencies, while also helping educators adapt immersion learning for diverse program formats and resource levels. The workshop draws on a longitudinal qualitative dataset of over 300 MBA learning journals collected across ten cohorts of international residentials at University of California, Irvine School of Business, and the iterative design practices that emerged from analyzing those reflections over time. Participants will practice a design sequence that links three elements into a coherent learning system: a competency target (for example cultural intelligence, systems thinking, resilience, collaborative leadership, navigating uncertainty), a disruption theme grounded in real-world global forces, and a small set of signature learning activities that create structured encounters and reflection. Participants will also work with ready-to-use learning journal prompts and a competency rubric, then leave with a draft one-paragraph residency pitch, an assessment plan, and a revision checklist they can apply immediately in their own contexts.
