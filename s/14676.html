To Criticize or Praise? How Expertise Determines the Effect of Feedback Valence on innovativeness

Authors: Zhixia CHEN NEOMA Business School; Michael Zaggl NEOMA Business School; Mehdi Bagherzadeh NEOMA Business School

Session overview

Firms rely widely on crowdsourcing to gain innovative ideas from external partners. However, despite the importance of innovation in crowdsourcing, the impact of feedback valence on innovativeness remains unclear. Different theories and empirical evidence have inconsistent results, suggesting that negative feedback and positive feedback may either facilitate or inhabit innovativeness. The mixed evidence leads a need to unpack this significant relationship. Thus, our study aims to explain the impact of feedback valence on innovativeness while considering the role of expertise. According to feedback intervention theory, it suggests that feedback valence highlights the discrepancy between the participants’' current level of innovativeness and the expected standards, prompting them to respond by adopting one of two fundamentally opposing behaviors. One behavior is that feedback valence will lower their current performance and another behavior is that feedback valence will enhance their innovativeness. We argue that the level of expertise can explain this inconsistence. We conduct one field experiment, and our expected results will show that when expertise level is higher, negative feedback will have a positive impact on innovativeness; whereas when expertise level is lower, positive feedback will have a positive impact on innovativeness.
