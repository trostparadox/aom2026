From Commitment to Impact: How Stakeholder Engagement Shapes Circular Economy Outcomes

People

Authors: Anna Prisco University of Naples Federico II; Lorenzo Turriziani University of Naples Federico II; Mauro Sciarelli

Session overview

Organizations increasingly adopt circular economy strategies to address grand societal challenges such as climate change, resource depletion, and social inequality. Despite strong rhetorical commitment, many circular initiatives fail to generate substantive social and environmental impacts, remaining largely symbolic. This study argues that the critical missing link lies in the strategic design and enactment of stakeholder engagement. Drawing on stakeholder theory and configuration theory, we conceptualize stakeholder engagement as an engagement architecture composed of interdependent elements (engagement breadth, engagement depth, governance formalization, and incentive alignment) rather than as a set of isolated or additive practices. Adopting a configurational and process-oriented perspective, the study examines how different combinations of these elements shape firms’ ability to translate circular economy strategies from symbolic adoption into tangible socio-environmental impact. Methodologically, the study proposes a comparative multi-case design in which circular economy initiatives are treated as inter-organizational phenomena embedded in complex value ecosystems. The study contributes to stakeholder theory by advancing a configurational understanding of engagement and to circular economy research by reframing implementation gaps as fundamentally relational rather than primarily technological. Managerially, it offers guidance on designing stakeholder engagement as an implementation capability that enables resilient collaboration and measurable impact on the circular economy.
