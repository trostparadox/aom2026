Using Micro Training to Increase Workplace Inclusion for People with Epilepsy

DEI
Asha Rao (California State University-East Bay); Daria Vasina (California State University-East Bay); Surindra Sarnikar (California State University-East Bay)
Globally, 1.3 billion people experience significant disability. They represent 16% of the world’s population (WHO, 2023). Despite legal and social advances, people with disabilities (PWD) continue to experience barriers to inclusion, particularly in the workplace. This study focuses on disability sensitization through micro training to raise awareness and support for a particular group of PWD, people with epilepsy (PWE). Affecting over 3 million people in the United States and 50 million worldwide, epilepsy is one of the most common neurological disorders.(CDC, 2024). PWE consistently face high rates of unemployment and underemployment, not necessarily because of the condition itself but due to stigma, misconceptions, and lack of workplace support (Cui et al., 2015; West et al., 2006). Research demonstrates that employers and coworkers harbor unfounded concerns about safety, productivity, and reliability, making stigma a greater barrier than seizures themselves (Jacoby et al., 2005; Gibson et al., 2017). This study examined the impact of micro training to increase knowledge of epilepsy on 139 participants’ ability to support PWE, reduce stigma, and provide appropriate accommodations at work. Micro-training delivers short bursts of information to learners in bite size content that is easy to absorb.Our findings suggest that micro training has strong potential to reduce stigma and increase support for people with epilepsy at work.
