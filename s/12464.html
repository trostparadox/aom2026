Session overview

Corporations are increasingly expected to play a decisive role in global climate governance, with their decarbonization commitments widely viewed as vital complements to governmental action. Yet, fundamental questions remain about their effectiveness: beyond disclosure and target-setting, which actions do firms undertake to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, and under what conditions do these actions translate into actual emissions reductions? This symposium brings together scholars to present emerging evidence on corporate decarbonization and to discuss how management research can rigorously study these issues in ways that balance real-world relevance with clean, credible empirical approaches. We highlight research that evaluates the effectiveness of climate targets, and the varied pathways firms deploy to pursue them, including internal decarbonization policies, supply chain adaptations, and the use of carbon offsetting. To complement the paper presentations, the symposium features a panel discussion that situates the presented research on corporate decarbonization within existing literature, and discusses future directions for research on corporate decarbonization, including considerations for theory development, empirical design, and managerial relevance.
