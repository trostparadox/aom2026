Human Capital Driving ESG Performance: High-Tech vs. Traditional Industries in Taiwan

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
HR

People

Authors: Yi-An Chen National Yunlin University of Science and Technology; Chin-Yi Chen National Yunlin University of Science and Technology

Session overview

Prior research on human capital predominantly focuses on its micro-level impact on financial performance, leaving its macro-level explanatory power for non-financial outcomes like ESG underexplored. Integrating Upper Echelons Theory and the Resource-Based View, this study investigates the driving mechanisms of human capital on ESG performance using a sample of 622 Taiwanese listed firms in 2023. Empirical results reveal distinct sectoral pathways: in high-tech industries, top management team (TMT) stability, educational attainment, and R&D investment are pivotal drivers of ESG outcomes. Conversely, in traditional industries, employee stability serves as the primary mechanism alongside education and R&D. Human capital variables contributed an incremental R² (?R²) of 11.8% in the high-tech sector and 14.2% in the traditional sector, confirming strong predictive validity. These findings demonstrate that human capital transcends internal operational benefits to become a sustainable competitive advantage. This study elucidates the micro-macro interface between human capital and ESG performance, offering distinct strategic pathways for sustainability management across knowledge-intensive and labor-intensive industries.
