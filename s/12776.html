Session overview

This symposium unpacks the dynamics through which diversity-related threats manifest, their impact on organizational outcomes (e.g., support for diversity initiatives, employee well-being, silence on workplace injustice), and how organizations can mitigate these threats. In the first presentation, Bendersky et al. demonstrate how different organizational contexts can generate higher levels of "diversity threat" - the perception that diversity efforts harm organizational integrity. In addition, the authors demonstrate that higher levels of diversity threat negatively impact employee well-being. In the second presentation, Vo & Onyeador investigate an extra-organizational threat: anti-DEI litigation. The authors demonstrate that anti-DEI litigation can negatively impact support for diversity initiatives and exacerbate beliefs that, rather than reducing inequality, diversity initiatives lead to anti-Asian/White discrimination. However, the authors also find that when organizations speak up and defend their initiatives, support can be sustained. Despite this demonstrated benefit of speaking up, Birnbaum et al. establish in the third presentation that advantaged groups (i.e., men) can remain silent on social justice issues. While silence may be well-intentioned (i.e., uncertainty about how to respond), observers may misinterpret silence as evidence of harboring bias. Similarly, Arnett et al. find in the fourth presentation that employees can experience "diversity error risk" - or fear saying the wrong thing regarding diversity issues. While diversity error risk can undermine advocacy for diversity efforts, this effect is mitigated amongst organizations with high levels of psychological safety. Together, the presentations in this symposium illuminate the tension between silence and speaking up as threats to organizational diversity initiatives intensify.
