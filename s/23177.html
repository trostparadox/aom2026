Signals in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: How AI Framing and AI Patents Shape IPO Valuations

CTO
Yan Pang (Peking University); Chang Lu (Peking University); Yijiao Zhang (Peking University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative force, reshaping industries and driving innovation across the global economy. Grounded in signaling theory, this study investigates how AI disclosures and innovation influence initial public offering (IPO) premiums, focusing on two distinct signaling mechanisms: what firms say (e.g., framing AI in IPO prospectuses) and what firms do (e.g., AI-related patents that reflect tangible innovation). Using data from 2,594 firms listed on China’s A-share market between 2011 and 2023, the results reveal that AI-framing exert a stronger positive effect on IPO premiums compared to AI-patent. Additionally, the study highlights key moderating factors—market innovation orientation, institutional investor composition, and venture capital participation—that influence the effectiveness of the two explicit and implicit signals.

Credible Signals Or Empty Gestures? Managerial Response Quality In Online Review Platforms

CTO
Badr-Eddine Lahsini (Africa Business School); Ravi Prakash Ranjan (Mohammed VI Polytechnic University)
Once hotels begin responding to reviews, do they genuinely improve — or simply manage appearances? Using a Difference-in-Differences design and entity-level sentiment analysis, we examine whether sentiment toward previously criticised aspects shifts following response adoption. Response attributes, including length, delay, similarity, and personalisation are tested as moderators of this relationship. Drawing on TripAdvisor data, we track sentiment around each hotel's most criticised entity before and after adoption. Sentiment improves post- response, pointing to real service gains; longer, comprehensive replies amplify this effect, while delayed and templated ones undermine it

More Than Human, More Than AI: Hybrid Intelligence in Science Communication

CTO
Thiago Bittencourt (University of Brasilia); Renato Calhau Coda (University of Brasilia); Pedro Silva (University of Brasilia); Josivania Silva Farias; Abner Santos Belém
This study examines how different types of intelligence—human, artificial, and hybrid—shape audience engagement in audiovisual science communication. Using a two-stage experimental design, participants evaluated identical scientific content presented in videos produced under human, AI, and hybrid conditions. Regression analyses revealed that, while perceived attractiveness consistently predicted engagement across all conditions, credibility and warmth significantly affected only the hybrid configuration. Specifically, all hypothesized relationships were supported exclusively in the hybrid condition, indicating that hybrid communicators activate affective and cognitive persuasion mechanisms in a complementary manner. Grounded in Service-Dominant Logic and Transformative Service Research, the findings demonstrate that hybrid intelligence functions performed better at integrating resources, enhancing value co-creation, and supporting the transformative goals of science communication. By balancing emotional resonance with epistemic legitimacy, hybrid configurations emerge as an effective model for fostering sustained public engagement with scientific content.

Social Media Project Types A Project Type Framework for Social Media Project Delivery

CTO
John Lewis Rice (University of Sharjah); Nigel Martin (The Australian National University); Bridget Rice (Zayed University)
The purpose of the research was to survey 94 IT project managers and, through the application of a four quadrant project characteristics framework, determine the best Social Media project approaches for six publicly listed test firms. Results show that SM projects can be narrowly configured to concentrate on business problem resolution and capabilities and affordances deliverables, including testing and experimentation work items that suborn ‘listen and learn’ technology systems. Alternatively, widened project approaches, structured with measured planning, tighter controls and synergised multi-party project organizations, can produce integrated IT functions and systems that augment customer relations, and supplier and business stakeholder alliances. In addition, project leadership and control functions were asserted as critical human centred characteristics that provide adjunct support for project resources management and work item delivery. The article highlights intrinsic connections between project characteristics and IT systems deliverables, while building into the cumulative research directed at IT project management.
