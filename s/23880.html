Better at Home? A Diary Study Comparing Daily Evening Recovery on Telework and On-Site Days

OB
Jan Sinner Beckmann (Julius Maximilian University of Würzburg); Verena C. Haun (Julius Maximilian University of Würzburg)
This study examines the relationship between telework versus on-site workdays and employees’ daily recovery from job stress. We hypothesized that telework days are associated with increased autonomy, reduced social support from colleagues, and more non-work-related interruptions, which in turn is related to evening psychological detachment and energy levels. Using a daily diary design across two workweeks with 79 employees in hybrid work arrangements (N = 532 days), multilevel path analyses indicated higher energy levels at bedtime following telework days, but no differences in psychological detachment. Only autonomy positively mediated the relationship between telework (compared to on-site) days and both energy and psychological detachment. Overall, these findings enhance the understanding of recovery processes within hybrid work contexts.
