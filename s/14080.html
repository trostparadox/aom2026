Knowledge Sharing in Healthcare Across Levels: Nurses, Their Leaders and Teams

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
HCM

People

Authors: Minna Marinova Paunova ESCP Business School; Diana Pérez Arechaederra ESCP Business School

Session overview

Knowledge sharing (KS) is essential for enhancing team performance in healthcare, especially in high-stakes environments like intensive care units (ICUs). This study investigates the multilevel factors influencing individual nurse KS, focusing on leader KS and team KS, as perceived by leaders. Moderation effects of leader power perception and nurse identification with their team are also examined. Using survey data from ICU nurses in Denmark, multilevel mixed-effects regression models were employed on responses from over 100 nurses across 13 ICUs. Key findings reveal that leader KS positively influences individual nurse KS, with perceived leader power moderating this relationship. Contrarily, team KS exhibited a negative effect on individual KS, suggesting diffusion of responsibility. However, nurses with higher team identification demonstrated stronger KS, even when overall team KS was low. This study underscores the complexity of KS dynamics in healthcare, emphasizing the roles of leadership behavior, team cohesion, and psychological factors. Practical implications include empowering leaders as KS facilitators and fostering team identification to mitigate potential downsides of high team-level KS. Future research should explore broader datasets and include qualitative analyses of leader-nurse interactions.
