Session overview

This symposium, ADHD in the East and West, brings together five empirical mixed-methods studies that examine how attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) shapes entrepreneurial action, social network strategies, team cognition, recruitment reactions, and leadership across cultural contexts. Although ADHD is often discussed under the broader umbrella of neurodivergence, its distinct cognitive, motivational, and self-regulatory profile, as well as its high rates of comorbidity with other neurodevelopmental conditions (e.g., autism, learning disorders), warrant focused theoretical and empirical attention (Tran et al., 2025). Spanning eastern and western samples drawn from incubator-based ventures, large organizations, clinical interviews, and cross-national survey data (e.g., Vietnam, China, Poland, United States), the papers employ multilevel, experimental, qualitative, and survey methodologies to demonstrate when ADHD-related characteristics function as liabilities, resources, or context-dependent traits that can be supported through neuroinclusive practices. Across studies, particular emphasis is placed on organizational and institutional conditions (e.g., team climates, self-regulation supports, leadership practices, and venture or employment programs) that showcase ADHD as a condition that can yield positive outcomes. By focusing on ADHD, this symposium provides more condition-specific research within neurodiversity scholarship and avoids premature generalization across various forms of neurodivergence. In doing so, the symposium advances theory in entrepreneurship, social capital, team cognition, and human resources, while offering actionable insights for neuroinclusive hiring, team design, leadership development, and venture support programs.
