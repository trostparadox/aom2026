Session overview

Management and organizational studies have engaged with democracy along dimensions ranging from democratic organizing to the effects of organizations on political democracy. In this symposium, presenters will offer new insights into how researchers think and talk about democratic organizing, including how scholars theorize the nature of management itself. Presenters also will consider how practitioners attempting democratic organizing reshape managerial domains into interdependent moral practices while facing challenges and boundaries in efforts to advance democratic participation in society. While the presentations in this symposium draw from a rich variety of sources, they share an appreciation for and a basis in American or classical pragmatism. The presentations at times criticize dominant arrangements and at other times appreciate efforts, some non-traditional, to understand and practice organizing as a socially important and potentially democratic practice.
