Algorithmic Authority and Ethical Judgment in Hierarchical Organizations

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Hemachandran Kannan Woxsen University; Raul V Rodriguez

Session overview

AI-based retrieval systems are rapidly becoming part of everyday work in hierarchical, high-reliability organizations; however, we still know little about how they reshape ethical judgment at the point of decision. This study examines how a defense-focused RAG system influences authority, responsibility, and moral reasoning in command-driven environments. Based on qualitative interviews with analysts and trainees who rely on the system for research and decision preparation, we find that AI outputs often acquire perceived authority that rivals human expertise in the military. Under time pressure, users increasingly defer ethical reflection to the system, subtly shifting responsibility away from themselves to the system. Hierarchical norms further limit open moral dissent, even when discomfort is present. At the same time, participants exposed to structured ethical simulations show greater willingness to question AI recommendations. These findings suggest that AI reshapes not only how people process information but also how responsibility is allocated in high-stakes decisions. For managers, this study highlights the need to design ethical safeguards into AI adoption, rather than adding them after harm occurs.
