When Ladders End, Do Careers Derail? The Alienating Effect of Hierarchical Plateau

OB
QI CUI (Renmin University of China); Jun Liu; Zhichao Qian; Lifeng Zhong
In flattening organizations, hierarchical plateau, defined as the perception that further promotion is unlikely, represents a potent stressor. While prior research has extensively documented the attitudinal fallout of career stagnation, it has largely overlooked how this structural constraint precipitates actual behavioral misconduct. Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, we develop and test a model in which work alienation serves as the critical psychological mechanism linking hierarchical plateau to organization-directed deviance. We argue that the structural blockage of advancement fosters a state of powerlessness and meaninglessness, creating a resource loss spiral that erodes adherence to organizational norms. We further theorize that servant leadership, by replenishing developmental and socioemotional resources, buffers against the onset of alienation. We test these hypotheses through a scenario experiment (N = 292) and a complementary three-wave field study (N = 500). Results consistently indicated that hierarchical plateau increased work alienation, which in turn predicted greater organizational deviance. Importantly, servant leadership attenuated the positive relationship between plateau and alienation, thereby mitigating the indirect effect. This research extends the plateau literature by mapping a pathway from structural constraints to behavioral risks and highlights leadership as a key resource-replenishing mechanism.
