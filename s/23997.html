Joint Ownership Logics: Understanding Governance Choices in Alliances

STR
Jessenia Davila (Universidad Pontificia Comillas); Anabel Mendoza López (Carlos III University of Madrid); Marta M Elvira (IESE Business School); Africa Arino (IESE Business School)
This study examines how dyadic ownership configurations shape alliance governance choices between equity joint ventures (JVs) and non-equity contractual alliances (CAs). Integrating transaction cost economics with socioemotional wealth (SEW) logic, we argue that alliance governance is jointly negotiated and depends on the ownership composition of both partners, rather than the focal firm alone. We theorize that family–family (F–F) dyads are more likely to form JVs because shared SEW priorities heighten concerns for control, relational safeguarding, and long-term commitment, even at the expense of strategic flexibility. We further propose that governance-related cognitive frame similarity amplifies this preference by reinforcing shared interpretations of control and stewardship. Using data on 260 alliances involving Spanish firms (2007–2022), we find support for these arguments. Our findings advance alliance governance theory by highlighting the joint role of ownership logics and cognitive alignment in shaping interfirm governance choices.
