Can AI Mitigate Cross-Cultural Barriers? Relationship Learning and International Innovation

IM
Yulong Du (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Sihan Li; Kee-hung Lai (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Xiaobo Wu
This study examines the impact of relationship learning on innovation performance in the context of the intersection between cross-cultural and emerging technologies, focusing on the underlying mechanisms of relationship learning and the dual moderating effects of cultural distance and AI adoption. Drawing on survey data from Chinese firms engaged in cross-border collaborations,empirical results show that joint sense-making partially mediates the relationship between information sharing and relationship-specific memory, which in turn mediates the effects of both information sharing and joint sense-making on innovation performance. Importantly, cultural distance exerts an asymmetric moderating effect: it significantly weakens the impact of joint sense-making on relationship-specific memory, while its effect on information sharing is comparatively weak. Further analyses show that AI adoption selectively mitigates this negative effect of cultural distance by supporting joint sense-making, but does not significantly alter the information sharing–based learning pathway. Robustness checks using alternative Hofstede-based cultural distance indices, instrumental-variable approaches to address the potential endogeneity of AI adoption, and bootstrap analyses confirm the stability of the findings. By distinguishing between different relationship learning mechanisms, this study advances understanding of how cultural distance and AI jointly shape inter-organizational learning and innovation, and offers actionable insights for firms seeking to leverage AI to overcome cross-cultural barriers in global collaboration.

From Galápagos to DeepSeek: Experiential–Discursive Decoupling in Technology Ecosystems

IM
W. G. Will Zhao (University of Waterloo); Lina Ba (University of Bath School of Management)
The fragmentation of the global economy has become a defining challenge for international business scholarship, yet existing frameworks focus predominantly on value chain restructuring driven by geopolitical pressure. This paper extends decoupling theory to technology ecosystems, where fundamentally different dynamics operate. We introduce the concept of ‘experiential–discursive decoupling’ to explain why technology ecosystems that achieve rapid domestic deployment often fail to diffuse internationally, even absent deliberate geopolitical barriers. High domestic institutional integration enables accelerated ecosystem rollout (the experiential dimension) while simultaneously constraining international legitimation and standard adoption (the discursive dimension). We develop a two-by-two framework juxtaposing domestic institutional integration with international standards engagement, demonstrating how different configurations produce varied internationalization outcomes. Drawing on comparative cases spanning mobile telecommunications, platform ecosystems, and artificial intelligence from Japan, South Korea, mainland China, and Taiwan, including the striking contrast between DeepSeek’s open architecture and OpenAI’s current proprietary model. We show that domestic technological success can become a structural impediment to global reach. This institutional trade-off intensifies under geopolitical fragmentation, as technology ecosystems crystallize along bloc lines rather than converging toward global standards. The paper contributes to understanding technological ecosystem leadership in a fragmenting world, with implications for MNE strategy in navigating parallel technological worlds.

Internationalization of Digital Cultural Products: Navigating the Duality of Cultural Foreignness

IM
Yining Luo (Zhejiang University); Wanying Xing (Zhejiang University); Shibo Zhou (Copenhagen Business School); Ziyi Chen (University of Melbourne)
The internationalization of digital cultural products (DCPs) presents a strategic paradox: how to reconcile their digital nature with cultural attributes. While prevailing internationalization perspectives predominantly characterize cultural distance as a friction to be minimized, we propose a nuanced framework by disentangling the construct of foreignness. We argue that at the content level, cultural novelty functions as an asset of foreignness by stimulating curiosity; yet, at the value level, cultural distance remains a liability of foreignness by creating cognitive friction. We test these hypotheses using a longitudinal panel of three globally standardized mobile games across 45 markets from 2020 to 2024. Using two-way fixed effects models, we find robust evidence for this duality. Furthermore, we delineate the boundary conditions of digital transmutability: individualism impedes viral diffusion, while in developed markets present a context where users exhibit heightened sensitivity to cultural friction. Crucially, we reveal the defensive function of social connectedness, which serves as a buffer where network effects override cultural distance. This study advances IB theory by reconceptualizing the duality of foreignness, contextualizing platform strategy within social and economic boundaries, and offering a dynamic resolution to the classic standardization-adaptation dilemma.

Digital Business Environment and the Location Strategy of MNEs:Evidence from the Yangtze River Delta

IM
Sha Xi (Tianjin Chengjian University); Xinyan Wang (Tianjin Chengjian University)
Based on transaction cost and institutional theory, this study discusses the impact of the local digital business environment on the site selection strategy of multinational enterprises. We build a four-dimensional indicator covering digital infrastructure, digital government services, digital regulatory framework and data governance, and use the panel data from 2010 to 2023 in 27 cities in the Yangtze River Delta region of China. Results show that the digital business environment has significantly increased foreign direct investment. Furthermore, the impact of digital government services and infrastructure is the most significant. In addition, the digital business environment has a great impact on foreign direct investment in small cities, playing a strategic leverage role in alleviating the disadvantage of clustering. The model shows that there is a complementary relationship between the development of digital systems and knowledge-intensive assets. By expanding the institutional perspective and transaction cost perspective to the digital environment, it promote the development of location theory and provide practical inspiration for multinational enterprises and local decision-makers.

Culture’s Consequences? Diversity of AI Governance Models Across the Globe

IM
Hien Le (Georgia State University); Charles Dhanaraj (Georgia State University)
Our research focuses on two related questions: How do we frame the governance problem to capture the variance across countries efficiently? And what drives these different governance architectures and their impact on international investment and innovation flows? AI is emerging as the single most important issue worldwide and has induced intense competition among countries. Our research presents a conceptual framework to understand, explain, and predict the direction of evolution of these governance models. The implications of divergence of governance architectures extend far beyond regulatory compliance. They affect where companies choose to invest, how technologies are designed and deployed, which innovations reach the market, and ultimately how AI systems embed different values into sociotechnical infrastructures. By operationalizing governance as a three-dimensional construct, namely, fairness, privacy, and risk, our work provides a realistic platform for positioning the diverse models of governance emerging globally. We show how culture influences the distinctiveness of governance architectures and theorize their economic impact on innovation and investment. Our work presents an early look at the antecedents of evolving governance frameworks and how they will influence international investment and innovation flows.
