Who Owns the Firm? Tracing Caste Divides and Inequality in India’s Unincorporated Enterprise

Author: Pushpendra Singh K J Somaiya Institute of Management

Session overview

The unincorporated sector forms an important part of India’s informal economy that provides work and wage to millions of people outside the formal system. Within this sector, caste and social identity continue to shape ownership and productivity. Caste-based practices are institutionalized by the market economy following systematic exclusion of the marginalised community. Thus, the study examines the influence of caste on enterprise ownership, structure, and outcomes in India’s unincorporated enterprises. Drawing from unit-level data of the National Sample Survey (67th round, 2010-11; 73rd round, 2015-16) and the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) 2021-22 and 2023-24, the study analyses ownership, operation and performance of these enterprises. The findings of the study show that the number of enterprises has increased over time. However, this growth has also augmented inequality in ownership that echoes social hierarchy and institutional structures rather than individual effort. The privileged groups are concentrated around the elevated and well-connected parts of the enterprise economy, while marginalized groups continue to operate in low-capital investment activities having limited scope for expansion. The study suggests that reducing inequality in the unincorporated sector requires broader access to credit and infrastructure, along with institutional reforms that address the social foundations of exclusion.
