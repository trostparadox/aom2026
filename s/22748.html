Creative Ideas in the Making: Understanding the Interpersonal Processes of Idea Elaboration

OB
Rohit Subhash Piplani (University of Melbourne); Ziyue Cui (The University of Connecticut); Travis Grosser (The University of Connecticut)
Despite longstanding interest in creativity and innovation, most management research has focused on idea generation and implementation, with comparatively limited attention given to understanding and measuring how colleagues help employees elaborate early-stage, “half-baked” ideas. Addressing this oversight, the present study conceptualizes idea elaboration as the set of behaviors through which colleagues assist in evaluating, refining, and emotionally supporting others’ emerging creative ideas, and develops a scale to capture this construct. Focusing on Stage 1 of a multistep scale development process, we use qualitative interviews and open-ended surveys to generate items grounded in workplace experiences, followed by systematic item reduction, exploratory factor analysis, and sorting-based content validation. Results indicate that although qualitative findings initially suggested three components, namely constructive evaluation, idea development, and socio-emotional support, exploratory factor analyses support a parsimonious two-dimensional structure comprising idea development and socio-emotional support, and a refined set of items demonstrates strong definitional correspondence and distinctiveness from related constructs. In Stage 2, we will further evaluate the scale’s psychometric properties through confirmatory factor analysis and tests of reliability, convergent validity, discriminant validity, and nomological validity. Together, this research advances theory by formally defining and measuring interpersonal idea elaboration, a critical yet understudied phase of the creative idea journey through which social interaction shapes the development and viability of creative ideas.

Innovating Out of Tension: Conceptualizing and Measuring Paradoxical Innovative Behavior

OB
Jinzhao Qu (Hainan University); Peilin Zuo (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Hang Yang (Renmin University of China); Jiaxin Wang (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Boqiang Zong (Changsha University of Science and Technology)
Prior research suggests that employee innovation increasingly unfolds in contexts of heightened uncertainty driven by digital transformation, globalization, and rapid socio-economic change. Yet existing innovation theories struggle to reconcile a central tension in organizational life: while organizations grant employees autonomy to stimulate innovation, they simultaneously impose accountability structures that constrain innovative action. This unresolved autonomy–accountability tension limits our theoretical understanding of how employee innovation emerges under uncertainty. To address this paradox, we introduce paradoxical innovative behavior (PIB)—a dynamic behavioral pattern through which employees simultaneously exercise individual agency and align with organizational expectations. We conceptualize PIB as a distinct form of bottom-up innovation that enables employees to realize novel ideas while navigating competing demands. Following a rigorous scale development process, we develop and validate a six-item measure of PIB. Results across multiple samples support a robust single-factor structure with strong reliability and construct validity. By reconceptualizing innovative behavior from a paradox perspective and providing a validated measurement instrument, this study advances research on bottom-up innovation and offers a foundation for examining how employees integrate autonomy and accountability in uncertain organizational contexts.

How Does Team Growth Mindset Influence Employee’s Bootleg Innovation?

OB
Yumeng Shao (Southeast University); Lulu Zhou (Southeast University); Xufan Zhang; Decai Wang
Teams serve as fundamental engines of organizational innovation. Within teams, individual bootleg innovation, an informal and covert form of bottom-up innovation, plays a critical role in driving innovation performance. Drawing on implicit theory of cognition and using multi-wave, multi-source survey data from 560 employees across 79 research and development (R&D) teams in high-tech firms in China, this study investigates the cross-level relationship between team growth mindset and employee’s bootleg innovation. The results show that attention allocation which includes two key components, search selection and search intensity, mediates this relationship. We find that search selection demonstrates an inverted U-shaped relationship with bootleg innovation, and this curvilinear pattern is moderated by search intensity. In addition, team collaboration moderates this curvilinear association. These findings extend implicit theory by providing a cross-level explanation of how team cognition translates into individual innovative behavior, reveal the dual effects of attention allocation on bootleg innovation, and identify key boundary conditions shaping these effects. The study also provides actionable insights for managers seeking to cultivate team contexts that stimulate informal innovation.

Creativity-Contingent Rewards and Creativity: Role of Motivational Climate

OB
Nishtha Arora (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Manish Saini (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Abhishek Vilas Sonavane; Arushi Verma (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow)
Work teams rely on rewards to stimulate employee creativity. We examine how team context influences the consequences for rewards and creativity. Drawing on data from a medical device manufacturing organization from India, we examine how and when creativity-contingent rewards (CCRs) influence creativity. Grounded in self-determination and achievement goal theory, we find that CCRs are simultaneously linked to autonomy need frustration and competence need satisfaction. More importantly, their overall impact on creativity depends on the team’s shared understanding what constitutes success in the team. By identifying motivational climate as a lens through which employees interpret contingent rewards, our study offers insight into how the social meaning of rewards influences need satisfaction or frustration, and ultimately creativity.
