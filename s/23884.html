The Curse of the Committed: How Leader Role Identity Exacerbates the Unethical Consequence of Leader

OB
Suosuo Jia (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics); Li Guo (University of International Business and Economics); Ying Zhang (University of International Business and Economics)
Despite leadership’s inherently relational nature, many leaders experience profound loneliness, a reality often captured by the phrase “lonely at the top”. Yet, research has largely overlooked how leaders behaviorally navigate this unique form of social disconnection. Drawing on self-protection theory, we conceptualize leader loneliness as a role-based threat that activates leader’s defensive system, triggering selfishness and subsequent expediency, a subtle form of unethical behavior. Crucially, we further theorize an identity paradox: leader role identity intensifies this defensive process. Using multi-source data from leader-subordinate pairs, we find support for our proposed model. Results confirmed the mediating role of selfishness. Furthermore, results supported the moderating role of leader role identity, indicating that leaders who strongly identify with their role are more likely to exhibit selfishness and expediency than their less identified counterparts. We discuss implications for theory and practice, as well as study limitations and directions for future research.
