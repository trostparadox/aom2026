The Uneven Gains from Crime Reduction: Gendered Labor Market Responses in El Salvador

SIM
Jiang Dai (The University of Edinburgh); Yizhe Dong (The University of Edinburgh); Wenxuan Hou (The University of Edinburgh)
Does crime reduction benefit men and women equally? We exploit El Salvador's 2022 State of Exception—a sudden, nationwide anti-gang crackdown that reduced homicides by over 70%—as a natural experiment. Using administrative data covering the universe of formal firms, we apply a difference-in-differences design based on pre-crackdown crime exposure. Employment in high-crime areas rose by 7%, with gains concentrated among male hires, younger firms, and crime-sensitive sectors. Notably, firms expanded by opening new locations, not merely enlarging existing ones—a response absent under earlier, less decisive interventions. Household survey evidence reveals a persistent gender gap: women's employment gains were smaller and slower, reflecting both a reallocation of domestic responsibilities as male employment rose and stronger frictions limiting their access to formal jobs. These results show that large security improvements stimulate firm growth but do not, on their own, deliver inclusive labor market recovery.
