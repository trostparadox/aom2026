Session overview

Africa is one of the most exciting places to study organizations today: it is a continent defined by fast-moving markets, rapid digital adoption, and some of the most entrepreneurial firms in the world. Firms here innovate while navigating volatility, informal networks, and rapid digitization, offering rare visibility into organizational change (Assenova & Sorenson, 2017; Sydow et al., 2022). This convergence of growth and constraint creates ideal conditions for field experiments and new theory-building (Agarwal et al., 2025). Yet despite this potential, rigorous evidence from African organizations remains scarce (Schimmelpfennig et al., 2025). This panel brings together scholars and industry leaders who have built organizational datasets and run field experiments in African contexts to address core questions in strategy, entrepreneurship, and research methods. The focus is on learning with African partners how organizational adaptation, innovation, and collaboration unfold under conditions that are central to management academics and practitioners, yet underrepresented in existing evidence (George et al., 2016). The symposium will place researchers and African practitioners in direct conversation, and the discussion will focus on two core areas: methods and topics. On the methods side, speakers will discuss what it takes to run rigorous research with firms in Africa—how collaborations are formed, how data access is negotiated, how experiments are implemented in live organizations, and how results are translated into value for both academics and managers. On the topics side, the panel will showcase frontier research themes emerging from African contexts, including digitalization and AI adoption inside firms, productivity and management practices, sustainability and inclusive business models, and the role of mission-driven organizations in expanding access and opportunity.
