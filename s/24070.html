How Customer Climate Risk Drives Supplier Green Innovation in Sustainable Supply Chains?

OSCM
Jing Ning (Ocean University of China); Yahui Xu (Ocean University of China); Yufang Zhang (Ocean University of China)
In the context of mounting climate risks and their cascading effects on supply chains, it is crucial to understand how individual firms' climate risk shapes sustainable transitions. However, existing literature largely views climate risk as a passive stressor, overlooking its signal value in coordinating upstream responses. This study explores the mechanisms through which customer climate risk influences suppliers' green innovation, utilizing 2008-2023 customer-supplier data from Chinese A-share listed firms. Redefining customer climate risk as an active, high-cost strategic signal transmitted through annual report disclosures, the empirical results demonstrate that customer climate risk acts as a significant catalyst for suppliers' green innovation, a process that is partially mediated by the supplier's heightened environmental concern. Furthermore, the analysis of boundary conditions reveals divergent moderating effects: while suppliers' AI adoption amplifies this positive transmission, supply chain diversification functions as a dampening moderator that attenuates the impact. These findings advance the understanding of climate risk from a localized shock to a cascading signaling force, underscore the critical roles of digital decoding capabilities and structural focus in fostering sustainable supply chain transitions.
