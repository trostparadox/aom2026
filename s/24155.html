Commoning Complexity: Organizing Shared Sensor Data for Action Through Relational Infrastructures

CTO
Julia Christis (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Lukas Falcke (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam); Philipp Tuertscher (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam)
Standardized digital data enable collaboration on an unprecedented scale, yet introduce coordination challenges - particularly in data commons where heterogeneous actors must balance collective standardization with individual interests. While standardization requires joint action, premature closure can limit participation and constrain innovation. Recent relational process literature on data identifies two options for addressing standardization challenges and accommodating for actor heterogeneity. One perspective suggests that mismatches between digital representations and physical reality can be aligned through reconciling the data representation itself, while a second perspective emphasizes actors’ ability to overcome discrepancies by developing and reconciling their own data practices. However, neither fully accounts for the socio-materiality of data in commons settings where data are collected and analyzed for both collective and individual interests by many heterogeneous actors. To address this puzzle, we conducted a longitudinal, single-case process study of CoSense, a national sensing community facilitated by the Dutch National Health Institute (HealthGov). Our findings identify three infrastructural mechanisms through which experts and citizens progressively reconcile heterogeneity across data practices, knowledge systems, and authority to co-produce actionable environmental sensor data. Our study bridges the two relational process perspectives, demonstrating a punctuated pathway where communities initially push for standardization, uncover mismatches, then strategically reintroduce heterogeneity across practices, knowledge systems, and authority to reconfigure and re-stabilize standards in the data commons.
