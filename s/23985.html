When Ecosystems Evolve: Survival Modeling of Complementor Entry into Ecosystems Over Time

STR
Alexander Robert Ulrich Göritz (University of St. Gallen)
This study examines when and why complementors enter evolving ecosystems. When complementors can neither reliably predict the benefits of entering an evolving ecosystem nor the (opportunity) costs of (not) entering ex-ante, purely rational cost-benefit calculations prior to entry are quixotic. I argue that the likelihood of complementor entry is subject to between-ecosystem heterogeneity, since ecosystems handle complexity and uncertainty differently. I test data on 30 ecosystems over 20 years on a monthly basis. I find that unique complement variety increases the likelihood of complementor entry by approximately 39% and that prior-month density increases the likelihood of complementor entry by approximately 71%. I also find that these effects decline over time. These findings bear both theoretical and practical implications.
