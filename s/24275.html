High Tech Needs High Touch: Team Emotional Capability and Innovation in Human–AI Collaboration

HR
Yujie Sun (Tongji University); Shumin Yan (Tongji University); Zhidan Tan (China University of Mining and Technology (Xuzhou))
While Generative AI has significantly lowered the barriers to idea generation, the subsequent phase of creative implementation remains fraught with uncertainty and equivocality. How do teams navigate the "black box" of AI outputs to achieve convergence and realizable innovation? Drawing on Sensemaking Theory, this study explicates the micro-foundations of human-AI collaboration. We propose that Team Emotional Capability (TEC) serves as a critical affective antecedent that enables teams to stabilize AI-induced perturbations, thereby facilitating Team Sensemaking and subsequent Agile Taskwork.Using multi-wave, multi-source data collected from 145 teams (N= 453 members) in technology-intensive enterprises, we found that TEC positively influences Team Innovation Performance through a serial mediation pathway: TEC fosters collective sensemaking (cognitive convergence), which in turn triggers agile taskwork (behavioral iteration), ultimately enhancing innovation outcomes. Furthermore, this process is structurally bounded; AI Role Clarity acts as a critical moderator. Specifically, the positive effect of TEC on sensemaking—and its indirect effect on innovation—is strengthened when teams have clear clarity regarding human-AI responsibility boundaries. These findings challenge the techno-centric view by highlighting that the efficacy of hybrid intelligence hinges on a team’s ability to "stabilize" emotions and "structure" roles. The study offers a novel theoretical framework linking affective resources, cognitive processes, and agile behaviors in the era of AI.
