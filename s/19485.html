Systemic Innovation from Collective Inaction: The Case of Japan’s Hydrogen Station Industry

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4

Author: Tomonari Komiyama

Session overview

This study examines how systemic innovation can emerge from collective inaction by analyzing the development of Japan’s hydrogen station industry. Although firms in energy and automotive sectors shared the goal of achieving carbon neutrality, they delayed investment in hydrogen infrastructure because of high costs, uncertain demand for fuel cell vehicles (FCVs), and fear of free-riding, resulting in prolonged stagnation. The case illustrates a classic “chicken-and-egg” dilemma between vehicle diffusion and fueling infrastructure, which cannot be solved by individual firms alone. Using a single-case design, the study investigates the creation of Japan H2 Mobility LLC (JHyM, 2018), a cross-industry platform firm established in 2018 under strong government coordination. Drawing on documentary data, interviews, and long-term participant observation, the analysis identifies three phases: a shift from collective inaction to discussion, from discussion to collective commitment, and finally to coordinated action through the formal establishment of JHyM. Government leadership, public signaling, and subsidy coordination were critical in mitigating first-mover disadvantages and aligning private investment with national climate policy. The cross-industry platform firm functions both as a transaction platform that standardizes contracts and allocates resources, and as an innovation platform that integrates data, promotes knowledge sharing, and optimizes station placement. Together, these roles created positive network effects, accelerated infrastructure construction, and enabled systemic innovation toward a robust hydrogen ecosystem.
