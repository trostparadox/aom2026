Unhealthy Eating Behaviors in Work: A Qualitative Study

Authors: Selin Çulcuoglu Öztürk Bogaziçi University; Ozgun Burcu Rodopman University of South Florida; Zehra Ersahin Social Sciences University of Ankara

Session overview

The aim of this study is to explore the bidirectional relationship between unhealthy eating behaviors and work experiences. To fulfill this aim, 21 semi-structured interviews have been conducted with employees in education sector working in Turkiye. The data obtained from these interviews are thematically analyzed with Gioia’s inductive methodology. Findings of this study present temporal demands, work structures, cognitive absorption and relational stressors that disrupt the eating behaviors of employees. Based on reports of participants, unhealthy eating behaviors were identified not only as consequences of work strain but also as responses given to negative emotions experienced in the workplace. Thus, unhealthy eating was identified by participants as serving the purpose of coping, compensation and control. Findings also show that unhealthy eating behaviors decrease energy levels, work engagement and emotional regulation of employees which in return lead to adaptive reductions in work performance and increase in self-criticism. Consequences of unhealthy behaviors, in turn, have been reported to intensify stress which result in recursive stress-eating-performance cycles. Findings also identify relational, personal and structural resources that interrupt or buffer these stress-eating-performance cycles. Moreover, this study contributes to Organizational Behavior literature by extending Job-Demands Resources Model, Cognitive Resources Theory and Affective Events Theory through conceptualizing unhealthy eating behaviors as a processual and organizationally embedded phenomenon. The findings also highlight the need for organizational interventions that will address the social and structural conditions of work that shape eating behaviors within the workplace.
