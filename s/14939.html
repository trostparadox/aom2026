Session overview

This professional development workshop seeks to bring diverse perspectives on the impact of labor market structures on entrepreneurship into dialogue. A central insight from this literature is that labor market structures influence both the availability and the nature of employment opportunities, thereby shaping their relative attractiveness compared to entrepreneurship (Sørenson and Fassiotto, 2011; Rider et al., 2023). Classic mechanisms introduced by Merton (1938), Stinchcombe (1965), and Light (1972) have inspired a recent surge of work highlighting how employment discrimination (Mariani, 2023; Hwang and Phillips, 2024; Botelho and Abraham, 2017), regulation (Thébaud, 2015; Agarwal et al., 2021; Bao, 2024), and organizational characteristics (Kacperczyk, 2012; Sørenson and Sharkey, 2014) shape opportunity structures and subsequent entrepreneurial entry and outcomes. What has emerged is a rich literature revisiting long-standing mechanism through new theoretical lenses, methodological approaches, and empirical contexts. The goal of this PDW is to create space for forward movement in this research area. In the first part of the PDW, leading scholars will present their emerging work and reflections on the impact of labor market structures on entrepreneurship. In the second part, roundtable discussants will offer feedback on participants’ extended abstracts.
