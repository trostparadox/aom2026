When Does Algorithmic ManagementWork in Manufacturing?

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
OMT

People

Author: Kazuki Kozuka Kyoto University

Session overview

Algorithmic management (AM), which refers to the delegation of management functions such as task allocation, monitoring, evaluation, and reward to algorithms, has become a central feature of digital labor platforms. Such systems are now spreading to manufacturing, but even in factories that have adopted similar technologies, the outcomes vary widely, from strong algorithmic control to resistance and symbolic adoption. This raises a fundamental question: under what conditions does algorithmic management become established as a stable governance regime in production systems? Using survey data from 127 Japanese manufacturing workers and fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA), this study conceptualizes the manufacturing industry as a unique governance regime characterized by stable employment, artisanal skills, and internal labor markets. It then analyzes how organizational structures such as labor mobility, division of labor, centralization, and organizational size determine the feasibility of algorithmic management. The analysis shows that low labor mobility and high division of labor are quasi-necessary conditions for algorithmic management. However, truth table analysis shows that high levels of AM do not stably hold in any empirically meaningful configuration, and the "1" matrix in fsQCA is empty. Although two organizational attractors are identified: small-scale, highly mobile factories and large-scale, centralized, artisanal factories, neither constitutes a stable algorithmic regime. These results reconceptualize algorithmic management in manufacturing as a contested organizational project rather than a technological necessity. Algorithmic control is structurally attractive yet institutionally constrained, highlighting the importance of organizational architecture as a crucial intermediary connecting algorithms and governance outcomes.
