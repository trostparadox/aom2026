When Do Nudges Disrupt Bias? The Role-Stereotyping Gradient in Organizational Role Evaluations

DEI
Kruttika Dash (Tata Institute of Social Sciences); Sasmita Palo (Tata Institute of Social Sciences)
Organizations struggle to reduce implicit gender bias despite decades of diversity initiatives.Traditional interventions like mandatory training often fail or trigger backlash. This study examines whether low-intensity behavioral nudges can reduce gender bias in hiring decisions. We conducted a quasi-experimental field study with 421 final-year business students in India who evaluated candidates for three organizational roles: Analytics Team Lead, Pricing Team Lead, and Corporate Doctor. The treatment group (N=109) received eight email reminders over ten days promoting perspective-taking, individuation, and counter-stereotypical thinking. The control group (N=312) received no intervention. Results show nudges significantly increased female candidate selection for Analytics (59% vs. 45%) and Corporate Doctor (57% vs. 44%) roles, but had no effect for Pricing (49% vs. 46%). We interpret these findings through a role-stereotyping gradient framework: nudges work for moderately stereotyped roles but fail when the illusion of objectivity is strong. This work identifies boundary conditions for nudging effectiveness and raises questions about mechanisms, durability, and generalizability that require field validation with professional samples. We discuss implications for designing context-calibrated bias reduction strategies and outline a research agenda testing mechanisms and field applications. Keywords: implicit bias, nudging, behavioral interventions, gender diversity, hiring decisions, role stereotypes, organizational decision-making
