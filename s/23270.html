Family Roots, Startup Wings: Balancing Control and Growth in Family Startups Seeking VC Financing

ENT
Valerio Pelucco (Luiss university)
Little is known about how family firms finance growth in their early stages. This paper reveals that 5.4% of all VC rounds support family startups. The latter access VC financing later and take longer to deliver investor exits than non-family startups. When seeking VC financing, family startups raise smaller financing rounds, resulting in less dilution and fewer board seats for their investors. Exploring performance outcomes, this paper demonstrates that while investors are less successful when investing in family startups, the latter disproportionally benefit from VC financing, achieving higher revenue growth post-VC financing than non-family startups.

Family Offices in Entrepreneurial Finance: The Role of Ownership Competence (WITHDRAWN)

ENT
Anirudh S (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Sai Chittaranjan Kalubandi (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Rejie George Pallathitta (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Suresh Bhagavatula (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore); Apurva Sanaria (Indian Institute of Management Bangalore)
Family offices have become increasingly active investors in entrepreneurial ventures, yet their venture selection preferences remain unclear. Prior research focuses mainly on professional venture capitalists and corporate VC’s emphasizing on their portfolio-based and strategic investment logics. We argue that family offices invest as owners rather than intermediaries, using ownership competence to choose ventures where which they can actively shape. Conceptualizing ownership competence as developed through long-term ownership of operating firms, we examine how it shapes investment choices across four dimensions: industry relatedness, geographic proximity, investment stage, and syndication. Using a novel dataset of Indian family office investments, we find that family offices with higher ownership competence are more likely to invest in industry-related and geographically proximate ventures, to invest at earlier stages, and to invest without syndication. These findings provide evidence that ownership competence influences how family offices apply judgment in venture investment decisions

Ownership Network Structure in Family and Nonfamily Business Groups

ENT
Bao Wu; Yuxin Zhang (Zhejiang University of Technology); Hanqing Chevy Fang (Missouri University of Science and Technology); Jiaqi Huang; Esra Memili (University of North Carolina at Greensboro)
This study investigates how family and nonfamily business groups differ in the structural design of their ownership networks. Using 31,010 firm-year observations from Chinese listed firms (2010–2022), we construct ego-centered ownership networks for each focal firm and examine three structural dimensions—network size, network control, and network density. We find that family business groups maintain larger, denser, and more control-concentrated ownership networks than nonfamily groups. We also uncover two counterintuitive patterns: later-generation family leaders preserve network size but reduce apex control while increasing network density, and state ownership lowers network size and density yet strengthens family control within a streamlined network. These findings recast ownership networks as dynamic governance architectures shaped by family priorities, intergenerational transitions, and institutional pressures, offering new insights for research on business groups, family firms, and hybrid state–family governance.

Unlocking Willingness to Open Innovation: Family Ownership, R&D Capability, and Tech Competitiveness

ENT
Taewoo Kim (Middle Tennessee State University); Shaomeng Jia (University of Louisiana at Monroe); Veronika Humphries (University of Louisiana at Monroe)
In today’s competitive business landscape, open innovation has evolved from a strategic option to an essential requirement for survival and growth. This imperative is particularly pronounced for family venture firms with constrained resources, yet our understanding of family firms’ open innovation and its underlying determinants remains inadequate. This study addresses this knowledge gap by examining how family ownership is related to firms’ willingness to engage in open innovation. To deepen our understanding of this focal relationship, we further investigate how R&D capabilities and perceived technological competitiveness moderate it. Based on resource dependence theory, we proposed that family ownership is positively related to firms’ willingness to open innovation, and that R&D capabilities and perceived technological competitiveness can enhance this impact. Our empirical analysis using multi-year data from South Korean SMEs reveals the conditional nature of the impact of family ownership and a significant enhancing effect of moderators.
