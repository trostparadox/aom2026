Trust in the Machine? A Cultural Comparison of Students and AI

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 2
IM

People

Author: Yanli Chen American Public University System

Session overview

AI is transforming the way we learn and do our jobs. Managers will use AI extensively in the future. We need to find out how they rate AI data. But we don't know whether culture changes how this works. We merged the "Technology Acceptance Model" with Hofstede's cultural concepts. We examined the influence of cultural values on students' faith in AI. We concentrated on "Power Distance" (deference to authority) and "Uncertainty Avoidance" (apprehension of the unfamiliar). We conducted an experiment using 420 business students. There were 210 people from China and 210 people from the United States. Students read ChatGPT-4's business tips. Then they gave it a score. We saw a dramatic change. Initially, Chinese students trusted the AI more. They thought of the AI as knowledgeable experts. Students in the US were more doubtful. They looked over the AI's work more closely. This is what we mean by "algorithmic authority." People from various cultures interpret AI in different ways. This research contributes to the topic of International Management (IM). It illustrates that people don't always trust AI the same way. It also helps firms throughout the world train their future personnel.
