Humanness as Organizational Work: How Managers Behave in AI-Supported Decision Environments

OB
Maria Carmela Annosi; Davide De Gennaro; Beatrice Isabella Johanna Maria Van Der Heijden (Radboud University Nijmegen); Filomena Buonocore
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly involved in public-sector decision making, raising concerns about the future of human judgment, responsibility, and care. This study asks: how do managers enact humanness in AI-supported decision environments? Drawing on 55 interviews and two focus groups with public managers in Italy, we show that humanness persists not by default but through ongoing organizational work. Through a qualitative bricolage approach, we identify three forms of humanness work: interpretive practices that recontextualize algorithmic outputs, accountability practices that reassert human responsibility, and relational practices that sustain dignity and trust in automated encounters. Our findings advance theory by reframing managerial work under AI as an expanded form of moral and emotional labor, and by conceptualizing humanness as a sociomaterial accomplishment. We offer practical implications for designing AI and supporting managers tasked with preserving the legitimacy of public service in the digital state.
