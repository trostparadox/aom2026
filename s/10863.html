Session overview

Scientific discovery increasingly depends on specialized, capital-intensive research technologies—from advanced microscopes and fabrication facilities to supercomputing infrastructure and AI-based tools. Yet management and strategy research has largely emphasized knowledge and labor inputs to science, leaving the role of scientific capital underexplored. This panel symposium brings together recent research to examine how scientific capital shapes the rate, direction, and organization of science-based innovation. Drawing on novel large-scale data, administrative records, ethnographic fieldwork, and formal theory, the panel highlights three core insights. First, reliance on costly instruments has risen sharply across many scientific fields, reshaping the division of innovative labor between universities, firms, and government core facilities. Second, shocks to the price and availability of material inputs meaningfully affect scientific output and research trajectories. Third, capital-intensive science reorganizes work and expertise, elevating the role of technical occupations and shared facilities as sites of coordination and recombination. Building on these findings, the symposium advances a forward-looking research agenda addressing artificial intelligence as a research technology, public funding constraints, automation, and restrictions on skilled labor mobility.
