Setting Him Up for Success (or Failure): Race and Motivated Evaluations of Male Leaders

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Vivian Xiao University of Washington Bothell; Brian Lowery Stanford University

Session overview

Although displays of dominance are typically thought of as congruent with expectations of male leaders and therefore rewarded, prior work demonstrates that this effect is not uniform across race. In fact, when enacted by Black men, such displays of dominance are seen as overly threatening and punished, while signals of communality—typically seen as incongruent with expectations of male leaders—buffers against this perception of threat. Existing work has suggested that the target man’s race influences the stereotypes associated with him and therefore the responses to dominant versus communal behavior. We build upon this work by suggesting responses to these behaviors are influenced by the perceiver’s race as well. In two studies, we find that among samples of both White and Black perceivers, support for dominant leaders is jointly determined by both the target and the perceiver’s racial group membership. Support for dominant leaders occurs primarily when perceivers evaluate racial in-group members, particularly among perceivers who are most motivated to support their racial in-group and the group’s interests—highly racially identified perceivers. We suggest that this pattern of evaluation reflects a motivated process; by supporting “strong,” dominant leaders from the racial in-group but not a racial out-group, perceivers act to support their own racial in-group’s interests. We thus highlight a heretofore unexamined factor in determining how male leaders of different races are evaluated—shared racial group membership between perceiver and target—opening up new avenues for research on leader evaluation at the nexus of race and gender.
