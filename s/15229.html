Too Nice to be Noticed? When Warmth and Competence Collide in Digital Recruitment

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3

People

Authors: chenyan wang Nanjing University; Yangyi Chen Nanjing University

Session overview

This study integrates Signaling Theory and the Stereotype Content Model to investigate how employer brand signals—warmth and competence—influence recruitment attractiveness. Leveraging NLP and machine learning to analyze 5,000 real-world job postings, we examine how organizational characteristics moderate these symbolic effects. Our findings reveal that while competence signals consistently drive applicant attraction, the efficacy of warmth is highly contingent upon industry context. Specifically, we identify a "Warmth Trap" in tech-oriented sectors, where excessive warmth signals inadvertently deter applicants by suggesting a lack of professional rigor. Conversely, firm size acts as a secondary boundary condition, with signaling effects remaining largely universal across scales. By uncovering these systematic contextual differences, this research identifies the critical interpretive frames that dictate the success of employer brand signals in the digital talent market.
