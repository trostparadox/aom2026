Leader Integrity and Innovative Work Behavior: Resilience as an Adaptive Pathway

Session overview

This study examines how leader integrity influences innovative work behavior (IWB) through employee resilience. Although prior research has identified numerous leadership antecedents of IWB, less attention has been paid to foundational leadership qualities that shape employees’ willingness and capacity to persist in innovation under uncertainty and potential failure. Drawing on social exchange theory, we propose that employees who perceive their leaders as consistent and morally grounded are more likely to develop resilience, an adaptive capacity that enables persistence through setbacks and sustained engagement in discretionary, failure-prone behaviors such as innovation. Using triadic (n = 230), multi-source data collected in Montenegro (employees, supervisors, and family members), the study significantly reduces same-source inflation by separating the measurement of leader integrity, employee resilience, and innovative work behavior across independent sources. Results confirm that leader integrity is positively associated with employee resilience, which mediates its effect on IWB. By identifying resilience as a key explanatory mechanism, the study shifts attention from predominantly relational explanations of integrity-based leadership toward employees’ adaptive capacity to cope with uncertainty and setbacks. These findings underscore the role of integrity-based leadership and adaptive employee capacities in fostering innovation. Overall, the findings highlight integrity-based leadership as a critical antecedent of employees’ adaptive capacity, which in turn supports sustained innovative work behavior.
