Session overview

A new field is emerging: polycrisis. It transforms the entrepreneurial landscape by showing how crises now cascade and intertwine, eliminating recovery time and continuously exhausting resources, a pattern intensifying worldwide. Yet entrepreneurship research confronts two critical blind-spots in understanding how entrepreneurs navigate these conditions. First, scholarship applies one-size-fits-all approaches when ecosystems, organizations, and individual-founders each demand distinct strategies and resources. Second, studies document entrepreneurial navigation mechanisms like innovation, collaboration, and pivoting, while ignoring the hidden factors that stimulate entrepreneurs to implement these mechanisms in the first place. This PDW bridges entrepreneurship research with psychology, strategy, organizational behavior, and ecosystem literatures to capture the full spectrum of forces driving entrepreneurial action under polycrisis. These span internal processes shaping decisions under uncertainty, identity transformations enabling adaptation, creative thinking turning scarcity into opportunity, and community embeddedness creating resilience when formal structures collapse or entrepreneurial mechanisms prove insufficient. As entrepreneurs face depleted funds, reluctant investors, and disappearing collaborators, actionable frameworks are imperative for explaining which uncharted forces can be leveraged and how to shape navigation strategies at each level. The novelty of the topic and format lies in the innovative walk-in structure where participants rotate through facilitated stations using flip-charts and sticky notes to explore hidden factors across macro, meso, and micro levels. This design fosters interaction amongst participants and promotes inter-disciplinary bridge-building, creating a collaborative research community spanning countries and universities. The anticipated breadth of interest encompasses scholars united in identifying and articulating the hidden forces shaping customized responses to polycrisis. -
