Anthropomorphism and Fairness in AI-Mediated Feedback : The Role of Mind Perception and Autonomy

OB
Yasir Mushtaq (University of Massachusetts Lowell)
As organizations increasingly delegate performance evaluation to artificial intelligence (AI) systems, critical tension emerges between managerial control and employee autonomy. This study addresses the paradox by examining how anthropomorphic design cues in AI feedback systems shape employees’ perceptions of procedural fairness and trust, contingent on perceived autonomy. In a between-subjects online experiment (N = 184), participants interacted with either an anthropomorphic or a non-anthropomorphic AI assistant in a simulated workplace scenario. Anthropomorphism significantly heightened perceptions of agency and experience. Perceived agency robustly predicted procedural fairness and trust in AI, whereas perceived experience influenced fairness only when autonomy was high. Moderated mediation analyses revealed that anthropomorphism affected perceived fairness indirectly through mind perception, with autonomy moderating the experiential pathway. These findings extend Mind Perception Theory by differentiating cognitive and affective routes to fairness and refine Self-Determination Theory by demonstrating autonomy’s buffering role against the backfiring of affective anthropomorphism.

Human-AI Trust: Theory Development and Empirical Examination in Organizational Contexts

OB
Yingyao Yang (Wuhan University); Yanping Li (Wuhan University)
With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, trust plays a crucial role in enabling employees to cope with the uncertainty and unpredictability inherent in AI systems. In organizational contexts, trust facilitates effective human–AI collaboration and mitigates potential risks associated with AI adoption. This study reconceptualizes human–AI trust and advances a “bidirectional–integrative” theoretical framework that captures the reciprocal trust dynamics between employees and AI. Study 1 defined the concept of human–AI trust from a bidirectional mutual trust perspective and identified its three-dimensional structure (employees’ cognitive trust and affective trust in AI, and feeling trusted by AI). Study 2 developed and validated the scale of human–AI trust with three-dimensional structure to organizational contexts. Study 3 investigated the internal relationships among the three dimensions and assessed the predictive validity of the scale with respect to relevant working outcomes (acceptance attitude, continuous use intention, and employee–AI collaboration). This research enriches the research of human-AI trust, providing a foundation for future theoretical research and offering scientific guidance for enterprises to understand and enhance the trust between employees and AI.

How and When Employee-AI Cooperation and Competition Influence Employee Quiet Quitting

OB
Juan Wang; Wenxiu You (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Ziteng Wang (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
In the wave of the fourth industrial revolution, artificial intelligence (AI) is fundamentally reshaping the logic of human-AI interaction. Drawing on the appraisal theory of emotion, this study examines how employee-AI goal interdependence (cooperation and competition) influences quiet quitting through joy and anger. We further investigate the moderating effects of AI intelligence and AI empathy on these relationships. A multi-method approach was adopted, including a laboratory experiment using FaceReader (N = 64) and a three-wave empirical questionnaire survey (N = 394). Results show that employee-AI cooperation negatively predicts quiet quitting, while employee-AI competition positively predicts this behavior. Specifically, employees’ joy mediates the negative relationship between cooperation and quiet quitting, whereas employees’ anger mediates the positive relationship between competition and quiet quitting. Furthermore, AI intelligence strengthens the positive effect of employee-AI cooperation and joy, thereby intensifying the indirect effect of joy in the above relationship. AI empathy weakens the positive effect of employee-AI competition on anger, thereby attenuating the indirect effect of anger in such a relationship. This research enriches the literature on human-AI interaction and offers practical guidance for managers to achieve “AI for good”.

Employee-AI Collaboration and Covert Competition: A Dual-Strategy Perspective of Social Hierarchy

OB
Chao Zhang (Lanzhou University of Technology); Yi Chen (Renmin University of China)
With the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) in organizations, employee-AI collaboration has become a crucial mode of task accomplishment. Existing research has primarily focused on its impacts on task-oriented outcomes such as performance, innovation, and procrastination, while paying insufficient attention to how such collaboration reshapes employees’ interpersonal behaviors in the workplace, particularly competition. To address this gap, our study focused on the core question of how human-AI relationships shape interpersonal relationships. Drawing on the dual strategies theory of social hierarchy, we developed a serial moderated mediation model, aiming to explore how and when employee-AI collaboration affects employees’ covert competitive behaviors toward colleagues. Using a three-wave survey of 302 Chinese employees, the results reveal that, on the one hand, employee-AI collaboration enhances individuals’ role breadth self-efficacy, which subsequently strengthens their tendency to adopt prestige-based strategies, thereby promoting covert competition. On the other hand, employee-AI collaboration also elicits a sense of identity threat, which suppresses employees’ use of dominance-based strategies and consequently leads to an increase in covert competition. Furthermore, the study demonstrates that leaders’ AI symbolization not only positively moderates the effects of employee-AI collaboration on role breadth self-efficacy and identity threat, but also amplifies the serial mediation effects by reinforcing these underlying mechanisms. Our study shifts the focus of employee-AI collaboration research from task-related outcomes to interpersonal relationship-oriented outcomes. We enrich the application of the dual strategies theory of social hierarchy in the AI era and provides practical guidance to manage AI-driven changes in workplace interpersonal relationships.
