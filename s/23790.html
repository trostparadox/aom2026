Metricized Scaffolds: AI-Enabled HR Training Drives Transfer Exploration-Exploitation Dynamics

HR
Nina Xie (Lingnan University); Yuanyuan Claire Wang (Hong Kong Metropolitan University); Yujun Liu (Lingnan University); Yiduo WANG (Lingnan University); Xutang TONG (Lingnan University)
AI-enabled simulation platforms are increasingly used in Human Resource Development (HRD) to practice interpersonal capabilities such as difficult conversations, negotiation, and consultation. These systems act as metricized scaffolding: they elicit repeated performances, convert them into scores and rubric-like diagnostics, and provide rapid cues for revision. A central HRD challenge is whether improvement inside a training environment becomes transferable capability—competence that holds when the dashboard disappears. Integrating scaffolding logic with exploration–exploitation learning dynamics, we argue that metricized simulations readily accelerate exploitative refinement (routine improvement under clear criteria), but that transfer in complex interpersonal work increasingly requires exploration—reframing, probing, and adaptive judgment. To explain when algorithmic scaffolding “fades” into self-regulation, we introduce Algorithmic Feedback Literacy (AFL): learners’ capability to interpret and calibrate algorithmic feedback and translate cues into portable self-scaffolds (e.g., scripts, checklists, practice routines). We examine this model in an AI-enabled virtual internship and career development program that pairs repeated simulation practice with a subsequent authentic consultation assessment. Using platform performance traces, instructor-rated transfer outcomes, and 102 learner reflections, we relate practice exposure and within-platform gains to transfer and surface two developmental pathways— productive internalization versus metric adaptation—that help explain when metricized improvement travels beyond the evaluation system.
