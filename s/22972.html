Re-Writing the Script: The Impact of #MeToo on the Evaluation of Gender Representation in Movies

OMT
Arnaud Cudennec; Lionel Paolella (University of Cambridge); Amanda Sharkey (University of Notre Dame)
How do social movements reshape market judgments? Movements like #MeToo aim to reconfigure cultural norms and standards, potentially increasing positivity toward products that embody movement-aligned norms and/or negativity toward those that do not. These impacts are filtered through heterogeneous audiences who may amplify, attenuate, or resist calls for change. Using a unique longitudinal dataset of movie ratings and textual reviews from 20,000 users on Sens Critique—a major French rating platform—spanning 2009 to 2024, this study investigates how #MeToo influenced gender bias in movie evaluations. We compare ratings of movies with a high representation of women or men, respectively, against those with demographically balanced casts. We find a significant penalty for movies with women-led casts prior to the onset of #MeToo, as well as a significant premium for movies that are male-led. However, #MeToo marks a significant cultural inflection point for women-led films: evaluations of these cultural products improve dramatically thereafter relative to demographically balanced films, especially among female, younger, and urban evaluators. In contrast, evaluations of male-led films do not differ pre- vs. post- #MeToo; in both periods they receive higher ratings than demographically balanced movies. These findings bridge research on social movements and social evaluations, showing how normative shifts differentially affect movement-aligned versus movement-resistant products and underscoring the uneven cultural consequences of social movements across heterogeneous audiences.

“In the Sanctuary of My Window” : Women’s Underground Qarzolhasane Circles in Iran

OMT
Amin Yousefi (Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University)
How do women build collective protection when authoritarian religious patriarchy makes formal organizing unsafe? Drawing on around 100 in-depth interviews with Iranian women involved in solidarity circles—informal, often underground mutual-aid groups—we build on social nonmovement theory to develop an organizational account of women’s activism in Muslim societies. We identify three mechanisms of sanctuary building that make women’s protective organizing durable under authoritarian patriarchy: repurposing legitimate community sites (like mosques) to coordinate, diffusing through empathy rather than ideology, and converting mutual backing into everyday bargaining power in the household. We conceptualize sanctuary as converting constraint without contestation—by retreating from institutional norms rather than complying with them. Sanctuary serves both as a refuge from paternalistic and religious/conservative social norms and as a refuge for individuals committed to a more indigenous feminist-progressive set of values. Building on these insights, we also contribute to research on women’s activism in Muslim societies by reframing religion from a doctrinal constraint to an infrastructural host for nonconforming organizing. By inhabiting religiously legitimate venues, women convert sanctioned women-only association into an organizational scaffold for everyday nonconforming feminist activism.

This Body Will Not Obey: The 46-Year of Struggle for Legitimacy in Iranian Women’s Resistance

OMT
Mehri E. Baloochi (University of Alberta)
Reading legitimacy through the lens of power relations reveals that power and legitimacy operate through co-constitutive, self-reinforcing dynamics. Power relations determine evaluative criteria, shape perceptions of appropriateness, and suppress alternatives, while legitimacy justifies and reproduces these power relations when actors work through imposed definitions and comply with the prescribed social order. This raises a critical puzzle: How can those disadvantaged in power relations challenge and reshape legitimacy when powerful actors construct legitimacy arrangements and ensure compliance? I examine women’s 46-year contestation of compulsory veiling in Iran (1979-2025), where the state staked its religious legitimacy on women’s visible compliance. Using historical and critical discourse analysis across documents, images, speeches, and videos, I identify three legitimacy practices through which disadvantaged actors challenge power-laden arrangements. Disrupting delegitimization destabilizes prescribed criteria through embodied performances; demanding legitimacy asserts alternative criteria while seeking validation; enacting legitimacy performs alternative realities with no appeal to power, rendering power’s evaluative authority irrelevant. This study contributes to legitimacy literature by demonstrating how oppressed actors can construct alternative legitimacy criteria through embodied practices and transform evaluative relationships. The findings also reframe institutions as ongoing performative accomplishments requiring continuous enactment rather than stable cognitive schemas.

Managing Deviance: Toward a Theory of Strategic Illegitimacy

OMT
Nathan Mortkovitch (Saint Mary's College of California)
We develop a theory of strategic illegitimacy: calibrated, publicly justified departures from institutional prescriptions that enable learning and scaling before rules settle. We reconceive illegitimacy not as a state to be repaired but as a dynamic, managerially orchestrated strategy characterized by three dimensions: magnitude, timing, and authorization. We specify three governance mechanisms: impression management, stakeholder mobilization, and regulatory arbitrage, and three critical contingencies: audience attention, stakeholder perception, and regulation ambiguity that collectively determine when deviance yields innovative value versus coordinated sanction. Our model offers a novel answer to a central paradox at the intersection of institutional theory and the innovation literature by detailing the socio-cognitive and structural processes through which actors secure provisional tolerance to convert experimental nonconformity into durable, codified standards.

Masking to Conform: An Emergent Theory of Neurodeviance

OMT
Walker Ray Dornisch (Royal Holloway - University of London)
Despite increasing interest in neurodiversity at work, there is limited extant literature about the institutional-level forces that motivate neurodivergent people to conform by masking. Accordingly, this paper considers how neurodivergent individuals are perceived, labelled, and sanctioned as deviant according to societal expectations of conformity. Informed by Durkheim’s theory of social solidarity, Merton’s strain theory, and Becker’s labelling theory, we theorise that neurodivergence is a unique form of deviance (i.e. neurodeviance) that originates from society rather than from individuals. We argue that neurodivergent individuals who mask to conform and avoid the perceived threat of social sanctions from deviant labels inadvertently deviate from the authentic self, whereas those who unmask deviate from society. In either case, neurodivergent individuals experience strain from structural pressures, which varies according to whether their societies are characterised as mechanical (i.e. similar) or organic (i.e. different) and is moderated by organisational climates for inclusion. We identify four types of neurodeviance produced by society: disavowed neurodeviance, covered neurodeviance, uncovered neurodeviance, and avowed neurodeviance. Our conceptual model reveals how societies motivate neurodivergent individuals to choose between conflicting ends (i.e. individual type vs collective type), and the inevitability of deviance (i.e. deviant to self vs deviant to society). Our study introduces theories of deviance to neurodiversity at work scholarship and advances understanding of concealable stigmatised identities, masking, and disclosure. We conclude by proposing neurodeviance as a distinct subfield within the neurodiversity at work scholarship and call for further inquiry into the mechanisms of organisational climates for inclusion as moderators of neurodeviance.
