Learning From Ingroup ‘Loyal Skeptic’ LLM Agents Reduces Motivated Reasoning on Polarized Issues

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM

Authors: Yuanze Liu University of Chicago; Joshua Jackson University of Chicago

Session overview

Motivated reasoning sustains polarization by encouraging people to apply asymmetric epistemic standards, scrutinizing uncongenial claims while accepting congenial ones. We propose a role-model learning account: identity can function as a motivational resource for epistemic rigor when high standards are modeled by legitimate ingroup members. To test this mechanism in a controlled and scalable way, we introduce loyal skeptic large language model (LLM) role models, ingroup-identified agents that affirm shared identity while consistently demonstrating fact-based reasoning, including calibrated uncertainty, evidence seeking, and openness to disconfirming evidence. LLM agents are well suited for this purpose because they can be programmed to follow the same epistemic standard with perfect consistency, sustain repeated interactions without fatigue, and deliver identity-aligned cues while holding interaction structure constant, enabling clean tests of identity alignment in norm learning and allowing us to curate stable “golden set” stimuli for subsequent human interventions. In a preregistered computational simulation, LLMs were configured as partisan learners and exposed to one of three conditions: an ingroup loyal skeptic teacher, a neutral teacher, or no teacher. Learners evaluated congenial, uncongenial, and neutral statements across baseline, learning, and transfer stages. Results show strong baseline motivated reasoning and a pronounced condition-by-stage pattern: learners exposed to ingroup loyal skeptics display reliably higher fact-based reasoning in transfer than neutral-teacher or control conditions, whereas neutral teachers yield limited durable effects. These findings motivate ongoing human experiments embedding identity-aligned AI role models to shift epistemic norms in polarized contexts.
