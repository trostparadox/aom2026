Session overview

OSCM educators are confronted with mounting pressure to redesign teaching as artificial intelligence redefines operational decision-making and sustainability constraints reshape traditional performance trade-offs. Yet many educators lack structured pedagogical tools that preserve academic rigor while responding to rapid technological and institutional change. Moreover, the growing use of AI in teaching raises critical concerns related to AI literacy, algorithmic bias, transparency, and the responsible use of AI tools, highlighting the need for educators to understand both the capabilities and limitations of AI systems (Raman et al., 2024). Addressing these challenges requires strengthening faculty competencies, particularly pedagogical, digital, and AI-related skills, as emphasized in recent research on higher education teaching competences and AI-integrated instructional frameworks (Moreira et al., 2023; Ng et al., 2023; Ning et al., 2024), which together provide a foundational baseline to guide the development of AI-competencies for OSCM scholars. The proposed PDW responds by providing accessible, discipline-specific teaching designs and decision frameworks that OSCM educators can readily implement and tailor to their courses. It presents three strategies: (1) an AI Factory Simulation that supports experiential learning around AI-enabled operational decision-making; (2) an MVP 2×2 co-delivery governance matrix designed to identify when practitioner involvement enhances educational value while maintaining faculty control over learning outcomes and assessment; and (3) a competency-building toolkit for educators that addresses issues of reliability, integrity, and transparency. The session is highly interactive and allows participants to work hands-on with templates by adapting each tool to their own course contexts. Hence, by the end of the PDW, participants will leave with ready-to-use materials, including a simulation skeleton, a co-delivery charter, and a short-term AI teaching adoption plan. The PDW is designed for OSCM faculty, doctoral instructors, and curriculum leaders seeking future-oriented yet academically robust pedagogical approaches. It aligns with OSCM’s mission to bridge research, practice, and education while strengthening the legitimacy of OSCM teaching in the age of AI and sustainability. Each workshop component combines brief framing inputs with structured small-group work, ensuring peer learning and the production of tangible outputs within the session timeframe.
