Aligning Without Hierarchies - A Framework for Collective Sensemaking in DAOs

Author: Elisa Sauerbier University of Mannheim

Session overview

In an era marked by institutional distrust and systemic complexity, Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) have emerged as radical experiments in post-bureaucratic organizing. Operating without managerial hierarchies, these blockchain-enabled communities coordinate collective action through smart contracts and token-based governance, spanning contexts from investment networks to social-impact collectives. Yet DAOs face a persistent paradox: despite high procedural formalization through code, most struggle to sustain trust, alignment, and shared purpose among pseudonymous members. This paper explores how sensemaking processes substitute for managerial coordination in such settings. Drawing on organizational sensemaking theory, I investigate how DAOs mobilize cultural and meaning systems - narratives, rituals, and value discourses - as coordination devices in the absence of hierarchy. Conceptually, the study shifts focus from structural determinism to cultural contingency. Empirically, it builds on a qualitative single-case study of AthenaDAO which focuses on advancing women’s health research through blockchain-based collaboration. Through semi-structured interviews with active members across different working groups, the study seeks to trace how sensemaking dynamics stabilize expectations, purpose, and sustain collective commitment in pseudonymous, globally distributed contexts. As a work in progress, this study is in its early data collection phase. Anticipated contributions include extending sensemaking theory to digitally native, post-bureaucratic organizations and identifying cultural substitutes for formal authority in extreme decentralization. The findings will advance organization design scholarship by articulating how “we-ness” and coordination emerge through distributed sensemaking rather than managerial oversight in algorithmically governed organizations.
