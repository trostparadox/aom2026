Resource Scarcity Shapes Work–Family Opportunity Cost and Temporal Focus

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Authors: Yi-Ren Wang Asia School of Business; Michael Thomas Ford University of Alabama

Session overview

Individuals in dual-earner, interdependent relationships must continuously adapt not only to their own work-related resource constraints but also to those experienced by their partners. Drawing on Resource Scarcity and Interdependence Theory perspectives, this study examines how personal and partner resource constraints shape temporal focus, work–family opportunity cost consideration, and goal orientations. Results based on two-wave data from 270 heterosexual couples, analyzed with the Actor–Partner Interdependence Model, reveal that work overload, financial stress, and powerlessness are associated with distinct cognitive shifts, including narrowed temporal focus and heightened consideration of opportunity costs in work–family trade-offs. These cognitive shifts predict stronger proof or avoidance goal orientations and, in turn, relate to engagement in self-development and psychological distress. Actor effects were robust for both husbands and wives. Partner effects were asymmetrical: wives were more sensitive to husbands’ resource constraints, showing increased work-family opportunity cost consideration, whereas husbands were more responsive to wives’ temporal focus shifts, exhibiting higher avoidance goal orientation. Additionally, partners’ goal orientation and work-family efficacy cross over to affect each other’s psychological distress. Together, these findings illuminate how resource constraints affect dual-earner couples and cross over to influence partners’ cognition, goal regulation, and mutual adaptation under conditions of scarcity.
