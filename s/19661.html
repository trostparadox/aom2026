Sustainability Inattention in Routine Purchases: Eye-Tracking Evidence from Yogurt Choices

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM

Author: Nelyda Campos-Requena Universidad del Desarrollo

Session overview

Sustainability cues, such as organic certification and recyclable packaging labels, are increasingly embedded in firms’ environmental, social, and governance (ESG) communications. Yet, in routine low-involvement purchases, these cues apparently have modest effects on choices. This behavior could be explained by inattention that makes ESG cues weak even when consumers report pro-environmental attitudes. Building on bounded rationality and attribute processing strategies, this study assesses (in)attention to sustainability cues using a discrete choice experiment (DCE) combined with screen-based eye tracking. To do so, the study employs a between-subjects design that manipulates the presentation format of a yogurt product: approximately half of the participants complete the DCE with attribute information displayed in text-only format, while the remainder receive image-supported cues for the same attributes. Each participant evaluates six choice sets (one block from a 24-block design), choosing among three yogurt alternatives and a no-buy option. Alternatives vary in price, flavor, nutritional information, and environmental attributes, including organic certification and recyclability of packaging. Eye tracking provides objective measures of attention at the attribute level (e.g., dwell time and fixation counts), enabling the identification of visual attribute non-attendance. Using random utility models accounting for ANA, we examine whether ESG cues are systematically screened out relative to standard product attributes and whether image formats increase attention to ESG cues and reduce cognitive burden. The study contributes by providing process evidence on how ESG product signals compete for attention in routine markets and by deriving implications for ESG communication design.
