Digital Technologies and Strategic Flexibility in SME Internationalization: Integrating Real Options

IM
Jamshid Alinasab (Jamshid Alinasab); Seid Mohammad Reza Mirahmadi; Zahra Darwish Motevalli (University of Mazandaran)
The rapid diffusion of digital technologies is reshaping how small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) internationalize, raising new questions about strategic flexibility. This systematic literature review integrates real options and effectuation perspectives to explain how digital tools – including e-commerce platforms, analytics, artificial intelligence, and digital marketplaces – influence SMEs’ ability to create, preserve, and adjust internationalization opportunities. Drawing on 112 sources (2015–2025), we synthesize evidence that digital technologies lower entry costs and risk, thereby functioning as embedded real options, while also enabling effectual behaviors such as iterative market experimentation and stakeholder co-creation. However, digitalization can introduce new constraints (e.g., platform lock-in, algorithmic overconfidence) that may erode flexibility. We develop an integrative framework illustrating how digital dynamic capabilities mediate the relationship between technology adoption and strategic flexibility under varying contextual conditions. Providing a set of theoretical contributions and identifying research gaps, this review calls for multi-method longitudinal studies to estimate the boundary conditions under which digitalization enhances versus constrains SME agility in international markets. Implications include guidance for managers to leverage digital tools for option-rich, reversible international strategies and for policymakers to support SME digital capacity while mitigating lock-in risks.
