A Self-Verification Approach to LGB Workplace Emotions

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
DEI

People

Authors: Rajdeep Kaur Auburn University; Jasmine Nicole Kelley University of Tennessee at Chattanooga

Session overview

We investigate, using self-verification theory, how internalized lesbian, gay, and bisexual (LGB) identity attitudes influence emotional experiences of shame and pride. Self-verification theory suggests that individuals are motivated to maintain consistent self-views by seeking feedback that confirms their existing self-perceptions, whether positive or negative. Positive internalized attitudes, such as identity affirmation and centrality, can lead to experiences of authentic pride when supported by workplace interactions. Conversely, negative attitudes like internalized homophobia or concealment motivation may increase feelings of shame, undermine emotional health and reduce productivity. We propose that two key workplace factors, outness (openness about one’s sexual orientation) at work and perceived respect, moderate the relationship between internalized identity attitudes and emotional responses. Specifically, higher levels of outness may strengthen the positive relationship between negative internalized attitudes and feelings of shame, and the relationship between positive internalized attitudes and feelings of pride. Additionally, perceived respect from colleagues and supervisors may reduce the negative impact of shame and strengthen the positive effect of pride, thereby increasing overall well-being and performance. In this paper, we build a theoretical framework for understanding the emotional dynamics of LGB identity in the workplace. We highlight the relationship between internalized attitudes and emotional experiences of shame and pride, suggesting practical implications for fostering inclusive workplaces that enable LGB employees to thrive.
