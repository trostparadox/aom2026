Session overview

This professional development workshop equips Organization Development and Change (OD&C) practitioners, internal consultants, coaches, and HR/people leaders with a practical, evidence-informed method for diagnosing the covert dynamics that shape overt organizational behavior. Building on Noumair, Pfaff, St. John, Gipson, and Brazaitis (2017), participants learn an expanded systems psychodynamic framework and a structured diagnostic tool, X-Ray Vision, that bridges scholarship and practice through concept definitions, key references, guiding principles, and field-tested questions. Through brief inputs, guided practice, and reflective dialogue, attendees translate core concepts (anxiety, defenses, roles, authority, boundaries, leadership, and intergroup relations) into inquiry prompts for interviews, observation, and document review. They practice linking “what is happening” (interaction patterns, decision bottlenecks, conflict cycles, performance dips, and change resistance) with “what it may mean” (covert dynamics that organize experience and action). A central focus is strengthening self-as-instrument: using one’s emotional and somatic responses as data, while avoiding over-personalization by triangulating self-data with other sources. Participants apply the X- Ray Vision tool to a case vignette and to a current challenge from their own context, generating a working diagnostic hypothesis, prioritized next-step questions, and ethical considerations for intervention and feedback in complex systems. Participants leave with (1) a concept-to-citation map that reconnects practice with theory; (2) a process for collecting, integrating, and testing hypotheses across multiple data sources; (3) a customizable diagnostic worksheet for immediate use on a live case; and (4) language for communicating findings in ways that are rigorous, compassionate, and action- oriented. The X-Ray Vision tool supports clearer contracting, sharper sensemaking, and more sustainable, ethically grounded change interventions.
