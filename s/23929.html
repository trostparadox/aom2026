Degradation as a Strategic Innovative Resource

TIM
José Osvaldo De Sordi; Carlos Rigato; MARCO ANTONIO SILVEIRA (Universidade de Marilia (UNIMAR)); Marcia Carvalho De Azevedo
In contrast with the literature in the field of management, which portrays degradation as being exclusively negative, which ought to be avoided and combated by managers, the present study examines experiences of the degradation of products, services and processes instituted by organizations of their own accord. For this purpose, we conducted interviews exploring degradation experienced by 20 managers working with large corporations. The information collected was analyzed using content analysis techniques and analytical techniques from the Gioia methodology. The analyses highlight the degradation heuristic as an innovative tactic, with strategic potential, providing positive results for both companies and society. A set of attributes capable of distinguishing the degradation heuristic from other similar ones was identified, namely those that downgrade products, services or processes, but without reversibility, that is, the ability to revert to the original level of performance (upgrade). The analyses demystify some managers’ cognitive biases, such as degradation only applying to digital products and services with high added value. From the analyses and discussion, it appears that the degradation heuristic must be perceived by managers as useful and valid as the more traditional and widespread heuristics, such as adaptation, frugalism, copying, exaptation and improvement. In this respect, the article also addresses a set of important aspects to be observed by managers who intend to incorporate degradation actions into the list of innovative tactics in their organizations.
