Furloughed From the Family Firm: An Affective Events Theory Perspective

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Michele Medina Craven Mississippi State University; Clarissa Rene Steele Kansas State University; Timothy R. Moake Middle Tennessee State University

Session overview

Furloughs can have negative impacts on employees. Because family firms often emphasize the “family” aspect of their culture, employees in family firms may not see furloughs as a legitimate cost-savings strategy. Based on an online survey of furloughed employees, employees in family firms with high role satisfaction experience a higher negative perception of interpersonal justice, while in non-family firms, high role satisfaction was related to more negative perceptions of distributed and interpersonal justice. Employees in family firms understood the rationale for and communication of furloughs, but questioned their necessity, whereas employees in non-family firms struggled with furlough implementation and their treatment in the process.
