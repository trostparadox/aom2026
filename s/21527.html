Constructing Problem Spaces through Designing GenAI-Enabled Synthetic Personae System

Author: Shiyan Zhang Lancaster University

Session overview

This study explores how iterative system development can serve as a generative mechanism for problem space construction in contexts where both technological capabilities and organizational needs are emergent. Drawing on design science research (DSR) and informed by sociotechnical and sensemaking perspectives, the study presents a case of co-developing a GenAI-enabled synthetic personae system with a small nonprofit arts organization unfamiliar with the possibilities of the technology. Rather than beginning with predefined use cases or a stable set of requirements, the project embraced a situated, collaborative process in which the design process also serves as a probe into the problem space. The resulting artifact—a synthetic personae system that enables multi-voiced deliberation—was not a finished solution but a vehicle for surfacing latent needs, coordinating expectations, and scaffolding reflective learning. Theoretically, this study contributes to emerging pathways in DSR that foreground problem-space revelation, offering insight into how design processes unfold when organizational users lack clear problem definitions or technological fluency. We argue that in such settings, design is not a response to a problem but a means of articulating what is at stake. Our work opens new avenues for understanding the role of GenAI systems in supporting not only solution development but also collective sensemaking, especially in under-resourced and creatively oriented organizational contexts.
