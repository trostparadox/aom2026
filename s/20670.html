Examining Skilled Migrants’ Acculturation and Thriving at Work

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3
IM

People

Authors: Maggie Wan Texas State University; Margaret A. Shaffer University of Oklahoma; Mihaela Dimitrova WU Vienna

Session overview

Drawing on Conservation of Resources theory and the socially embedded model of thriving, we examine the influence of skill migrants’ acculturation strategies (assimilation, integration, separation, and marginalization) on thriving at work through general self-efficacy, and how cultural distance moderates these relationships. We test the model using three-wave survey data from 427 skilled migrants working full time in foreign countries. Results show that integration and separation enhanced thriving at work through higher general self-efficacy, whereas marginalization impaired thriving at work through lower self-efficacy. Assimilation showed no significant effects. In addition, cultural distance strengthened the indirect effects of separation on thriving via self-efficacy.
