Tensions in Transforming Human Resource Management in Multinationals

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
IM

People

Author: Linyu Liu Aalto University

Session overview

The mega trend of sustainability has sparked heated scholarly discussions on international business operations, including its implications for human resource management (HRM). This paper emphasizes a crucial yet under-researched factor in HRM – tensions – in transforming, both theoretically and practically, HRM towards a pluralist approach. Drawing on the insights from HRM and international business (IB) literature, I propose a theoretical framework of tensions arising from multiple stakeholders, multiple goals, and multiple contexts when transforming HRM within multinational corporations (MNCs). Instead of focusing on the potential negative consequences of tensions and their remedies, I embrace the duality and paradox perspectives to emphasize the permanent and dynamic nature of tensions and highlight the complementarity of opposites, with potential to generate positive impacts. Through applying the framework to the case of two specific HRM practices - talent management and diversity management, I illustrate the competing goals and interests, and the intricate interplay of tensions exacerbated by the complexities of global operations. By showcasing the interconnected nature of tensions stemming from multiple stakeholders, multiple goals, and multiple contexts, this paper underscores the necessity of considering them holistically in the pursuit of advancing HRM within MNCs. Finally, I explore the implications of the proposed framework for international HRM studies, as well as international HRM policies and practices.
