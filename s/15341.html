Session overview

Integrating AI into organizational settings promises to dramatically increase productivity. Much of the existing research on human-AI collaborations focusses on how cognition shape human AI collaboration and on the cognitive outcomes resulting from these collaborations including decision-making, learning or innovation. Yet besides these rational aspects lies a powerful but mostly underexplored layer: emotions. This panel brings together interdisciplinary scholars to examine the potential dark side of emotions in human-AI collaborations. For instance, while using AI chatbots can alleviate feelings of loneliness (De Freitas et al., 2025), interacting with such bots can risk emotional manipulation through tactics such as guilt appeals (De Freitas et al., 2025). Beyond that, workers who perceive AI as a threat to their job security can experience heightened stress and burnout. By focusing on both negative and positive emotions, and their potential to yield unintended harms, we aim to advance a richer theoretical understanding of the role of emotions around AI and derive practical guidance for more emotionally intelligent, responsible, and resilient human-AI collaboration.
