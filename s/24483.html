Paradox Engagement as Ontological Practice: An Ontology-of-Self Framework

OMT
Zhe Cao (The University of Queensland)
Paradox theory has flourished, but its proliferation of cognitive, emotional, and embodied models signals a deeper issue. While recent debates refine the ontology of paradox, they leave unexamined the ontology of the self that encounters it. This paper challenges paradox theory’s tacit assumption of a bounded, reflective subject. I propose an Ontology-of-Self Framework (OSF) that distinguishes narrative, relational, and no-self ontologies and explains how each discloses a different world of paradox. To account for how these ontologies coexist in practice, I introduce the Dynamic Attunement Zone (DAZ), a meta-ontological space in which modes of engagement interact through resonance rather than synthesis. By surfacing this lived yet hidden foundation, OSF reframes paradox as an ontological condition rather than a problem to manage, transforming apparent fragmentation into structured pluralism and opening new horizons for paradox research, qualitative methodology, and cross-cultural organization theory.
