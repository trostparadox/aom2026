Designing Authentic Learning in Management Education: Industry-Engaged Projects, Student Motivation,

Author: Ruei-Teng Hung Chaoyang University of Technology

Session overview

This study examines the pedagogical value of industry-engaged authentic learning in a project-based Tourism English course within the context of management education. Using a quasi-experimental design with 131 junior undergraduates, the experimental group participated in a structured interaction with a professional tour guide, while the control group completed identical course tasks without industry engagement. Regression analyses showed that the industry-engaged intervention significantly enhanced students’ motivation to learn and learning outcomes at the group level. Structural equation modeling within the experimental group further indicated that these effects were embedded in the integrated course design rather than explained by simple individual-level mechanisms. The study advances a design-based perspective on industry-engaged learning, providing practical insights for curriculum design in management and tourism education.
