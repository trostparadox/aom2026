Ethically Questionable Practices in Health Care: Explaining Provider Intentions in Puerto Rico

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
HCM

People

Author: Carlos Javier Sanchez

Session overview

Ethically questionable practices (EQPs) in health care represent a persistent challenge to the sustainability, integrity, and legitimacy of health systems worldwide. While prior research has extensively documented fraudulent behaviors after they occur, considerably less attention has been devoted to the cognitive and organizational mechanisms that shape providers’ intentions before such practices materialize. Addressing this gap, this study proposes and empirically tests an integrative model that combines the Fraud Triangle Theory—pressure, opportunity, and rationalization—with the Theory of Planned Behavior—attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control—to explain health care providers’ intentions to engage in EQPs toward insurers in Puerto Rico. Using a quantitative, cross-sectional design, primary data were collected from health care providers across multiple sectors, including physicians, hospitals, laboratories, pharmacies, and medical equipment suppliers. The proposed model was analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). Results indicate that rationalization plays a central mediating role, transforming financial and non-financial pressures, as well as perceived opportunities, into cognitively justified intentions to engage in EQPs. In contrast, the attitudinal and normative components of the Theory of Planned Behavior exhibit weaker direct effects, operating primarily through indirect pathways. Overall, the findings suggest that ethically questionable practices in health care are not impulsive behaviors but deliberate cognitive processes that are morally justified prior to execution. This study contributes to health care management scholarship by integrating criminological and behavioral theories into a unified, empirically testable framework and offers practical insights for ethics-based governance, compliance design, and preventive policy interventions.
