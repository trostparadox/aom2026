When Putting Family First Hurts Family Last: Conditional Backfire Effects of Career Sacrifice

CAR
Yin Zhu (Central University of Finance and Economics); Zhen Wang (Central University of Finance and Economics)
Career sacrifices for family refer to self-sacrificial behaviors where individuals consciously forgo valuable career opportunities or resources to meet family needs or fulfill familial obligations. Despite their career-related costs, these sacrifices are widely perceived as beneficial to family life. Moving beyond this traditional perspective, this study explores their negative impacts on family functioning. Integrating conservation of resources (COR) theory and stereotype threat theory (STT), we propose that employees’ career sacrifices for family evoke family–work stereotype threat (FWST)—the concern or fear of being evaluated against and confirming negative stereotypes about those with family obligations—particularly in an organizational climate that values work over family. In turn, FWST elicits dysfunctional family-directed behaviors (i.e., family withdrawal and undermining). We further posit that employees’ regret over their sacrifices intensifies the detrimental influence of FWST on these maladaptive outcomes. Results from a three-wave field survey provided broad support for our predictions. Notably, career sacrifices most strongly predict family withdrawal and undermining via FWST when both perceived organizational family-sacrifice climate and regret are high. These findings challenge the prevailing assumption that career sacrifices inherently strengthen family functioning, illuminate their “dark side”, and advance work–family research by introducing novel mechanisms and boundary conditions.
