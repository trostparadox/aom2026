Substitution to Interdependence: Knowledge & Networks in Translocal Entrepreneurship

ENT
Wenwen An; huitian Wu (Guangdong University of Technology); Shuqin Bao (Nankai University)
In current business practice, an increasing number of entrepreneurs cross regional boundaries to seek development opportunities, making translocal entrepreneurship a significant phenomenon characterized by both "entrepreneurship" and "migration." However, achieving innovation within unfamiliar social networks and market environments remains an urgent practical challenge. Based on a configurational perspective, this study employs fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) on a sample of 63 Chinese translocal new ventures to explore the multiple conjunctural mechanisms by which knowledge resources and network embeddedness influence innovation performance. The findings reveal that high innovation performance in translocal entrepreneurship does not stem solely from the accumulation of single factors but depends on the configurational fit and coupling of specific knowledge structures (technology, market, customer problems, and ways to serve market) and dual networks (local and translocal). The study identifies three typical pathways—independent technology-driven, adaptive local interaction, and integrative cross-regional synergy—and reveals the non-linear interactive nature of knowledge and networks, which evolves from "functional substitution" to "structural interdependence" across different innovation modes. This paper constructs a "knowledge-network" configurational framework, enriching the theoretical explanation of translocal innovation and providing references for firms to optimize cross-regional resource allocation and for governments to implement network-enabling policies.
