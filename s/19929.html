Author: Yi Jin Leow Singapore Management University

Session overview

Rapid judgments of leaders often begin with visible cues. Building on “what is beautiful is good” halo research, we conceptualize entrepreneur physical attractiveness as aesthetic capital, a diffuse status cue that shapes investor expectations and resource allocation under uncertainty. We integrate halo processes, implicit leadership theory, status characteristics theory, and signaling theory to propose that attractiveness affects investment through leadership-relevant trait inferences (fitness, grooming, charisma, competence, and trustworthiness). We further theorize two boundary conditions: (a) gendered aesthetic capital, such that attractiveness premiums are larger for male than female entrepreneurs in male-typed entrepreneurial contexts; and (b) diagnostic information, such that high-quality pitch content attenuates (but does not eliminate) attractiveness effects. To test these claims, we propose a preregistered 2×2×2 within-person experiment in which working adults act as venture-capital investors evaluating eight one-minute video pitches that orthogonally manipulate entrepreneur attractiveness (high vs. low), presenter gender (male vs. female), and pitch quality (good vs. poor). Participants provide per-pitch investment ratings and allocate a constrained portfolio budget across ventures; post-decision measures capture perceived traits and manipulation checks. Analyses will use mixed-effects models and multilevel mediation to estimate the attractiveness premium, its trait-based mechanisms, and interactions with gender and pitch quality. The project contributes to organizational behavior by linking micro-level impression formation to consequential resource allocation decisions and clarifying when appearance operates as a noisy signal versus a bias in investment contexts.
