Unequal Engines of Innovation: Investigating Factors for the Growth of Cleantech Startups in India

ENT
Lopa Mishra Jana (Indian Institute of Management Sambalpur)
Purpose: India’s cleantech entrepreneurship reflects stark regional disparities, with high-GDP states such as Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Gujarat dominating innovation while low-income yet resource-rich states like Bihar, Odisha, and Assam lag behind. This study examines how state-level economic capacity, environmental stress, and policy design interact to shape the geography of cleantech startup formation. Design/methodology/approach: A mixed-methods framework integrates Difference-in-Differences (DiD) econometric analysis with grounded theory–based case studies across seven states— Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Bihar, Odisha, Assam, and Rajasthan. Using data from DPIIT, MNRE, and RBI (2016–2022), the study evaluates the causal effect of green energy policies on startup density while contextualizing results through comparative institutional analysis. Findings: States implementing green energy initiatives experience increase in cleantech startup formation, controlling for GDP, credit, and infrastructure. The evidence highlights three archetypes: Amplifier States where policy and capital ecosystems reinforce growth; Adaptive States where necessity drives frugal innovation; and Transitional States where coherent hybrid policies enable rapid development. Practical implications: Findings support state-specific innovation cells, venture funds, and university–industry incubator networks to foster cleantech entrepreneurship in emerging regions. Originality/value: This research provides one of the first causal subnational analyses of cleantech policy impacts in India. It validates Porter’s Hypothesis in a developing-country context and reframes India’s green transition as driven by “unequal but converging engines of innovation.”
