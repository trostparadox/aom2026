AI's Ethical Impact on Corporate Misconduct: The Role of the CEO Power Triangle Framework

TIM
Chenhao Hu (Shandong University); Jie Lyu (Shandong University); Zeyu Chen (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes deeply embedded in organizational decision-making, scholars increasingly debate its ethical consequences—yet existing theories offer fragmented and often conflicting predictions. Drawing on a dual-attribute view of AI and the fraud triangle theory, this study explains when and why AI adoption constrains versus facilitates corporate misconduct. We argue that AI’s agentic attribute generates a nonlinear pressure– rationalization accumulation mechanism, while its technological attribute induces a linear opportunity suppression mechanism, producing a U-shaped relationship between AI adoption and misconduct. We further introduce a CEO Power Triangle framework to theorize how power intensity, power pressure, and power monitoring condition this relationship. Using panel data on Chinese listed firms from 2011 to 2023 and examining both financial and environmental misconduct, we find strong support for our framework. This study contributes to research on the ethical consequences of AI by foregrounding its dual attributes and demonstrating how corporate governance structures shape their behavioral implications.
