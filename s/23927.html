When Automation Augments: Friendly Employee Treatment and Firm Innovation

TIM
Yousy Li (Carlos III University of Madrid); Wei (Luke) Zhang (PSL University)
This study investigates when and how automation augments firm innovation by considering the role of employee-centric corporate social responsibility (CSR). Using data on Chinese manufacturing firms from 2011 to 2019, we find that the positive relationship between robots and innovation is more pronounced in firms with more investment in employee-oriented CSR. Among different CSR dimensions, growth-oriented initiatives such as training, promotion, and compensation, exert the strongest amplification effects on both innovation inputs and outputs, while caring- and safety-oriented initiatives’ role are limited. Further analysis shows that robots increase demand for highly educated and technology-oriented employees, explaining why skill-enhancing CSR practices are most complementary to automation. Overall, we highlight that strategic investment in employee welfare enhances human-machine complementarities and firm innovation.
