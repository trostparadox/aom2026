Session overview

Competitive dynamics research examines rivalry as a sequence of competitive moves and countermoves, rather than as a static equilibrium. As competitive dynamics are increasingly shaped by digital technologies, ecosystem structures, and geopolitics, rivalry is also moving beyond traditional boundaries. Firms compete not only within industries, but across ecosystems, across national borders, and alongside actors such as platform leaders and nation states. Responding to this change, this presenter symposium is designed to answer three research questions. First, how should rivalry be conceptualized when ecosystems and nation-states become central actors in shaping competition, and when AI becomes an arena of strategic contestation. Second, what micro-level mechanisms, including language and attention, help explain shifts between competition and collaboration. Third, how do competitive dynamics theories travel across disciplinary boundaries, and how can insights from other disciplines, in turn, renew and extend competitive dynamics scholarship. Collectively, the four papers advance a broader view of competitive dynamics as a multi-actor, multi-arena, and multi-level phenomenon. In doing so, the symposium extends the conceptual boundaries of competitive dynamics by connecting it with adjacent perspectives on ecosystems, corporate communication, and the attention-based view, among others.
