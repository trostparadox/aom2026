Information Processing Capacity in Healthcare

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5

Author: Wilbert Tylan Davis Temple University

Session overview

Despite substantial health information technology investments and widespread electronic health record (EHR) implementation, healthcare organizations struggle with fragmented data systems and underutilized patient engagement tools. This research examines how interoperability capabilities influence hospitals' meaningful use maturity and subsequently impact patient engagement and advanced analytics capabilities. Drawing on Organizational Information Processing Theory (OIPT), this study proposes that interoperability infrastructure enables hospitals to develop information processing capacity— operationalized as meaningful use maturity—which subsequently enables advanced mechanisms including patient engagement tools and clinical decision support systems. This capacity-mediated relationship positions EHRs as complex information processing systems that fundamentally change how organizations handle clinical data, rather than simple technology adoption decisions. The study employs structural equation modeling with mediation analysis using the 2023 American Hospital Association Healthcare IT Database covering 2,600+ U.S. acute care hospitals. Four hypotheses test meaningful use maturity as the mediating factor between interoperability capabilities and advanced EHR functionalities. This research contributes theoretically by extending OIPT to healthcare digital transformation with empirical evidence of capacity-mediated relationships between infrastructure and organizational mechanisms. For practice, findings provide hospital executives evidence-based guidance for sequencing digital health investments, demonstrating that organizational capacity development must precede advanced technology implementation. For policy, the research informs federal health IT initiatives by showing how interoperability mandates drive organizational technology development beyond basic compliance toward transformative care delivery capabilities aligned with the 2025 CMS Interoperability Framework.
