Leadership Emergence and Leader Effectiveness: Simulation with an Agent-Based Model

Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3

People

Authors: Chen Tang American University; Siqi He University of Iowa; Jun-Yeob Kim University of Central Florida; Sang-Hoon Lee Loyola Marymount University

Session overview

Leadership emergence and leader effectiveness are distinct yet often interconnected outcomes in leadership research. While leader emergence is the process by which individuals become influential or emerge as leaders, leader effectiveness is the leaders’ actual impact on group outcomes. Prior research often treats these as interchangeable, obscuring the temporal order: emergence must precede effectiveness. Building on Galvin et al.’s (2024) theoretical framework, the current study employs an Agent-Based Model (ABM) to dynamically simulate this process. Our ABM defines agents based on three trait attributes: generative (effectiveness-driving), instrumental (emergence-driving but effectiveness-detracting), and latent-expressive (status-conferring). Critically, in the ABM, we simulate leadership emergence first, followed by the leader's effectiveness over time. Once an agent emerges as a leader, a ‘leadership event’ occurs where followers’ traits shift to emulate the leader, simulating the reinforcement of leader characteristics within the team. By manipulating the alignment of these trait attributes, we explore how traits that facilitate initial emergence may subsequently benefit or harm long-term team effectiveness.
