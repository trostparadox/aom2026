Narcissistic Leaders’ Empathy: Impacts on Follower Task Performance and Leader Effectiveness

OB
Bo Lv (Renmin University of China); Xin Liu (Renmin University of China); Yinxu Zhou (Renmin University of China); Wansi Chen
Leaders are increasingly encouraged to act empathetically because of mounting evidence linking leader empathy with improved follower task performance and leader effectiveness. However, guided by attribution theory, we argue that leader empathetic behavior does not invariably produce positive reactions from followers as these depend on the motives they attribute to such behavior. Specifically, follower reactions are shaped by whether they interpret leader empathetic behavior as being driven by impression management or prosocial motives. Furthermore, narcissism, a common trait in leaders characterized by low empathy, serves to shape followers’ attributions of leader empathetic behavior. When leader narcissism is higher, empathetic behavior tends to trigger perceptions of impression management motives, which undermine follower performance and leader effectiveness. When narcissism is lower, the same behavior is more likely to be seen as driven by prosocial motives, which enhances performance and effectiveness. We tested these ideas in two studies: an experiment with 194 U.S. employees (Study 1) and a two-wave, multi-source survey of 178 leader-follower dyads in China (Study 2). Findings across two studies consistently supported our hypotheses except for the main effect of leader empathetic behavior on perceived impression management motives. This research advances the literature on empathy, narcissism, and broader leadership.
