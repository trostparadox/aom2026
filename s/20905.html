What Makes Academics Satisfied with Online Conferences and Why They Stay Loyal? Multinational Study

Authors: Nevena Bevanda Association of Economists and Managers of the Balkans - Udekom Balkans; Nikolina Vrcelj Association of Economists and Managers of the Balkans - Udekom Balkans

Session overview

We examine how digital communication and platform-based interaction reshape how academics experience, evaluate, and remain committed to online scientific conferences. The proposed model integrates traditional conference attributes (program quality, personal and professional development, and networking) with technology-related factors, namely perceived usefulness and online content engagement. Data were collected through an online survey of 153 researchers from 23 countries and analyzed using structural equation modeling. The findings show that satisfaction is mainly driven by perceived usefulness and online content engagement, while traditional conference attributes play a negligible role. This suggests that participants assess online conferences based on digital interaction rather than conventional in-person features. Furthermore, satisfaction strongly predicts emotional commitment, perceived functional value, and behavioral loyalty, confirming its central role in shaping long-term participant relationships. Satisfaction mechanisms appear to differ between physical and digital academic events, highlighting the need to deconceptualize existing evaluation frameworks. Practically, the results suggest prioritizing usability, interactivity, and content accessibility over traditional conference designs. These insights contribute to a more nuanced understanding of how online academic conferences should be conceptualized, evaluated, and managed.
