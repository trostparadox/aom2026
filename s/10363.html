Session overview

Organizations learn at multiple levels. Individual decision makers learn from feedback about their own actions; teams and organizations learn from repeated interactions and performance trends; and societies learn about norms and institutions as they evolve. Yet these learning processes are typically studied in separate silos—reinforcement learning and aspirations in one literature, social thresholds and tipping points in another, theory based strategy and governance in a third. This symposium brings these threads together. We focus on learning in a deliberately broad sense: how decision makers learn about themselves (their own beliefs and thresholds), about others (in teams, markets, and social groups), about the business landscape (strategic search on rugged landscapes), and about the social environment (norms, conventions, and institutional change). Across levels, we ask: How should actors encode feedback, structure experiments, and allocate attention so that learning is both effective and strategically aligned? Methodologically, the symposium spans: • Analytical and computational models of hierarchical, contextual multi armed bandits that treat theories as objects of choice. • Agent based models of governance and social learning under uncertainty. • Large scale, incentivized experiments and behavioral simulations on exploration–exploitation, threshold behavior, and interdependent decision making. • Multi method inquiry combining think aloud protocols, digital simulations, and preregistered experiments to uncover cognitive mechanisms.
