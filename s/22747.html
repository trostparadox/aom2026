A Cross-Level Investigation of Employee Creativity: A Dual-Path Construal Model

OB
Laura Parks-Leduc (James Madison University); Cody Bradley (James Madison University); Li-Yun Sun (Macau University of Science and Technology)
Despite increasing research interest in exploring the dynamic processes leading to employee creativity, this area of study has shown a dominant emphasis on the feasibility of concrete and specific processes, lacking a comprehensive perspective that incorporates the general desirability of creative outcomes. Viewing creativity through a construal level theoretical lens, we theorize that employee creativity is a function of both concrete (i.e., creative process engagement) and abstract (i.e., creative goal striving) processes. Furthermore, we leverage prior creativity work that has recognized value in addressing creativity from an interactionist perspective, where creativity results from the interplay of actor and context variables. Specifically, our model integrates variables that collectively capture each of the recognized core elements involved in creativity: attributes (i.e., actor characteristics), intentional activities (i.e., processes), environmental context (i.e., situation factors), and products (i.e., creativity as an outcome). Hypotheses are tested using a multi-wave, multi-source, and multi-level study involving 1,505 employees and their 258 direct work supervisors. Overall, our study presents a novel perspective of creativity via dual-process pathways and as influenced by both person and situation factors.

Do You Like Your CEO on Social Media? CEOs’ Text Posts and Employer Brand

OB
Qianyao Huang (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Shengming Liu (Fudan University); Jun Yin (Peking University); Anni Chen (Southeast University); Xuanhong You (Rutgers University-New Brunswick); Jiexuan Zhang; CHENGKAI PAN (East China University of Science and Technology); Hongsong Guo (Central University of Finance and Economics)
This research investigates the impact of CEOs’ social media communication on employer branding from a construal level perspective, with a particular focus on text posts. It employs a construal level theoretical framework to assess the effect of vividness in CEOs’ posts, shaped by content tangibility and framing proximity, on employer brand commitment. The findings, derived from a field experiment in a large Chinese education training organization, reveal that text posts with tangible content and proximal framing enhance perceived vividness, thereby strengthening employer brand commitment. This research contributes to understanding the role of CEOs in direct employee engagement via social media and offers insights into effective corporate communication strategies for enhancing employer brand commitment. The study also highlights the nuanced impact of social media in organizational contexts, providing practical implications for CEOs in leveraging digital platforms for internal branding.

Elevated Vision, Grounded Voice: How Dialectical Thinking Bridges Construal Level to Voice

OB
Riguang Gao (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics); Ni Lu (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics)
Employee voice, defined as employees’ voluntary expression of constructive suggestions or concerns, plays a vital role in organizational innovation and adaptability. Building on Social Cognitive Theory, this study investigates how employees' construal level influences their voice behavior, with a focus on the mediating role of dialectical thinking and the moderating role of power distance. We tested this model through a three-wave survey involving 194 supervisor-employee dyads from three banks. Results show that construal level significantly predicts dialectical thinking, which in turn facilitates employee voice. This indirect effect is significant only under low power-distance conditions. The findings underscore how contextual factors can either suppress or amplify employees' cognitive readiness to speak up, offering both theoretical insight into cognition-behavior processes and practical guidance for fostering open communication in organizations.
