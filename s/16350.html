Session overview

Business schools worldwide are wrestling with how to secure assessment in the age of AI, without retreating to proctored exams that capture a moment in time and assess recall, not the genuine attainment of knowledge and skills. The solution lies in a framework that captures not just what students produce, but how they produce it and what they learn in the process. Traditional assessment—essays, reports, rubrics— cannot distinguish AI-assisted learning from AI-substituted work. This creates what we call the attribution void: a growing gap between what students submit and what we can confidently attribute to authentic learning. This hands-on PDW introduces a paradigm shift model for teaching and assessing in the age of AI, developed and tested across three years of practice across multiple management disciplines. The approach integrates two innovations: (1) the Human-Centric AI-First (HCAIF) pedagogical framework, combined with (2) Person-Process- Product (3P) assessment. Drawing on programmatic assessment principles from medical education, this approach transforms assessment OF learning into assessment FOR learning. Participants will work in facilitated groups to redesign assessment in their own courses using these frameworks. The workshop brings together facilitators from three universities across two continents (University of Newcastle Australia, University of Doha, Qatar, and RMIT Australia) who have implemented this approach across diverse contexts. Participants leave with actionable implementation plans, assessment rubrics, and a community of practice for ongoing support. Finalist for the MED Best PDW Award 2026.
