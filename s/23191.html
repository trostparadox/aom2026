Entrepreneurship and Resilience: Toward a Dynamic and Developmental Research Agenda

ENT
Andrew Bettinger (Syracuse University)
Entrepreneurship is widely recognized as a context marked by uncertainty, strain, and recurring adversity, making it a fertile setting for studying resilience. Yet, despite growing scholarly attention, entrepreneurship research has not converged on a coherent understanding of what entrepreneurial resilience is or how it develops over time. This systematic review synthesizes 62 empirical studies to examine how resilience has been conceptualized and operationalized within entrepreneurship research. Drawing on the Resilience Portfolio Model as an organizing framework, the review identifies three dominant characterizations of entrepreneurial resilience: resilience as coping, resilience as adaptation, and resilience as development. The review concludes by outlining a research agenda that encourages scholars to move beyond static or event-driven perspectives and to examine entrepreneurship as an ongoing context through which resilience capacities can be cultivated over time.

No Single Path to Well-Being: A Set-Theoretic Study of Entrepreneurs’ Psychological Resilience

ENT
Satoshi Kawakatsu (Kyoto University)
This study reexamines the role of psychological resilience, which has been regarded as a central concept in explaining entrepreneurs’ well-being under adversity. Some research has examined resilience through linear approaches that implicitly assume a stress-regulation logic, treating resilience as a core driver of well-being. However, this perspective has paid limited attention to the possibility that entrepreneurial characteristics operate in context-dependent and complementary configurations, resulting in multiple causal pathways leading to the same outcome. Drawing on a configurational perspective, this study assumes that psychological resilience is not necessarily a necessary condition for well-being, but instead functions as an effective psychological resource only in conjunction with specific entrepreneurial characteristics. To examine this argument, the research applied fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to survey data collected from entrepreneurs in Japan during the COVID-19 pandemic. The results revealed that psychological resilience was not a factor that generated well-being on its own, but rather functioned as a complementary psychological resource when embedded within multiple configurations. Among female entrepreneurs, contrary to expectations, psychological resilience was shown to play a role close to that of a necessary condition for achieving well-being, particularly under conditions of severe adversity. The findings highlighted a conceptual conflict between viewing entrepreneurial resilience as an adaptive resource grounded in stress coping and interpreting it as agentic action based on self-determination. Based on these findings, this study reinterprets the relationship between entrepreneurial resilience and well-being from the perspective of conservation of resources (COR) theory and integrates them within a higher level of theoretical coherence.

The Resilience Trap: Rethinking Entrepreneurial Resilience and Success Through Liminality

ENT
Philipp Köhn (University of Siegen); Gernot Pruschak (Bern University of Applied Sciences); Philipp Julian Ruf (University of Siegen); Marco Mismetti (Stockholm School of Economics); Alfredo De Massis (IMD Business School & University of Chieti-Pescara)
This study examines how entrepreneurial resilience shapes venture success during the uncertain phases of venture creation. Drawing on Liminality Theory, resilience is conceptualized as a transitional capacity influencing how entrepreneurs move through ambiguity toward venture viability. Using longitudinal data from the Second Panel Study of Entrepreneurial Dynamics (PSED II), we analyze 867 nascent U.S. entrepreneurs (2,533 observations) tracked from 2005 to 2011. Cox proportional-hazards models estimate linear and nonlinear effects of resilience on the likelihood of reaching positive cash flow while accounting for funding and key controls. Results show a negative linear association between resilience and success. When nonlinearity is considered, resilience exhibits a significant U-shaped relationship, indicating that moderate resilience is associated with the lowest probability of profitability, whereas both low and high levels are more favorable. Funding shows a positive main effect but does not moderate the nonlinear pattern. These findings challenge the assumption that resilience is uniformly beneficial for entrepreneurial success.

The Impact of Entrepreneurial Resilience on Product Innovation: The Broaden-and-Build Perspective

ENT
Xiaolin Li; Huihui Wang (Shanghai University of International Business and Economics); XINYUE YAN (Fudan University)
Purpose This study explores how entrepreneurial resilience drives product innovation—a critical market-facing outcome in entrepreneurial marketing—under conditions of technological disruption and environmental uncertainty. Understanding this relationship is essential for firms seeking to maintain competitiveness through continuous innovation. Design/methodology/approach Drawing on broaden-and-build theory, this study uses a mixed-methods design. Quantitative data were collected from 252 Chinese entrepreneurs, and qualitative insights were obtained through in-depth interviews. Structural equation modeling was employed to test the proposed framework. Findings The results reveal a sequential mediation model in which challenge stressors serve as cognitive expansion triggers, and taking-charge behavior acts as a behavioral construction mechanism. Together, these link entrepreneurial resilience to enhanced product innovation. Moreover, self-transcendence values positively moderate this process, amplifying the effectiveness of resilience in driving innovation. Research limitations/implications This study is based on cross-sectional data from a single-country context, which may limit generalizability. Future research could adopt longitudinal or cross-cultural designs to further validate the findings. Practical implications Entrepreneurial firms should foster resilience and create environments that promote positive stress framing and proactive behaviors. Aligning innovation strategies with founders' value systems can further enhance innovation performance. Social implications This research highlights the broader relevance of cultivating entrepreneurial resilience in fast-changing markets, offering insights for entrepreneurship training programs and innovation policy design in emerging economies. Originality/value This study identifies a distinct psychological pathway through which resilience contributes to market-oriented innovation. By integrating emotional, cognitive, behavioral, and value-based mechanisms, it deepens understanding of how psychological capabilities influence entrepreneurial innovation under uncertainty.

Unpacking Entrepreneurial Resilience: A Capability-Based Model and Measurement Approach

ENT
Noah Bellwald; Alexander Paul Fust (University of St. Gallen); Tatyana Tsukanova (EHL Hospitality Business School)
Entrepreneurs operate in volatile and uncertain environments, making resilience a critical entrepreneurial capability. Despite its importance, research on entrepreneurial resilience remains conceptually fragmented and often relies on general psychological measures that insufficiently capture the specific demands of entrepreneurship. This research develops a capability-based conceptual model of entrepreneurial resilience, specifying the construct as a multidimensional, domain-specific capability. To enable empirical investigation of this model, the study develops and validates a measurement instrument aligned with its internal structure. Following established scale-development guidelines, items were deductively derived and refined through expert input. Study 1 (N = 325) identifies a ten-factor structure using Exploratory Factor Analysis. Study 2 (N = 371) confirms the structure and demonstrates strong reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Study 3 validates a parsimonious 10-item short form using the combined dataset (N = 696). Across studies, entrepreneurial resilience emerges as a multifaceted capability encompassing cognitive, emotional, behavioral, and social processes. By integrating conceptual clarification with rigorous measurement development, this research advances entrepreneurship theory and provides practical tools for research and application.
