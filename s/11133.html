Alignment of Job Demands and Resources in Healthcare Workers

Session overview

Drawing on the Job Demands–Resources (JD-R) model and Restricted Employee Sustainability Theory (REST), this study examines how the (mis)alignment between job demands and resources unfolds over time and shapes motivational and health impairment processes in healthcare work. Using a longitudinal experience sampling design, we collected daily self-reports and objective sleep data from healthcare professionals across a three-week period. Multilevel modeling and polynomial regression with response surface analysis were applied to capture within- and between-person dynamics of demand–resource fit and misfit. Results show that when job demands exceed available resources, exhaustion increases over time, supporting the health impairment pathway. Conversely, high congruence between demands and resources at elevated levels predicts higher engagement and performance, consistent with the motivational pathway. Sleep efficiency, assessed via wearable devices, plays a nuanced role: while it does not significantly buffer the stress–exhaustion relationship within individuals, low sleep efficiency combined with high demand–resource misalignment predicts particularly low engagement and performance between individuals. Integrating JD-R and REST, we conceptualize employee experiences as subsisting, surviving, or thriving states depending on the dynamic balance of demands, resources, and recovery. The findings highlight the temporal and contextual nature of sustainability at work and underscore the importance of aligning demands with adequate resources in healthcare settings.
