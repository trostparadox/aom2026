Circular Supply Chains for High-Value Medical Devices: Practices, Governance, and Healthcare Impact

Authors: VIJAY LAKSHMI Burman University of Eastern Finland; Hanoku Bathula The University of Auckland

Session overview

The healthcare system is still heavily reliant on a linear supply chain of medical equipment, which has led to high consumption of materials, carbon emissions, and continuous waste generation. High-value medical products, such as robotic surgery disposables, endoscopic equipment and specialised instrument trays, contain substantial embedded material and economic value, but are frequently disposed of as single-use because of regulatory, organisational, and supply-chain requirements. The purpose of this study is to design and assess supply-chain models for circular flows of high-value and low-volume medical products in European healthcare systems. The research is based on multi-method approach, integrating systematic literature reviews, empirical supply-chain mapping in selected hospitals, and life-cycle-informed environmental and economic analyses. With a focus on governance structures, contractual relationships, and incentive alignment between hospitals, manufacturing companies, and service providers, the study investigates end-to-end value chains, such as procurement and clinical utilisation, reprocessing, remanufacturing, and recycling. Resulting in more resource-efficient and resilient sustainable healthcare sector based on solid evidence instead of a conceptual dream.
