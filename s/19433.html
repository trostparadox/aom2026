Can (A)I Help in Making Decisions? A Typology of Entrepreneurial Mindsets Toward AI Advisors

Session overview

Decision-making is a highly demanding, high-stakes activity for entrepreneurs and often holds make-or-break power for their firms. With the advent of Industry 4.0, Artificial Intelligence (AI) is now taking on an advisory role to optimize and augment entrepreneurial decision-making. However, little is known about its psycho-social dynamics. The founders’ mindset towards AI advisors determines whether and how they accept AI as advisors, make sense of the advice, and act on the suggestions. Building upon the judge-advisor system perspective and entrepreneurial mindset concept, this study explores different types of mindsets held by founders toward AI advisors. Using a qualitative, interview-based approach; data was collected from 21 startup founders in West Bengal, India. The themes generated through the analysis map a typology of entrepreneurial mindsets built upon a continuum of entrepreneurs’ feelings, thoughts, and actions with respect to AI advisors. Leveraging this further, the study illustrates four distinct personas of entrepreneurs based on the dominant mindset they hold toward AI advisors. The study contributes to the development of the judge-advisor system paradigm in the entrepreneurial context and focuses on the growing relevance of non-human advisors. The findings encourage founders to adopt a calibrated mindset towards AI as an advisory source. By fostering a smart and balanced mindset, policymakers can enable founders to extract the benefits of AI advisors while preventing the accompanying threats.
