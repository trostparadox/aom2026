Session overview

In this professional development workshop (PDW), we focus on the process of publishing race and entrepreneurship research, with particular attention to the systemic challenges scholars face in navigating manuscript development, editorial evaluation, and peer review. Despite growing interest in race-conscious entrepreneurship research, scholars encounter persistent barriers and limited guidance on how to position this work within mainstream conversations. This PDW directly addresses these obstacles through a panel dialogue organized around three themes. First, we examine how prevailing epistemological and methodological assumptions create barriers for scholarship on marginalized populations. Second, we explore gatekeeping in the peer-review process, including epistemic injustice and shifting evaluative standards that disproportionately affect race-conscious work. Third, we discuss theoretical positioning strategies and intertextual practices that enable scholars to construct compelling cases for contribution. Following the panel, participants will discuss pre-submitted extended abstracts with editors from leading entrepreneurship journals. The goal of this PDW is to equip scholars with practical, theory-informed strategies for developing, positioning, and advancing race and entrepreneurship research in mainstream journals.
