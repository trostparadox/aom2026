Feeling One Thing, Displaying Another: Emotional Dissonance Stays Hidden in Robot-Enabled Work

OB
Ramyani Kundu (Indian Institute of Management Ranchi); Tanusree Dutta
The increasing deployment of service robots and artificial intelligence (AI) in tourism and hospitality has transformed frontline service work, intensifying employees’ emotional demands. While prior research has extensively examined employee outcomes such as stress, anxiety, emotional exhaustion, and emotional labor strategies, the conflict between employees’ genuinely felt emotions and mandated emotional displays remains under-theorized. Drawing on a structured review and critical synthesis of research on AI- and robot-enabled service work in tourism and hospitality, this study foregrounds robot-induced emotional dissonance as a missing yet consequential construct. Findings reveal that although emotional conflict is widely present, it is rarely named explicitly and is instead fragmented across adjacent emotion-related constructs. The review further shows that emotional labor theory is applied selectively, with limited attention to emotional dissonance as a component or outcome of emotional labor. The study ends with conceptualizing robot-induced emotional dissonance as employees’ sustained conflict between genuinely felt emotions and organizationally prescribed emotional displays toward service robots and AI-enabled technologies, while being ambassadors and advocates of technological integration. In doing so, the study clarifies conceptual boundaries, highlights patterns of theoretical omission, and offers a research map for advancing the study of robot-induced emotional dissonance in technology-oriented service work.
