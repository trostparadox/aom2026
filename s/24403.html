Corporate Nationalism and Outward Foreign Direct Investment: A Place Attachment Perspective

IM
Naihao Li (University of International Business and Economics); Sijie Wang (University of International Business and Economics); Yuxin Guo (Beijing Technology and Business University); Chenye Wang (University of International Business and Economics); yun'zhe Chang (University of International Business and Economics)
How corporate nationalism influences firms’ outward foreign direct investment (OFDI) has garnered increasing scholarly attention and remains a central issue in foreign direct investment research. Existing literature offers conflicting views: while some argue that Corporate Nationalism diminishes OFDI, others highlight that Firms with nationalist tendencies are more vulnerable to trade sanctions and have stronger motivations to bypass tariff barriers through OFDI. Building on place attachment theory, we theorize that Firms with nationalist tendencies have strong psychological attachment in their homeland countries, which could reduce the firms’ OFDI. We further argue that this negative effect is stronger for firms that receive more media attacks, while it is mitigated for those that receive more media praise. We test these propositions using a 16-year panel dataset of 4050 firms across 85 industries. Our empirical results support these arguments. This study contributes to foreign direct investment research by reconciling conflicting views through a contingency framework, showing that Corporate Nationalism has a negative effect on OFDI through main mechanisms of place attachment, with media attacks exacerbating this effect while media praise serving as a mitigating force.
