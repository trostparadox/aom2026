Session overview

Innovation is increasingly shaped not only by markets and technological opportunity, but by geopolitics, national security concerns, and state intervention in science and technology. Governments are restricting cross-border knowledge flows, tightening immigration and training regimes, and reorienting innovation systems toward national priorities, fundamentally altering firms’ access to scientific knowledge, talent, and complementary assets. This symposium brings together four papers that examine how rising geopolitical tensions reshape innovation systems at both national and organizational levels. Drawing on population-scale data, novel empirical strategies, and perspectives from strategy and policy, the papers document how geopolitical frictions affect scientific collaboration, researcher mobility, and firms’ reliance on global science. Collectively, they show that perceived hostility and policy uncertainty can redirect talent flows, alter knowledge trajectories, reduce innovation efficiency, and alter organizational strategy, even in the absence of formal restrictions. At the same time, the findings highlight persistent asymmetries in how science is produced globally but used locally, and the continued centrality of leading innovation hubs in translating science into valuable technologies. The symposium advances understanding of innovation strategy in an era where geopolitical conflict increasingly defines the environment in which public and private actors generate, diffuse, and capture value from new technologies.
