Failing Sustainability Governance: Unpacking the Role of Authority

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 7

People

Author: Nina Peitler Vienna University of Economics and Business

Session overview

Authority is crucial for enabling actions (Haugaard, 2017) and is therefore particularly important in domains where urgent action is required, such as sustainability. Understanding who holds authority, how it is shared, contested, or maintained, and the legitimacy sources on which it rests is essential. A substantial body of research has examined how sustainability is governed, emphasizing the contestation around authority and legitimacy during the last decades, of both public and private authority in governing sustainability. However, despite its relevance, existing studies often implicitly assume authority or view it as equal to legitimacy, not analyzing authority as a concept on its own within this context. At the same time, literature in other fields, such as international relations or sociology, looks more specifically at authority and can shed light on authority in sustainability governance. This paper aims to combine these bodies of literature in an integrative review to unpack the concept of authority in the governance of sustainability. Thereby, it aims to contribute to our understanding of authority in sustainability governance and how public and private authority influence it, while also bringing new insights from the sustainability literature into the existing literature on authority.
