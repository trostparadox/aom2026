Author: Virpi Sorsa University of Eastern Finland

Session overview

This essay examines the interplay between academic identity, precarity, and agency in neoliberal academia. Using personal narrative as a reflexive lens, it explores how identity work unfolds amid pressures for productivity, visibility, and “excellence.” Drawing on Goffman’s (1959) dramaturgical metaphor and Bourdieu’s (1988) concept of academic field, the analysis highlights tensions between authenticity and performativity in competitive scholarly environments. The essay engages with literature on audit cultures and precarious labor, showing how hyper-competition erodes intellectual autonomy and amplifies emotional strain. Against this backdrop, it argues for reflexivity as an ethical and methodological stance, following Samra- Fredericks’ (2010) call to reinstate the “I” in strategy research. Reclaiming agency requires resisting abstraction and commodification, fostering spaces that value authenticity, diversity, and critical engagement over narrow metrics. The essay envisions a more humane and inclusive academic future grounded in relational scholarship.
