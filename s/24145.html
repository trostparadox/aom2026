Meaning Without Means: HRM and the Production of Meaningful Work in Chilean Education

CMS
Francisca Alvarez Figueroa (Pontificia Universidad Católica de Valparaíso); Johanna Aguilera
This research explores how public education employees in Chile construct and sustain meaningful work amid institutional constraints. Responding to calls for social and contextual perspectives on meaningful work, it examines how HRM and organizational arrangements shape employees’ sense of purpose and dignity. Drawing on a qualitative, interpretivist-constructivist approach and based on 48 interviews with managers, professionals, and administrative staff across four public education providers, the study finds that meaningful work is a socially constructed and relational process rather than an individual psychological state. Four themes illustrate how meaning both sustains and strains employees: HRM structures shaping precarity, finding meaning in community vulnerability, constructing meaning through self-recognition, and cultural self-sacrifice. The study contributes to public HRM scholarship by revealing how meaningful work can simultaneously support and constrain workers, linking these dynamics to decent work and advocating HRM reforms that enhance fairness, reciprocity, and institutional care.
