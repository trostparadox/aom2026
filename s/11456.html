Session overview

Sustainability is more than technical competencies – it is the result of a particular way of thinking and being which is expressed in actions. But how to develop a mindset in a classroom where impatient students expect to learn skills and acquire knowledge that prepares them for the job market? In this PDW we tell the story of how educators from different disciplines and in different contexts have found an answer: using art. Art bypasses our rational analytical frames, creating emotional and cognitive impressions. Through music, visual arts, handcrafts or theatre performance, art delights and challenges the individual; art provokes, raises questions, and activates critical reflection. Art is increasingly recognized as a pedagogical tool for shaping a sustainable future (Antonacopoulou, & Taylor, 2019; De Monthoux, 2022; Lehtimäki et al., 2024; Magsamen, & Ross, 2023) In this PDW we will hear the stories of educators from Brazil, Netherlands, India, Russia, Georgia and USA, sharing how they are using art (film, visual arts, music, culinary arts, performance) with students and what happens when they do so. Then, participants will experience two forms of using art: creating a haiku and using images. Listening to stories, the audience will be able to connect different frameworks to develop a sustainability mindset: Sustainability Mindset Principles (Rimanoczy, 2021), Sustainable Development Goals (UN, 2015) and PRME i5 framework (PRME, 2023) Inner Development Goals and learn how art-based interventions in education for sustainability (Ivanova, Rimanoczy, & Singhal, 2026) are a powerful pedagogic resource for engaging students.
