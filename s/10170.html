Session overview

Knowledge exchange in organizational and professional settings depends not only on formal structures and systems, but also on who participates, how individuals are socially positioned, and how technologies structure interaction, visibility, and access. However, knowledge exchange can remain uneven in visibility, valuation, and durability, underscoring the importance of examining the mechanisms that shape participation in these processes. This symposium brings together a set of papers that examine knowledge exchange as embedded in social and technological architecture. Across diverse settings, the five papers study how gender, occupational tenure, evaluative processes, and digital platforms shape participation in knowledge exchange to examine how value is differentially realized across actors. Taken together, the symposium advances a more integrated understanding of how social and technological conditions jointly structure participation and advantage in knowledge exchange.
