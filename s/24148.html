Gendered Sensemaking in AI-Supported Managerial Decisions

CTO
Mehdi Roopaei (University of Wisconsin-Platteville); Afrooz Ketabchi (Rite-Hite)
AI-based decision support is rapidly becoming an infrastructure for managerial judgment, reshaping how decisions are justified, contested, and audited. Yet managers engage algorithmic recommendations unevenly. This conceptual paper develops a phenomenon-driven framework explaining gendered patterns in interpreting, trusting, resisting, and integrating AI advice. Drawing on sensemaking theory (Weick, 1995; Maitlis & Christianson, 2014), sociomateriality (Orlikowski, 2000; Orlikowski & Scott, 2008), and trust in automation (Lee & See, 2004; Hoff & Bashir, 2015), we argue that women managers often enact advantaged sensemaking—contextual interpretation, calibrated reliance, ethical vigilance, and communicative integration—that can improve legitimacy and learning. However, speed-centric evaluation norms and low-transparency AI designs can misrecognize these practices as hesitation, producing organizational blind spots and career penalties. We offer propositions, boundary conditions, and implications for AI design, governance, and leadership evaluation that elevate appropriate reliance and legitimacy rather than mere adoption.
