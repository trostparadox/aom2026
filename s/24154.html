Rules Without Rulers: Code of Conduct Adoption as Discursive Governance in Open Source

CTO
Hantong Zhao (Harbin University of Commerce)
How do strangers coordinate complex work when no manager can compel cooperation? In open-source software, a common response is the Code of Conduct (CoC)—a textual policy that specifies interaction norms and enforcement. We theorize CoCs as a sociomaterial technology of governance that works discursively: by creating common knowledge about acceptable conduct, CoCs lower interactional risk in code review and shift language toward epistemic humility (uncertainty-aware inquiry) and politeness (facework). These micro-discursive shifts accelerate knowledge integration. Using a three-year panel of 32,450 pull requests from 1,205 GitHub repositories, we exploit staggered CoC adoption with event-study difference-in-differences designs and NLP measures of humility, politeness, and affect. After adoption, review discourse becomes more humble (ß = 0.31, p < .001) and polite (ß = 0.20, p < .001) and less negative (ß = -0.11, p < .001). Pull requests merge faster (~16%; ß = -0.17, p < .001), are more likely to merge (+3.6 pp; ß = 0.04, p < .001), and require fewer review cycles (ß = -0.13, p < .001), especially in high-turnover and geographically dispersed projects. We discuss implications for digital governance and nonhierarchical organizing.
