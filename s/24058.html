CSR and Youth Development: How Participation Shapes Capabilities and Wellbeing

SIM
Oluseyi Aju (Leeds Beckett University); Egena Ode (Manchester Metropolitan University)
Corporate social responsibility (CSR) research has paid little attention to the developmental impacts of CSR on young people, particularly the mechanisms through which youth participation translates into sustained developmental and wellbeing outcomes. To address this gap, we develop a stakeholder-centric theoretical framework that positions young people not just as evaluators of CSR, but as developmental stakeholders whose engagement can expand capabilities and shape life trajectories. Drawing on positive youth development theory and the capability approach, we introduce the concept of youth developmental authenticity (YDA), defined as young people’s experience of CSR participation as genuinely furthering their development and emerging sense of purpose. We theorise how youth CSR participation gives rise to YDA, how YDA enables capability development, and how these processes promote youth thriving and wellbeing. We further specify how individual youth attributes and CSR programme design features condition these relationships. In doing so, the paper promotes society-centric CSR by theorising youth-specific mechanisms of impact and offers guidance for the design of CSR initiatives that meaningfully support youth development.
