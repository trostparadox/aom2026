Entrepreneurial Ecosystems: The Measurement Paradox

ENT
Alberto Bongiorno (University of Messina); Maria Cristina Cinici (University of Messina)
Entrepreneurial ecosystems (EEs) have become central to understanding how networks of actors, institutions, and contextual factors shape entrepreneurship within a specific context. Despite growing interest, ecosystem measurement remains fragmented and contested, with ongoing debate over appropriate metrics to guide methodological selection across diverse contexts. This paper addresses these challenges through a systematic integrative review of 38 peer-reviewed studies focused on EE measurement. The analysis reveals methodological heterogeneity, which was then organized into three distinct clusters based on their dominant measurement logic: indicator-based, multidimensional tools, and multi-methods approaches. The review further identifies four main gaps that demonstrate measurement validity as contingent rather than universal. In response, we propose the EE-Fit Framework, which operationalizes contingency-based principles through a structured choice matrix integrating research objectives, data availability, and unit of analysis. By establishing that measurement appropriateness emerges from alignment among purpose, method, and context, the framework provides conceptual clarity for scholars while offering practical guidance for policymakers and practitioners in selecting measurement approaches suited to their specific analytical objectives, resource constraints, and ecosystem characteristics. Additionally, by emphasizing cross context adaptability, the framework advances a globally applicable approach to measuring entrepreneurial ecosystems, supporting comparative analysis across diverse institutional and regional settings.
