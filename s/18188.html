Session overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly embedded in creative work, reshaping how organizations generate, evaluate, and scale novel ideas. Rather than simply augmenting creativity, AI reorganizes creative processes and redistributes creative outcomes in ways that raise new theoretical and strategic questions. This symposium examines how AI intervenes at different stages of creativity and how these interventions affect quality and diversity of ideas, as well as competitive dynamics. Across multiple empirical settings, the papers treat creativity as a multistage process involving idea generation and idea evaluation, and as an outcome with distributional consequences rather than only average effects. Collectively, the papers show that AI can expand creative capacity, raise baseline performance, and improve the likelihood of high-quality outputs, while simultaneously compressing diversity, amplifying differences in returns to skill, and reshaping the mapping between effort, competence, and success. These effects depend critically on how AI is deployed, when it enters the creative workflow, and by whom. By integrating evidence from creative collaboration, innovation evaluation, cultural production, and competitive training environments, the symposium moves beyond binary debates about whether AI helps or harms creativity. Instead, it highlights fundamental tensions between exploration and constraint, efficiency and diversity, equalization and amplification. The symposium advances a process-oriented perspective on AI-enabled creativity, offering new insights for research in strategic management, technology and innovation management, and organization and management theory.
