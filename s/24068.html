When Quality Travels: Microfoundations and Relational Archetypes in Global Supply Chain Triads

OSCM
Ebrahim Soltani (Hamdan Bin Mohammed Smart University); Adrian John Wilkinson (Griffith University)
Although global supply chains are engineered for consistency, quality itself travels, moving through interpretive and relational handoffs that subtly reshape it across tiers. Prior research typically attributes these variations to dyadic relational properties such as trust, commitment, and communication, yet this macro-level focus masks the actor-level dispositions through which institutional and cultural conditions are interpreted and enacted. To address this gap, we draw on microfoundations theory to explain how actors pick up cues, interpret signals, and pass along quality expectations, and we augment this account with strategic triangle theory (STT) to show how these interpretations take patterned relational form within triads. Using inductive case studies of six firms in a high-growth global footwear supply chain, we identify three interpretive microfoundations (discretionary latitude, structural dependence, and customer-facing exposure) that shape everyday conduct. These microfoundations consolidate into triadic relational archetypes (amity, ambiguity, and enmity) that reorganise expectations and redistribute coordination responsibilities across tiers. As these archetypes interact, they generate recurrent quality trajectories: situational, stabilised, and surface quality, with systemic quality remaining aspirational. Together, these insights reframe supply chain quality management (SCQM) by showing that quality is not a fixed technical endpoint, but a triadic, socially process produced through actors’ interpretations and the relational configurations those interpretations set in motion, thereby challenging the field’s prevailing dyadic assumptions and its longstanding focus on macro-relational constructs.
