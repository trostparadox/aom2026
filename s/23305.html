Doughnut Entrepreneurship: A Goals-Based View of (Un)Sustainable Venturing

ENT
Ash Ganzoury (University of Colorado-Boulder)
Drawing on inductive qualitative research with entrepreneurs at sustainable startup accelerator programs in USA (2021-2022) and France (2023-2024), this study develops a goals-based framework for understanding sustainable venture trajectories and unsustainable entrepreneurship. The analysis reveals three primary contributions. First, I introduce goal hierarchy to sustainable entrepreneurship since I discovered that founders operate along two distinct dimensions: Domain (what type of sustainability—financial, social, or ecological) and Temporal (when sustainability goals are projected to be achieved— present or future). The intersection of Domain and Temporal dimensions produces a 3×2 matrix with a theoretically meaningful asymmetry. Second, I develop a formal definition of unsustainable entrepreneurship and diagnostic criteria. Third, I introduce the Doughnut Entrepreneurship Framework, which identifies two modes—the "inner hole" of unsustainable entrepreneurship and a "safe operating space" where ventures successfully integrate temporal sustainability across three goals domains. This research contributes to entrepreneurship theory by disentangling domain and temporal dimensions of venture sustainability, and to practice by offering diagnostic tools for accelerator mentors, entrepreneurial support organizations and founders navigating sustainability trajectories.

Achieving Sustainable Growth Through Configurations of Venture Capital Funding

ENT
Asli Irem Orgun Senel (Université libre de Bruxelles); Judith Behrens; Olivier Witmeur
This study examines how venture capital ownership structures shape the growth paths of entrepreneurial companies. Drawing on a configurational approach, we examine how board representation, equity ownership, and portfolio duration of venture capital funds lead to sustainable growth or asset-growth of entrepreneurial companies. Using fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) on a longitudinal dataset of 74 Belgian entrepreneurial companies, we identify two growth pathways. Sustainable growth is enabled primarily through hybrid configurations in which public and private venture capitalists (VCs) collaborate; public VCs provide patient capital and environmental, social, and governance monitoring; and private VCs exercise board-level control. This complementarity is central to sustainable growth but is not required for asset growth, which can be achieved through either public governance or private capital-led models. This research advances configurational theorizing in entrepreneurial finance by showing the interaction between public and private VCs in influencing the sustainability orientation of entrepreneurial companies. The implications further extend to entrepreneurs seeking funding combinations, VCs looking for partnerships for sustainable growth , and policymakers promoting sustainable finance.

Achieving Sustainable Competitive Advantage and Minimizing Tensions in Adopting Circular Economy

ENT
Mohammed Shamsul Karim (University of Essex); Mohammad Shahab Uddin (University of Chittagong)
The circular economy (CE) is increasingly recognised as a critical tool for achieving sustainability goals and addressing global challenges such as climate change and global warming. Transitioning from linear production and consumption models to circular and closed-loop processes requires the support of circular entrepreneurship. However, research on circular entrepreneurship has received limited scholarly attention, with few studies examining the tensions circular entrepreneurs face and how they develop dynamic capabilities (DC) to adopt and transition smoothly to the CE. This study aims to fill this gap by empirically investigating 32 companies in the context of a developing country, Bangladesh. Through a qualitative approach, semi-structured interviews were conducted with entrepreneurs and top-level managers. The findings indicate that circular entrepreneurs encounter various challenges across market, resource, and stakeholder discourses. The study explores how they develop dynamic capabilities—specifically sensing, seizing, and transforming—to overcome these challenges and gain a sustainable competitive advantage. The paper provides key insights into the managerial and policy implications, offering recommendations for future research on circular entrepreneurship and the CE transition through a DC lens.

The Role of Sustainability in Linking Entrepreneurial Leadership to Educational Innovation

ENT
Bahjat Mohammad Ogielat (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Ishraque Hossain Chowdhury (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Osama Alawneh (Jadara University); Mahmoud Allahham (Amman Arab University); Tarek Al Okaely (University of New England); Wasef Almajali (Amman Arab University); Pinki Maria Sarker
The given study explores the variables of entrepreneurial leadership and innovation in education in the Jordanian universities, the mediator variable is the sustainability. The research will also seek to identify the role of entrepreneurial leadership in ensuring sustainability practices, which on the other hand increases innovativeness in the learning scenarios and has the essence dimensions of innovativeness, proactiveness, taking risks, vision, and empowerment. A quantitative research design was selected and the sample of the respondents was a representative group of academic and administrative workers employed in the public and the private universities in Jordan. It used Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to test the hypothesis testing and mediating effect of sustainability that showed that entrepreneurial leadership has a positive impact on the sustainability practices and educational innovation at rather high level. In addition to this, sustainability was developed as a mediating variable; this means that the environmentally friendly and socially conscious university in terms of its policies have a more compelling case to translate the entrepreneurial undertaking into the creative learning products. These findings suggest that the leader who adds strategic vision and entrepreneurial orientation can instill sustainability to the institutional culture that in turn caused the endless changes and academic innovativeness. The research paper is part of the literature regarding leadership in higher education as it adds the concept of entrepreneurship and sustainability to the innovation model. It provides the policy implications on the operational policy of the policymakers and the heads of higher educational institutions in Jordan on the significance of developing the entrepreneurial spirit and sustainable governance into a vision of the educational change and sustainability of the institution competitiveness.

Sustainability of Place: Organizational Identity Work in Place-Based Enterprises

ENT
Peter Gustafsson (Umeå University); Zsuzsanna Vincze (Umeå University); Norbert Steigenberger (Umeå University); Quang Vinh David Evansluong (Umeå University)
This paper investigates how organizations integrate sustainability into their identities through processes deeply connected to place. We address the question of how micro-, small-, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) in peripheral regions create and enact sustainability-oriented identities by drawing on the geographic, material, and symbolic dimensions of place. While research has explored organizational identity and sustainability separately, less is known about how place influences the creation of sustainability identities. Prior studies on hybrid organizations have focused on tensions between utilitarian and normative identity features, but we examine the interplay between two normative features, place and sustainability, and how these can both align and conflict. We conducted a qualitative comparative case study of six MSMEs in northern Sweden. Data were collected through in-depth interviews, field observations, and archival sources. Using Gioia methodology, we identified first-order concepts, second-order themes, and three aggregate dimensions that explain how sustainability of place is enacted. Our analysis reveals a three-stage process. Firms first emplace sustainability by recognizing the fragility of local and distant resources and communities. They then anchor sustainability through materials, meanings, and locational commitments. Finally, they enact identity work cognitively, physically, behaviourally, and discursively to integrate sustainability into daily practices. These dynamics reveal both synergies and tensions between social and environmental sustainability goals. We contribute to theories of organizational identity, identity work, and place-based organizations by conceptualizing sustainability of place and showing how two normative identity features interact. Practically, our findings offer guidance for managers and policymakers seeking to foster sustainable transformation in peripheral and community-rooted enterprises.
