The Bright Side of Diversity: Mapping HRM Strategies to Promote D&I in Organizations

DEI
Simona Mormile (University of Salerno); Roberta Romano (University of Salerno); Gabriella Piscopo (University of Salerno); Paola Adinolfi (University of Salerno)
Purpose – This paper aims to systematically examine how Human Resource Management (HRM) strategies have been employed to promote diversity and inclusion (D&I) in organizations with the goal of identifying key themes, trends, and strategic approaches that organizations employ to foster inclusive workplaces. Design/methodology/approach – Adopting a bibliometric methodology, the study analyzes a curated dataset of 870 journal articles published between 2012 and 2025. Using the Bibliometrix package in R, the study conducts trend topic analysis and conceptual structure mapping to examine the temporal evolution, geographical distribution, and thematic clusters in the literature. Findings – The findings reveal a steady growth in academic interest in HRM strategies for D&I, with a notable surge in publications from 2019 onward, spurred by global sociopolitical movements and regulatory initiatives. Thematic analysis identifies well-developed clusters focused on gender identity, ethnicity, and career development, alongside emerging areas such as inclusive leadership, AI in HR processes, and organizational sustainability. The study also highlights gaps in intersectional theorizing and systemic approaches to inclusion, suggesting opportunities for future research. Originality/value – This paper offers the first comprehensive bibliometric synthesis of the literature on HRM and D&I, systematically mapping the field’s conceptual, intellectual, and social structure. It provides valuable insights for scholars, practitioners, and policymakers aiming to understand and advance inclusive HRM practices. By uncovering the “bright side” of diversity, the study reframes D&I not as a compliance obligation but as a strategic and cultural transformation imperative.
