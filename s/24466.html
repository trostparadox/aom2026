Why Virtual Reality Projects Fail: Disclosure Paradox in Experiential Technology Crowdfunding

CTO
JiaGeng Tu (University of Science and Technology of China); Hefu Liu; Jiahong Xu (Hainan University); Xiaoyu Yao (University of Science and Technology of China)
Despite the strategic importance of the Metaverse, virtual reality ventures on Kickstarter suffer from a success rate significantly lower than the platform average. We theorize that this disparity stems from the epistemic challenge of conveying experiential attributes like realism and immersion through a disembodied text medium. Drawing on the persuasion knowledge model, we propose that high intensity semantic claims lacking diagnostic evidence activate backer skepticism rather than signaling quality. By applying a transformer based deep semantic embedding approach to a dataset of 1021 campaigns from 2010 to 2025, we find that emphasizing realism and immersion paradoxically predicts failure. However, this negative effect is reversed by creator experience and description length which serve as warrants to validate the claims. Our study resolves the disclosure paradox in experiential technology crowdfunding by delineating the boundary conditions under which soft signals succeed or backfire.
