Engagement Under Uncertainty: A Process View of Tech-Based Crowdfunding Decisions

Authors: Md Parvez Alam The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley; Xuan Wang The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley; Jia Shen The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley; Geng Sun The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley

Session overview

Tech-based crowdfunding has become a mainstream source of entrepreneurial finance, yet technology campaigns face substantial uncertainty about feasibility and delivery. Prior work links engagement to crowdfunding outcomes but usually treats engagement as a unitary construction and assumes that more engagement uniformly improves performance. This perspective offers little insight into how entrepreneurs optimally allocate engagement to generate funding outcomes under uncertainty. We address this issue by adopting a process view of the crowdfunding decision making, in which backers move from initial attention to subsequent evaluation before committing resources. Building on this view, we distinguish between Superficial Engagement (SupE)— visibility-oriented interaction that attracts attention but provides limited evaluative information—and Substantial Engagement (SubE)—information-rich communication that supports feasibility assessment. We argue that SupE is effective for capturing attention but exhibits diminishing returns as evaluation intensifies, whereas SubE is less efficient for attracting attention yet increasingly effective in supporting funding commitment. We further theorize that founder platform experience shapes this allocation logic by reducing reliance on superficial cues and amplifying the value of substantive engagement.
