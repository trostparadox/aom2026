Session overview

Strategic disruption forces rapid transformation, yet many initiatives stall because organizations fail to convert capability investments like openness, analytics, and agile practices into coordinated, legitimate, and sustained behavior change. From a change management point of view, we position strategic renewal as an execution and institutionalization problem where renewal emerges when change is mobilized, enacted, and anchored. Thus, we propose an integrative model in which dynamic capabilities and open innovation provide reconfiguration potential, while agentic algorithmic management and agile change techniques accelerate coordination, visibility, experimentation, and learning, in the backdrop of possible risks related to resistance and eroded change legitimacy. We argue these antecedents influence strategic renewal primarily through the quality of transformational change activities, with Kotter’s framework as a mediating mechanism. Methodologically, we outline a multi-source, time-lagged field design and mediation tests linking dynamic capabilities, open innovation, algorithmic management, and agile change management to strategic renewal through Kotter’s change management model. Practically, the research sheds light on how renewing under disruption through combining agile and algorithmic management is possible with legitimacy-building interventions.
