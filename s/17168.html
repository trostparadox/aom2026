Think Big, Favoring Men: How Leader's Abstract Cognition Shapes Gender Inequality in Development

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Author: Yujun Sha City University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Gender inequality in leadership advancement persists despite women’s increasing workforce participation. Prior research has emphasized structural barriers and gender stereotypes as primary explanations, yet comparatively little attention has been paid to the cognitive conditions under which leaders themselves become more susceptible to biased developmental decisions. Drawing on construal level theory, this research examines how leaders’ abstract versus concrete thinking shapes gender differences in developmental supervision. Across one multi-wave field study and one controlled experiment, we find consistent evidence that leaders operating at higher construal levels provide significantly more developmental supervision to male employees than to female employees. This gendered pattern does not emerge when leaders operate at lower construal levels. We further demonstrate that perceived leader prototypicality mediates this effect: abstract thinking heightens reliance on prototypical leadership schemas that remain implicitly masculinized, leading male employees to be viewed as more development-worthy. By integrating construal level theory with leadership development and gender role congruity perspectives, this research challenges the assumption that abstract, strategic thinking necessarily promotes fairness. Instead, it reveals a paradoxical cognitive mechanism through which high-level construal, often valorized in leadership, can unintentionally reinforce gender inequality in access to developmental resources. These findings advance theory by identifying a previously overlooked cognitive pathway underlying gendered developmental supervision and offer practical guidance for organizations seeking to design more equitable leadership development systems.
