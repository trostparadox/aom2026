Modeling Nurses’ Workplace Violence Attributions: A Psychological Network Analysis

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 5
HCM

People

Author: Graham Hughes Lowman Kennesaw State University

Session overview

Workplace violence is a pervasive issue in nursing, yet little research has examined how nurses attribute its causes or how experience influences attributions. This study aims to address this gap in the literature by using qualitative data from 115 nurses to generate attribution items and quantitative data from 613 nurses to model attribution structures through psychological network analysis and directed acyclic graph analysis. Findings indicate that nurses attribute workplace violence to four primary factors: personal characteristics, organizational characteristics, the care environment, and workload. The network analysis indicated that these attributions are interrelated, suggesting nurses hold an integrated view of internal and external contributors to violence. Importantly, nurses who reported above-average exposure to workplace violence were less likely to attribute incidents to external factors, while maintaining similar levels of attribution to personal characteristics.
