CSR Signals and Altruistic Attributions: The Boundary Role of Employer Attractiveness

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Sara Dragicevic University of Montenegro; Teodora Globarevic University of Montenegro; Milena Mugoša University of Montenegro; Aleksandra Jovanovic University of Montenegro

Session overview

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) is widely assumed to enhance employer attractiveness and job pursuit intentions, yet the micro-level meaning-making through which job seekers interpret CSR signals remains under-specified. Drawing on signaling and attribution perspectives, we propose that perceived CSR increases intention to apply because it is interpreted as evidence of altruistic organizational motives. We further argue that this signaling effect is not uniform: when an organization is already perceived as highly attractive due to CSR, additional CSR information provides less incremental, motive-relevant information and should translate into altruistic attributions less strongly. We tested a first-stage moderated mediation model in a cross-sectional online survey in Montenegro (N = 795). Perceived CSR was positively associated with perceived altruism, which in turn strongly predicted intention to apply. Consistent with our theorizing, CSR-based employer attractiveness attenuated the CSR–altruism relationship, resulting in weaker indirect effects of CSR on intention to apply via altruism when CSR-based employer attractiveness was higher. These findings clarify altruistic attribution as a key mechanism in CSR-based recruitment and demonstrate diminishing marginal signaling value of CSR when CSR-based employer attractiveness is already high.
