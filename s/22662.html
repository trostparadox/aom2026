Session overview

This paper development workshop presents an emerging conceptual framework that explores how business schools can move beyond accommodation-based approaches to neurodiversity toward institution- wide models of neuroinclusive management education. Although higher education has expanded disability support services and reasonable accommodations, many management education practices—including classroom participation norms, assessment methods, team-based learning, and leadership development—continue to reflect assumptions that privilege neurotypical ways of learning, communicating, and demonstrating competence. As a result, neurodivergent learners often encounter structural barriers that limit participation, belonging, and educational success despite possessing valuable cognitive strengths and diverse perspectives. The workshop introduces an interdisciplinary framework integrating neurodiversity theory, Universal Design for Learning, ecological systems theory, and inclusive pedagogy to examine how management education can be redesigned to support a broader range of learners. Rather than viewing neurodiversity solely through an accommodation lens, the project positions cognitive diversity as a source of innovation, creativity, and enhanced collective learning within business schools.
