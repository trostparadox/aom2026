Session overview

Recently there has been increased interest in understanding paradox theory, including the adoption of Eastern traditions such as Daoism and yin-yang thinking into the domain of social issues. However, a wide range of Eastern-oriented thought remains underexplored, such as management philosophies, and ethical, religious and spiritual traditions. Since East Asian perspectives often place greater emphasis on paradoxical dynamics understood as contradictory and interdependent tensions among social forces, they may offer alternative ways of understanding social issues distinct from conventional Western approaches. In this PDW, five experienced scholars will introduce research on underexamined forms of paradoxical thinking in East Asian contexts, particularly in China and Japan. This PDW is designed to help participants explore the ethical and spiritual traditions that influence how Eastern cultures approach paradox. It also encourages the audience to bring these perspectives into their own research. In addition, we expect to promote collaboration and open dialogue among scholars from different fields, such as social issues management, paradox theory, organizational behavior, and corporate responsibility, and to generate new insights for the study of management philosophy, business ethics, and spirituality.
