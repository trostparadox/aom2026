The Social Cost of High Performance: Interactive Effects of Task Interdependence and Need for Power

Author: Radhika R Indian Institute of Management Lucknow

Session overview

While it is true that academicians have long been directing scholarly attention toward the benefits of high performance, emerging perspectives document a whole new horizon of the social costs associated with high performance. However, this growing line of inquiry has progressed in isolation with studies largely presuming coworker reactions as discretionary and focussing on their comparative experiences such as envy and threat. The understanding of coworker reactions beyond these lenses remains limited. Thus, drawing on social information processing theory, we shift the focus to constrained work conditions and the high performer’s motives, thereby developing a framework to explain the synergistic effects of task interdependence and perceived need for power in tandem on high performer ostracism. Our two-wave, multisource field study (N = 282) demonstrated significant three-way interaction effects. The findings contribute to the burgeoning research on socio-relational consequences of high performance by revealing the boundary conditions that regulate coworker ostracism.
