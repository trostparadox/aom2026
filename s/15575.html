Session overview

Geopolitical tensions introduce significant challenges for multinational enterprises (MNEs), fundamentally altering their strategic landscape. This symposium focuses on the individual and organizational socio-political identities and structural constraints that determine not only how MNEs respond to, but often trigger these tensions. While extant research typically assumes rational adaptation to exogenous shocks, it overlooks that national security scrutiny and geopolitical friction are often attracted by the firm’s own identity and affiliation. By integrating these perspectives, this symposium investigates how distinct internal attributes - ranging from political connections and CEO national identity to state ownership - drive MNEs to buffer against political shocks, resist foreign takeovers, reconfigure innovation strategies, or prolong the cross-border acquisition withdrawal process.
