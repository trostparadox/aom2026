Conditional Indirect Effects of Identity-Related Mistreatment on Cynicism Through Competence

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Authors: Odianna Che The University of Texas at Arlington; Lars Uriah Johnson The University of Texas at Arlington; Marla L. White Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University; Aylime Bueno The University of Texas at Arlington; Shona G. Smith University of Houston

Session overview

Integrating Self-Determination Theory and literature on identity-related mistreatment, we developed and tested a model of observed identity related mistreatment and the resulting thwarting of competence needs satisfaction. As observers grappled with the reality of others like them being mistreated, we expected them to not only report lower competence needs satisfaction, but also higher levels of cynicism. As such, we hypothesized the relationship between identity-related mistreatment and cynicism is indirect through competence needs satisfaction. We further argue this undermining of psychological health is conditionalized by the observer’s fairness values such that those higher on the construct would be more sensitive to identity-related mistreatment, resulting in stronger a relationship between the mistreatment and competence needs satisfaction as well as mistreatment in the indirect relationship with cynicism. We evaluated our hypotheses in a U.S. population sample, finding support for the hypothesized main effects, indirect effects, and conditional effects.
