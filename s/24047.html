From Influencers to Followers: Advocacy Processes of Social Enterprises in Resource-Scarce Contexts

SIM
Katharina Eichinger (Technical University of Munich); Claudia Doblinger (Technical University of Munich); Frank Martin Belz (Technical University of Munich)
Electric mobility, as a new technology recently introduced to Kenya, has the potential to transform the country’s transportation landscape, which is shaped by millions of petrol-fueled motorcycle taxis (boda bodas). However, this new technology requires new regulations and policies, while the population lacks awareness – two challenges various social enterprises in the electric motorcycle industry address through processes of policy and sociocultural advocacy. Using a multiple-case study approach, we compare the processes of seven social enterprises’ policy and sociocultural advocacy, as well as their outcomes, through the lens of entrepreneurial agency. We identify four main processes common to all social enterprises in our sample: individual lobbying, empowering beneficiaries (i.e., boda boda riders and women), co-opting intermediaries, and coalitioning. However, their degree of agency varies over time. While Influencers employ high degrees of agency in all four processes and amplify their agency over time, Followers transfer and withdraw agency in individual lobbying and coalitioning but amplify their agency in co-opting intermediaries and start blending individual lobbying with empowering beneficiaries through experimental approaches. This study contributes to the emerging scholarly discourse on the political role of social enterprises in creating macro-level changes by showing how social enterprises simultaneously engage in both types of advocacy. We also find that, in our context, policy and sociocultural advocacy have twofold outcomes – an overt outcome related to economic goals and a more covert outcome related to social goals. We label this inclusive viability.
