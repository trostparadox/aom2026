Reframing Resistance to Change: A Relational Perspective on Help-Seeking and Change-Oriented Culture

ODC
Filomena Pagnozzi (University of G. D'Annunzio); Simone Di Plinio; Gilda M. Antonelli
This conceptual paper reframes resistance to organizational change as a relational and socio-technical phenomenon. Rather than a static attitude or irrational reaction, resistance is viewed as an adaptive signal that emerges through interactions among individual behaviors and organizational culture. Drawing on Social Exchange Theory, Socio-Technical Systems Theory, and Complexity Theory, the paper develops an integrative framework that connects help-seeking and help-giving behaviors with the dynamics of change-oriented organizational cultures. At the individual level, these behavioral styles reveal how people negotiate reciprocity, autonomy, and belonging during transformation. At the organizational level, resistance is understood as an emergent property of a culture that integrates technological, relational, and value-based dimensions. Far from external drivers, digital tools are embedded within organizational culture, reshaping trust, fairness, and inclusion. By linking these interdependent levels, the framework positions resistance not as opposition but as a relational signal of sense-making, a manifestation of how individuals and systems adapt and learn through change. The paper also outlines directions for developing a multidimensional psychometric tool that captures implicit aspects of prosocial orientation, identity alignment, and perceived technological disruption. This study contributes to a human-centered understanding of organizational transformation, suggesting that resistance can become a catalyst for learning and renewal.
