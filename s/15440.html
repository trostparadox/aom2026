Mercenaries in the War for Talent: Freelancers’ Careers Between HR and Line Managers

Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1

Authors: Jolanta Maj Wroclaw University of Technology; Aneta Hamza Orlinska EM Normandie

Session overview

In the contemporary “war for talent,” organizations increasingly rely on freelancers to secure critical expertise while excluding them from formal career systems. Drawing on 60 in-depth interviews with freelancers (n = 40) and managers (n = 20), and using a grounded-theory approach, this study develops a relational, process-oriented model of freelancers’ career development situated at the intersection of freelancers, managers, and HR. Using a war metaphor, we conceptualize freelancers as organizational “mercenaries”: highly skilled, mobile actors who contribute to organizational performance but remain positioned outside institutionalized systems of career ownership, evaluation, and development. Our findings reveal a triadic dynamic in which HR largely withdraws from responsibility for freelancers’ careers, managers assume an informal boundary-spanning role, and freelancers engage in self-directed career construction. While managers may partially compensate for HR’s absence by providing feedback, mentoring, and access to development opportunities, such practices remain discretionary and uneven. As a result, freelancers experience ambiguous career ownership, oscillating between autonomy and marginalization. This study contributes to careers and HRM research by theorizing freelancers’ careers as relational and contingent outcomes of organizational positioning rather than purely individual choice. By introducing the metaphor of mercenary careers, we show how the war for talent produces not only flexibility but also new forms of exclusion from career and performance systems.
