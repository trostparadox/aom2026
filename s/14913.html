Session overview

Mobility is a foundational resource for participation in higher education, work, health care, and civic life. Yet access to reliable, affordable, and safe mobility remains uneven across income, geography, disability status, work schedules, and digital inclusion (Pan, Martin, & Shaheen, 2022). This PDW treats mobility equity as a management consulting problem: how can multi-stakeholder coalitions design interventions that deliver immediate performance benefits (e.g., attendance, retention, workforce participation, and time savings) while building sustainable, scalable capability (e.g., governance, responsible data use, and trust)? Using CREST Car Share as a case-in-point, the workshop offers an interactive “consulting lab” that moves from problem framing to pilot design, measurement, and scaling. The workshop is designed for scholars, consultants, practitioners, and scholar-practitioners interested in field-based interventions, consulting tool development, and community-engaged research. It integrates equity-centered pedagogy with applied consulting practice by demonstrating how student-engaged consulting models can be embedded within academic courses and professional training. Participants will examine how student consulting teams can be structured, supervised, and assessed to work with mobility organizations and nonprofit partners on problem framing, program evaluation, and translational research. Although mobility is the focal context, the tools, consulting models, and instructional approaches are intentionally designed for transferability across domains concerned with community engagement and quality-of-life outcomes. Participants will work in small groups to produce three concrete outputs: (1) a consulting-grade Mobility Equity Intervention Canvas (problem definition, beneficiary segmentation, service design, operating model, adoption and legitimacy plan), (2) a measurement and learning plan (Dadashzadeh et al., 2022) (equity-sensitive metrics, data sources, privacy and ethics safeguards (Belen-Saglam, Yuan, Heering, Ashraf, & Li, 2025), and feedback loops), and (3) a scaling and governance pathway (partnership architecture, funding logic, risk controls, and boundary conditions). Facilitators and invited field partners (Toyota, KINTO, and the CREST team) will share implementation realities—such as adoption barriers (Butler, Yigitcanlar, & Paz, 2021), safety and trust considerations, operational constraints, and platform dynamics—and help participants stress-test feasibility and improve the actionability of their designs. Beyond producing deployable consulting deliverables, the PDW supports translation into actionable scholarship. Teams will identify mechanisms and boundary conditions, then select an evaluation approach suitable for field settings (e.g., quasi-experiments, mixed-method program evaluation, design science, or action research cycles). The PDW is designed for scholars, practitioners, consultants, and scholar-practitioners seeking to advance evidence-based interventions that matter in real organizations and communities. By grounding consulting work in field observation, iterative intervention learning, and concrete deliverables, the workshop aligns with the MC Division’s mission to bridge theory and practice through actionable knowledge.
