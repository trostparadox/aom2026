Kinship in Crisis: Clan-Based Informal Institutions and Export Resilience in China

IM
Yongbing Chen (Xiamen University); Jialin Du (Renmin University of China); Chaoqun Zhan (Hong Kong Baptist University); Muxuan Wei (Xiamen University)
Global disruptions pose profound challenges to the continuity of firms’ international operations, underscoring the strategic importance of organizational resilience. We conceptualize export resilience as a firm’s capacity not only to withstand performance deterioration during a disruption (resistance) but also to restore export growth in its aftermath (recovery). Drawing on institutional theory, we theorize clan culture as a historically embedded informal institution that enhances both dimensions of export resilience by by mobilizing trust-based networks for collective action, informal financing, and knowledge sharing. We find that Chinese exporting firms headquartered in regions characterized by stronger clan cultures experience less severe export contractions during a major global disruption and achieve more robust post-disruption recoveries than firms located in less clan-oriented regions. These effects are particularly pronounced where formal institutions are weaker and among firms with stronger diaspora connections. This study advances international business research by demonstrating how locally embedded informal institutions shape firms’ capacity to navigate global crises; extends organizational resilience theory by identifying clan culture as a critical external, institutional driver of resilience; and enriches institutional theory by quantifying informal institutions and examining their interaction with formal institutional environments.
