Female Leadership in Operations Strategy: Advancing Lean Manufacturing for Cost Efficiency in China

OSCM
Shilu Sun (Beijing University of Chemical Technology); Na Li (Beijing University of Chemical Technology)
Amid China’s economic slowdown, rising labor and material costs, and intensifying market competition, manufacturing firms face growing pressure to reduce costs and improve operational efficiency. Lean manufacturing has therefore become a central strategy for sustaining competitiveness. This study examines the role of female CEOs in promoting lean manufacturing and evaluates its economic impact using panel data from Chinese A-share listed manufacturing firms from 2007 to 2023. Drawing on upper echelons theory and social role theory, we propose that female CEOs’ risk-averse, detail-oriented, and participative leadership aligns closely with lean principles. Empirical results show that female CEOs significantly enhance lean adoption, with stronger effects in China’s economically advanced eastern regions and during firms’ growth and maturity stages. Moreover, lean initiatives led by female CEOs are associated with notable reductions in selling and administrative expenses, without diminishing R&D investment. These findings advance theory at the intersection of gender diversity and operations strategy and offer practical guidance for manufacturers seeking to achieve sustainable performance through effective leadership and organizational innovation.

Female Representation in Top Management Teams and the Supplier Stability of SMEs

OSCM
Jiaojiao Qin (Zhejiang Sci-Tech University)
Women have increasingly held positions in the top management teams of enterprises all over the world, influencing corporate operational decisions. However, this phenomenon is not sufficiently examined in theory. To address this void, this study investigates the relationship between female representation in top management teams (TMTs) and the supplier stability of small- and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). We also consider factors that may affect this relationship: (1) female executive power, which may influence the latitude of action they pose on operational decisions; (2) firm reputation and relative operational performance, which may influence the firm’s trustworthy in the eyes of suppliers. Analyzing a panel dataset of Chinese SMEs, we show that female representation in TMTs improves supplier stability of SMEs, and this relationship is stronger at an intermediate level of female executive power or at high levels of firm reputation. Our findings deepen our understanding of how female executives influence operational outcomes and provide important insights for SMEs.

Is Lean and Industry 4.0 Always a Good Match? A Configurational Approach

OSCM
Mercedes-Victoria Auqui-Caceres (University of Padua); Andrea Furlan; Matteo Podrecca (University of Bergamo)
The integration of Industry 4.0 (I4.0) technologies with lean is considered a critical element for improving its operational performance which ultimately may translate into better financial performance. In this paper, we challenge the assumption that all types of I4.0 technologies combined with lean practices lead to better financial performance. Following a configurational approach, we use a qualitative comparative analysis method on a sample of 30 small to medium-sized Italian companies to identify I4.0-lean configurations associated with high (or low) economic performance (return on assets - ROA). Our findings demonstrate that not every I4.0-lean configuration leads to a superior performance. We examine which combinations of bundles of I4.0 technologies and lean practices are associated with high (or low) financial performance, providing feasible explanations for the ambiguous results of previous studies. Results provide valuable insights into how firms can optimize their I4.0-lean integration strategies to improve financial performance.

Dynamic Capabilities and Supply Chain Digital Transformation: The Moderating Role of Risk

OSCM
Lizbeth Estefania Gutierrez Rodriguez (University of Salamanca); Carmen González-Zapatero (University of Salamanca); Javier González-Benito (University of Salamanca)
Supply Chain Digital Transformation (SCDT) brings adaptability, responsiveness and valuable cumulative learning. However, its implementation is not always straightforward, especially in supply chains that experience high levels of risk. This work examines the crucial role of dynamic capabilities in such transformation, distinguishing their effect upstream in the supply chain, in-house and downstream. Additionally, it explores the direct effect of supply chain risk, internal, originating from supply chain partners, and external, originating from political or sociological shocks, on such transformation. Finally, it proposes the negative moderating effect of this risk on the enabling role of dynamic capabilities. The analysis, based on a sample of 193 Mexican SMEs, confirms the positive and significant effect of dynamic capabilities on the three levels of digital transformation. While no direct impact of risk on transformation is observed, the negative moderating effect of external risk on the relationship between dynamic capabilities and supply chain digital transformation is confirmed. These findings provide new perspectives on the factors that drive SCDT and allow for a better understanding of the contextual elements that shape the influence of dynamic capabilities.

Integrating the Future: A Study on the Critical Factors Affecting the Implementation of Industry 4.0

OSCM
Muhammad Shoaib (Zhejiang Gongshang University); Rongjian Yu (Zhejiang Gongshang University); Hamza Asif Khan (Shenzhen University); Muhammad Taha (Brigham Young University)
In the era of digitalization, Industry 4.0, comprising a suite of developing intelligent and digital technologies, has garnered considerable and diverse interest from scholars and practitioners in logistics operations. However, the literature is deficient in thoroughly examining the relationship between Industry 4.0 and logistical operations. To address this research gap, we adopted data from a systematic literature review (SLR) comprising 91 studies. Firstly, we identify success factors through SLR integrating Industry 4.0 enabling technologies and logistics operations. Later on, a hybrid fuzzy Delphi approach will be employed to assess these factors, and the fuzzy decision-making trial and evaluation laboratory (F-DEMATEL) will be used for prioritization by consulting a panel of experts and presenting a comprehensive framework model. The results of the study demonstrate that “Traceability and Trackability” (I6), “Enhanced operational efficiency” (I17), “Proactive decision-making” (I13), and “Costs reduction” (I7) are considered prime factors. The framework model will help researchers and industrial practitioners understand and consider these factors before implementing Industry 4.0 technologies in logistics operations.
