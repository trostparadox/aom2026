Session overview

The scholarly pursuit to explain the HRM-performance ‘black box’ has highlighted the importance of line managers in translating intended HR practices into actual practices perceived by employees (Wright & Nishii, 2013). While prior research has demonstrated the impact of line manager HR implementation and identified various behavioral drivers (Mirfakhar et al., 2018), studies have predominantly focused on their ability, motivation, and opportunity (AMO) (Kurdi-Nakra et al., 2022). Important gaps remain in understanding line managers’ agency and why their engagement in HRM responsibilities varies (Guest, 2021). Moreover, scholars have also argued that HR implementation is only one facet of line managers’ ever-increasing people management responsibilities (Kulik, 2025), which intersects with their leadership activities (Purcell & Hutchinson, 2007). Recent work has broadened both the measurement (Knies et al., 2020) and conceptualization of such responsibilities (Kehoe & Han, 2020), signaling the need for deeper and more holistic inquiries into line managers’ HRM and people management involvement. This symposium responds to these calls by examining the what and why of line managers’ agentic behaviors across HRM and people management domains. The four papers collectively reconceptualize line managers’ engagement, highlighting and providing empirical insights into the micro-level cognitive processes – such as attribution and sensemaking – that shape their engagement with HR and people management responsibilities, while recognizing influences from relational dynamics and organizational contexts. By advancing understanding of line managers as active contributors rather than passive conduits, the symposium aims to invigorate HR scholarship and chart new research directions on line managers’ HRM involvement in contemporary organizations.
