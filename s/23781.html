Best of Both Worlds? Effective Hybrid Working Policies for Dual Career Parents

HR
EMMA THIRKELL (Northumbria University); Katharina Bader (University of York); Benjamin Bader; Carly Joyce Richards (Northumbria University); John Blenkinsopp (Oslo New University College)
Hybrid work, a flexible work arrangement characterised by dividing work time between office and remote locations, has become the work mode of choice for many working parents. Yet, research suggests that hybrid work may not effectively solve the work-care challenges parents experience, especially when both parents work (dual career parents). In this study, we apply a needs-supply fit perspective assessing parental hybrid working needs and contrasting them with current organisational policies and practices in order to better understand how organisations and human resources managers can address parental hybrid working challenges and develop effective policies for dual-career parents. We employed a qualitative, sequential and multi-informant research design including interviews with 38 working parents investigating their hybrid working experiences and needs, followed by interviews with 15 HR professionals assessing current practice against these needs. Based on our findings, we develop a novel typology of hybrid working policies and identify key shortcomings and misconceptions that hamper parental hybrid working experience, with important implications for theory and practice of hybrid work.
