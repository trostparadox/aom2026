University Collaboration and Firm Innovation: The Role of Knowledge Applicability and Complexity

TIM
Metin Onal Vural; Kremena Slavova (CUNEF University)
University–industry collaboration is widely viewed as a means for firms to access scientific knowledge and enhance innovation, yet firms vary substantially in the innovation benefits they derive from such collaboration. Drawing on the technological search literature, this study examines how firms’ internal knowledge structures shape the relationship between university–industry collaboration and high-impact technological innovation. We focus on two structural properties of firms’ knowledge bases—applicability across technological domains and complexity arising from dense interdependencies—and theorize that they shape firms’ ability to recombine scientific and technological knowledge. Using panel data on 90 U.S. therapeutic biotechnology firms, we find that collaboration with universities is associated with higher innovation impact, but this association is stronger for firms with more broadly applicable knowledge bases and weaker for firms with more complex knowledge bases.
