Session overview

EDI (Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion) initiatives in supply chains are often framed as routes to greater resilience, innovation, and social performance (Tang, 2018). Yet progress is frequently slow, with organisations reverting to business as usual even when inclusive procurement and supplier empowerment promise real value and social benefit (Bodrožic & Gold, 2024). In African contexts, where supply chains are economic lifelines and everyday sites of inclusion, this gap between aspiration and lived practice is particularly consequential. This Professional Development Workshop equips AFAM participants with a behavioural and transformative lens for understanding why EDI change efforts stall and how to design interventions that hold in practice. We centre on status quo bias, the tendency to favour existing suppliers, routines, and procedures because change feels risky, costly, or uncertain (Samuelson & Zeckhauser, 1988). In a structured 90-minute interactive session, participants will identify where “slow progress” shows up in their own supply chain contexts, map decision points where inertia is most likely (such as supplier selection, contract renewal, and policy roll-out), and apply a conceptual model linking EDI interventions to transformative outcomes while accounting for contextual constraints and change levers. Participants will then refine research questions and reflect on a feasible study design suited to African settings, including multi-case qualitative approaches and triangulated data sources. Participants leave with a concise model narrative, a draft study plan or paper outline, and a peer connection for follow-up collaboration.
