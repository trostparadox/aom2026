Session overview

Contemporary organizations increasingly operate through fluid teams characterized by permeable boundaries and continuous membership change, yet prevailing theories remain rooted in assumptions of stability and boundedness. This symposium challenges these static foundations by examining how the change of members—driven by internal mobility, job rotation, and project-based assignments—fundamentally transforms the cognitive, structural, and relational fabric of team effectiveness. Integrating four empirical studies spanning individual, dyadic, and team levels of analysis across organizational contexts in China, India, and Japan, we investigate the boundary conditions of traditional team processes, the hidden performance costs of membership turbulence, and the gradual erosion of shared cognitive infrastructure in dynamic composition.
