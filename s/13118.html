Session overview

This symposium explores how individuals actively sustain meaning during consistent constraints present in their work. Prior research has examined the evolution of individuals’ meaningful work and how one can proactively make their work more meaningful. However, we have a less thorough understanding of how individuals cope with the inherent challenges of their work that they cannot change. The presentations deepen our understanding of how individuals persist in finding meaning rather than passively accepting such challenges. Collectively, they show how individuals sustain meaning in continuous and discontinuous paths amidst internal values conflicts, changing life circumstances, and economic difficulties. Additionally, the presentations adopt a longitudinal lens, examining how past career experiences influence individuals' approaches to finding meaning in their work, the process of attaining meaning and money over time, and the career consequences of abandoning the pursuit of meaningful work in a professional context. The research presented draws on rich insights from diverse contexts, including those of python hunters, funeral directors, entrepreneurs, dancers, and musicians, enabling researchers to identify the underlying mechanisms by which individuals create and preserve meaning that might otherwise remain unobserved. The symposium aims to engage scholars interested in meaningful work and the micro-processes individuals undertake to sustain meaning, while offering a platform for discussion and recommendations for future research.
