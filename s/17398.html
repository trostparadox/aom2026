Session overview

Contemporary discourse on teaching and learning in management education increasingly emphasizes the design of learning, technologies, analytics, and measurable outcomes. While these advances are valuable, they often obscure a central question: what do excellent teachers actually do? This Professional Development Workshop revisits and builds upon the seminal empirical work of Ken Bain (What the Best College Teachers Do) to re-center teaching as a form of professional judgment, relational intelligence, and intellectual craftsmanship. Drawing on Bain’s longitudinal studies of outstanding educators across disciplines, the PDW introduces participants to key principles of teaching excellence, including the creation of natural critical learning environments, respect for students as serious thinkers, formative assessment approaches, and continuous reflective practice. The workshop situates these principles within the specific context of management education and examines their relevance in the face of current challenges, such as large classes, cultural diversity, and the growing presence of AI-enabled teaching tools.
