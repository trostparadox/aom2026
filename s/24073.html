Challenge or Opportunity? How Foreign Divestment Shocks Affect Supply Chain Resilience

OSCM
Wenquan Zheng (Dongbei University of Economics and Finance); Enshuo Liang (Dongbei University of Economics and Finance); Hongliang Li (Dongbei University of Finance and Economics); Jingyan Yang
Given the current wave of de-globalization, scholars and practitioners are increasingly interested in how foreign divestment (FD) reshapes supply chains—a question that is especially critical for firms sitting at chain hubs and holding substantial foreign ownership stakes. Yet direct empirical evidence remains scarce. Addressing this gap, our study examines both the mechanisms through which FD affects supply chain resilience and the moderating roles of key chain attributes. Using panel data for Chinese listed companies from 2010 to 2022, we find that FD weakens supply chain resilience overall. This aggregate impact, however, masks two opposing forces: a negative effect stemming from heightened supply chain disruption risk and a positive effect created by new supplier equity embedding. Moreover, strong supply chain financing capacity and low demand–supply fluctuations dampen the negative effect, whereas high supply chain transparency and a central network position amplify the positive one. We close by discussing the theoretical and managerial implications of these findings.
