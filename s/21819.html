Those Who Help Themselves: Entrepreneurial Self-Emancipation from Institutional Lock-In

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6

Author: Yi Elaine Zhang Oklahoma State University

Session overview

Institutional arrangements often constrain individual agency by locking people into dominant organizational forms, thereby limiting career choice and suppressing entrepreneurial action. We develop a theory of entrepreneurial self-emancipation, arguing that entrepreneurship emerges not only from opportunity recognition but also from actors’ efforts to free themselves from institutional lock-in. Focusing on employment-tied health insurance as an illustrative case, we show how structural dependence on employers constrains mobility, discourages entrepreneurial entry, and traps human capital within wage employment. These constraints extend beyond individual outcomes: by restricting who can pursue entrepreneurial paths, institutional lock-in impedes technological experimentation, economic dynamism, and broader societal development. We theorize self-emancipation as a process through which individuals act within, against, and beyond institutional constraints to reclaim autonomy over career trajectories. In doing so, we reposition entrepreneurship as a corrective force that partially compensates for institutional failures rather than a phenomenon enabled by institutional support. This framework advances institutional and entrepreneurship theory by highlighting how self-initiated action under constraint shapes entrepreneurial emergence and its broader societal consequences.
