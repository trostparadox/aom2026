Strategic Incrementalism: Calibrating Trust as an Infrastructure for AI-Enabled Service Innovation

Authors: Zhiyu Zhang University of Nottingham; Jimmy Huang University of Nottingham; Gongtao Zhang Nottingham Univercity Business School; Kim Hua Tan Nottingham Univercity Business School; Jing Dai

Session overview

While theories of digital service innovation often advocate for large-scale overhauls, such approaches frequently ignore the operational realities of resource-constrained Professional Service Organizations (PSOs). In high-stakes environments, a socio-technical chasm exists between the promise of artificial intelligence (AI) and its integration into risk-averse human networks. This research challenges the "totalizing transformation" narrative by theorizing strategic incrementalism—a piecemeal approach where organizations intentionally select high-salience, lower-complexity AI modules to calibrate trust rather than just automate tasks. Drawing on an inductive case study of a national-tier leading hospital in China, we examine how AI affordances function as pragmatic logic-markers that anchor expert trust before its institutionalization. Our findings identify trust not as a static antecedent, but as a calibrated operational capability and a "protective shield" that mitigates medical disputes. We contribute a multi-level model of trust scaffolding, offering a roadmap for PSOs to achieve systemic reconfiguration by prioritizing the "low-hanging fruit" of narrow AI.
