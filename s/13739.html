From Integration Shock to Stability: Cultural Alignment in Post-Merger Indian Consultancy

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12
ODC

People

Authors: Elham Malik VIT Vellore; Siddhartha Dubey; Anjali Agarwal

Session overview

Cultural integration remains one of the most persistent causes of failure in mergers and acquisitions (M&A), particularly in environments of environmental uncertainty and rapid organizational growth. This extended abstract examines a longitudinal case of cultural integration in Kognitivus Consulting, an Indian management consultancy that integrated teams from two organizations with markedly different capability maturity levels and work cultures. Drawing on qualitative case evidence, the study proposes a three-phase integration framework—stability, uniformity, and sustainability—to explain how organizations can progressively restore operational reliability, harmonize cultural practices, and institutionalize long-term performance systems. Data were collected through managerial interviews, internal documents, HR process audits, and performance dashboards, both during and after the COVID-19 disruption. Findings indicate that early stabilization mechanisms, transparent communication, adaptive leadership, and digitally enabled HR systems significantly reduced attrition, improved productivity, and accelerated cultural alignment. The study contributes to the M&A and organizational change literature by offering a process-oriented model that links human systems integration with capability maturity transitions. Practical implications highlight the strategic role of HR architecture, leadership sense-making, and digital governance in managing post-merger uncertainty in emerging economy contexts.
