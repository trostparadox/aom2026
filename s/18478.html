Hierarchical Classification of Management Variables: An LLM Approach

Authors: Isaiah James Lynch Virginia Commonwealth University; Frank A. Bosco Virginia Commonwealth University

Session overview

Automated data extraction tools are rapidly transforming meta-analysis, enabling researchers to move at a speed and scale that far exceeds traditional methods. However, meta-analysts must still ensure that instances meet specified inclusion criteria, for example that the specific reported variable actually pertains to the construct of interest. Manual classification approaches create a bottleneck, as they cannot keep pace with the rate of automated data retrieval. To bridge this gap, this study evaluates zero-shot hierarchical text classification techniques as a scalable solution for management research. We utilized two distinct architectures, a generative large language model (Gemini-3.0-Pro) and a discriminative embedding model (SBERT), to classify nearly 3,700 variable instances according an existing organizational research construct taxonomy. We then score each against a human-coded analogue. Gemini demonstrated superior accuracy, achieving a Micro-F1 of .90 compared to SBERT’s .77. An analysis of specific variable categories revealed performance divergence independent of the model tested: Objective person characteristics and employment intentions were classified with near-perfect accuracy, whereas people attitudes, job attitudes, psychological states, and leadership variables exhibited comparatively lower accuracy. Our findings suggest that generative models are sufficiently advanced to act as autonomous coders, while discriminative models can provide a cost-efficient alternative.
