Session overview

Dual-career couples are an increasingly common yet under-theorized context for understanding contemporary work and careers. Although research on work– family dynamics, calling, and career development has grown substantially, much of this work continues to privilege individual-level explanations, overlooking the relational processes through which work experiences and career trajectories are jointly constructed. This symposium advances a relational, dyadic perspective on dual-career life by bringing together four complementary papers that examine how partners co-create well-being, meaning, balance, and career sustainability over time. Drawing on diverse theoretical traditions—including work–family enrichment, job demands–resources, boundary theory, interdependence theory, and calling research—the presentations explore relational mechanisms such as capitalization, responsiveness, boundary negotiation, and shared meaning across heterogeneous dual-career contexts, including childfree couples, couples navigating asymmetric callings, and academic dual-career partners. Collectively, the symposium highlights dual-career couples as a critical site for advancing process-oriented theories of work, careers, and employee well-being, with implications for organizational research and practice.
