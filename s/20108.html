Designing for Hope and Desirable Futures: Potential for Management Education and Learning?

Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5

People

Author: Judy Matthews Queensland University of Technology

Session overview

Management education and learning are situated in fast changing times where the established order is no longer the norm, global warming and climate changes, failing economies and threats to democracies across the globe. Lindebaum articulates a situation of global polycrises from climate change to wars, to the challenge of failing economic systems as threats to democracies. This paper responds to the challenge by that management education and learning must reconfigure and regenerate to develop hope and desirable futures in management education (Hudson et al. 2025, Lindebaum, 2025;141- 148). The research question we explore is: How might we develop learning and teaching processes that initiate, generate hope and potential pathways for individuals and organisations to contribute to desirable futures? Design thinking has been incorporated into management education and learning at the undergraduate, graduate and executive education levels, as well as through corporate programs. While most attention has been placed on exploring the cognitive benefits of design frameworks, processes, mindsets, principles and practices, some research identified that design thinking can be used to stimulate hope. The characteristics of a design thinker are empathy, openness to new ideas, visualisation, optimism, collaboration, integrated thinking (Brown, 2008). How does design thinking generate hope? We briefly present some examples of contexts where experiments in teaching design thinking generate hope in seeking desirable futures.
