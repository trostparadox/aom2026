Session overview

Digital technologies are reorganizing institutional arrangements within financial and monetary systems. Traditional banking is increasingly intersecting with new actors—including telecom operators, platform-based FinTechs, crypto firms, and central banks—reshaping market boundaries, authority relations, and regulatory practices. Rather than replacing existing systems, digital money markets emerge through ongoing processes of coordination, contestation, and adaptation, as actors navigate responsible inclusion, market power, regulation, and risks such as fraud within evolving institutional environments. While prior research has examined industry emergence and innovation (Wormald et al., 2021; Oborn et al., 2019), financial inclusion (Diniz et al., 2024; Senyo et al., 2021), and complementary currencies (Kuk & Giamporcaro, 2024; Kuk et al., 2024), our understanding of how digital money markets are organized in practice remains limited. This symposium brings together scholars whose work advances such an organizing perspective. Collectively, the papers examine how digital money markets are shaped through multi-stakeholder ecosystems, regulatory and institutional work, spatial and social practices of inclusion, and competing models of responsible digital financial organization in the Global South. By foregrounding ecosystems, institutions, and practices, the symposium aims to deepen theoretical understanding of digital money markets as ongoing organizational accomplishments, and to stimulate further research at the intersection of technology, organization, and practices.
