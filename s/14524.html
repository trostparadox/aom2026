Beyond Strain: How Daily Work Interruptions Shape Interpersonal Dominance Through Need for Control

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8
OB

People

Authors: Fangzhou Liu Sun Yat-Sen University; Yifu Wu Shanghai University of Finance and Economics

Session overview

Work interruptions are pervasive in contemporary organizations, yet research has primarily examined their intrapersonal consequences, leaving their interpersonal implications poorly understood. Drawing on control compensatory theory, we develop a within-person model proposing that daily work interruptions threaten employees’ sense of control, thereby increasing their daily need for control and motivating dominant behavior in subsequent work interactions. We test this model across two experience-sampling field studies of full-time employees in China, with surveys administered twice daily over two workweeks. Multilevel path analyses provide consistent support for a positive indirect effect of daily work interruptions on work dominance via need for control. Moreover, this indirect effect is stronger when employees perform more complex tasks and weaker among employees high in openness to experience. By conceptualizing interruptions as social events that trigger control-restoration processes, this research extends the work interruption literature and advances control compensatory theory by linking daily disruptions to interpersonal influence behavior at work.
