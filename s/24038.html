Institutional Change in Air Transport: The Case of Air Cargo in Kenya

PNP
Jean-Christophe Nicaise; Pascale Hardy (Maastricht School of Management); Diedrik de Boer (Maastricht School of Management)
While prior research shows that the results linking air cargo business to economic growth are robust, little is known about how the institutional framework impact this business sector, restricting the stakeholders to learn from each other’s best practices and best adapt to the disruptive environment. This is especially important in times of significant expected growth of air cargo in Kenya and its impact on the country’s economy. This paper is seeking to assess the institutional arrangements related to air cargo in Kenya, a sector becoming crucial to the nation’s economy. The results of interviews with key actors at national and international levels of the air cargo eco-system reveal how institutions are seeking reforms to initiate a paradigm shift of the institutional framework and administrative traditions in place, to create new opportunities for innovation and competition. Those reforms are mainly aiming to ensure the development and sustainability of an air cargo hub in the country as well as accommodating with the latest disruptive factors, be it of technological nature or else. This paper discusses innovative institutional arrangements in air cargo business in an under-research country as well as expands the literature on institutional change and air transport activities.
