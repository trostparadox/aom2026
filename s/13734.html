Session overview

A long tradition of scholarship explores the various pressures employees face in deciding how to present themselves at work. As workplaces evolve and become increasingly diverse, so too do the contexts and factors that influence how employees manage their images. This symposium aims to extend current scholarship on impression management and identity work, given the ever-changing self-presentation environment, by investigating how employees manage their self-image through in-workplace behaviors (e.g., professional risk-taking, work appearance, disclosure, and concealment) and non-work behaviors (e.g., social media presence, hobbies). The papers in this symposium integrate theories and research on impression management, norms, authenticity, and identity to further our understanding of how individuals navigate outward-facing aspects of the self. Additionally, we seek to explore the nuanced downstream consequences of various impression management behaviors, contributing to scholarship on self-presentation across multiple domains.
