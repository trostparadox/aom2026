Session overview

Over the past two decades, organizations that fund, accelerate, and structure new venture creation have proliferated. Alongside incubators, accelerators, and venture capital firms, newer organizational forms providing more hands-on, “organizational co-founding” support have emerged. Venture studios, for instance, actively recombine talent, ideas, and resources to systematically generate new ventures (Coelsch-Foisner et al., 2024; Moiana et al., 2025). Similarly, “talent investors” fund individuals even before a venture idea exists while traditional actors are also evolving – for example, through evergreen VC funding models and hybrid accelerator-VC structures – blurring boundaries between funding, incubation, and venture creation. We argue that these developments matter for entrepreneurship research for two reasons. First, the emergence of new forms and the continued evolution of existing forms likely reshape entrepreneurial processes and outcomes, including how problems are defined, how ideas are generated, how teams are composed, and how ventures are assembled. Second, these settings provide analytically fertile contexts – often highly structured, repeatable, and data-rich – for investigating foundational questions as well as future paths in entrepreneurship research. For example, they enable closer examination of how opportunities are ideated (Nair et al., 2022), how founding teams form (Lazar et al., 2020), and how ideas and teams are orchestrated at scale (Berglund et al., 2021).
