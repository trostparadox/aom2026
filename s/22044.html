From Pay Increases to Employee Outcomes: Rethinking Merit Pay Effectiveness

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Yvonne Tenkorang University of New Haven; Maurice "Mo" Cayer University of New Haven

Session overview

Abstract Drawing on mixed research findings on merit pay, a widely used yet contested pay-for-performance practice this poster presents a conceptual framework explaining why merit pay systems continue to produce mixed outcomes and how they can be redesigned to be more meaningful in contemporary organizational contexts. Despite persistent criticism regarding its motivational effectiveness, merit pay remains a dominant compensation practice. Using academic literature and practitioner evidence, the study reframes merit pay as a social and organizational process rather than a purely financial incentive. We propose the Merit Pay Effectiveness and Fairness (MPEF) Framework, which explains how employee psychological responses mediate the relationship between merit pay design and behavioral outcomes. System features such as increase size and timing, performance ratings, compa-ratio positioning, and communication shape employee perceptions of fairness, trust, recognition, and motivation, which in turn influence engagement, performance, retention, and collaboration. The framework also incorporates economic context, including inflation and constrained merit budgets, which affect how leaders design and communicate merit decisions. By integrating research and practitioner insights, this poster offers a practical guide for HR leaders navigating compensation decisions amid inflation, hybrid work, and evolving employee expectations. Future research should empirically test the framework using quantitative designs examining how economic context, system design, implementation quality, and employee perceptions jointly shape merit pay outcomes.1
