Hybrid Work as Organizational Change: A Socio-Technical Transformation at MSF Italy

ODC
Marcello Russo (University of Bologna); FILOMENA RIEMMA (University of Naples "Parthenope"); Viviana Colombi Evangelista (Università degli Studi di Napoli "Parthenope")
This paper examines how hybrid work can be leveraged as a driver of organizational change rather than treated as a logistical or policy-based adjustment. Drawing on a qualitative single-case study of Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) Italy, the study explores how a mission-driven organization redesigned hybrid work through an integrated, participatory, and socio-technical approach. Empirical evidence is based on internal documents, 40 interviews, two focus groups, and non-participant observations, analyzed through an abductive, Gioia-inspired methodology. Findings show that MSF Italy’s hybrid transformation emerged from the deliberate alignment of digital tools, organizational processes, and physical spaces, supported by extensive employee participation and iterative experimentation. Rather than resolving tensions inherent in hybrid work, the organization developed routines that enabled the ongoing navigation of paradoxes such as flexibility versus coordination and autonomy versus cohesion. The study contributes to organizational change and development literature by conceptualizing hybrid work as an organizational learning process and by highlighting participation as a central mechanism for sustaining socio-technical change in mission-driven contexts.
