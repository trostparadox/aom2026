Exploring the New, Protecting the Known: Curiosity and Adjustment in Culturally Novel Environments

OB
Sten Langmann (Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University); Pieter-Jan Bezemer (Edith Cowan University); Fangfang Zhang (Curtin University - Perth)
Adjustment research has largely overlooked curiosity, despite its central role in motivating engagement with novelty. Existing theory provides limited insight how curiosity influences adjustment, particularly across contexts varying in cultural novelty. Drawing on qualitative data from 66 interviews with expatriates across diverse cultural contexts, we show that curiosity unfolds into patterned curiosity profiles that link to distinct behavioral pathways that guide how expatriates interpret experiences and manage discomfort. We also show that cultural novelty reorders the functional value of curiosity-driven behaviors, such that responses that constrain adjustment in low-novelty contexts can facilitate adjustment in high-novelty environments. A complementary quantitative study tests these mechanisms, confirming the stability and generalizability of our findings. Together, our findings position curiosity as a theoretically consequential yet underexamined trait in adjustment research, highlighting the dynamic interplay between trait, behavior, and context.
