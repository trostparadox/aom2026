Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI), particularly large language models (LLMs) and large foundation models (LFMs), is transforming healthcare not merely through automation, but by redefining how knowledge, agency, and accountability are organized within and across institutions. As AI decision-support systems and learning models increasingly operate post-deployment, they introduce new forms of epistemic ambiguity, conditions in which knowledge itself is probabilistic, contested, and context-sensitive, and where the interpretation of rules, responsibilities, and evidence becomes dynamic. These conditions pose direct challenges to foundational theories in management and strategy, including the Resource-Based View, Transaction Cost Economics, and Agency Theory, all of which presuppose relatively stable boundaries of knowledge, control, and accountability. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) is among the first to focus explicitly on theoretical innovation, rather than implementation alone, in the study of AI-enabled healthcare systems. It asks: How do organizations govern learning systems that evolve after deployment and perform differently across organizational contexts? How do firms, professionals, and regulators construct legitimacy and trust amid algorithmic opacity and the absence of gold-standard benchmarks? And how can existing theories be extended, or fundamentally reimagined, to account for distributed agency, emergent accountability, and post-deployment learning? The PDW will be organized in two parts. First, leading scholars will present research that reframes these theoretical puzzles through studies of AI decision-support systems, foundation-model applications, healthcare startups, and regulatory ecosystems. Second, an intensive mentorship format, featuring a 1:4 faculty-to-participant ratio and approximately 15 minutes of tailored feedback per participant, will enable doctoral students and early-career researchers to refine their own projects, linking theoretical framing with empirical design and publication strategy PhD students would be required to submit an extended abstract and their CV to be selected to participate in the second part of the PDW (roundtables).
