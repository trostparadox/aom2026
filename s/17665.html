‘Expat’ or Escapee? Workplace and Social Factor Influences on the Global Mobility of Black Workers

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
CMS

People

Author: Jamel Catoe University of North Carolina at Charlotte

Session overview

Management research traditionally frames expatriation as a voluntary pursuit of career advancement or lifestyle enhancement. However, this focus often overlooks the experiences of marginalized groups and the systemic factors that drive emigration. This study investigates why Black American professionals are choosing to work abroad, critically examining the conventional "expat" narrative by exploring how organizational and societal environments influence global mobility. Utilizing a qualitative approach, this research is based on in-depth interviews with 27 Black American workers living outside the United States. By integrating organizational and migration theories, the study employs a thematic analysis to examine how participants’ decisions to relocate were influenced by a complex interplay of organizational and societal factors. Notably, the analysis identifies a greater variety of "push" factors than "pull" factors. The findings challenge traditional views of U.S. expatriates as solely career and lifestyle migrants, highlighting how adverse social conditions and societal "mega-threats" permeate the workplace to catalyze decisions to work abroad. This research contributes to a more nuanced understanding of expatriation and organizational culture, emphasizing that management must address the systemic tensions reflected within their organizations to foster environments where minoritized employees feel safe, valued, and retained.
