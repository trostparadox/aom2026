Does One Size Fit All? The Effects of Learning Contract Conditions on Learning Intentions

MED
Amne Samhat (Concordia University); Steven Shaw (Concord University)
This study examines the relationship between the learning contract (LC) conditions, categorized into Employee Learning Contract (ELC) and Academic Learning Contract (ALC), and employees’ intention to learn (ITL), incorporating self-efficacy (SE) as a mediating mechanism. It also investigates whether Learner self-directedness (LSD) as a moderator explains not only how change in SE (CSE) is predicted by LC condition but also how CSE influences ITL. Using experimental vignette methodology over two consecutive days, a total of 175 full-time employees from diverse industries participated in the study and were exposed to either ALC or ELC, then asked to report their SE, LSD, and ITL levels. Results showed that the LC condition matters differently depending on the Learner's self-directedness: low self-directed learners benefit more from the ELC, while high self-directed learners show stronger learning intentions in either format, particularly in ALC. Moreover, CSE was found not only to mediate the LC-ITL relationship but also to positively predict ITL. These findings indicate that LC condition effectiveness varies systematically with learner characteristics. Specifically, ELCs appear to function as effective motivational tools for employees with lower levels of LSD, for whom more structured contracts provide the clarity and direction needed to translate learning opportunities into ITL.
