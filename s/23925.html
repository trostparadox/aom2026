Where to Invest? Strategic Capital Allocation for Human–AI Collaboration

TIM
Du Liu (East China Normal University); Danmo Lin (James Madison University)
Organizations face a critical capital allocation problem in the AI era: how to optimally invest between human oversight and artificial intelligence capabilities when both are capital assets with stochastic, endogenously determined returns. This paper develops a capital budgeting model to characterize the optimal investment mix between human and AI capital under AI performance uncertainty. We conceptualize human oversight and AI reliability as complementary capital stocks that jointly generate cash flows. The model yields closed-form solutions for optimal investments and identifies conditions under which human and AI capital act as strategic complements versus substitutes. We extend the framework to incorporate financial constraints through an endogenous budget mechanism, showing how capital rationing distorts not only the scale but the composition of human-AI investments. Further extensions embed AI hallucination risk and human learning-by-collaboration, providing a dynamic capital allocation framework that distinguishes between transient adoption phases and steady-state operation. The results provide formal investment rules for financial managers, highlight how financing frictions alter technology adoption paths, and generate testable predictions for empirical corporate finance research on AI investment.
