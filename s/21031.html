Session overview

This PDW mobilizes a group of international scholars in the field. It aims at expanding and integrating our collective knowledge, and at crafting new avenues for collaborative and multi-disciplinary research on Explainable Methods in Management and HRM Research. This PDW explores how Explainable Methods, for example Big Data Analytics, Machine Learning, Text Analysis can move beyond prediction to foster theoretical advancement in Management Research. Drawing on presented studies this session engages scholars in bridging the gap between data-centric innovation and theory-driven inquiry. Presenters will discuss challenges and opportunities in the era of LLMs to increase explainability, contextual richness, robustness and fairness throughout the research endeavor, from question formulation to model interpretation. Through interactive discussions and illustrative examples, participants will gain theoretical and practical tools for designing transparent, replicable, and theoretically grounded studies in management research. We propose that this PDW will be of significant interest to the Human Resources (HR), Research Methods (RM), and Communication, Digital Technology and Organization (CTO) Divisions. The topics of discussion presents a variety of new opportunities and challenges for management and organizations, and HR plays a pivotal role in managing these new complexities. HR is in the right position to implement some of the challenges presented (e.g., Chalutz-Ben Gal., 2023), for example by fostering transparency around Algorithmic management (e.g., Meijerink & Bondarouk, 2023). Despite these important roles for Management and HR, many questions remain unanswered. For example: “Which AI-driven technologies are currently used in organizations and HR practice, and which use case are (in)effective?”, “How does different explainable models affect fairness issues (e.g. Simon et al., 2023)?”. This PDW aims at deepening our collective understanding of AI technology implementation in real-world settings, and aims at assisting towards more explainable methods, and wants to encourage management scholars and practitioners to collaborate when addressing these critical questions.
