Strength in Social Connections Employee Resilience Through Network Theory of Social Capital

OB
Fizza Kanwal (University of North Texas); Virginie Lopez Kidwell (University of North Texas)
Drawing on the network theory of social capital, we propose that closed-network structure (i.e., a tight-knit community of employees’ social connections) and open-network structure (i.e., an employee acting as a bridge between two disconnected groups) enhance employee resilience by providing access to emotional and informational resources, respectively. We further hypothesize that gender and race homophily moderate the relationship between closed-network structure and emotional resources, whereas gender and race heterogeneity moderate the relationship between open-network structure and informational resources. Across three US-based studies employing survey and vignette methodologies, we found support for our conceptual model. Study 1 demonstrated that both closed and open-network structures had a positive direct influence on employee resilience. Study 2 provides evidence for the mediating roles of emotional and informational resources, with emotional resources exhibiting a stronger mediating effect in the relationships between both network structures and employee resilience. In addition, Study 2 demonstrates that social ties characterized by gender and racial similarity are more effective in providing emotional resources, thereby confirming the moderating role of gender and racial homophily in closed networks. Finally, Study 3 employs a vignette experiment to establish the causal nature of the proposed relationships. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.
