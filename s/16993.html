Session overview

Women continue to face gender bias and stereotypes across organizational contexts, contributing to constrained opportunities and barriers to professional advancement. While prior research has focused on the content and consequences of stereotypes held by organizational actors, less is known about how women and others respond to these stereotypes. Do organizational actors anticipate gender bias and adjust their behavior and communication accordingly, or do they act as they would in its absence? This symposium brings together five studies that examine how anticipated and actual gender bias shape decisions and communication across contexts including negotiation, leadership, victim evaluation, and everyday interaction. Using field studies, laboratory experiments, and observational data, the papers show how expectations about gender stereotypes influence negotiation outcomes, support for victims, and hiring decisions when decision-makers anticipate third-party prejudice. The symposium also highlights how women adapt their communication strategies— such as conversational receptiveness and dominance—in response to expected stereotyping. Together, these studies demonstrate how anticipating gender bias systematically shapes interaction, opportunity, and inequality for women in organizations.
