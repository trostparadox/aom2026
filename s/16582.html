Session overview

“Community” is increasingly everywhere in organizational and social life - from purpose statements and annual reports to ESG initiatives and workplace culture decks. Yet this surge in community rhetoric also invites conceptual opaqueness. Community might better be understood as a contested concept which invites us to dig deeper. This proposed symposium focuses on the growing centrality of community in how organizations construct identity, structure stakeholder relationships and respond to societal expectations. It brings together three streams that utilize different lenses to explore the key tenets as well as the paradoxical tensions that the community concept evokes: (1) Institutional Theory & Cultural Rationalization: Why/How modern rationalized organizations symbolically invoke “community” to build and stabilize identity, actorhood, or legitimate purpose; (2) Stakeholder Theory & Engagement: Why/How organizations design stakeholder processes that include or exclude communities of place and practice, and whether engagement becomes empowerment; (3) Governance & Impact: Why/How firms materially affect marginalized communities, through labor practices, supply chains, CSR, climate concerns and initiatives, and what regulatory mechanisms can give “community” real teeth as a mode of organizational power. The session aims to offer an integrative synthesis and an avenue for the continued dialogue on the future research directions for management scholars across OMT, STR, and SIM. Across the three streams, panelists discuss: What kind of community do organizations invoke in this day and age? For whom, and with what consequences?
