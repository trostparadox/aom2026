Online Career Advice for Autistic Adults: A Review of “Best” and “Worst” Job Lists

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Author: Gundars Kaupins Boise State University

Session overview

One hundred and sixty-eight websites list the best and worst jobs for autistic adults. These resources carry significant weight for these adults, families, and counselors due to their high visibility, often garnering between 10,000 and 120,000 views. However, this abundance of online advice risks reinforcing harmful stereotypes. This poster highlights biases within these websites by identifying the most frequently recommended 'best' and 'worst' career paths, analyzing contradictory advice, and reviewing how specific job characteristics align with the American Psychiatric Association’s DSM-V definition of autism, utilizing the U.S. Department of Labor’s O*NET database. Computer programming was the most frequently listed best job, and retail sales the worst. Best jobs focused on working conditions that provide comfort and support for autistic adults. The worst jobs focus on safety threats, work overload, and high consequences of error. The poster concludes by discussing the implications for the autistic community and offering directions for future research.
