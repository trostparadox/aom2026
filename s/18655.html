Session overview

This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to re-examine and extend discourse theory for the digital age, addressing how platformization, algorithmic mediation, and digitally accelerated communication challenge foundational assumptions about discourse, power, and meaning in organizations and markets. The study of discourse is the study of meaning-making through language in use. Discourse is central to how strategies are justified, organizations are formed, technologies are legitimated, identities are enacted, and power is exercised. Discursive practices do not merely reflect organizational realities, they actively shape what actors see as possible, desirable, and appropriate. Studying discourse, therefore, allows scholars to examine how collective beliefs and evaluative commitments are produced, stabilized, and transformed over time. While discourse theory has been central to management and organization studies, much of the existing literature assumes relatively stable texts, human authorship, and bounded communicative arenas. Digital environments unsettle these assumptions by introducing new actors (algorithms, platforms), new modalities (metrics, affect, visuals), and new temporalities (speed, volatility, future orientation). This PDW provides a space for scholars to collectively rethink discourse theory’s core concepts, methods, and analytical units in light of these transformations.
