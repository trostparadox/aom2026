Session overview

Multinational enterprises (MNEs) are subject to growing pressure to address their responsibilities with respect to sustainability-related stakeholder demands across geographies and institutions. Nevertheless, the degree of firm responsiveness remains inconsistent and is often contingent on idiosyncratic constraints. Firms often deliberately set boundaries on corporate responsibility, which involves choosing whom to respond to, which issues to attend to, and where corporate responsibility starts and ends. Such boundaries are especially challenging in cross-national environments that exhibit institutional heterogeneity and complexity. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) examines responsiveness in firms, which we regard as a key phenomenon in the study of international management. In doing so, the PDW also acknowledges the reality where a lack of responsiveness, including so-called non-responsiveness, often indicates structural limits, such as institutional complexity, stakeholder voicelessness, and ecological dependence. The workshop proposes a multi-level approach that will enable researchers to better examine the way in which MNEs react to sustainability pressures across headquarters, subsidiaries, supply chains, and institutional environments. Through moderated panel discussions, breakout sessions, and plenary synthesis, researchers will be able to discuss and explore how boundaries for responsibilities are set in global sustainability contexts. This PDW is proposed for researchers who are interested in enhancing theoretically grounded and methodologically rigorous research in the field of international management and sustainability.
