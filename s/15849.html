Session overview

Human capital has been shown to play a significant role in startup performance, shaping outcomes such as hiring, venture financing, and scaling. This symposium brings together a unique set of papers that examine how the experiences and other salient characteristics of founders, early joiners, and inventors influence startup performance. Specifically, the first paper investigates how startups can compete with powerful incumbents for AI talent when recruiting cofounders and early hires. The second paper examines whether and how young founders can overcome experience constraints by teaming with senior cofounders or bringing senior joiners into the venture. The third paper studies how early joiners’ entrepreneurial experience versus corporate experience affects venture scaling. The final paper focuses on technology commercialization and investigates how alternative framing approaches, especially emphasizing the inventor versus the invention, shape investor evaluations. Taken together, these papers provide new evidence on the mechanisms through which human capital affects startup performance, leveraging novel datasets and offering insights for entrepreneurship, strategy, and innovation research.
