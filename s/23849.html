When Negative Feedback Promotes Well-Being: The Roles of Rumination and Feedback Constructiveness

OB
Shuwei Hao (Xi'an University of Science and Technology); ?? ? (Xi'an University of Science and Technology)
Leaders are often reluctant to deliver negative feedback due to its well-documented detrimental impact on employee well-being. However, emerging evidence suggests that negative feedback may also promote well-being under certain conditions. This paper aims to investigate how and when negative feedback can enhance employee well-being. Drawing on cognitive theories of rumination and integrating social information processing theory, we develop and test a moderated mediation model in which negative feedback enhances employee well-being through adaptive work-related rumination (i.e., problem-solving pondering). Feedback constructiveness is proposed to moderate both the likelihood that employees engage in problem-solving pondering and the extent to which such rumination enhances well-being. We tested this model using a three-wave survey (N = 246) with Chinese employees. Results demonstrate that negative feedback triggers problem-solving pondering, which in turn enhances employee well-being. As hypothesized, Feedback constructiveness serves as a key facilitator at both stages of this process. These findings provide a more comprehensive understanding of negative feedback’s impact on employee well-being and extend cognitive theories of rumination by identifying critical contextual contingencies.
