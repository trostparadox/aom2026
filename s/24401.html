Re-Ontologising Opportunity: A More-Than-Human Perspective of Regenerative Entrepreneurship

ENT
Yuki Xueqi Yuan
Ecological grand challenges expose the limits of anthropocentric, market-first opportunity theories. In contexts characterised by biophysical thresholds, feedback loops and long time horizons, venture viability is co-produced with ecosystems, infrastructures and contested governance, not solely through market uptake. This paper re-ontologises entrepreneurial opportunity by developing an ecological–relational, more-than-human perspective grounded in regenerative entrepreneurship. We ask how regenerative entrepreneurs define, evaluate and enact opportunities when success criteria are inseparable from socio-ecological regeneration, and how this orientation shapes business model innovation and entrepreneurial power. We draw on a multisite qualitative study within the Becoming Regenerative (B-Regen) project, comprising 115 semi-structured interviews with founders and regenerative designers across 78 ventures in 13 countries (lead sites: the UK, the Netherlands and Japan). Our analysis identifies four interlinked mechanisms. First, entrepreneurs define opportunity through regenerative boundary conditions (e.g., non-toxicity, biodiversity outcomes and livelihood resilience), treating market demand as a continuation test rather than a definitional anchor. Second, opportunities co-emerge through more-than-human agency: materials, microbes, soils, reefs and place-based communities act as co-producers, constraints and validators of feasibility and legitimacy. Third, business model innovation operates as translation and integrity protection, embedding governance routines, monitoring and proof infrastructures to keep regenerative meaning durably attached as offerings move through conventional markets. Fourth, entrepreneurial power is exercised primarily as boundary work, reframing categories and redefining scale as locally grounded translation, while negotiating trade-offs between integrity and speed. We theorise these dynamics as socio-ecological interface work, offering a process model that advances opportunity ontology and strategic entrepreneurship for regenerative organising.
