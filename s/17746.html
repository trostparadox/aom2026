Eye-Tracking for Management Research and Practice: Opportunities, Challenges, and Best Practices

Session overview

Eye-tracking (ET), a non-invasive research methodology that captures moment-by-moment ocular responses, is increasingly gaining traction in management research, particularly in information-rich settings relevant to organizational neuroscience. By leveraging the close link between eye movements and cognitive processing (the eye–mind assumption), ET provides high temporal sensitivity and access to largely non-conscious attentional mechanisms that shape managerial judgment, sensemaking, and performance. As such, ET holds substantial potential for advancing both management theory and evidence-based managerial practice. In this context, this PDW will: (i) introduce the psychophysiological foundations of ocular behavior and explain how eye-tracking data are collected; (ii) discuss the types of management and organizational research questions for which ET is particularly well suited; (iii) present key methodological principles for designing ET studies, including hardware selection, data quality assessment (accuracy, precision, and data loss), common analytical approaches, and reporting standards; and (iv) if time allows, engage participants in hands-on activities, including exploratory analysis of eye-tracking data and a first-hand demonstration of mobile eye-tracking equipment. The session will conclude with an open discussion of best practices, emerging applications, and practical challenges in using eye-tracking for management research and practice.
