The Emergence of Calling: A Systematic Review of Antecedents and the Discernment Process

CAR
Gabriel Simbad Manzanares (Complutense University of Madrid); Patricia Villamor-Manero; Elvira Carpintero-Molina
Although interest in calling has expanded markedly in recent decades, research on how individuals initially come to perceive a calling remains fragmented across empirical studies. The present review provides a systematic synthesis of this literature, focusing on the antecedents and developmental dynamics involved in perceiving a calling. Following PRISMA guidelines, 46 empirical studies published since 1997 were reviewed. The analysis identifies a wide range of factors shaping the perception of calling, spanning external influences (such as significant others, life events, and guiding forces), internal characteristics (including meaning-related processes, personality traits, vocational identity, and engagement), and overt behaviors related to exploration and self-reflection. Taken together, the findings portray perceiving a calling not as a discrete event, but as a gradual, recursive, and identity-related process, often shaped by both enabling and constraining experiences and retrospectively organized through narrative sensemaking. Building on this synthesis, future research should integrate longitudinal, cross-cultural, narrative, and developmental perspectives, with particular attention to adolescence as an underrepresented stage of vocational development. Further work is also needed to advance theory on how callings emerge and to develop and test interventions informed by the antecedents identified in this review.
