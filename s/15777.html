Responsibility Without Recognition: Daughters’ Invisible Work in Family Business Succession

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

Authors: Marina Vujovic University of Montenegro; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro; Milica Zvicer University of Montenegro; Nerma Kapetanovic University of Montenegro

Session overview

Although family business scholarship increasingly recognizes daughters as potential successors, their contributions often remain poorly formalized and symbolically discounted. We develop the concept of a recognition gap, a persistent mismatch between daughters’ enacted contributions and the formal, symbolic, and material recognition granted to them during succession (e.g., role clarity and decision rights, being named and treated as a successor, compensation and ownership clarity). Drawing on 15 semi-structured interviews with second-generation daughters in Montenegrin family firms (1,065 minutes, ~110k words), we trace how such gaps emerge and persist over time. We introduce a temporal lens through the gray zone, the period between informal entry (sustained involvement without formal role acknowledgement) and formal entry (formal employment and/or clearly acknowledged role), and show how prolonged ambiguity can normalize daughters’ work as “help” rather than as a basis for legitimate authority. Our analysis identifies three recurring processes sustaining the gap: assumed availability and responsibility, gender-coded successor narratives, and self-restraint aimed at preserving family harmony. We also observe boundary cases in which early role formalization and explicit egalitarian rules weaken the gap. We contribute by shifting attention from “who succeeds” to how contributions are translated (or not) into legitimate successor status over time, bridging research on invisible work and succession legitimacy in family firms.
