Entrepreneurial Risk as a Collective Act: Reframing of Risk in African Entrepreneurship

ENT
Forster Ampadu (University of Salerno); Bice Della Piana (University of Salerno); Yochanan H. Altman; Sara Carbone (University of Salerno)
Entrepreneurial risk-taking is conventionally conceptualised within Western individualistic models as a rational, self-centred assessment of uncertainty and expected returns. Such approaches inadequately capture entrepreneurial behaviour in contexts where economic activity is deeply embedded in communal, moral, and relational structures. This paper advances a culturally grounded reconceptualisation of entrepreneurial risk by examining how the African philosophy of Ubuntu reframes risk-taking as a collective act. Drawing on institutional theory and the social embeddedness perspective, the study develops a conceptual framework that positions Ubuntu as an informal institution shaping the interpretation, regulation, and distribution of entrepreneurial risk. The analysis demonstrates that Ubuntu redefines risk not as an individual exposure to loss, but as a shared moral responsibility mediated through social networks, relational trust, and communal accountability mechanisms. Within this context, entrepreneurial action is legitimised and sustained through informal risk-sharing arrangements, reputation-based governance, and reciprocal support systems that function as social risk cushions. By integrating institutional and embeddedness theories, the paper shows how cultural norms and social relations jointly reduce perceived individual risk, encourage persistence in the face of uncertainty, and enable socially sanctioned experimentation. The study contributes to entrepreneurship scholarship in three key ways: first, by challenging universalist assumptions that equate risk-taking with individual endeavour; second, by extending institutional theory through the incorporation of indigenous African philosophies as analytically central constructs; and third, by offering a culturally sensitive framework for understanding ethical, resilient, and collective forms of entrepreneurial risk-taking.
