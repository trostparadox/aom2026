Session overview

Doctoral students and early-career scholars are routinely advised to “find a good mentor,” yet far less guidance exists on how to learn effectively from mentors once those relationships begin. As a result, many mentoring relationships unintentionally socialize students into passivity, deference, or over-dependence on the mentor – outcomes that can undermine scholarly identity, confidence, and long-term development. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) introduces a mentee-led approach to mentoring that equips early career scholars to take the lead on their development while co-creating productive and courageous relationships with advisors and senior scholars. Grounded in contemporary research on adult learning, mentoring, and identity development, the workshop introduces the ACE Framework (Align–Collaborate–Evolve) and seven core competencies that enable mentees to engage mentors intentionally across different phases of the relationship. Through guided reflection, structured peer dialogue, and applied exercises, participants will work through common mentoring challenges including initiation, misalignment, feedback difficulties, dependency, and transitions toward independence. Particular attention is given to later-stage mentoring dynamics, including separation and redefinition, which are rarely addressed in formal doctoral training (and in mentoring more generally). Designed for doctoral students across stages and institutions, and, equally, for early-career faculty, this highly interactive session emphasizes agency, co-creation, and developmental courage. Participants leave with practical tools, a personalized mentoring learning plan, and a clearer sense of how to learn with mentors rather than from beneath them.
