The Illusion of Consensus: Team Development Dynamics in Global Virtual Teams

Session overview

This study examines how classic team development patterns—particularly the forming–storming–norming–performing (FSNP) framework—unfold over time in global virtual teams (GVTs), and whether members’ perceptions converge into shared team-level states. Drawing on weekly longitudinal data from 1,069 individuals nested in 266 GVTs across eight weeks, we integrate stage-based team development models with a multilevel emergence perspective. Using descriptive analyses, within-team disagreement indices, intraclass correlations (ICC[1], ICC[2]), and within- and between-analysis (WABA), we distinguish aggregate developmental trajectories from within-team perceptual convergence. Results show that, at the aggregate level, members’ perceptions exhibit an FSNP-like progression, characterized by a brief Forming phase and an early rise in Performing. However, perceptual convergence within teams remains weak: members’ interpretations of developmental stage persistently diverge throughout the project lifecycle. These findings suggest that team development in GVTs is best understood as a configural phenomenon characterized by patterned within-team heterogeneity rather than uniform convergence. Theoretically, the study advances multilevel models of team development by empirically delineating the limits of perceptual convergence—and thus shared team-level states—in global, virtual contexts. Practically, the results caution that aggregate indicators of developmental progress may obscure persistent perceptual misalignment among team members.
