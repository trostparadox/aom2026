Institutional Factors and International SRHRM: Insights from Four-Quadrant Framework

IM
Meng Ye (Chongqing University); Qian Zhang (Durham University); Jingyuan Tang (Chongqing University)
As corporate social responsibility (CSR) expectations intensify, multinational enterprises (MNEs) face increasing demands to implement socially responsible human resource management (SRHRM) across diverse institutional contexts. This study examines the influence of institutional factors, such as parent-firm CSR orientation and institutional distance on international SRHRM practices. Drawing on institutional theory, this study develops a four-quadrant framework that distinguishes four SRHRM dimensions: legal compliance, employee-oriented, CSR promotion, and local sustainable development. Using data decoded from 371 CSR reports (2018-2022) for 87 international construction companies, the analysis reveals that parent-firm CSR orientation positively influences all SRHRM dimensions, whereas institutional distance selectively affects only local sustainable development practices. Furthermore, employee-oriented SRHRM practices mediate the relationship between parent-firm CSR orientation and local sustainable development, while CSR promotion practices show limited mediation effects. These findings highlight the differentiated institutional responsiveness of SRHRM dimensions and the strategic role of internal HRM mechanisms in translating global CSR commitments into locally embedded outcomes. The study advances a nuanced understanding of how institutional forces shape international SRHRM and offers actionable insights for managers and policymakers aiming to balance global consistency with local legitimacy in responsible HRM practices.
