Dynamic Importance Hierarchy of Multiple Goals in Institutional Change

Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4

People

Author: Mengzhe Zou Bocconi University

Session overview

How do organizations respond to performance feedback when managing multiple goals? While most behavioral theory studies have emphasized a sequential attention model, this perspective assumes a fixed goal hierarchy. However, in many settings, goals are not clearly ranked but are interdependent. Existing studies overlook how interdependencies affect attention allocation and how institutional forces reshape the goal hierarchy. I propose that when goals are asymmetrically interdependent, organizations exhibit a V-shaped behavioral response to feedback on the primary goal: positive performance relative to aspiration of the primary goal enables sequential attention to secondary goals, while negative performance relative to aspiration triggers compensatory activation. I further argue that institutional pressure affects the hierarchical importance of the goals and thus influences the response to performance feedback. Empirically, I test this framework in the context of the traditional venture capital industry, focusing on how performance feedback on profitability influences investment in social enterprises. Using a panel dataset of 710 VCs from 2015 to 2022, I find evidence for the theorized V-shaped relationship between profitability performance gap (performance relative to aspiration) and social enterprise investment, and I find that the socially oriented policy regime reinforces the V-shaped relationship. This study contributes to the behavioral theory of the firm by integrating sequential attention and compensatory activation mechanisms in multi-goal settings and by demonstrating how institutional environments influence internal goal responsiveness.
