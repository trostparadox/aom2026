Session overview

Career advancement is frequently assumed to be the cumulative result of sustained high performance. However, careers research suggests that early tenure periods play a disproportionate role in shaping promotion trajectories through signaling, visibility, and role construction processes. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) introduces a structured 90-day strategic framework that conceptualizes promotion readiness as an intentional, design-oriented process rather than a passive outcome of performance alone. Drawing on career self-management theory, signaling theory, and research on early impressions and identity work, the workshop integrates empirical insights with applied tools to help participants translate performance into advancement-relevant signals. Participants will examine how formal role expectations, informal evaluation criteria, and stakeholder relationships interact to influence promotion outcomes, particularly during early or transitional career stages. The session incorporates guided reflection, peer discussion, and hands-on strategy design exercises to support participants in diagnosing gaps between performance and promotion readiness, identifying structural and contextual constraints, and developing individualized 90-day advancement strategies. Attention is given to how inequality, power dynamics, and organizational norms shape access to advancement opportunities. This PDW is designed for doctoral students, early- and mid-career scholars, and practitioner-scholars seeking evidence-based approaches to career advancement in academic and organizational contexts. The workshop contributes to careers scholarship by translating theory into practice while fostering dialogue about how promotion readiness can be conceptualized, operationalized, and studied in future research.
