How Societal Values and Practices Differentially Shape Outcomes of Mistreatment: A Meta-Analysis

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Fang Li The University of Hong Kong; Yiwen Zhang The University of Hong Kong

Session overview

Workplace mistreatment harms employees, but reactions differ across cultures. This meta-analysis examines how societal practices (prevailing behaviors) and societal values (cultural aspirations) differentially shape these reactions. Based on 759 studies (915 samples), we confirmed the detrimental effects of workplace mistreatment on employee well-being, job attitudes, and performance. Meta-regression results showed cultural practices and values shape the impact of mistreatment in distinct ways. Both cultural values and practices influenced how mistreatment related to overall well-being. Cultural values, however, had a more pronounced influence on shaping job attitudes in response to mistreatment. Further analysis revealed cultural values are particularly influential for shaping outcomes involving internal evaluations (e.g., psychological well-being, turnover intentions) and value-laden discretionary behaviors (e.g., organizational citizenship behavior).
