The Faces of Impact: An Economies of Worth Perspective to Impact Investing

Authors: Corentin Christophe University of Luxembourg; Mohamed Farhoud University of Luxembourg; Namira Samir; Mickael Geraudel University of Luxembourg

Session overview

As capital is increasingly mobilized toward social and environmental challenges, impact investing (II) has emerged as a prominent yet contested form of alternative finance. Drawing on the Economies of Worth (EW) framework, this study examines how impact investors enact and justify competing objectives in practice. Based on 83 in-depth interviews with impact investors across venture capital, private equity, and foundations, complemented by organizational data, we show that impact investors’ evaluative work unfolds through a dynamic process of discord, compromise, and stabilization. Discord arises when dominant Market tests of worth fail to capture social and environmental value, producing persistent difficulties in justifying investments to multiple audiences. Investors respond by forming hybrid compromises that connect Market, Civic, and Green orders of worth. Over time, some compromises stabilize as shared practices, partnerships, and evolving mindsets that embed hybrid principles into evaluation routines. By foregrounding the justificatory work and facilitators that sustain these arrangements, the paper shifts attention from impact outcomes to the coordination processes that enable impact investing to function despite disagreements among the different actors in the field.
