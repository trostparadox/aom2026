Cognitive Cost of Conflict: How Hedge Fund Activism Undermines Managerial Attention to Cyber Risk

TIM
Jonathan Hoke (University of Münster); Bernd Martin Kemper (University of Münster); David Bendig (University of Münster)
This study investigates the impact of hedge fund activism on the governance of information systems risk, specifically managerial attention to cybersecurity. Integrating stakeholder theory and the attention-based view, we theorize that the "salience shock" of an activist campaign creates a structural conflict that degrades the internal control environment for long-term risks. We argue that the urgent financial agenda imposed by activists acts as a cognitive constraint, diverting scarce managerial resources away from preventative initiatives like cyber risk. Using a panel dataset of S&P 500 firms from 2010 to 2019, we measure cyber risk attention via a dictionary-based analysis of unscripted earnings call Q&As, a primary channel for voluntary risk disclosure. Our results, robust to difference-in-differences and instrumental variable specifications, confirm that hedge fund campaigns are associated with a significant 18.3% reduction in cyber risk attention. We contribute to the accounting information system literature by identifying a governance-driven antecedent to cyber neglect, quantifying the "cognitive tax" where short-term financial pressure crowds out the attention required for effective risk governance and operational resilience.
