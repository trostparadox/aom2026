Perceived Price Opacity and the Investment Appeal of Oil Paintings in the Art Market

MOC
Qi Cao (Hanyang University); Haowei Wang (University of Macau); Yuchen Dai (Nanjing Normal University); Zheng Liang; QIANQIAN qianqian LI
In the art market—characterized by high information asymmetry, low transparency, and strong cultural–financial duality—the investment appeal of oil paintings has become increasingly salient. However, existing research has rarely examined how perceived price opacity shapes investors’ psychological mechanisms and overall investment evaluations. This study proposes an integrated theoretical model to explain how price opacity influences investment attractiveness through three key psychological pathways: perceived scarcity, status signaling, and speculative expectation. Data were collected from 524 respondents with prior experience in art investment. Partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) was employed to test the proposed hypotheses. The results show that perceived price opacity exerts significant positive effects on perceived scarcity, status signaling, and speculative expectation. These findings suggest that, within the unique context of the art market, opaque pricing is interpreted as a signal of rarity, exclusivity, and undiscovered value rather than as a market inefficiency. Moreover, all three psychological constructs significantly enhance investment attractiveness, with status signaling demonstrating the strongest effect. Mediation analyses further confirm that each construct transmits the influence of price opacity onto investment attractiveness. The study contributes theoretically by reframing price opacity as a psychological driver that enhances the perceived value of art investments. Practically, the findings offer insights for auction houses, galleries, and art asset managers regarding pricing strategy and investor communication in markets where symbolic and financial values intertwine.
