Female Leadership and Strategic Innovation in the UAE Family Businesses: A Conceptual Exploration

STR
Sarah Urabi (University of Wollongong in Dubai); Jaeyoung Cho (University of Wollongong in Dubai); Hatim Urabi (University of Wollongong); Mohammed Nuseir (Al Ain University)
This conceptual study examines how female transformational and transactional leadership shape strategic innovation in the United Arab Emirates family businesses. Rather than treating these leadership styles as substitutes, we argue that they operate in complementary ways, translating leadership behavior into strategic renewal through organizational learning and entrepreneurial orientation. Transformational leadership is theorized to stimulate strategic innovation by articulating a compelling direction, encouraging intellectual engagement, and cultivating relational trust that lowers barriers to experimentation and knowledge sharing. Transactional leadership, by contrast, may support strategic innovation by formalizing expectations, aligning incentives, and strengthening accountability, thereby enabling more disciplined and reliable exploitation of emerging opportunities. By integrating leadership styles with capability-building mechanisms in the family business context, this study extends theorizing on women’s leadership and innovation and offers governance-relevant insights into how family firms can balance stewardship and continuity with the demands of strategic innovation.
