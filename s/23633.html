Beyond Walls: When Managerial Rituals Hold Remote Organizations Together

MC
Cécile Gras (CORHIS -Université Paul Valery Montpellier France); Florence Noguera (Paul Valéry Montpellier 3 University); Laura Khachan (Toulouse Business School)
This article focuses on frontline managers of hybrid teams or, more broadly, teams whose members are spread across different locations. Based on semi-structured interviews conducted with a sample of 19 managers of hybrid teams, we sought to identify the difficulties encountered by these managers and the management practices developed in a dispersed work context. These analyses led us to discuss the contribution of managerial rituals in countering the disintegrating effects of remote working. By incorporating rituals adapted to remote working, managers can alleviate feelings of isolation and boost employee motivation.

The Impact of Psychological Safety on Employee Work Engagement and Turnover Intention

MC
Raghida Abdallah Yassine (Adelphi University); Alan Cooper (Adelphi University); Massimo Posillico (Adelphi University)
This research explored the relationships amongst psychological safety, employee engagement, and turnover intention, with engagement as a mediator while controlling for gender. Drawing on existing literature and the self-determination theory, we hypothesized that psychological safety enabled work engagement, while in turn, work engagement reduced turnover intention. Results revealed that both psychological safety and work engagement were significantly related to lower turnover intentions, while psychological safety did not significantly contribute to overall work engagement. One notable exception was the subcomponent of engagement, vigor, which was significantly associated with both psychological safety and turnover intention. These findings imply that even though work engagement may not mediate between psychological safety and turnover intention, vigor could be a substantial dimension to explore further. Future research is recommended to reanalyze these relationships on an extended sample, taking into consideration the limitations of the current study, discussing inconsistencies with previous findings, and extending the theoretical framework to enhance understanding of these dynamics in organizational settings.

Meaningful Employee Rewards in Higher Education: Intervention Research Using MERF

MC
Vanessa Susan Hills (Western Michigan University); Amanda Jeppesen (Western Michigan University)
This intervention research details the implementation and resulting impact of the Meaningful Employee Rewards Framework (MERF) within the department of a U.S. institution of higher education. The intervention and framework are designed to align the intent of non-monetary employee rewards and their actual impact by ensuring that rewards are meaningful and valuable to employees. Grounded in symbolic interactionism theory, MERF provides a practical iterative process comprised of five phases: (1) identify, (2) contextualize, (3) communicate, (4) evaluate, and (5) reflect. Through MERF, leaders and employees co-construct the meanings of rewards rather than treating them as static, one-size-fits-all mechanisms; this is done to better align the current disconnect between the intended impact of non-monetary employee rewards and the perceived impact by employee recipients. As an initial pilot study, this intervention research asked participants about how they perceived the impact of MERF and gained insight into what makes non-monetary rewards meaningful to employees. The intervention demonstrated meaningful impact, paving the way for future iterations and refinement of the framework.

Workspace Analysis: Expanding Job Analysis to Shape the Built Environment

MC
Cristina G. Banks (University of California); Lawrence A. Witt (University of Houston); Dustin Maneethai
Healthy workplaces enhance motivation and well-being by providing behavioral and physical environments that meet employee needs. Unfortunately, designers/architects often prioritize architectural trends and cost over worker needs. We discuss the elements of a healthy workplace and how the current design process often fails to capture the key needs of workers. We describe and advocate a new method for designing workspaces based on job analysis, enabling fellow AoM members to learn how to improve worker well-being by collaborating with architects and designers.

Socio-Economic Management of Household Waste in Intermunicipal and Intercultural Contexts

MC
Edgar Rebolledo Toro (LICGP); Fredy Oyarzo (LICGP); Diego Manriquez-Robles (Rice University)
This article aims to understand the determinants that influence the management of household solid waste within the framework of inter-municipal cooperation in contexts of intercultural governance. Drawing on the Socio-Economic theory and methodology (Savall & Zardet, 1987, 2005), the study identifies various dimensions of organizational dysfunctions as key determinants. These dysfunctions negatively affect the development of human potential, hindering cognitive interaction among actors, which in turn impedes effective dialogue and negotiation. Consequently, the ability to reach agreements and achieve the coordination required for effective cooperation is compromised.
