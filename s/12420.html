Session overview

This presenter symposium examines how Muslim professionals navigate faith, identity, and agency across career contexts within predominantly secular organizations. Bringing together six empirical and conceptual papers, the symposium advances management scholarship by centering Muslim experiences that remain underrepresented in research on careers, diversity, and organizational life. Drawing on qualitative interviews, content analysis, autoethnography, and interdisciplinary theory, the papers explore how Muslim professionals manage identity visibility, respond to media and workplace stigmatization, negotiate faith commitments alongside organizational norms, and exercise agency under conditions shaped by Islamophobia and the “Muslim penalty.” Collectively, the contributions highlight how religion functions not only as a site of constraint but also as a source of meaning, resilience, and strategic action in career development. The symposium integrates perspectives from Management, Spirituality, and Religion; Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion; and Career research to illuminate how organizational climates, leadership practices, and broader sociocultural narratives shape belonging, professional legitimacy, and career outcomes for Muslim professionals. By foregrounding Muslim voices and experiences, the symposium offers new theoretical insights and practical implications for advancing inclusive organizational policies, equitable career practices, and more contextually grounded DEI scholarship.
