Shaping Project Culture Through Vendor Engagement: Evidence from Government IT Implement

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1

Author: Wendy Mitchell Wilson Georgia State University

Session overview

Insufficient preparation of vendor resources contributes to misaligned expectations, fragmented collaboration, and diminished project learning capacity— conditions that frequently undermine large-scale, complex information technology (IT) projects in the government sector. This study examines how the engagement readiness of vendor functional and technical resources shapes project culture and influences implementation outcomes. It suggests that early knowledge transfer and readiness activities build relational and cultural foundations in addition to technical capability. Drawing on technological frames of reference theory (Orlikowski & Gash, 1994) and social identity theory (Brown, 1999), the study explains how shared understandings and emergent team identities develop during project initiation and become foundational elements of project culture. Project culture is viewed as a multilayered construct shaped across individual, organizational, and interorganizational levels, with engagement readiness fostering cognitive alignment, relational confidence, and cultural integration between vendors and clients. While prior research identifies numerous drivers of IT project failure (including ambiguous requirements, weak governance, insufficient organizational readiness, and ineffective change management) vendor readiness remains underexamined as an antecedent to project culture formation. This study addresses that gap by analyzing how vendor preparation for client engagement influences early project interactions and subsequent implementation trajectories. By linking engagement readiness to project culture and measurable project outcomes, the study contributes to information systems and project management scholarship and offers practical guidance for designing vendor onboarding and readiness interventions that mitigate both technical and engagement risks in complex government IT initiatives (Maruping et al., 2019; Attah et al., 2024).
