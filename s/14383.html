Session overview

Recent geopolitical disruptions, such as governments reasserting their authority over business, countries adopting protectionist policies, and periodic shocks from military conflicts and political upheavals, have revealed a critical vulnerability- the fragility of hyper-efficient global supply chains (GSCs) and renewed fundamental questions about how MNEs should define their firm boundaries and how to govern their operations. In practice, efforts to enhance efficiency and resilience often pull the MNE in opposite directions. Efficiency-driven strategies such as just-in-time inventory, single-sourcing, and highly specialized suppliers can leave MNEs more exposed to geopolitical disruptions. Conversely, resilience-driven strategies such as holding buffer stocks, maintaining excess capacity, or duplicating suppliers and locations tend to raise costs and erode MNEs’ cost-based advantages in intensely competitive global markets. This session integrates insights from research on state–market interdependence, the evolution of GVC governance, the uneven geography of knowledge and production, and supply chain literature examining this efficiency-resilience trade-off. Building on the premise that MNEs incur significant costs when adjusting boundaries and governance structures in response to geopolitical uncertainties and risks, we ask whether resilience strategies complement or crowd out firms' efficiency objectives. In other words, panelists consider whether resilience should be treated as a distinct strategic objective or as a component of firms' long-run pursuit of efficiency. The panel offers implications for management theory and practice regarding how to configure GVCs that achieve both efficiency and resilience in the face of geopolitical uncertainties and risks.
