Beyond Individuals and Teams: A Systematic Review of Organizational-Level Impacts of Virtual Work

HR
MITALI MATHUR; Varun Elembilassery (Indian Institute of Management Ranchi)
Existing reviews on virtual work have extensively examined its adoption, effectiveness, benefits, and challenges, largely emphasizing individual-level outcomes (e.g., employee well-being and job satisfaction) and team-level dynamics (e.g., collaboration, trust, and performance). However, limited attention has been paid to understanding the organizational-level implications of virtual work. Addressing this gap is critical for developing a holistic perspective on how organizations are affected when operating in virtual modes, including how changes in organizational constructs cascade to teams and individual employees. This study conducts a systematic literature review to examine the impact of virtual work on key organizational-level constructs. The review reveals that only a small body of research has explored outcomes such as organizational performance, culture, control mechanisms, socialization, diversity and inclusion, ethics, work environment, and human resource practices. The findings suggest that while virtual work may enhance organizational performance, it also poses significant challenges by disrupting the consistent transmission of organizational culture, intensifying technocratic control that may erode trust, increasing ethical risks, hindering effective socialization, and exacerbating feelings of exclusion. Building on these insights, the study advances a set of propositions to inform future research and encourage deeper investigation into the underexplored organizational-level consequences of virtual work.
