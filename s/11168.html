INNOVATION READINESS CRISIS: Why 89% of Firms Invest in Generative AI for R&D Yet Only 8% Succeed

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Author: Yanli Chen American Public University System

Session overview

Many firms invest heavily in generative AI (GenAI) for research and development. However, only 8% can successfully scale these investments. This creates a major problem. We study this paradox using two approaches. First, we analyze 6,800+ GenAI patents filed between 2023 and 2025. Second, we survey 650 firms in pharmaceutical, software, and manufacturing sectors. We develop a new model called the Innovation Readiness Maturity Model (IRMM). This model shows four key capability areas: technological infrastructure, human capital readiness, organizational structure, and innovation culture. We find that technology capability alone explains only 23% of adoption success. However, when we combine all four areas, we explain 71% of success. This is a threefold improvement. We discover that cross-functional coordination is the strongest predictor of scaling success. Firms with high technology investment but weak organizational readiness fail six times more often. Through patent analysis, we identify three different GenAI technology clusters. Each cluster has different organizational requirements. Our findings challenge the assumption that building good technology is enough. We find that organizations must also prepare on multiple other dimensions. We propose new interventions. These include diagnostic assessments, staged implementation plans, and adaptive governance. These interventions improve success rates from 8% to 58% in our simulations. This research contributes to TIM scholarship in three ways. First, we measure organizational readiness using real evidence. Second, we show how machine learning and patent analysis reveal technology adoption mechanisms. Third, we advance theory about the conditions that enable transformative technology integration.
