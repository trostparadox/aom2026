Session overview

Digital entrepreneurship increasingly relies on social media platforms as primary infrastructures for customer engagement, legitimacy signaling, and value creation. For ventures targeting adolescents and emerging adults—particularly Generation Z and the emerging Alpha cohort—these platforms are not neutral marketing channels but psychologically consequential environments that shape attention, emotion, and meaning-making. This conceptual paper argues that entrepreneurship research must integrate adolescent psychology to understand how social media–based markets operate. Drawing on developmental psychology and digital emotion regulation (DER), the paper conceptualizes social media platforms as algorithmically governed marketplaces that mediate entrepreneur–customer interactions through visibility metrics, peer endorsement, and recommendation systems. Adolescence is a developmentally sensitive period marked by heightened emotional reactivity, identity exploration, and responsiveness to social evaluation (Erikson, 1968; Arnett, 2007; Steinberg, 2008). Neurodevelopmental research shows that adolescents’ socioemotional sensitivity outpaces regulatory control, increasing susceptibility to contextual feedback (Ahmed et al., 2015; Casey, 2019). Consequently, adolescent customers are especially responsive to algorithmic cues that shape perceptions of authenticity, desirability, and legitimacy. Integrating the DER framework, the paper theorizes how adolescent social media use links affect regulation and identity processes to consumer behavior (Hollenstein & Faulkner, 2024). Social media interactions function as emotion-regulation strategies that influence engagement, preference formation, and brand attachment (Meier & Johnson, 2022; Valkenburg, 2022). This research positions adolescent psychology as a core mechanism shaping market behavior in social media–based entrepreneurship.
