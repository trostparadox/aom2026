AIGC Usage and Employee Behavioral Outcomes (WITHDRAWN)

OB
Jie Xu (Guangxi Minzu University); Jiazi Liang (Guangxi Minzu University); Su Pengpeng (Guangxi Minzu University); Yun Zhang (Guangxi Minzu University)
Drawing on the transactional model of stress and coping, this study investigates how Artificial Intelligence-Generated Content (AIGC) usage influences employees’ work withdrawal behavior and innovative job performance, with a focus on the mediating role of challenge stress appraisal and the moderating role of proactive personality. Data were collected through a two-wave multi-source (employee–supervisor) data involving 244 employees and 26 supervisors from two companies. The results indicate that AIGC usage is positively associated with challenge stress appraisal. In turn, challenge stress appraisal is negatively related to work withdrawal behavior and positively related to innovative job performance. Furthermore, proactive personality moderates the relationship between AIGC usage and challenge stress appraisal, such that this relationship is stronger for employees with higher levels of proactive personality. In addition, proactive personality moderates the indirect effects of AIGC usage on both work withdrawal behavior and innovative job performance via challenge stress appraisal, with stronger indirect effects observed among employees high in proactive personality.
