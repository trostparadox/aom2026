Session overview

Artificial intelligence in organizations has traditionally been conceptualized as a tool embedded in routines, structures, and decision processes. Recent advances in agentic AI systems, however, challenge this assumption by enabling AI to initiate action, coordinate tasks, adapt routines, and influence outcomes over time. These developments raise foundational questions for Organization and Management Theory regarding agency, coordination, accountability, and organizational form. This caucus examines the implications of treating AI systems as organizational actors rather than passive tools. Participants will engage with AI agents that are assigned organizational roles and embedded within collective task environments, allowing direct observation of how coordination, routines, and authority emerge in human–AI collectives. These shared experiences provide a basis for theory-driven discussion on how classic OMT constructs operate when non-human agents participate in organizational life. The session focuses on advancing theory around questions such as: When does agency meaningfully emerge in non-human actors? How do organizational routines evolve when AI both performs and modifies them? How is coordination achieved in the absence of formal hierarchy? What happens to accountability when outcomes arise from distributed human–AI action? And do agentic AI systems represent a new organizational form or an extension of existing ones? By grounding conceptual inquiry in observed organizational dynamics, this caucus aims to stimulate theory development on AI-enabled coordination, routines, and organizational forms, and to identify promising directions for future OMT research. This caucus is designed for OMT scholars interested in experimental theorizing and boundary-expanding approaches to coordination, routines, and organizational agency. It particularly invites researchers who are curious about engaging AI systems not only as objects of study, but as participants in organizing processes. The session emphasizes exploration, conceptual provocation, and collective sensemaking around emergent human–AI organizational forms. Scholars open to speculative theory development, novel constructs, and unconventional empirical starting points are especially encouraged to attend. No prior technical background is required.
