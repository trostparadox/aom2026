Session overview

AI-Native organizations - firms built from the ground up with AI embedded into value creation, strategy and operations across business functions - are emerging as distinct organizational phenomenon and “reshaping the global startup ecosystem”. Unlike ‘AI-First’ incumbents that retrofit intelligent systems onto legacy processes, ‘AI-Native’ organizations build their organizational design and operating logic around AI from inception. Early evidence indicates that AI-Native startups scale fundamentally different, altering how ventures are built, grown and resourced. By achieving product-market fit with leaner teams, higher automation levels, and accelerated iteration cycles, they challenge traditional entrepreneurial growth assumptions. Despite early indications of a distinct organizational form and different growth trajectories, academic research has not kept pace with this emerging phenomenon. As a result, we lack conceptual clarity on what constitutes an AI-Native organization, how it differs from AI-First, traditional ventures and other digital organizational forms (e.g., ‘digital-first’ or platform organizations), and what its emergence implies for entrepreneurial theory and practice. This PDW responds to these gaps by bringing together scholars and professionals at the intersection of AI and entrepreneurship to examine AI-Native organizations, co-develop a forward-looking research agenda and foster an interdisciplinary community. Accordingly, the PDW is organized along three key themes: I. Conceptualizing AI-Native organizations, II. Advancing research methods and III. Developing implications for theory and practice. By engaging with these themes, participants will sharpen their conceptual foundations, clarify methodological options, and co-develop a research agenda for studying organizations that are ‘AI-from-day-one’.
