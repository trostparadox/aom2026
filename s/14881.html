When Leader Technostress Spills Over to Member: The Moderating Role of AI on Task Adaptivity

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

People

Authors: Da Gyeong Lee Seoul National University; Jin Nam Choi Seoul National University

Session overview

This study examines how leader technostress transmits to subordinates and undermines task adaptivity, and how task characteristics and AI use moderate this process. Drawing on stress contagion theory, Job Demands–Resources theory, and Adaptive Structuration Theory, we propose a moderated mediation model. Data from 217 employee–supervisor dyads in Korean organizations were analyzed using confirmatory factor analysis and PROCESS macro analyses. Results show that leader technostress positively predicts member technostress (ß = .29 to .37, p < .001), which in turn reduces task adaptivity (indirect effect 95% CI: –.0916 to –.0119). Task routinization significantly strengthens this mediated pathway (ß = .19, p < .001), whereas task complexity does not exert a significant buffering effect. Crucially, employee use of AI tools attenuates the negative association between member technostress and task adaptivity (ß = –.159, p < .05), weakening the overall mediated effect. These findings advance understanding of digital-era stress processes and position AI augmentation as a valuable resource that preserves adaptive performance under technostress. Practitioners should develop leadership digital resilience training, incorporate novelty into routinized roles, and prioritize augmentative AI features alongside real-time technostress monitoring.
