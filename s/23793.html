From Control to Autonomy: Delegative Leadership and Project Success in Healthcare Projects

OB
Qaiser Mohi Ud Din (Harbin Engineering University); Aqsa Tahir (Harbin Institute of Technology); Li Zhang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Ali Raza Akhter (Harbin Institute of Technology)
Effective leadership is vital for achieving project success amid rapid technological progress and heightened workplace complexity, especially in high-pressure settings such as healthcare. This study, grounded in Self-Determination Theory (SDT), examines the influence of delegative leadership on project success in the Chinese healthcare sector, evaluating the mediating roles of psychological ownership and job withdrawal, and the moderating effect of technological turbulence. The study uses multi-wave data from 281 employees in project-based healthcare firms and employs structural equation modeling (SEM) to analyze direct and indirect correlations among these variables. The results indicate that delegative leadership substantially enhances psychological ownership and diminishes job withdrawal, hence mediating its positive effect on project success. Moreover, technological turbulence enhances the positive effects of delegative leadership on psychological ownership and its alleviating influence on work withdrawal, especially in high-turbulence environments. These results highlight the essential importance of autonomy and adaptability in influencing project success in dynamic healthcare environments. This study integrates Self-Determination Theory (SDT) to analyze the interplay among leadership, employee behaviors, and environmental circumstances, yielding distinctive insights into the mechanisms that contribute to leadership performance. The results offer pragmatic counsel for healthcare administrators, highlighting the need to empower personnel and adapt leadership approaches to manage technological disruptions effectively. This research advances the literature by explaining how delegative leadership fosters project success through multiple mediating pathways and examining the influence of technological turbulence on these relationships in high-pressure, rapidly evolving contexts.
