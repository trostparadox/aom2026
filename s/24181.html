Past Performance is No Guarantee: Boundary Conditions of High Performer Voice Endorsement

MOC
Sen Zhang (Kyoto Tachibana University)
While high performers’ voice typically benefits organizations and receives managerial voice endorsement, recent research suggests that supervisors may view high performers as threats to hierarchical stability. Consequently, supervisors may not invariably endorse high performers’ voice. This study examines the conditions under which high performers’ voice fails to gain endorsement. Drawing on associative-propositional evaluation (AP-E) theory, I propose that managerial endorsement of high performers’ voice depends on the threat associations activated when they encounter such input. Two factors critically influence this associative process: coalition voice and supervisors’ power motivation. More specifically, coalition voice amplifies supervisors’ threat associations of high performers’ voice, particularly among supervisors with high power motivation, leading to reduced endorsement. Results from a scenario-based experimental study conducted in the United Kingdom (Study 1) and an event-contingent survey-based field study (Study 2) conducted in the United States supported all my hypotheses. The theoretical implications for voice literature and directions for future research are discussed.
