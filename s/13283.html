Pandemic Politics: How Perceptions of Long-COVID Accommodations Differ by Political Affiliation

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3

Author: Samantha G Deane Rutgers University-New Brunswick

Session overview

This study sought to explore how perceptions of fairness for workplace disability accommodations differed across political affiliations. Looking at how perceptions of fairness changed when the condition being accommodated was an autoimmune condition and when it was Long-COVID, and how this COVID impact differed by political affiliation. Using an online lab experiment of 204 individual conducted on Prolific in 2022, this study found that participants who identified as Republican perceived all accommodations as less fair than the other political associations. More interestingly, Republicans perceived Long-COVID accommodations as significantly less fair than autoimmune disorder accommodations, even when the described condition symptoms and accommodation details were held constant.
