Session overview

The transition toward value-based care (VBC) represents one of the most complex and paradox-laden transformations in contemporary healthcare systems, especially in the United States. Organizations are expected to simultaneously reduce costs and improve quality, standardize processes while personalizing care, comply with regulations while innovating digitally, and deploy automation without eroding professional judgment or trust. These competing demands do not appear as isolated trade-offs but as persistent, interdependent paradoxes embedded in the system, organizational, and decision levels. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) provides an interactive, case-based forum for scholars to explore how paradox theory can be applied to enhance understanding and navigation of decision-making during VBC transitions. Rather than presenting healthcare as a narrow empirical setting, the workshop treats it as an extreme case of institutional and systemic complexity that illuminates broader theoretical questions central to organization and management theory. Participants will engage with a focal healthcare decision case derived from real-world payer–provider dynamics, examining how a single system-level decision produces different forms of friction and value across provider types (e.g., specialty clinics, hospitals, behavioral health organizations). Through guided small-group discussions and plenary synthesis, participants will apply paradox lenses (performing, organizing, belonging, and learning) to diagnose tensions, surface assumptions, and explore both-and responses. The PDW is designed for scholars at all career stages interested in paradox theory, healthcare organizations, institutional complexity, and decision-making under persistent tensions. Participants will leave with conceptual tools, discussion frameworks, and teaching ideas applicable well beyond the healthcare context.
