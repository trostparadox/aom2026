Moral Talk or Pragmatic Talk? How Language Framing Shapes Technology Acceptance

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 9
OB

People

Authors: Yilin Wang Nankai University; Qinghong Yuan

Session overview

Although prior research on technology acceptance has predominantly emphasized the roles of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use, much less is known about how organizations’ communication strategies shape employees’ willingness to accept new technologies. Drawing on dual-process theory, we argue and find that the fit between language framing and technological transparency influences technology acceptance. Specifically, under low transparency, a moral frame increases technology acceptance, whereas under high transparency, a pragmatic frame is more effective. We further examine the mechanism underlying these effects, showing that processing fluency mediates the interactive effect of language framing and transparency on technology acceptance. In addition, we identify individual–organization value congruence as a boundary condition: the matching effect is weaker when value congruence is high. We test and support our theory across four experiments in multiple algorithmic decision-making contexts.
