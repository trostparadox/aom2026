Old Ties, New Ideas? Relational Embeddedness, Reverse Knowledge Transfer, and Exploratory Innovation

IM
Chansoo Park (Memorial University of Newfoundland); HUIMIN OUYANG (Central University of Finance and Economics)
This study investigates reverse knowledge transfer (RKT) and exploratory innovation (ERI) within Chinese multinational corporations, with a focus on the role of relational embeddedness in internal knowledge flows. Drawing on social network theory, we investigate how relational embeddedness translates into exploratory innovation through reverse knowledge transfer. Using structural equation modeling (SEM) via AMOS 24.0, we tested the relationships between these variables. The findings show that reverse knowledge transfer plays a central mediating role, accounting for more than half of the total effect of relational embeddedness on exploratory innovation. We further indicates that firm age conditions these relationships. Specifically, the relationship between relational embeddedness and reverse knowledge transfer is significantly stronger in older firms than in younger firms, while the effect of relational embeddedness on exploratory innovation is not significant in older firms. This study contributes to the literature by clarifying when and how internal relational networks drive breakthrough innovation in EMNCs across different stages of firm maturity.

Learning Through Diversity: How Environmental Regulatory Exposure Shapes Green Innovation in EMNEs

IM
Jong Min Lee (Yonsei University); Gabriel Lau (Yonsei University)
This study examines how emerging market multinational enterprises (EMNEs) respond to cross-national diversity in environmental regulation. Prior research suggests that internationalization exposes EMNEs to diverse environmental standards and may facilitate regulatory learning, yet offers limited understanding of the mechanisms through which such exposure translates into green innovation. Drawing on institutional theory, we argue that green innovation is particularly fostered when EMNEs operate across host countries whose environmental regulatory regimes are more stringent than those in the home country, and when such exposure varies across multiple host locations. We theorize that this form of environmental regulatory diversity creates learning pressures and legitimacy incentives that motivate EMNEs to upgrade their environmental capabilities through green innovation. Using panel data comprising 9,362 firm-year observations from 1,088 Chinese MNEs over the period 2008–2021, we find support for our arguments. We further show that these effects are contingent on organizational and institutional conditions, specifically top management teams’ technological expertise and the strength of intellectual property enforcement in home-country locations. These findings advance research on EMNE learning and sustainable innovation under complex global environmental governance.

Reframing Latecomer Technology Strategies and Internationalization in Emerging Catch-Up Economies

IM
Steffen Wolfer (TH Köln – University of Applied Sciences)
Latecomer firm development is inherently a strategic issue. This study contributes to unraveling the multifaceted nature and dynamics of strategic latecomer development and firm-level technological catching-up in emerging market economies, drawing on China’s photovoltaic and wind power equipment manufacturing industries as a comparative empirical setting. A typological framework based on fuzzy-set ideal type analysis is introduced to delineate taxonomic configurations of stylized latecomer technology strategies along two central focus dimensions: product-process technological capability development and multi-modal learning internationalization. The framework integrates twelve distinct evolutionary technology strategy archetypes, incorporating previously overlooked technology migration patterns. Empirical application of this framework reveals the equifinal diversity and prevalence of latecomer technology strategies in practice, indicating that certain strategy approaches are specific to particular industries. The real-world mapping exercise further elucidates the association between techno-economic firm performance and strategic configurations of technological capability development and learning internationalization as complex and not straightforward, being embedded within dynamic firm- and industry-specific environments. In this way, the study enhances the methodological, conceptual-analytical, and empirical understanding of the learning-based strategy context framing innovation and corporate catching-up trajectories of latecomer firms.

Unpacking the Black Box of Reverse Innovation: Local Constraints and Upstream Product Architecture

IM
Ilias KANBAR (University Jean Moulin Lyon 3)
Reverse innovation (RI) is usually studied through post-market diffusion, leaving early design and development processes largely underexplored. We examine how local contextual constraints shape upstream product architecture and inform RI before market entry. Based on an abductive qualitative case study, we trace how product architecture and innovation activities were configured and stabilized during the pre-commercialization phase across locations. The findings show that operational and regulatory constraints shape core architectural choices early on, and that innovation activities are selectively distributed along a global innovation value chain. In doing so, the study clarifies how upstream architectural decisions condition the feasibility of RI.
