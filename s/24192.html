Raja Yoga Enlightenment: Illuminating the Path to Socially Responsible Decision-Making

MSR
Soumya Dutta (Indian Institute of Technology Bombay); Ashish Pandey (Indian Institute of Technology Bombay)
Business schools are among the most influential institutions shaping how future managers think, decide, and act. Yet a persistent concern is that management education, with its emphasis on performance and profit, often neglects the inner capacities required for socially responsible judgment. This research begins with an urgent question. Can management education help future leaders make decisions that are not only smart but wise? We address this challenge by examining whether Raja Yoga, a contemplative practice grounded in Indian spiritual philosophy, cultivates inner capacities that support rational and socially responsible decision-making. Integrating perspectives from management learning, spirituality, and decision-making research, we theorize that self-transcendence and emotional awareness enable ethically grounded rationality, while mind wandering disrupts this process. Nine hypotheses were tested across four studies conducted over four and a half years with MBA students. Study 1, conducted in 2022 (N = 629), used experimental vignettes to show that rational decision-making styles predict socially responsible choices in ethically ambiguous scenarios. Study 2, conducted in 2022 and 2023 (N > 1,100), showed that self-transcendence enhances emotional awareness, which strengthens rational decision making, while mind wandering weakens these relationships. Study 3, conducted in 2023 and 2024, applied a randomized pre-post experimental design with two independent samples (N = 205), comparing an eight-week Raja Yoga-based intervention with structured self-reflection exercises. Study 4, conducted in 2024 and 2025 (N = 206), replicated this design using a game-based experiment to capture socially responsible decision-making behaviorally. Across studies, Raja Yoga improved self-transcendence, reduced mind wandering, and strengthened socially responsible decision-making. This research advances MSR scholarship by showing how spirituality-based pedagogy can meaningfully reorient management education toward responsible and human-centered leadership.
