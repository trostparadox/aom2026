Session overview

Organizations increasingly rely on symbolic initiatives to secure legitimacy in environments characterized by persistent visibility, evaluative pressure, and fragmented audiences. While existing theories of decoupling, legitimacy, and symbolic action explain why symbolic alignment emerges, they provide limited insights into how such alignments are intentionally designed, enacted and, sustained over time. This study develops a process theory of symbolic legitimacy through the concept of Organizational Theater. We conceptualize Organizational Theater as a dynamic and recursive system in which organizations translate anticipatory legitimacy concerns into performative enactments, stabilize these efforts through nested organizational arrangements, and produce temporary states of perceived alignment that are continuously evaluated by heterogeneous audiences. Audience responses generate feedback loops that reinforce, adjust, escalate, or destabilize theatrical configurations, thereby creating path-dependent symbolic action trajectories. By reframing decoupling as a nested and strategically designed process and positioning audiences as central drivers of legitimacy dynamics, this study advances our understanding of how organizations manage legitimacy in contemporary institutional environments.
