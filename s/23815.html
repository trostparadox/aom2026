When Does GenAI Enable Employee Thriving? The Roles of Fit, Support, and Need Satisfaction

OB
Alexander Goetmann (Lucerne University of Applied Sciences and Arts)
Generative AI (GenAI) is now routine in knowledge work, yet its motivational consequences remain poorly understood. Integrating Job Demands– Resources Theory with Self-Determination Theory, we propose a dual-route model: GenAI-related resources foster thriving through need satisfaction, whereas AI-specific hindrance demands undermine well-being through need thwarting. We tested preregistered hypotheses in a three-wave panel of knowledge workers using GenAI weekly (N = 741; 3-week lags). Predictors (T1) included task–technology fit, organizational AI support, AI self-efficacy, and AI technostress; mediators (T2) were need satisfaction and thwarting; outcomes (T2–T3) were thriving (vitality, learning) and strain (exhaustion, anxiety), with autoregressive controls. Latent-variable models with bootstrapped indirect effects revealed that task–technology fit (ß = .24) and organizational AI support (ß = .28) predicted need satisfaction, which in turn predicted increases in vitality (ß = .22) and learning (ß = .15). AI self-efficacy did not predict need satisfaction among regular users. AI technostress predicted need thwarting (ß = .25), but need thwarting did not predict strain change. These findings indicate that contextual resources, rather than individual confidence, drive need-satisfying GenAI work.
