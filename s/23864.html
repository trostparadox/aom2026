Navigating Adversity with Agency: Scale Development for Crisis Crafting Behaviors

OB
Huili YE (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Pengcheng Zhang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); xuhong Zhao (Huazhong Agricultural University); Bolun Li (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Chenglin Gui; Yunru Wang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
The prevailing viewpoint in organizational crisis research, particularly the threat-rigidity perspective, has long depicted employees as passive victims or defensive responders. However, this deterministic perspective may stem from a limited understanding of micro-level agency, overlooking how frontline employees proactively reconstruct work orders to survive and recover under extreme constraints. Across three studies, we propose the construct of Crisis Crafting Behavior, develop and validate an instrument to assess it, and examine its distinct value in predicting crisis outcomes. Specifically, in Study 1, we use a grounded theory approach with 32 R&D teams to identify the essence of crisis crafting, revealing two core dimensions: Crisis Routine Crafting and Crisis Pacing Crafting. Study 2 focuses on the development of the scale, establishing its content validity and validating its factor structure through Exploratory Factor Analysis. Study 3 confirms the scale’s structural validity and examines its incremental variance in predicting adaptive performance, creative performance, and turnover intention, beyond general job crafting. Overall, these studies reveal that crisis crafting is a distinct, agentic process driven by organizational support, empowering leadership, and resilience. Further, it provides unique explanatory power for organizational resilience outcomes that general job crafting cannot fully capture. Implications for both research and practice regarding “agency in adversity” are also discussed.
