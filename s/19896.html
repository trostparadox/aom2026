Why Analytical Tools Fail to Embed: Consulting Judgement in Complex Organisations

Presenter: Bernard M Groen Durham University Business School

Session overview

Organisations increasingly invest in advanced analytics and AI-enabled tools to support decision-making, yet many struggle to make effective use of these tools in practice. Drawing on experience across large, complex health systems, this Professional Development Workshop explores why technically sound analytical tools often fail to embed in organisational routines and decision processes. The session focuses on behavioural, organisational, and governance factors that shape how analytical outputs are interpreted, resisted, repurposed, or ignored by senior leaders and professional groups. Rather than treating analytics adoption as a technical or data problem, the workshop examines it as a consulting judgement problem: how advisors frame readiness, anticipate organisational responses, and engage with accountability, incentives, and professional identity in complex settings. Using AI and advanced analytics as contemporary illustrations, participants will reflect on recurring patterns that complicate advisory work, including misplaced expectations of objectivity, over-reliance on pilots, and tensions between analytical insight and organisational decision-making. The workshop is designed to support scholars and practitioners interested in consulting, strategy, and organisational change who want to develop more realistic and behaviourally informed approaches to advising organisations under complexity and uncertainty.
