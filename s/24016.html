Toward Sustainable Reimagination of Business and Management as a Force for Good

MED
Chidozie Umeh (University of York); Nelarine Cornelius; James Wallace (University of Hertfordshire)
Amid escalating global crises, spanning climate breakdown, inequality, democratic erosion, and DEI retrenchment, calls to reimagine business and management as a force for good have intensified. Yet reimagination alone risks rhetorical fatigue unless it transforms how we teach, theorize, and organise management education. This paper advances a framework for sustainable reimagination grounded in three imperatives. Reactivation revitalises established theories through processual, paradoxical, and multidimensional perspectives, refracted through feminist, decolonial, and Global South epistemologies. Operationalisation embeds reimagined ideas within pedagogical and institutional practices that challenge entrenched publishing, accreditation, and evaluative logics. Diversification broadens epistemic foundations by positioning plural knowledges as constitutive rather than supplementary. These imperatives transform reimagination from discursive provocation into a multidimensional praxis connecting theory, learning, and institutional reform. Addressing three pressing challenges (re-establishing the societal legitimacy of business schools, embedding ethics and inclusion in management curricula, and enabling organizational learning in higher education), the paper contributes directly to debates in management education and development on how business schools cultivate reflexive judgement, epistemic pluralism, and institutional responsibility, and specifies how sustainable reimagination can produce enduring theoretical, pedagogical, and institutional renewal.
