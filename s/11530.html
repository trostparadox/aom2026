Understanding and Managing Conflict in Family Businesses

Pennsylvania Convention Center — 115A

People

Participant: Douglas Baumoel Continuity, LLC

Session overview

Conflict in family business is notoriously common and often seems intractable. When poorly managed, these conflicts lead to broken families and failed businesses. This workshop will explore how conflict in enterprising families is Identity-based - very different from simple civil dispute, requiring very different approaches for managing conflict. This workshop will describe the family enterprise as interdependent systems of ownership, management, family, advisory and governance, showing how the potential for conflict is built-in to this interdependency. The nature of conflict in these systems will also be broken down into which issues respond to negotiation or force (ie, litigation or other types of power) and which require development as the primary approach for managing differences. Conflict will be presented as a system of competing interests, which can be understood best through various frameworks and insights that will be presented. In addition, common myths about conflict and its management for family enterprise systems will be explored. Participants will learn how to apply new tools to better understand and manage the complexity of family enterprise and obtain new approaches for helping stakeholders in family business manage differences across all interdependent systems of the family enterprise. The workshop will include the application of these frameworks to family business, family office and shared family wealth environments.
