Trust Matters: How Interpersonal Trust Shapes Employees’ Reactions to Coworkers’ Proactive Behavior

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 10
OB

People

Authors: Zhaodong Qiu NEOMA Business School; Qi Li Tsinghua University

Session overview

Recent proactivity research has increasingly examined how individuals, as bystanders, respond to their coworkers’ proactive behavior, yet findings remain mixed. Integrating upward social comparison and interpersonal trust perspectives, we propose that bystanders’ trust in the actor is pivotal for understanding when proactive behavior elicits constructive versus obstructive responses. Observing proactive behavior can trigger upward social comparison and elicit divergent appraisals, specifically feeling inspired versus feeling threatened, which in turn motivate adaptive responses such as learning from the actor and defensive responses such as hindering the actor. We further identify interpersonal trust as the linchpin that determines whether these appraisals translate into enacted behavior. Through a multi-wave survey with a critical incident technique, we found that distinct forms of trust operated differently across the two pathways. Specifically, affect-based trust strengthened the adaptive pathway by amplifying the link between feeling inspired and learning behavior, whereas cognition-based trust attenuated the defensive pathway by weakening the link between feeling threatened and hindering behavior. By illuminating trust-contingent mechanisms that connect proactive behavior to bystander reactions, we provide a novel trust-based perspective that reconciles prior inconsistencies and advances a trust-based account of proactivity’s social consequences in teams.
