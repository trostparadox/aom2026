The Tipping Point of IP: Curvilinear Appropriability Effects in University -Industry Collaboration

TIM
Takron Opassuwan (Chiang Mai University); Vadim Grinevich (University of Bradford); Hang Do (University of Southampton)
Although prior research has examined how firms’ appropriability mechanisms shape engagement in university–industry collaboration (UIC), the evidence remains mixed. In particular, formal appropriability mechanisms (e.g., patents) can both encourage and discourage collaboration with university partners. This study addresses these inconsistencies by examining how appropriability strength relates to firms’ openness in the UIC context. Using data on 10,860 manufacturing firms from the Thailand Community Survey, we find an inverted U-shaped relationship between appropriability strength and UIC engagement, measured by collaboration breadth and the propensity to collaborate through contractual and relational channels. We further show that this relationship depends on firms’ innovation orientation. For product innovators, the inverted U-shaped relationship between appropriability and UIC breadth is strengthened, whereas for process innovators it is weakened, yielding a convex pattern. High appropriability discourages product innovators from engaging through relational channels, while process innovators tend to shift from relational to contractual channels. Practically, the results indicate that moderate appropriability supports UIC, but excessive protection can deter collaboration, so UIC support and partnership design should be tailored to firms’ innovation orientation and governance mode.
