“We Are In This Together”: Role-Differentiated Experiences of Transition Management

Authors: Leila Bateman Children's Hospital of Philadelphia; Hillary Forbrich Children's Hospital of Philadelphia

Session overview

Change in organizational technology projects is increasingly rapid; to face it, employees need support in managing transitions (Accenture, 2024; Zhexembayeva, 2024). The Bridges Transitions Model offers a framework for understanding the psychological experience of transition, but has been examined very little in the context of real-woold complex organizational and project change (Samuel & Tsapayi, 2023; Leon et al., 2023; Raisbeck, 2021). This abstract applies the Bridges model in a large digital transformation project team and examines the utility of the model in understanding how project leaders experience transition and how introducing leaders to the model can be beneficial. Qualitative survey methods were used to assess transition experience before and after a retreat centered on transition management for project leaders. Results found role-differentiated experiences of the three transition phases with middle managers experiencing more feelings of loss and a longer period of transition compared with sponsors and team members. Post-retreat qualitative feedback found the introduction of the transition model concept to be beneficial to middle managers with greater understanding of the emotional and psychological aspects of transition. Future use of the model in practice in workplaces as well as further study in larger samples longitudinally offers opportunities both in academia and in practice. These findings offer more nuanced understanding of employee transition experience through change and a needed application of the Bridges Transition Model in a real-world organizational project setting. The findings can inform to more targeted research on psychological experience of middle-manager transitions through project change and organization support opportunities.
