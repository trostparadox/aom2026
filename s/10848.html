Session overview

As geopolitical tensions escalate globally (Institute for Economics and Peace, 2024), understanding how organizations and organizing processes contribute to peace has become both urgent and essential. Yet organization and management scholarship has only begun to address these challenges systematically. Building on momentum from the 2025 AOM SIM PDW on organizing for peace and aligned with the forthcoming Research in the Sociology of Organizations (RSO) volume "Organizing for Peace: Theory, Practice, and Transformation" (2028), this workshop aims to advance both theoretical development and community building in this emerging field. This PDW aims to bring together diverse theoretical perspectives, including institutional theory, paradox theory, negotiation, emotions, and nonmarket strategy to examine how actors organize for peace across sectors, levels, and conflict stages. Through an opening panel featuring leading organizational and peace scholars, followed by theory-focused roundtables, we will foster dialogue on key theoretical and methodological challenges while building a vibrant scholarly community. The workshop addresses three interconnected goals: (1) advancing diverse theoretical perspectives on organizing for peace, (2) addressing methodological challenges of researching peace in complex, sensitive, and volatile contexts, and (3) building collaborative networks among scholars working on this grand challenge. By convening researchers from business and peace, organizational theory, system change, and conflict management traditions, we aim to establish shared conceptual foundations while respecting theoretical pluralism, creating a platform for cumulative knowledge development in this vital domain.
