Hybrid Teams in the Army: Examining Team Member Experiences

Authors: John Adekunbi Pace University; Julia Eisenberg Pace University

Session overview

Hybrid teams have been extensively studied in a variety of contexts, including various types of corporate settings, yet their application within military organizations remains underexplored. This paper examines the effectiveness of hybrid teams in the United States Army, with a focus on how computer-mediated communication, team identification, and transformational leadership influences such teams. Hybrid teams, which combine face-to-face and virtual collaboration, are increasingly relevant in military operations where the integration of distributed work with communication technology advancements is essential. To examine these dynamics, the study surveyed 54 service members engaged in hybrid team settings. The research examines how communication platforms impact collaboration, the degree to which individuals identify with their teams, and how transformational leadership promotes team performance. Results underscore the importance of effective communication technologies, strong team identity, and transformational leadership in positively influencing hybrid team outcomes. By situating the discussion within the context of US Army settings, this study extends scholarship beyond corporate environments and highlights the unique challenges and opportunities of hybrid teams in more structured and hierarchical contexts. The findings provide practical implications for leadership development, communication strategies, and organizational effectiveness offering valuable insights for both scholars and practitioners.
