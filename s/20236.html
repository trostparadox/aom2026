Virtual Nirvana: How to Configure Immersive Meditations for Mental Well-Being

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 2

People

Authors: Fabian Tingelhoff University of St. Gallen; Jens Joachim Marga WHU - Otto Beisheim School of Management; Sofia Schöbel Osnabrück University; Nadine Meier University of St. Gallen; Daniel Hengel University of St. Gallen

Session overview

Immersive technology has emerged as a promising tool for supporting users’ mental well-being, yet we know little about its mechanisms, particularly in the context of meditation. We used an exploratory mixed-methods data collection, comprising 182 diary entries, 72 semi-structured interviews, and 2282 choice set observations, to explore these mechanisms. We analyzed our data through a sociomaterial perspective, conceptualizing immersive meditation as the interplay between material features and human practices that can jointly support mental well-being. We identify and analyze 14 mechanisms across four sociomaterial layers: Embodied Device, Interaction Interface, Experience Design, and Context of Use. These are interrelated through cross-dependencies, in which immersive technologies capture attention and intention and direct them toward continued immersive meditation practice, enabling mental well-being. Our study contributes to HCI and user experience theory, transformative service scholarship, and occupational recovery research, and supports businesses in introducing a technological solution that supports employees’ well-being.
