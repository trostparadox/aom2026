Two Pathways to Sustainable Innovation in Exporting SMEs

Authors: Barbara Cater School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Tomaz Cater School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Mateja Bodlaj School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana

Session overview

Exporting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) face increasing sustainability expectations in international markets, yet evidence on how different sustainability motives translate into sustainable innovation remains fragmented. Building on the distinction between instrumental motives and moral sustainability motives, we test a mediated model in which sustainability orientation links motives to sustainable product and process innovation. Using an online survey of 222 exporting SMEs from an EU-based economy, we estimate direct and indirect effects with structural equation modeling and bias-corrected bootstrap confidence intervals. Both instrumental and moral motives are positively related to sustainability orientation. Sustainability orientation, in turn, is positively related to both sustainable product innovation and sustainable process innovation. Instrumental motives also show positive direct associations with both innovation outcomes, whereas moral motives have no statistically significant direct effects. Indirect-effects tests support mediation via sustainability orientation for both motive types. The findings clarify distinct motive pathways and emphasize the value of distinguishing between product and process innovation.
