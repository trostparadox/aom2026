Consumer Legitimacy as a Conditional Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Element in Craft Sectors

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6

Authors: Nisrine Tahri University Mohammed 6 Polytechnic; David Hayes

Session overview

Entrepreneurial ecosystem frameworks developed in high-technology contexts face critical limitations when applied to traditional sectors in emerging economies. Current frameworks treat "demand" as market size, overlooking how consumer judgments of authenticity, craftsmanship quality, and cultural value actively shape entrepreneurial success. This study extends Stam and van de Ven's (2021) entrepreneurial ecosystem framework by examining whether consumer legitimacy operates as a conditional ecosystem element in Morocco's heritage artisan sector. We employed a consumer survey (n=99) to assess perceptions of authenticity, cultural heritage valuation, and purchase intentions toward craft products. Respondents rated the importance of heritage, purchase likelihood, and willingness to pay a premium using 5-point Likert scales—open-ended questions captured authentication mechanisms and premium pricing motivations. Results demonstrate that heritage valuation is modal: 55.6% of consumers rated cultural heritage as "very important," with strong correlations to purchase likelihood (r=.653, p<.001) and premium willingness (r=.538, p<.001). Three authentication mechanisms emerged: handmade verification, uniqueness/imperfection as authenticity signals, and cultural preservation. However, substantial heterogeneity exists: 22% rated heritage as relatively unimportant, and gender differences emerged in premium willingness despite identical heritage valuations. These findings suggest that consumer legitimacy operates as a conditional ecosystem element in heritage sectors, where cultural authenticity serves as a market signal. Unlike conventional demand measured by market size, consumer legitimacy creates market access contingent on authentication processes. This extends entrepreneurial ecosystem theory beyond high-technology contexts to traditional sectors where cultural authentication mechanisms differ fundamentally from innovation-driven dynamics.
