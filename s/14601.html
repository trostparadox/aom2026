Academic Spin-Offs in Emerging Entrepreneurial Ecosystems: An Attention-Based View from Greece

Authors: Aliki Nianaki National and Kapodistrian University of Athens; Christina Theodoraki Aix-Marseille University; Antonios D. Livieratos National and Kapodistrian University of Athens

Session overview

This study examines how ecosystem-level conditions influence founders’ attention allocation in academic spin-offs within an emerging entrepreneurial ecosystem. Drawing on the attention-based view of the firm, we investigate academic spin-offs in Greece through a qualitative, longitudinal study of 20 academic spin-offs consisting of 58 interviews with founders and key ecosystem actors. The findings show that although financial resources, intermediaries, and support organizations are formally present, weak coordination and institutional fragmentation limit their effective mobilization. Misalignment between venture capital investors and ASOs restricts access to private investment. As a result, founders rely heavily on public funding calls to generate short-term revenues, often diverting attention away from commercialization. Administrative procedures, intellectual property negotiations, and regulatory uncertainty further absorb founders’ limited time. Overall, the study shows that in less mature ecosystems, structural conditions systematically redirect founders’ attention toward coordination and survival activities, helping explain delayed commercialization and research-driven trajectories of these companies.
