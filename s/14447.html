Session overview

This symposium features four papers which bring unique perspectives to consider the way leaders manage the relational exchanges they have with others in order to succeed and thrive. The symposium will begin with a brief introduction and welcome statement by Robert K. Jepsen before moving into 4 rounds of paper presentations, and ending with concluding and integrative statements by discussant Berrin Erdogan and a group Q&A session. The first paper, authored by Robert K. Jepsen, showcases an adaptation of job demands-resources theory to address the rising concern of leaders' poor well-being by expanding our understanding of where leaders' resources come from, and why it matters. Next, this symposium highlights the work of Klodiana Lanaj, Sooyeol Kim, & Yejoo (Esther) Lee, where the authors examined how the personal resources leaders receive at home may benefit themselves and their followers at work. Third, the paper composed by Fadel K. Matta, E. Jordan McBride, Ryan S. Grant, & Charles W. Bennion proposes a new definition and measure of LMX, aligning the construct more closely with its role theory-based roots. The final paper to be presented, authored by Jinfeng (Phoenix) Chen, Lindsay Mechem Rosokha, Kelly Schwind Wilson, Amy L. Bartels, & Lusi Wu, creates three profiles of leader-follower dyads based on the relational functions they provide each other, showcasing the quantitative and qualitative differences between the profiles in terms of the benefits and detriments they are associated with.
