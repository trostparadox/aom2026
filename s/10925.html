Session overview

This symposium brings together four complementary papers that advance understanding of leader perfectionism and its implications for employee behavior, workplace relationships, and team performance. The first paper introduces an interactionist model demonstrating how leader perfectionism and performance pressure jointly foster micromanagement, which followers may interpret as either mentoring or objectification depending on their need for control. The second paper shows that leaders’ other-oriented perfectionism reduces the quantity but enhances the quality of employee voice through distinct cognitive pathways, with these effects contingent on followers’ own perfectionistic tendencies. The third paper examines how teams’ average levels of perfectionism and within-team diversity in perfectionism shape collective promotion focus and, in turn, team effectiveness. The fourth paper identifies task significance as a critical contextual moderator that determines whether perfectionistic leader demands elicit employee self-improvement or cheating, via developmental versus exploitative motive attributions. Collectively, these papers offer a multilevel, theory-driven account of perfectionistic leadership and its organizational consequences.
