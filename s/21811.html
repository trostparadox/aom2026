Synthetic Sincerity: When Generative AI Makes Signals of Care Cheap at Work

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11
OB

People

Author: Marian Hristov Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University

Session overview

Relational communication at work includes coaching feedback, recognition, and supportive check-ins. Employees often read these messages as signals of care and relational investment. Signaling theory suggests that warmth can be credible because it usually costs time, attention, and emotional labor. Spence (1973) and Connelly and colleagues (2011) describe how cost helps separate high-care from low-care senders. Generative artificial intelligence reduces the marginal cost of producing empathic and polished language. That shift can weaken the link between sincerity cues and underlying care. We call this credibility shift synthetic sincerity. We propose a cost collapse pathway. Higher GenAI reliance lowers recipients’ inferred cost, which lowers perceived sincerity and interpersonal trust. Mayer and colleagues (1995) and Rousseau and colleagues (1998) provide foundations for why trust matters in work relationships. We also propose a receiver adaptation. As words become cheaper, recipients lean more on follow-through, such as investing time, removing barriers, and incorporating feedback. Follow-through becomes a more credible separating signal. We ground the model in IRB-approved in-person interviews on leadership communication during AI and technology change. We will test the mechanism in a vignette experiment with working adults recruited via Prolific, using a 3-by-2 design that manipulates GenAI reliance and follow-through. A planned longitudinal field survey across three industries will examine trust trajectories and signal repair across repeated message action episodes.
