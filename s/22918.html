Conceptualizing Democratically Destructive Leader Communication

OMT
Andrew George McBride (Santa Clara University); Camille Grace Endacott (University of North Carolina at Charlotte)
As “epistemic authorities,” leaders play an important role in supporting or hindering democratic processes in organizations and institutions. In addition to influencing the structural features that support democracy, the way leaders communicate can enable or constrain people’s ability to meaningfully engage with decisions that affect their lives. Drawing on sensemaking and communication theories, we build a conceptualization of democratically destructive leader communication that focuses on how certain modes of communicating can distort people’s ability to make plausible sense of an issue, event, or decision. We define destructive leader communication as existing at two ends of a continuum: Under-determination, where leaders provide little to no clear input to collective sensemaking, and over-determination, where leaders attempt to steer collective sensemaking down one path. Our paper conceptualizes each end of the continuum and provides prototypical forms of each (e.g. strategic ambiguity, naturalization). We explain why these forms are destructive from the point of view of constraining or enabling democratic processes, even as they may facilitate other organizational goals (e.g. performance, cohesion). We then propose outcomes of the different forms of communication, with special attention to follower agency.

Unveiling the Conceptual Architecture of Leadership in the Plural: Towards a Multi-Anchor Approach

OMT
Manuel Castriotta (University of Cagliari); Francesca Chiara Serri (University of Cagliari); Giovanna Bertella (UiT The Arctic University of Norway)
In this work, we argue that heroic, individual-centered leadership represents only one possible, though arguably not the most suitable, approach to managing organizational and social dynamics. Despite considerable scholarly attention, forms of leadership in the plural remain far from clearly defined, still conceptually fragmented, and obscured by overlapping and ambiguous meanings. We therefore advance an alternative perspective that theoretically frames leadership in the plural as a constellation of enactment dimensions and context-specific configurations. Drawing on contingency theory and adopting a semantic lens through co-word analysis, we identify six latent dimensions that underpin leadership in the plural, along with their associated actors, structural contexts, and processes. This approach shows how and why plural forms of leadership unfold across diverse organizational settings. Beyond establishing the theoretical foundations for multiple conceptualizations of leadership in the plural, the study also demonstrates the potential of co-word analysis as a methodological tool for abstracting conceptual dimensions and disentangling ambiguities in research domains characterized by evolving multidimensional constructs. We conclude by discussing what this means for managers who want to develop and maintain plural leadership across different levels and settings in their organizations.

Don’t Wish Leaders Away: Seeing Leader and Leadership Through Ontological Lens of Deep Relationality

OMT
Ellina Watanabe (ESADE Business School - Ramon Llull University)
This paper explains how we can pursue relational leadership without losing an individual in the process. Responding to recent tensions of handling an individual in social flows of practice on one hand, and obscuring the role of individual, on the other, I take three ontological turns that address ontological fault lines of individual and collective horizons of leadership. I proceed to develop an ontological lens of deep relationality that integrates both leadership actors and social interactions as continuous, constitutive processes of becoming. Firstly, I reconceptualize individual actors as dynamic relational figurations rather than discrete entities. Secondly, I address arbitrary boundaries of situated collective interactions and reframe them as porous, ephemeral, and socially constructed. Finally, I ensure ontological continuity where relational flows continue on living beyond immediate social interactions. Through this lens, leadership is neither something leaders do and build, nor is it bounded to social site, but is an emergent expression of relational becoming. This paper contributes to leadership scholarship by establishing thoroughgoing relational continuity across horizons and elements, challenging atomism of individual, and opening new pathways for theory and practice. I conclude with a discussion of the implications of this deeply relational account for future research and methodology.

From Resources to Outcomes: A Mediated Model of Leadership, Innovation, and Performance in SMEs

OMT
Ali Iftikhar Choudhary (Deakin University)
Entrepreneurial small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operate in increasingly volatile and innovation-driven environments where sustainable performance depends largely on the effective mobilisation of intangible internal resources. Drawing on the resource-based view (RBV) and dynamic capabilities theory, this study develops and empirically tests a mediated model explaining how organisational culture and work values ethic influence organisational performance through design leadership and workplace innovation. Using cross-sectional survey data from 367 Australian entrepreneurial SMEs, structural equation modelling (SEM) was employed to examine the hypothesised relationships. The findings reveal that design leadership plays a pivotal enabling role by translating cultural and ethical resources into workplace innovation practices, such as participatory decision-making, experimentation, and process redesign. Workplace innovation emerges as the primary proximal driver of organisational performance. Mediation analyses demonstrate full mediation of the organisational culture–performance relationship through design leadership and workplace innovation, and partial mediation of the work values ethic–performance relationship, indicating that ethical values also retain a direct effect on performance outcomes. Theoretically, it contributes to the Resource-Based View (RBV) theory by explicating the process through which value-based resources are operationalised via leadership and innovation capabilities in entrepreneurial SMEs. It advances design leadership research by positioning design leadership as a central dynamic capability linking values to innovation and performance. Practically, the findings underscore the importance for SME leaders and policymakers of cultivating innovation-supportive cultures, strengthening ethical work values, and developing design leadership capabilities to enhance sustainable organisational performance.

When Fans Construct Leadership Through Evolving Narratives in Digital Discursive Spaces

OMT
Kedir Assefa Tessema (Wilkes University)
Coaching retention and dismissal decisions are often evaluated through performance metrics, yet executives act within legitimacy-sensitive stakeholder environments. This study examines how digitally networked fan communities negotiate coaching leadership legitimacy and how those negotiations escalate into reputational constraint for sport organizations. Using an event-centered qualitative design, I analyze naturally occurring Reddit match and post-game threads across four coaching contexts in association football, American football, basketball, and cricket. Fans’ evaluations operate as legitimacy work: commenters translate outcomes into publicly warrantable accounts of competence and accountability and tighten evidentiary standards as performance becomes unstable. Delegitimation consolidates through recurring escalation markers, including “no adjustment” narratives, pattern-repetition claims, and irreversibility formulations that frame dismissal as rational closure. Cross-sport comparison shows that accountability architecture shapes whether blame rapidly personalizes onto the head coach or remains distributed. The study offers a process account of stakeholder legitimacy negotiation around coaches, informing legitimacy monitoring and stakeholder management strategies.
