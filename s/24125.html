How Artificial Intelligence Shifts Risk-Control Orientations

OMT
Fan Zhang (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes increasingly embedded in financial decision-making, algorithmic risk control is commonly viewed as a means of improving predictive accuracy and operational efficiency. Yet prior research has concentrated primarily on AI’s immediate effects on observable risk outcomes, paying limited attention to how AI systematically reshapes the long-run evolution of risk-control behavior itself. We distinguish between two fundamental orientations of financial risk control—exploration and exploitation—and examine how AI adoption reconfigures their organizational balance over time. Drawing on evolutionary game theory, we develop a dynamic model to trace the evolutionary trajectories of decision-makers with and without AI-enabled support. The model demonstrates that because algorithmic risk control relies heavily on short-term, quantifiable indicators as reward signals, decision-makers progressively converge on exploitative risk-control strategies. AI adoption further produces three distinct system-level effects—catalyzing, reversing, and dampening—that intensify reliance on established rules and create structural conditions conducive to reward hacking. From an evolutionary perspective, we provide a mechanism-based theoretical lens for understanding AI adoption in risk control. From a practical standpoint, our findings suggest that although AI can enhance risk-control efficiency, financial institutions must guard against exploitative drift and intentionally reinvest in exploratory risk-control capabilities.
