Session overview

This PDW joins the lively intellectual discussion around creativity and innovation research, moving beyond idea generation to also explaining idea development and implementation, how ideas are refined, chosen, resourced, executed, and adapted. We organize the session as an “idea to implementation pipeline,” using five empirical papers to illustrate how different mechanisms shape creative progress from inception to realization. We begin with Mateja et al., who explore how human and AI collaboration structures early creative work through iterative prompting, revealing how search strategies co evolve between humans and AI. Moving downstream, Lazar et al. show how generative AI reshapes idea elaboration, compensating for weaker starting points while sometimes flattening stronger ones, highlighting the contingent value of AI as a creative collaborator. Ayoubi and Just then examine the evaluative stage, showing how AI summarization compresses information and alters perceptions of novelty, impact, and feasibility, reframing how ideas are judged and selected. Clark et al. then examine the execution stage, tracing how ventures pivot and adapt through psychological ownership and learning mechanisms. Finally, Fisher extends this to the resourcing phase, demonstrating how implementer judgments and evaluator biases favor teams over solo creators, influencing which ideas receive legitimacy and funding. We finish with a discussion by Justin Berg, followed by a roundtable discussion.
