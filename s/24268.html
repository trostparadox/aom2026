The Janus-Faced Role of AI in Entrepreneurship: When and How AI Usage Shapes Improvisation

OB
Dian Gu (Wuhan University of Technology); Huaying Wang (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics)
While Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in entrepreneurial processes, research has predominantly focused on its macro-level value, neglecting how entrepreneurs’ AI usage shape their decision-making process and subsequent behaviors, particularly improvisation. Drawing on Social Cognitive Theory and Effectuation Theory, we propose that differential AI usage (generative vs predictive) can potentially unlock or undermine entrepreneurial improvisation through distinctive decision logic of effectuation and causation, with cognitive flexibility as a critical boundary condition. Using a multi-wave survey of 406 entrepreneurs (Study 1), we found full support for our hypotheses. Our findings revealed that generative AI usage positively influences entrepreneurs’ improvisation by enhancing effectual logic, while predictive AI usage negatively influences entrepreneurs’ improvisation by strengthening causal logic. High levels of cognitive flexibility amplifies these indirect effects, as it enables entrepreneurs to better align their cognitive processes with the affordances of different types of AI usage. Our findings contribute to the entrepreneurial literature by unpacking the heterogeneous impacts of AI usage on entrepreneurial action and highlighting the role of individual cognition in leveraging AI tools in entrepreneurial decision-making logic.
