Session overview

As unethical behavior continues to rise in the workplace, so too does scholars’ interest in understanding its causes and consequences. Although prior research has made substantial progress in identifying the antecedents of unethical behavior, recent reviews have suggested that less is known about the consequences of unethical behavior for organizations, social dynamics, and unethical actors themselves. This symposium addresses this research agenda with contributions that (1) examine different qualities of unethical behaviors (e.g., lying, unethical leadership, unethical pro-organizational behavior pressure, unethical prosocial behavior), identify (2) psychological (e.g., moral appraisal, moral potency, motivation), (3) perceptual (e.g., leader-member exchange quality, trust), and (4) behavioral mechanisms (e.g., coordination) that explain the influence of unethical behavior on outcomes, as well as (5) highlight critical moderators of the processes set in motion by unethical behavior (e.g., contextual factors, personality traits [such as moral character, moral identity, and performance promotion motives], managerial experiences and traits, perpetrator competence). Notably, the set of papers examines various outcomes of unethical behavior as well (i.e., ethical and unethical behaviors, acquiescence, nonconformity, growth, performance, work meaning).
