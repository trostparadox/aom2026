Dual-Target Psychological Ownership and Turnover Intentions: The Mediating Role of Knowledge Hiding

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11

Authors: Biserka Markus University of Montenegro; Dragana Cirovic University of Montenegro, Faculty of Economics; Milica Vukcevic University of Montenegro; Isidora Sukovic Damar Institute; Zerina Borancic Damar Institute

Session overview

Drawing on psychological ownership theory, this study examines how dual-target psychological ownership – knowledge-based (KBPO) and organization-based (OBPO) – shapes knowledge hiding and turnover intentions in knowledge-intensive organizations. Based on data from 340 employees in Montenegro, a moderated mediation model was tested using Hayes’ PROCESS macro. Results indicate that KBPO is positively associated with knowledge hiding only when OBPO is low, highlighting a boundary condition for the potentially detrimental effects of personal knowledge ownership. Knowledge hiding, in turn, is positively associated with turnover intentions, with KBPO indirectly increasing turnover intentions via knowledge hiding under low OBPO. The findings underscore that strong OBPO mitigates the negative consequences of KBPO, fostering cooperative knowledge behaviors and reducing employee attrition risk. Conceptually, this research clarifies the dual-target nature of psychological ownership, reconciling conflicting evidence on its role in promoting knowledge sharing versus knowledge hoarding. Practically, the results suggest that organizations can reduce knowledge hiding and enhance retention by cultivating employees’ sense of organizational ownership. Future studies should employ longitudinal, multi-source, or behavioral measures to further validate these mechanisms.
