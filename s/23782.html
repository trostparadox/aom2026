How and When Socially Responsible Human Resource Management Practices Influence Employee Time Theft

HR
Tang Yumeng (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Wang Juan (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
Although existing studies have begun to focus on the impact of socially responsible human resource management (SRHRM) on employees’ positive behavior, they have neglected its influence on negative behavior, such as time theft behavior. Based on self-determination theory and the dual model of passion, this paper constructs the overall theoretical model to explore how and when SRHRM practices influence employees’ time theft behavior. Using one scenario-based experiment and one multi-wave field study, our results show that SRHRM practices effectively are negatively related to employees’ time theft behavior. Obsessive and harmonious passion mediate the relationship between SRHRM and employee time theft behavior. Furthermore, performance-avoidance goal orientation weakens the positive effect of SRHRM on obsessive passion, but performance-proving goal orientation has no significant effect on the path of SRHRM on harmonious passion. This study helps comprehensively understand the potential mechanism between SRHRM and employee time theft behavior, and offers practical implication for organizations aiming to reduce employees’ time theft behavior.
