Building Bridges to Belonging: An Experiential Outreach Model for Diverse Talent in Supply Chain

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Afrooz Moatari-Kazerouni Widener University; Jeffrey Lolli Widener University

Session overview

To address the critical talent shortage and lack of diversity in Supply Chain Management (SCM) and Business Analytics (BA), this study evaluates an outreach strategy designed to inspire underrepresented high school students. Despite the growing demand for skilled professionals, career pathways in these fields remain obscure to many young learners, particularly those from first-generation or minority backgrounds. Guided by Experiential Learning Theory, a single-day, industry-immersive event was designed and implemented to introduce participants to real-world logistics operations, data-driven decision-making, and career mentoring. Using a pre-post survey methodology with 32 first-generation high school students and/or students of color, shifts in career interest, conceptual understanding, and perceived belonging were measured. Results indicate that the most significant barrier to entry is not a lack of interest, but rather a fundamental uncertainty about how to begin exploring SCM/BA. Following the event, students reported statistically significant increases in both their intent to pursue related careers and their grasp of key concepts. Engagement with industry mentors emerged as a particularly powerful catalyst, with over 90% of attendees noting a boost in confidence. These findings underscore the potential of a holistic, multi-component design of events to diversify talent pipelines by making invisible careers visible and accessible. The study offers a transferable framework for educators and industry partners seeking to build inclusive pathways into high-demand business fields.
