Session overview

This panel symposium examines how corporations enable or constrain social enterprises through concrete roles and governance choices, and why these relationships often generate both opportunities and tensions. The panel shifts attention towards how corporations shape venture survival, scaling, mission integrity, and stakeholder welfare. We adopt a roles-based perspective that links micro-level interfaces (e.g., procurement, contracts, certification), meso-level governance arrangements, and macro-level ecosystem and regulatory contexts. Drawing on CSR, stakeholder theory, and hybrid organizing research, the panel highlights causal mechanisms, power dynamics, and boundary conditions. After opening reflections to establish a shared vocabulary and key tensions, panellists engage in an integrative discussion around CSR motivations, market power and mission drift, and empowerment in Base of the Pyramid contexts. Then it concludes with a strategic management approach, and audience discussion.
