Session overview

Organisation theory offers sophisticated accounts of how firms coordinate judgment under uncertainty, allocate decision rights, and construct accountability structures. These accounts assume that learning, whether organisational or computational, occurs where data can be pooled and inspected. When privacy constraints prevent data centralisation, this assumption breaks. Learning must occur across distributed sites that cannot share raw information. This workshop theorises distributed privacy-preserving learning not as a technical configuration but as a governance shock that fragments authority, renders accountability ambiguous, and weakens the legitimacy of algorithmic outputs. We draw on qualitative evidence from 50 executive interviews and 408 survey responses examining coordination under distributed conditions. Participants engage in a four-stage Distributed Learning Governance Cycle: mapping where data resides versus where learning occurs, auditing accountability gaps when no single party controls model development, stress-testing legitimacy under stakeholder scrutiny, and designing governance repairs. The workshop generates reusable artefacts: a completed Distributed Learning Governance Map (DLGM), a research question bank, and starter propositions linking learning architecture to organisational outcomes. For OMT scholars studying authority, decision rights, and accountability, this PDW offers conceptual tools for an altered empirical landscape in which learning no longer requires seeing the data.
