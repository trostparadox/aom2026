Practices of Complicity: Microhistory of Organizational Adaptation

MH
Juha-Antti Lamberg (University of Jyväskylä); Kasimir Jolkkonen (Aalto University); Eero Vaara (University of Oxford)
This study advances understanding of organizational complicity by revealing sedimentary processes of adaptation to oppressive systems. Analyzing thousands of archival documents, we examine how a forest industry organization adapted to the Nazi regime (1933–1945) through gradual legitimation. Over a period of more than 10 years, elite executives engaged in routine and non-routine legitimacy work to justify collaboration, punctuated by a few turning points that led to decisions to remain in the German market. Early actions fostered deeper involvement, and by WWII, the organization was using resources from occupied territories for the German war economy—actions unthinkable at the outset. Our process model elucidates how cumulative routines culminate in non-routine turning points, redefining legitimacy and creating deeper complicity over time.
