Working Mothers Performing Ideal Femininity in Chinese Banking

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 3
DEI

People

Author: Meng Huang University of York

Session overview

China’s three-child policy aims to expand family support, yet its organisational consequences for working mothers remain underexamined. Drawing on participant-driven photo-elicitation interviews with 24 banking professionals, this study shows two linked mechanisms: statutory entitlements are enacted as discretionary favours, and evaluation increasingly follows an availability-based risk calculus that treats motherhood as interruption. Together, these conditions crystallise into three implicit performative templates for working mothers: the dutiful mother, the always-available worker, and the supportive spouse, that shape hiring, allocation, appraisal, and promotion. Women strategically perform these templates to sustain legitimacy and minimise disruption, yet such performances also stabilise the criteria that justify exclusion and stratification. The poster develops performative templates as an analytic device for explaining how culturally normalised scripts enact inequality regimes in professional workplaces and highlights practical levers for reducing discretion and auditing availability-based metrics.
