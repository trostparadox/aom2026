A Neglected Minority: Immigrants’ Experiences of Workplace Mistreatment Across the Career Lifecycle

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
CMS

People

Author: Mehnaz Rafi University of Calgary

Session overview

Immigrant workers represent a distinct yet frequently overlooked group in the labor force, often merged with other minority populations in research. This scoping review synthesizes research on workplace mistreatment experienced by immigrants across two career stages: 1) recruitment and selection as job candidates, and 2) employment as job incumbents. The review documents widespread discrimination during hiring that contributes to unemployment, underemployment, and skill underutilization, particularly among racialized immigrants. It further shows that, once employed, immigrant workers face exploitative working conditions, elevated physical and psychosocial hazards, and higher exposure to interpersonal mistreatment. The review also highlights how mistreatment is compounded through intersectionality, with gender, race, religion, and cultural distance intensifying disadvantage. Overall, this review maps the scope of immigrant workplace mistreatment and underscores its implications for employee well-being, organizational effectiveness, and societal outcomes.
