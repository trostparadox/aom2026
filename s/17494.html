Session overview

Conglomerates once embodied the dominant logic of corporate strategy, offering a way to coordinate diversified activities under common ownership. Over time, however, these organizations increasingly faced challenges related to governance complexity, capital market scrutiny, and the pace of technological change. Their subsequent unraveling did not eliminate multi-business organization; rather, it transformed it. Today, corporate scope is increasingly structured through financial sponsors, partial ownership stakes, sequenced acquisitions and divestitures, and collaborative arrangements that blur traditional governance forms. These developments call into question how strategy scholars conceptualize coordination, advantage, and control, with implications for governance, corporate development, and capability formation. This 90-minute symposium brings together leading researchers on divestitures, private equity, asset redeployment, and interfirm collaboration to illuminate how contemporary forms of multi-business organization operate in practice. By placing these streams into dialogue, the panel advances a view of corporate strategy in which value is created not primarily by holding businesses together, but by continuously reshaping how they are owned, governed, and connected.
