Artificial Intelligence and External Knowledge Search for Innovation

TIM
Paolo Carioli (Copenhagen Business School); Mattia Pedota
A long-standing view in strategic management suggests that openness to external knowledge improves innovation performance only up to a point, beyond which further expansion becomes detrimental. This study shows that artificial intelligence (AI) reshapes this relationship. We argue that AI reconfigures firms’ cognitive architecture, altering how they manage the costs and benefits associated with engaging external knowledge sources. Drawing on firm-level panel data covering multiple industries and years, we find that the well-established inverted U-shaped relationship between collaboration breadth and innovation performance characterizes only firms that do not employ AI. Among AI users, this curvature attenuates considerably, yielding a flatter relationship in which innovation performance is highest when firms draw on a wide range of external knowledge sources. Overall, our findings identify AI as a transformative contingency and highlight the importance of aligning cognitively augmented capabilities with the scope of external search.

Artificial Intelligence, Collaborative Innovation, and Drug Development

TIM
Xiheng Gong (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University); Song Chen
Advances in artificial intelligence (AI) could reduce the cost and uncertainty of drug development in the clinical stage. We use panel data from publicly listed biopharmaceutical firms in China from 2016 to 2021 to study the impact of firm-level AI investments on drug development. We find that AI investments promote the enhancement of innovation efficiency in drug development, specifically manifested by an increase in the number of drug candidates entering phase I clinical trials. The mechanism comes primarily through collaborative innovation rather than independent innovation. This facilitation is stronger within a broader scope of innovation—especially for firms that have formed R&D alliances and those engaged in multiple therapeutic areas. Moreover, AI investments increase the number of biologics, oncology drugs, and hematological drugs in Phase I clinical trials. From the perspective of innovation direction, when R&D resources are limited, AI investments tend to drive innovation more in the fields of biologics and hematological drugs, while its impact on shifting the direction in the oncology drug sector is limited. Taken together, our study, based on three dimensions of innovation, reveals the advantages of AI investments in drug development.

Governing AI Innovation

TIM
Juan Manuel Gil (Butler University); Alberto Mendez-Morales (EGADE Business School)
Artificial intelligence is transforming innovation systems while exposing structural tensions within existing intellectual property regimes. Drawing on the Institution-Based View, this article examines how continuous learning, non-human generation, network effects, and data dependence challenge IP frameworks built for discrete and stable inventions, with particularly significant consequences for emerging markets. Grounded in calibrated differentiation as a foundational governance principle, we examine how pharmaceuticals, agriculture, and software have balanced innovation incentives with access imperatives. Integrating institutional analysis with scenario planning, we argue that current arrangements increase fragmentation risks, while coordinated reform can enable managed coevolution. We propose a hybrid IP architecture that aligns protection with institutional capacity and developmental stage, translating this framework into actionable guidance for policymakers, international organizations, and AI firms.

Governing Generative AI Innovation: Evidence from a Knowledge-Intensive IT SME

TIM
giuseppe Liccardo; Roberto Cerchione
The rapid diffusion of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is transforming innovation processes in knowledge-intensive industries, while simultaneously raising profound governance and legitimacy challenges. Although prior research recognises regulation as a key institutional influence on technological innovation, we still lack empirical insight into how regulatory expectations are translated into organisational practices and capability development, particularly within resource-constrained small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Addressing this gap, this study examines how firms and regulatory frameworks co-evolve during the adoption of GenAI through a qualitative case study of an IT SME integrating GenAI into its core activities. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews and analysed using a hybrid deductive and inductive approach. Innovation Systems (IS) theory is used to interpret system-level governance functions, while the Dynamic Capabilities (DC) framework explains how firms reconfigure resources in response to emerging regulatory uncertainty. By integrating firm-level capability development with system-level governance dynamics, we identify interrelated regulatory dimensions that structure how trustworthy GenAI innovation emerges in practice. These dimensions illustrate how regulation functions as an enabling infrastructure that shapes innovation trajectories by catalysing organisational capabilities. The study contributes to the existing literature by advancing a multi-level explanation of how regulation and innovation capabilities co-evolve in emerging digital technologies

How Human-AI Collaboration Influences Innovation Management in Large Firms?

TIM
Ferhat Demir (Prague University of Economics and Business)
Human-AI collaboration is gaining increasing attention in academia and practice, yet empirical insights into its influence on innovation management, particularly from a corporate perspective, remain limited. Adopting a process view and an exploratory approach, this study examines the role of AI across the full span of the innovation management process. Drawing on multiple case studies of 18 large firms, it identifies AI-enabled, AI-enhanced, and AI-replaced activities at each stage of the process. The findings show that most firms employ AI primarily in the exploration stage, with integration declining as they move through the innovation funnel. Based on five influential factors, the study also reveals distinct patterns of AI integration. The study further uncovers two stage-specific mechanisms (Digital Twins and AI Personas), and one cross-cutting mechanism (Innovation Agent), that translate AI-augmented activities into tangible outputs. Beyond stage-level effects, a key long-term outcome emerges: AI-driven time and cost efficiencies enhance large firms’ potential for disruptive innovation, which may become increasingly prevalent as these dynamics intensify.
