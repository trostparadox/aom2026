Disentangling Search and Learning Under Knowledge Interdependence: Dual-Industry Test, Pharma & Semi

STR
Tara Li (Hope College)
This study disentangles organizational search and learning in the innovation process and examines how knowledge interdependence differentially shapes each outcome. We theorize that interdependence can constrain search by increasing cognitive burdens, coordination complexity, and resource requirements, yet enhance learning by enabling cross-domain reinforcement and strengthening firms’ ability to assimilate and recombine knowledge. We further argue that knowledge distinctiveness conditions these effects by helping firms steer search toward areas aligned with their unique expertise and by improving the conversion of interdependent knowledge into deeper learning. We test these arguments in a dual-industry design—the pharmaceutical and semiconductor industries—using USPTO patent and citation data merged with Compustat from 1976–2014. Results are consistent across both industries: knowledge interdependence is negatively associated with search outcomes but positively associated with learning outcomes. Consistent with our moderation logic, knowledge distinctiveness mitigates the search penalty of interdependence and amplifies its learning benefit.
