ESOPs and Employer-Employee Relationship: The Moderating Role of Employee Ownership Breadth

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 2
CM

People

Author: Zheng Cheng University of Northern Iowa

Session overview

Employee Stock Ownership Plans (ESOPs) are often viewed as an agency-alignment mechanism and are linked to stronger effort, productivity, and retention, yet their implications for employer–employee relations remain unclear. Drawing on equity theory and psychological contracts, this study argues that ESOP effects depend on ownership breadth—how widely ownership is shared across employees. Broad coverage should reinforce fairness and shared-gains expectations and reduce conflict, whereas narrow coverage may appear exclusionary and trigger perceptions of inequity and breach. Using a 2010–2024 panel that links Form 5500 filings, Violation Tracker employment-related enforcement records, and Compustat financials (141,206 firm–years; 15,288 firms), preliminary results show a design contingency: ESOPs with narrow breadth are associated with more employment-related violations than non-ESOP firms, while broad-breadth ESOPs are associated with fewer violations among ESOP firms. Effects on penalty intensity (fines-to-assets) are limited, suggesting an incidence rather than punishment channel.
