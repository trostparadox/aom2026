Duration Matters Differently: Historical vs. Social Underperformance and Search Transition

STR
Guanhua Wang (Tsinghua University); Zhibin Lin (Durham University); Kaicheng Liao (Chinese Academy of Social Sciences)
Research on problemistic search has emphasized feedback intensity while undertheorizing duration, or how long underperformance persists. We examine how historical duration and social duration differentially influence transitions between local and distant search. Drawing on behavioral theory and vacillation theory, we conceptualize “problemistic search transition” as a cyclical process. Prolonged historical underperformance reduces transitions through internal attribution and aspiration adjustment, whereas extended social underperformance increases transitions through external pressures and aspiration inflexibility. Using 10,527 firm-year observations of U.S. high-tech manufacturers, we find support for these contrasting effects. Environmental dynamism and munificence moderate the effects as predicted, while complexity reveals boundary-refining patterns. This study advances theory by integrating temporal dimensions, reconceptualizing problemistic search as a cyclical transition process, and delineating environmental boundaries that refine the scope conditions of the behavioral theory of the firm.
