When AI Plays Devil’s Advocate: Role Configurations for Strategic Decision-Making

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 13
CTO

People

Authors: Shuqing Liu University of Pittsburgh; Kerr Manson; Thomas Ware; Dennis Galletta University of Pittsburgh; Narayan Ramasubbu University of Pittsburgh

Session overview

As AI becomes embedded in strategic decision-making, understanding how to configure AI roles to augment human capabilities is critical. Yet current approaches predominantly configure AI as an expert, providing recommendations. This can trigger algorithm aversion or over-reliance. We propose that AI can be alternatively configured as Devil's Advocates, questioning assumptions and surfacing potential weaknesses. Drawing on Categorization-Elaboration Model and Cognitive Load Theory, we develop a dual-pathway model: Devil's Advocate AI enhances decision outcomes through increased information elaboration (benefit pathway) while imposing additional mental workload (cost pathway). Two experimental studies test main effects, mechanisms, and boundary conditions. Study 1 compares Expert AI versus Devil's Advocate AI on decision quality, novelty, and risk identification in strategic contexts, examining dual-pathway mechanisms. Study 2 extends to multi- round interactions with working professionals, testing AI literacy and domain expertise as moderators. This research will contribute to human-AI collaboration configuration literature by examining whether AI advisors configured to challenge rather than recommend can improve strategic decision-making.
