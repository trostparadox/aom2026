From Identity Shielding to Identity Reclaiming: Navigating the Gender Stereotype Threat

Authors: Sunduz Yilmaz Purdue University; Gonca Gunay Istanbul Bilgi University

Session overview

This study investigates coping mechanisms employed by female board members to mitigate "gender stereotype threat." Utilizing the Gioia methodology to analyze 20 in-depth interviews with professional women board members in Turkiye, we identify a multi-layered strategic process. In early career stages, coping is primarily defensive, characterized by Performative Credibility (hyper-preparation) and Identity Shielding (adopting masculinized personas). As women ascend to senior roles, they transition toward proactive Counter-Marginalization and Identity Reclaiming. In this mature stage, leaders move away from mimicry, reframing feminine-coded traits—such as empathy and relational intelligence—as strategic governance assets. The findings contribute to leadership literature by providing a grounded model of "subversive agency," illustrating how elite minority leaders move beyond defensive survival to proactively reshape their professional environment
