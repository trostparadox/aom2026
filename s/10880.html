Session overview

Failure and rejection are integral parts of academic careers, yet they remain largely invisible in professional narratives focusing almost exclusively on career success. This is visible at the Academy of Management’s Annual Meetings, with the PDW “The Productivity Process: Research Tips and Strategies from Prolific Junior Faculty” being a highlight of the Organizational Behavior division for years. Despite that session’s focus on research productivity as “a process that can consistently and continuously be improved through learning” (Chawla et al., 2025), that PDW is notoriously known to trigger detrimental social comparisons with the star junior Faculty panelists. This is in line with recent research showing the damaging effects of star employees’ exemplar job performance on their more regular peers (Zhou et al., 2025). Specifically, attendees become acutely aware of their own academic failures, potentially increasing an impostor syndrome (i.e., a persistent sense of self-doubt and fraudulence despite clear evidence of competence) known to hinder career outcomes (Neureiter & Traut-Mattausch, 2017). To address this issue, this new PDW aims to reframe and demystify “failure” as a normal, necessary, and even valuable component of career development. To do so, the PDW will uncover stories of failures from outstanding tenured academics from elite institutions, similarly to Princeton Professor Dr. Haushofer who famously “bravely [posted] a ‘CV failure’ document listing his career lows with degree programs he did not get into” (The Guardian, 2016), and academic positions, awards, fellowships and papers he was rejected from (Haushofer, n.d.).
