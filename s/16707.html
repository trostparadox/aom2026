Session overview

Executive education increasingly relies on storytelling such as cases, lived experiences, leadership narratives, and organizational myths, to support reflection, identity work, and collective sensemaking among senior leaders. More recently, artificial intelligence has entered executive classrooms through AI-generated scenarios, narratives, and simulations. While both human and AI-generated stories are present, little shared understanding exists about how these forms should be intentionally designed, governed, and integrated as sensemaking infrastructure rather than used opportunistically. This PDW positions human and AI-generated storytelling as complementary but fundamentally different resources for executive sensemaking. The inquiry shifts from “How do we tell better stories?” to “What stories should humans design, what can AI generate, and where must human judgment remain central?” Rather than treating AI as a replacement for human storytelling, the workshop foregrounds human judgment, ethical governance, and pedagogical intent. Consistent with PDW philosophy, it is developmental, interactive, and experiential, explicitly avoiding paper presentations, technology demonstrations, or claims of best practices. The PDW aims to (1) examine how human and AI-generated stories differently shape executive sensemaking; (2) surface design principles for responsible AI-assisted storytelling; (3) identify boundary conditions and ethical risks; and (4) co-create replicable outputs, including a human–AI storytelling design canvas and a typology of executive storytelling. The three-hour workshop opens with a contrastive narrative exercise, followed by facilitated human–AI design labs on leadership identity, strategic sensemaking, and governance/ethics, a “productive accidents” session analyzing failures, and culminates in co-creation of transferable artifacts and reflection on unresolved theoretical questions and research–practice collaborations.
