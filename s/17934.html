Session overview

Entrepreneurship for the public good offers a promising pathway for addressing grand societal challenges such as poverty and educational inequality. Although research on environmental, social, and sustainable entrepreneurship has expanded substantially, much of this work remains descriptive, offering insight into what such entrepreneurship looks like but far less clarity about why it emerges, how it operates, or which conditions enable it to endure. As a result, the field lacks an integrative understanding of the drivers and mechanisms that motivate, enable, and sustain entrepreneurship for societal impact. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) brings together scholars across disciplines to examine the multilevel antecedents of entrepreneurship for the public good. We treat entrepreneurship for the public good as the focal phenomenon and use impact entrepreneurship as an integrative explanatory lens to clarify how and why such activity emerges, operates, and produces durable societal outcomes. The session focuses on psychological drivers (e.g., purpose, identity), organizational enablers (e.g., culture, governance), and societal conditions (e.g., norms, regulations). Through expert panel dialogue and interactive breakout discussions, participants will identify key theoretical gaps, develop new research ideas, explore promising methodological approaches, and build interdisciplinary connections. By emphasizing multilevel explanation and mechanism-based theorizing, this PDW advances calls for more integrative theory on how entrepreneurship for the public good is enabled, constrained, and sustained over time.
