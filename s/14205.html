Session overview

In an era of deep societal fragmentation, this panel symposium foregrounds cultural entrepreneurship (CE) as a vital lens for theorizing and producing impact-driven research on entrepreneurship, institutional change, and the cultural–political dynamics through which actors generate legitimacy and reshape fields. CE refers to the processes through which actors mobilize cultural resources—such as discourse, language, categories, and institutional logics—to enable innovation and institutional change. The symposium explores how CE can seed a novel agenda for understanding change amid fractured social landscapes and contested meanings. Bringing together a diverse group of researchers, the panel examines the evolving conceptualization of CE across varied contexts. Panelists will discuss how CE can drive impactful theory to address pressing societal problems; how peace narratives enable institutional actors to recombine contested meanings and reshape symbolic boundaries to navigate divided societies; and the potential "dark sides" of CE, including cultural appropriation and erasure. Finally, the symposium explores CE as a future-making practice that generates new possibilities, and interrogates the conditions under which cultural work remains effective when shared cultural infrastructures have fractured.
