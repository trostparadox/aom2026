Unlocking Knowledge: Integration Pathways in Cross-Border Acquisitions

IM
Rama Krishna Reddy (Indiana University South Bend)
Cross-border acquisitions persistently underperform despite firms' strategic intent to access valuable knowledge resources. We address this paradox by developing an integrated theoretical framework that explicates how cultural, linguistic, and knowledge distance differentially affect human and task integration dimensions, and how these integration processes enable explicit versus tacit knowledge transfer. Drawing on the knowledge-based view and organizational learning theory, we theorize that distance constructs do not directly impede knowledge transfer but rather operate through integration mechanisms that create the relational and structural foundations necessary for knowledge flows. We further theorize how cross-border acquisition experience moderates distance effects by developing distinct capabilities for managing cross-national complexities. By simultaneously examining these relationships, we advance understanding of the mechanisms through which cross-border acquisitions create or destroy value and offer a more nuanced theoretical perspective on how firms can effectively transfer knowledge across organizational and national boundaries following acquisitions.
