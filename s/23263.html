Entrepreneurial Orientation in Social Business Incubation Processes

ENT
DAIANE TRETTO DA ROCHA; Cristina Dai Prá Martens (Nove de Julho University); Pedro V Matos (ISEG Lisbon School of Economics and Management, University of Lisbon)
This study analyzes how the dimensions of Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO) manifest and are developed throughout the incubation process of Social Businesses (SBs) in Brazil and Portugal, considering both generalist and specialist incubators. A qualitative research design with a comparative strategy was adopted. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews with 12 incubators (six Brazilian and six Portuguese) and 12 graduated SBs, complemented by documentary analysis. The results show that EO dimensions are not systematically embedded in incubation programs. Portuguese incubators display greater maturity in integrating impact, management practices, and EO, whereas Brazilian incubators reveal higher fragmentation and less structured support. The study demonstrates that EO dimensions assume specific configurations in hybrid incubation contexts and proposes a conceptual model of EO-oriented incubation. In doing so, it advances the literature by examining EO as an organizational capability shaped by incubation practices and institutional environments, offering a comparative analysis of Brazil and Portugal and expanding understanding of EO in impact-oriented ventures. From a practical perspective, the study highlights how incubators can design more intentional and integrated training pathways, aligned with the specificities of SBs, learning mechanisms, and impact measurement and communication, in order to strengthen EO.

Equifinal Routes to the Deal: Market Orientation Configurations and Net Effects in Pitch Success

ENT
Sven Klijer (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf); Jannis Von Nitzsch (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf)
This study examines whether market orientation functions as a signal in early-stage entrepreneurial pitches and how its components shape business angel investment decisions. Drawing on signaling theory, we analyze 495 Shark Tank pitches using large language model-based text analysis combined with fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) and probit regression. We quantify four components of market orientation (customer orientation, competitor orientation, long-term orientation, and profit focus) from full pitch transcripts. The results reveal that market orientation is an effective signal with mixed effects. Three equifinal configurations leading to investment offers emerge, all characterized by the absence of customer orientation, and, in most cases, long-term orientation, combined with the presence of competitor orientation and/or profit focus. Regression analyses support the findings, showing negative net effects of customer and long-term orientation and positive effects of competitor orientation and profit focus. The study challenges prevailing assumptions the market orientation literature and advances signaling theory by demonstrating conjunctural causation among strategic signals in early-stage financing contexts.

Unlocking the Connection Between Entrepreneurial Orientation and Ambidexterity

ENT
Chen Han (Lancaster University); Paul Hughes (Lancaster University)
This study unlocks the relationship between entrepreneurial orientation and innovation ambidexterity by investigating the potential mediating effect of inter-organizational knowledge transfer and the moderating role of business ties. A theoretical model based on knowledge-based theory is developed and tested with survey questionnaires. The results generally support the bridging role of the interaction of knowledge transfer effectiveness and efficiency and the positive moderating effect of business ties. This study uncovers knowledge transfer as a crucial yet overlooked intermediary between entrepreneurial orientation and innovation ambidexterity, enhances our understanding of the fit between entrepreneurial orientation and business ties for knowledge transfer success, and extends our understanding of the heterogeneous impacts of knowledge transfer effectiveness and efficiency.

Who Said What? Making Sense of Entrepreneurial Orientation in Firm Communications

ENT
Bill Wales (University at Albany, State University of New York); Dmitry Kabanov (HSE University); Jasmine Nicole Kelley (University of Tennessee at Chattanooga)
Research on entrepreneurial orientation (EO) in organizational communication has garnered remarkable traction in premier management and entrepreneurship journals over the past decade. However, a lack of attention to the implications of this conceptualization of EO for theory development has led to confusion among scholars about precisely what is being captured within EO research based on computer-aided text analysis (CATA). In this study, we offer a systematic review of the CATA EO literature and discuss the importance of understanding communication contexts for theory development, as well as the idiosyncrasies and opportunities when exploring EO within organizational communications. Moreover, we consider how studying EO within communication contexts promises to shape organizational analyses and insights in future research. We detail and illustrate our insights, drawing upon 72 research articles published between 2010 and 2025.
