Session overview

Abstract This 2-hour workshop delves into the rich tapestry of Indigenous Indian Management by exploring its unique cultural, institutional, and behavioral dimensions. It aims to provide participants with a holistic understanding of how Indian philosophical traditions, historical influences, and socio-economic contexts have shaped management practices in India. Through a blend of presentations, discussions, and interactive exercises activities, the workshop hopes to equip attendees with insights into the distinctive frameworks of Indian management and their relevance in today’s globalized business environment that has shifted from G-7 to G-20 nations, from triadic markets to BRICS, and from bi-polar to unipolar to a multipolar world. The workshop focuses on three pillars that define Indian management scholarship and practices: core cultural ideas, institutional uniqueness and hybridity and behavioral manifestations (Pandey, Budhwar, & Bhawuk, 2022). The part of the workshop will highlight the impact on many constructs, models, theories, ideas and practices arising from the Indian culture like ideas like the Indian concept of self, Panch Kosh (five layers of human existence) and Karm Yoga (ethics of action), Ashtang Yoga and Yoga based practices. Many of these ideas are found to have impact on organizationally relevant outcomes like leadership, decision-making, psychological capital, conflict resolution approaches and so forth. Participants will explore how these ideals foster an interconnected worldview, holistic well-being, and multiple pathways for individual and organizational growth. The workshop will emphasize the role of Indian institutions, such as family and community networks, as sources of social capital. It discusses hybridity in the institutions in India which combine multiple institutional logics provide unique features of Indian management. Insights into behaviors like nurturant Task leadership and context sensitivity demonstrate how Indian culture influences managerial actions. Participants will understand the paradoxical coexistence of traditional and modern behavioral tendencies in the Indian workplace.
