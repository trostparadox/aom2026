Capturing Technological Windows: Configurations of Capabilities and Responses Toward Innovations

TIM
Zhou Liang (Beijing Institute of Technology, Zhuhai); Ruicong Xiao (Harbin Institute of Technology)
Technological windows create both significant potential and profound uncertainty for firms, yet firms exposed to the same window often exhibit different innovation outcomes. Drawing on a configurational perspective, this study investigates how firms’ capability foundations and response patterns jointly shape technological innovation performance when a technological window opens. Using the emergence of generative artificial intelligence, marked by the release of OpenAI’s ChatGPT, as a representative technological window, we analyze Chinese listed firms by fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) to identify configurations of capability foundations (knowledge base and recombinant capabilities) and response patterns (the timing and scale of human capital investment), while accounting for environmental contingencies. The findings reveal that technological innovation under opportunity windows is not driven by opportunity exposure or resource input alone, but emerges from specific alignments between capability foundations and response patterns. Multiple equifinal pathways lead to high technological innovation, whereas misalignment consistently results in low innovation outcomes, even under the same technological window. By reconceptualizing technological innovation as a configurational and processual outcome, this study advances research on technological windows and innovation management, and offers a new framework for understanding firm heterogeneity in responding to transformative technological change.

Innovation Performance Feedback, Vertical Integration and Firms’ Technology Portfolio Change

TIM
Sheng Ma (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Innovation performance feedback is considered a crucial driver for high-tech firms in deploying and adjusting their innovation activities. While prior research has focused on how this feedback drives external technology search, little is known about how it influences the internal transformation of a firm's technology portfolio. Drawing on the behavioral theory of the firm, this paper investigates how innovation performance feedback drives technology portfolio change from the interactive perspective of motivation and capability. Using data from Chinese listed high-tech firms from 2003 to 2023, the empirical results reveal a differentiated, non-linear relationship between innovation performance feedback in different directions and technology portfolio change. Specifically, innovation performance below aspirations exhibits an inverted U-shaped relationship with technology portfolio change, whereas innovation performance above aspirations shows a U-shaped relationship. These patterns are explained by the divergent trend of risk-taking motivation and risk-bearing capacity under different feedback conditions. Furthermore, we find that a higher degree of vertical integration weakens the inverted U-shaped relationship for firms performing below aspirations, while strengthening the U-shaped relationship for those performing above aspirations. Our study offers insights for firms into adjusting internal technology portfolio by leveraging innovation performance feedback and structural factors.

Navigating the Fog: Technological Uncertainty, Capability Degradation, and CVC Portfolio Failure

TIM
Mario Marcel Bihler (TU Dortmund University); Steffen Strese (TU Dortmund University)
While corporate venture capital (CVC) investments are theoretically viewed as a mechanism to manage uncertainty, the impact of environmental volatility on portfolio failure remains underexplored. We address this gap by investigating how technological uncertainty in a corporate parent’s industry affects its CVC portfolio failure rate. Integrating Real Options Theory (ROT) with the Attention-Based View, we examine the theoretical tension between “uncertainty-as-option-value” and “uncertainty-as-capability-degradation” and test how this association is moderated by the portfolio’s share of late-stage investments and industry competitiveness. Based on 720 firm-year observations from S&P 500 firms between 2001 and 2019, we employ a fixed effects fractional logit regression. We find that technological uncertainty significantly increases the CVC portfolio failure rate, challenging ROT. A higher share of late-stage investments weakens this failure-inducing effect, whereas high industry competitiveness strengthens it. Our study contributes to the ROT literature by identifying organizational boundary conditions, advances CVC research by reconceptualizing portfolio composition as a cognitive buffer, and highlights the compounding risks of the speed-accuracy trade-off in competitive environments.

Performance Feedback Configurations and Knowledge Search Breadth: A Moderated Polynomial Regression

TIM
Zihan Zhao (Peking University); Yi Yang (Tongji University); Zhen Yan (Zhejiang University)
Performance feedback fundamentally shapes strategic behavior yet research rarely addresses the simultaneous pursuit of multiple goals. We adopt a configurational approach to examine how innovation and financial feedback congruence and incongruence impact knowledge search breadth. Integrating the behavioral theory of the firm with the cognitive augmentation perspective we find that consistent negative feedback triggers the most extensive search while consistent success reinforces organizational inertia. We also identify an asymmetric financial attention bias where the combination of high innovation and low financial performance drives wider search than the inverse. Crucially, this asymmetry is pronounced only when artificial intelligence capability is low, revealing that financial survival remains the primary managerial focus under human bounded rationality. Our study establishes firm artificial intelligence as a cognitive stabilizer that standardizes search responses and mitigates path dependent biases in the feedback response process. These findings contribute to behavioral research by shifting the analytical focus toward holistic configurations and identifying artificial intelligence as a strategic capability that reshapes organizational attention logic.

Scientific Knowledge, Technology Market Participation, and the External Influence of Innovation

TIM
Haoyue Huang (IE Business School); Marco S. Giarratana
How does participation in markets for technology shape the external influence of a firm’s innovations, and how does scientific knowledge condition this relationship? As innovation increasingly follows an open paradigm, technology suppliers’ inventions are exposed to external evaluation through markets for technology. Yet we know little about how such engagement affects whether firms’ innovations exert broad influence on subsequent technological development. This study examines how participation in markets for technology as a supplier through technology outsourcing shapes the external influence of firms’ patented innovations, and how firms’ scientific knowledge moderates this relationship. Focusing on the artificial intelligence (AI) domain, we combine AI-related outsourcing contracts with patent-based measures to construct a firm-year panel that aligns firms’ market participation and inventive activity within the same technological domain. We find that greater participation in technology markets is associated, on average, with lower external influence of firms’ innovations. However, this negative relationship is significantly attenuated for firms with strong scientific knowledge bases. Scientific knowledge enables firms to abstract from heterogeneous market feedback, allowing market participation to refine innovative search and sustain innovations with broader technological influence beyond firm boundaries.
