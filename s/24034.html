Impact of Hindrance Stressors on Work-Life Balance and Presenteeism: A Mixed-Methods Study

PNP
Stephen Teo (University of Economics Ho Chi Minh City); Diep Nguyen (The University of Newcastle); Mary Anthony (The University of Notre Dame Australia); Michelle Spratling (Curtin University - Perth)
A mixed-method study grounded in the integrated stress-resource-coping framework utilised a quantitative sample of 432 respondents and 31 semi-structured, in-depth interviews with Australian teachers to examine their experiences during the 2020–2021 global COVID-19 pandemic. The combined quantitative and qualitative findings revealed clear patterns of whereby resource deficits increased compensatory effort, intensified workload, and eroded boundaries between work and non-work time, thereby amplifying job frustration and undermining opportunities for recovery. Teachers also reported how collegial support and meaningful work buffered these processes. This study clarifies how distinct stressor domains operate and interact in crisis conditions. The findings refine understanding of challenge and hindrance stressors by demonstrating how resource-related hindrances exert indirect effects through workload escalation and boundary erosion, highlighting the central role of work-life balance in sustaining teacher well-being under prolonged strain.
