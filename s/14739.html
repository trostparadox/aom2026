Session overview

A transition to a circular economy (CE) is inherently systemic, interdisciplinary, and dynamic, spanning operations, strategy, design, policy, and social change. This complexity makes CE particularly challenging to teach, as it requires educators to integrate diverse perspectives while translating often abstract concepts into engaging and actionable learning experiences. Furthermore, CE remains a contested concept in terms of definitions, levels of analysis, and normative assumptions, which can be daunting even for experienced educators seeking to draw on research to design impactful teaching. For this reason, this PDW supports educators in teaching CE by sharing tested teaching designs, formats, and tools that enable meaningful and effective learning experiences. It brings together educators who have developed and refined innovative approaches to CE education across undergraduate, postgraduate, executive, and practitioner-oriented settings. Rather than promoting a single “best practice,” the workshop embraces plurality, emphasizing context-sensitive pedagogical design and enables discussions on burning CE topics. Participants will actively engage with a portfolio of teaching designs, try out hands-on tools and reflect collectively on how these can be adapted to different audiences and educational contexts. The PDW is highly interactive, enabling participants to deeply engage, exchange, and co-develop teaching ideas they can readily apply in their own courses and programs. The PDW is designed to complement another proposed broader sustainability-focused PDW “Teaching Sustainability for Practice Across Educational Audiences”, by offering a deep, practice-oriented focus on circular economy education.
