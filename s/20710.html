Session overview

This symposium includes four papers from research teams across seven institutions in three continents (i.e., North America, Asia, Europe). Deploying a variety of theoretical perspectives and methodologies (e.g., quantitative, qualitative), these studies aim to advance theoretical discussions on workplace humanness by focusing on the following three interconnected themes: (1) novel antecedents and consequences of (de)humanization in the changing landscape of work (e.g., digital ostracism, digital monitoring), (2) a deep examination of the rich dynamics surrounding humanness at work, including various forms of its denial (e.g., self-dehumanization, self-objectification) and distortion (e.g., self-super-humanization), and (3) extensions of the (de)humanization phenomenology to theoretically meaningful yet understudied occupational contexts (e.g., military institutions during warfare and heroic occupations such as firefighters).
