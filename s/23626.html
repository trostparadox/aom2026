Sustainable Integration of GenAI Into the Professional Practices of A Digital Services Company

MC
Victoire-Manon CANOVAS (Magellan Research Center)
This paper focuses on the conditions for the sustainable integration of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) into the professional practices of a digital services company (DSC). Generative AI is often presented as a lever for performance and innovation, but its use also raises multiple human, social, environmental, ethical, organizational, security, and economic issues. Using a research-intervention approach and a qualitative methodology based on 56 semi-structured interviews, this research aims to analyze the organizational tensions and trade-offs associated with the integration of GenAI, as well as the conditions that promote its sustainable integration. This work is part of a management science thesis, at the beginning of its second year, conducted under a CIFRE (Convention Industrielle de Formation par la Recherche) agreement, a French program funded by the Ministry of Higher Education and Research, which allows a doctoral student to conduct their work within a company while being supervised by an academic laboratory. Two exploratory theoretical frameworks are used: the theory of organizational paradoxes and socio-economic management. This doctoral paper explains the research process, methodological design, and analytical tools used to understand the organizational, managerial, and socio-economic conditions that promote the sustainable integration of GenAI into professional practices.

Diagnosing AI Transformation in Consulting Engagements: The Balanced Automation Framework

MC
Ghassan ABUKAR
Consulting engagements for AI-driven transformations frequently fail not because of technology, but because consultants lack a reliable way to diagnose and improve a client's readiness for human-AI collaboration. This paper provides that tool: the Balanced Automation Framework (BAF). The BAF is a deployable diagnostic that changes how AI engagements are run. Instead of relying on generic AI advice, consultants can use the BAF to systematically assess client capabilities, design targeted interventions for competency development, and structure a phased implementation that aligns with the client's specific context. This shifts the engagement from a technology-centric deployment to a capability-building partnership, materially increasing the probability of successful client transformation.

Beyond Usefulness: Organizational Readiness and GenAI Adoption Under Professional Risk In Consulting

MC
REBECCA POWALS (Rutgers Business School); Jonathan H. Ng (Rutgers Business School); Zeki Pagda
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly visible in consulting work, yet its adoption remains uneven in client-facing contexts where professional judgment, accountability, and reputational risk are salient. While consultants often recognize the potential usefulness of GenAI tools, decisions to use them are shaped by whether organizational conditions legitimize use and reduce perceived professional risk. This study argues that adoption in consulting contexts is not driven by perceived usefulness alone, but by organizational readiness that authorizes responsible experimentation. This study reconceptualizes Facilitating Conditions as an organizational readiness mechanism that legitimizes GenAI use and reduces perceived professional risk. Using a mixed-methods design, the study analyzes survey data from consulting professionals (n = 119) in varied roles/firms, using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM), complemented by qualitative interviews (n=12). Quantitative results indicate that Facilitating Conditions are the only predictor that retains explanatory power for behavioral intention once shared variance among adoption constructs is considered, while performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence exhibit attenuated effects. Qualitative findings explain this pattern by showing how organizational policies, leadership modeling, governance clarity, and psychological safety shape individual readiness to engage with GenAI. The study makes three theoretical contributions. First, it identifies professional-risk contexts as a boundary condition for expectancy-based adoption models. Second, it reconceptualizes facilitating conditions as a multidimensional organizational readiness signal rather than a background enabler. Third, it demonstrates how organizational readiness mediates the translation of individual attitudes into adoption intentions. Together, the findings advance technology adoption theory by highlighting how organizational structures condition individual decision-making when emerging technologies introduce accountability and legitimacy concerns.

Management Consulting and AI: Institutional Logics and Professional Reconfiguration

MC
Shola Ajiboye (Case Western Reserve University); Nianwei Yin (Nanjing University); Jemima A. Frimpong; Henrietta Ngozi Onwuegbuzie; Amos Allie (Case Western Reserve University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming management consulting by reshaping how consultants generate value, engage clients, and establish professional legitimacy across diverse institutional environments. While prior research often frames AI adoption in consulting as a technologically driven or market-led phenomenon, less attention has been paid to how regional governance regimes, institutional logics, and development trajectories shape AI-enabled consulting practices. Drawing on a comparative qualitative study of 21 management consultants across Africa, Europe, North America, and South America, complemented by institutional analysis of China and the United Arab Emirates, this paper develops a typology of three AI-enabled consulting models: market-driven augmentation, state-aligned co-creation, and leapfrogging hybridization. The findings demonstrate that AI does not transform consulting uniformly through efficiency gains alone. Instead, AI is institutionalized through region-specific governance regimes, market structures, and professional norms, reconfiguring consultants’ roles toward institutional translation, governance mediation, and ethical stewardship.

“Buried” in Change: Leadership and Employee Reactions to GIS Industry 4.0 Implementation

MC
Femke Morsink; Rashimah Rajah (University of Twente); Pauline Weritz
Although Industry 4.0 technologies promise significant benefits, many organizations struggle with their successful implementation. In addition to technical challenges, social aspects such as employee reactions and leadership play an important role in this process. This research focuses on the introduction of a Geographic Information System (GIS) within the underground infrastructure sector, a context in which digitalization is crucial but has received little scientific attention. Using a qualitative single case study based on seventeen interviews with managers and employees, the research examines how employees respond cognitively, affectively, technologically, and behaviorally to GIS implementation. These responses are shaped by leadership behaviors, supportive organizational and team conditions, and individual characteristics. The analysis identifies interrelated dimensions that together form a socio-technical framework explaining how appraisals, emotions, and behaviors evolve during technological change. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.
