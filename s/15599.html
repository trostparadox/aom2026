How and When does Perceived Green Requirement Shape Employee Green Behavior? (WITHDRAWN) WITHDRAWN

Authors: Jie Tang University of Leeds; Kerrie Unsworth University of Leeds; Sally V. Russell University of Leeds; Rebacca Pieniazek University of Leeds; Anna Viragos University of Leeds

Session overview

Despite the documented importance of organizational green requirement, little is known about how employees perceive green requirement and how such perceptions translate into varying levels of employee green behavior. Drawing on goal hierarchy theory, this study explores how and when perceived green requirement shapes green behavior using a three-wave time-lagged survey design. Results reveal the double-edged effect of green requirement perception on employee green behavior. On the one hand, perceiving stronger green requirement is positively associated with employee green behavior, particularly among employees with low personal green goal. On the other hand, green requirement perception also elicits green ambivalence among employees with low personal green goal, whereby they simultaneously hold positive and negative evaluations toward engaging in green behavior, which in turn undermines their green behavior. These findings advance research on employee green behavior by shifting attention from organizational environmental management to individual-level perceptions and psychological processes. From a practical perspective, organizations can design and frame green requirement in a goal-concordant approach. Doing so not only enables to leverage explicit environmental communications to make green requirement and expectation salient, but also proactively minimizes potential negative evaluations, particularly among employees with low personal green goal.
