Session overview

This Professional Developement Worskshop, which deals with “combining research and management consulting,” explores the worlds of academic research and management consulting around the roles of the researcher and the management consultant. Consulting and research are more often dissociated, with the consulting world that is considered as a field responding to managerial issues from organizations and the research that is aiming to create and disseminate knowledge. Some approaches, such as qualimetric intervention research (Savall and Zardet, 1987, 2021), propose the production of scientific knowledge based on results derived directly from interventions carried out in organizations, through the role of the intervener-researcher. This PDW aims to provide a space for collective reflection among consultants, Phd students, intervener-researchers, researchers, and practitioner-researchers in order to share their experiences and their practices for combining these two worlds in a sustainable manner. Participants will have the opportunity to discuss topics related to challenges such as time management, adapting language between the academic and professional worlds, addressing ethical issues, and more. The PDW is based on testimonials from four different experiences of internal consultants, external consultants, practitioner-researchers, and intervener-researchers. These experiences combine management consulting, professional practice, and academic research. Through plenary sessions and roundtable discussions, participants will identify the main challenges associated with reconciling these two positions in a sustainable manner. The PDW aims to offer to the community of consultants, researchers and practicioner-researchers practices that are more reflective and field-based, in order to support the challenges faced by companies while contributing to academic research in management.
