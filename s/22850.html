Empowering vs. Directive CEO Leadership and Innovation Performance in High-Tech Firms

OB
Jiayi Du (Fudan University); Jih-Yu Mao (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Zhang Yue; Zirui Zhou (Fudan University); lifan Chen (Renmin University of China); Mei Song (East China Normal University)
Previous research has suggested that empowering rather than directive chief executive officers (CEOs) promote firm innovation performance. However, we challenge this prevailing conclusion by shedding a positive light on CEO directive leadership in evoking the innovation performance of high-tech ventures. Specifically, we propose that the effects of CEO directive leadership and empowering leadership hinge on task interdependence. Using a field study with a sample of 83 CEOs and their direct top management team (TMT) members from high-tech companies, our results reveal that when TMT task interdependence is low, CEO directive leadership is positively associated with TMT decision-making speed, which in turn increases high-tech ventures’ innovation performance. In contrast, when TMT task interdependence is high, CEO empowering leadership is positively associated with TMT decision-making speed and subsequent high-tech ventures’ innovation performance. Our findings offer meaningful implications for theory and practice.

Health-Oriented Leadership and Innovation: The Roles of Trust and Role Breadth Self-Efficacy

OB
Shenghui Wang (Dongbei University of Finance and Economics); Ye Li (University of Aberdeen); Ming Yi (Sichuan University)
Although emerging research has demonstrated the advantages of health-oriented leadership on employee health outcomes, less research has explored whether the benefits of health-oriented leadership could extend beyond the health domain into the healthy survival and development of organizations. This research began with the question of whether and how health-oriented leadership can positively impact employees' innovative behavior. It investigated the underlying mechanisms and boundary conditions of this relationship through the lens of social information processing theory. Employing a mixed-methods approach that combines a field study with three experimental studies across the Eastern and Western sample to boost generalization, we found that health-oriented leadership, on the one hand, provides the interpersonal benefits of developing employees' trust in their supervisor, and on the other hand, offers the intrapersonal benefits of enhancing role breadth self-efficacy (RBSE). These favorable evaluations subsequently bolster employees' innovative behavior. Furthermore, employees' attachment avoidance positively moderates the positive indirect relationship between health-oriented leadership and employees' innovative behavior through trust in supervisor; employees’ attachment anxiety positively moderates the positive indirect relationship between health-oriented leadership and employees' innovative behavior through employees' RBSE. Theoretical and practical implications regarding health-oriented leadership and employees’ insecure attachment are discussed to inspire future research.

Trickle-Down Effects of Leader Digital Openness on Employee Innovative Performance

OB
Qinxuan Gu (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); yiyun zhang (Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
Drawing on social learning theory, we propose a moderated chain mediation model to explore how leader digital openness influences employee innovative performance. Using the data collected from 303 leader-employee dyads, our findings reveal a positive indirect relationship between leader digital openness and employee innovative performance through a sequential learning mechanism. Specifically, leader digital openness enhances employee innovative performance by fostering employee digital openness first, which in turn facilitates digital skill. Moreover, we also find that leader digital interaction serves as a key contextual enhancer. It not only reinforces the direct positive link between leader digital openness and employee digital openness, but also amplifies the sequential mediating effect of leader digital openness on employee innovative performance through the chain of employee digital openness and digital skill. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed accordingly.

Growth Need Strength and Creativity: The Role of Work Reflection and Visionary Leadership

OB
Yonghua Liu (Zhijiang College of Zhejiang University of Technology); Jingru Huang (Zhejiang Financial College)
Employee creativity is crucial for organizations to maintain competitiveness. Although existing research has explored the relationship between employees' growth need strength and creativity, the underlying connection between the two remains unclear. Based on trait activation theory, this study examines how visionary leadership, as an environmental cue, activates employees' growth need strength, thereby promoting work reflection and creativity. Data were collected from 516 full-time employees across 111 teams using a three-phase, time-lagged design. Multilevel path analysis and Monte Carlo simulations were used to test the hypotheses. The results showed that Employees' growth need strength is positively related to creativity. Work reflection mediates the relationship between growth need strength and creativity. Visionary leadership strengthens the positive relationship between employees' growth need strength and work reflection. Visionary leadership also moderates the mediating role of work reflection between employees' growth need strength and creativity.
