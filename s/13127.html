The Eternal Organization: Decoding Benedictine Resilience from a Dynamic Capabilities Perspective

Author: Carolina von Ritter HHL Leipzig Graduate School of Management

Session overview

Benedictine monasteries exemplify a unique and enduring organizational form that has maintained operational resilience for over 1,500 years. Throughout their history, these communities have successfully countered existential threats, including physical attacks, economic turmoil, and legal and reputational insecurities. While contemporary management often struggles to reconcile short-term survival with long-term sustainability amid geopolitical and technological volatility, the Benedictine model offers an alternative logic of endurance. Adopting a dynamic capabilities perspective, this study investigates how monastic governance builds and sustains an organization’s resilience. Our findings reveal a historically embedded repertoire of strategies that combine institutionalized sensing through ethical vigilance, resource-based seizing via operational slack, and tradition-anchored transformation through disciplined flexibility. We demonstrate that monastic longevity results from disciplined flexibility, where institutional stability enables adaptive change under extreme disruption. This challenges resource-dependent views of resilience by showing how codified governance and ritualized routines foster dynamic capabilities over the course of centuries. In practice, we provide a blueprint for value-anchored leadership, demonstrating that a stable moral-institutional framework is the necessary foundation for organizations to thrive trans-generationally.
