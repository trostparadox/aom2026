From Ashtrays to Acquisitions: The Strategic Ripple of Smoking Bans

STR
You Zhang (The University of Queensland); Hong Zhu (Peking University); Qi Zhu (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University); Wenbin Hu (Queensland University)
Acquisition research faces a central paradox: although acquirors gain formal control over target firms, employees often fail to deploy, transfer, or renew their human capital as expected. Prior work has emphasized legal, structural, and incentive mechanisms but has largely overlooked employees—the true carriers of human capital—whose characteristics shape its mobilization. We address this gap by advancing an employee-centric perspective that identifies employee health as a foundational condition for resolving the paradox of unrealized human capital in acquisitions. Healthier employees possess greater cognitive, cooperative, and emotional capacities to sustain and regenerate human capital during post-acquisition integration, thereby enhancing target attractiveness. Exploiting the staggered adoption of U.S. state smoking bans as an exogenous shock, we find that firms in smoke-free states are more likely to be acquired— particularly those with complex, large-scale human capital and greater structural complexity. These findings provide strong support for an employee-centric theory of acquisition.
