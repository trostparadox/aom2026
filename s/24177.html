The Social Side of Teamwork: Informal Socialization and Psychological Change Experiences

MOC
Sana Mumtaz (University of Niagara Falls, Canada)
While workplace socialization has been associated with positive outcomes in the organizational context, the distinctive role of ‘informal socialization’ has received limited attention, particularly in the teamwork dynamics. Given the challenging and distinct roles of multi-professional teams in organizations especially after the Covid period, this theoretical article has integrated the social identity theory for conceptualizing how informal socialization in such teams leads to socio-psychological changes in members. It has been suggested that informal socialization in multi-professional teams is likely to lead to status-based sub-groups when psychological safety is weak. Further, status based sub-groupings encourage the unequal workload distribution in these teams such that low-status employees are likely to get excessive workload in comparison to high-status members. To provide a holistic framework about proposed change experiences of multi-professional teams, two perspectives have been presented in understanding the long-term psychological change experiences of these employees in a model. It is argued that members having a weak social identification are likely to experience consistent stress and eventual cynicism because of misalignment between their personal identity and that of group dynamics. However, members having social identification within their teams are likely to prioritize their professional identity and intergroup relationships, thus becoming happy workaholics with time.
