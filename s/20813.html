Validating Sustainable Career Competencies in Late-Career Employees in Japan

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11

Authors: Kunio Ikari Kyushu University; Kyoko Yamazaki Meiji University

Session overview

As workforce aging accelerates, sustaining motivation, meaning, and perceived success among late-career employees has become a critical organizational challenge. Although prior research has proposed various frameworks to conceptualize sustainable careers, existing measures have largely been developed and validated among early- and mid-career populations. Consequently, less is known about how career-related competencies operate in late-career contexts, particularly under traditional employment systems such as those found in Japan. Addressing this gap, the present study examines the criterion-related validity of a newly developed measure of Sustainable Career Competencies for employees in their late 50s. The scale is designed to capture late-career– specific competencies relevant to sustaining engagement and meaningful career functioning. Using survey data from 567 Japanese employees in their late 50s, we test a mediation model in which work engagement functions as a motivational mechanism linking sustainable career competencies to key late-career outcomes. Structural equation modeling revealed that adaptive career agency and career social resources were positively associated with work engagement, whereas passive career orientation was negatively associated with work engagement. Work engagement, in turn, was positively related to career success, meaning of work, and future work self. Bootstrapped mediation analyses indicated significant indirect effects for all competency dimensions. These findings provide evidence for the criterion-related validity of a late-career–specific sustainable career competency framework and highlight the motivational dynamics underlying career sustainability in later career stages.
