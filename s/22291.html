I Value That: The Impact of Value Alignment in Recruitment Sources on Applicant Reactions

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
DEI

People

Authors: Henri Maindidze Portland State University; Jason G Randall Portland State University; Caraline Malloy Baruch College, CUNY; Jennifer Walavalkar Portland State University

Session overview

Drawing on signaling theory, we examined whether perceptions of personal and organizational value alignment via online recruitment materials influence applicant attitudes and intentions, with PO fit as the mediating explanatory mechanism. Specifically, an experimental vignette design was employed to examine the comparative impact that value alignment with content from organization-dependent (e.g., organizational websites) and organization-independent (e.g., online employer reviews) sources has on organizational attraction and job pursuit intentions. A sample of job seekers recruited from career fairs viewed simulated online employer review sites and company websites with content manipulated to represent high and low levels of DEI valuation. Data collection is ongoing and nearing completion (current N = 209; target N = 240), and anticipated results will reveal the impact of value alignment on applicant reactions, suggesting that applicants interpret signals about organizational values in online recruitment materials, with potential ramifications for applicant reactions and for organizational approaches to conveying their values to both recruiting new employees and to their current employees.
