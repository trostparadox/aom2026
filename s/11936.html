Session overview

Mentor coaching is often perceived narrowly as a credentialing requirement for certification. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) reframes it as a strategic process, far beyond credentialing, which strengthens leadership pipelines, accelerates succession planning, and fosters resilient cultures. As Grover and Furnham (2016) emphasize, coaching as a developmental intervention in organizations has consistently demonstrated improvements in both individual effectiveness and organizational performance. Participants will explore core areas of mentor coaching from agreement setting to client growth facilitation – through case studies, experiential exercises, and peer-to-peer simulations with a focus on effects and outcomes. This exploration highlights managerial coaching as an innovative practice that embeds learning into daily work, producing immediate performance improvements (Ellinger, Beattie, and Hamlin, 2018). The PDW emphasizes identity exploration and layered feedback as tools for adaptive leadership, while also addressing common pitfalls. We submit that this approach builds psychological capital (hope, efficacy, resilience, and optimism) directly tied to sustainable outcomes (Nicolau et al., 2023). The PDW further emphasizes peer coaching’s role in fostering collaborative networks (Milne et al., 2022) and reinforces Wang et al.’s (2022) findings that the coaching process stimulated individuals to promote work satisfaction and facilitate sustainable changes. This session equips consultants, coaches, executives, and scholars with tools and evidence-based frameworks to embed mentor coaching into organizational systems, ensuring measurable return on investment in engagement, retention, innovation, and leadership readiness.
