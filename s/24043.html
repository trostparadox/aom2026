Institutional Pressures, Organizational Capital, and Sustainability Reporting Readiness in SMEs

SIM
Issam Benhayoun (Moulay Ismail University)
This study interrogates the mechanisms through which small and medium-sized enterprises cultivate readiness and disposition toward adopting the International Sustainability Standards Board framework, interrogating the viability of globally harmonised sustainability reporting architectures within resource-constrained organisational milieus. Synthesising institutional theory with Bourdieu's theory of practice, the research articulates a hierarchical component model wherein institutional field pressures and professional habitus operate upon ISSB adoption trajectories through the mediating conduit of organisational capital accumulation. Empirical evidence, derived from 110 accounting professionals embedded within Moroccan SME contexts and subjected to partial least squares structural equation modelling, reveals that institutional pressures and habitus exert no direct influence upon adoption readiness. Rather, their effects are entirely mediated by organisational capital, which demonstrates pronounced influence upon practical readiness—itself the paramount determinant of dispositional orientation toward ISSB reporting praxis. These findings bear critical policy implications, underscoring the imperative for capacity-building interventions over regulatory impositions in fostering meaningful SME engagement with sustainability standards. Moreover, the study illuminates how global frameworks may inadvertently perpetuate structural inequalities between large corporations and SMEs absent deliberate attention to organisational capacity deficits. By foregrounding a Bourdieusian analytical lens, this research contributes novel theoretical sophistication to sustainability reporting scholarship, elucidating the contextually-embedded dynamics that generate heterogeneous adoption patterns across emerging economies.
