Knowledge Hiding as a Strategic Behavior: Machiavellianism, Embeddedness, and Frustration

OB
Hai Viet Nguyen; Hao-Chieh Lin (National Sun Yat-Sen University); Hai-Trung Nguyen
This study examines how Machiavellianism shapes employees’ contextual responses to organizational conditions by investigating its interaction with job embeddedness and organizational frustration in predicting distinct forms of knowledge hiding. Drawing on conservation of resources theory, we argue that Machiavellian employees strategically adjust their hiding behaviors in response to resource constraints. Survey data from 492 working employees in Taiwan show that although Machiavellianism is generally associated with knowledge hiding, only evasive hiding, rather than playing dumb or rationalized hiding, emerges under conditions of high organizational frustration and high job embeddedness. This pattern reflects a context-sensitive, self-protective response in which individuals safeguard strategic interests while managing reputational concerns. By distinguishing among knowledge-hiding strategies, this study advances research on knowledge hiding, job embeddedness, and the contextual expression of Machiavellianism.
