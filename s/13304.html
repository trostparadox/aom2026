Session overview

This study critically examines undergraduate students’ perceptions and usage of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) tools, such as ChatGPT, within higher education, drawing on a survey of 551 first-year Curtin University business students across multiple campuses based in Australia, Singapore and Malaysia. The findings reveal near-universal exposure to GenAI, with over 90% of students having used such tools, though most do so infrequently. Students primarily leverage GenAI for learning support and academic writing, perceiving notable benefits in efficiency and access to alternative explanations. However, the integration of GenAI is characterized by ambivalence: while 83% believe these tools can enhance learning, students express significant ethical concerns, particularly regarding academic integrity, plagiarism, and the reliability of AI-generated content. The study identifies a pronounced demand for AI literacy, with students calling for explicit instruction in prompt engineering, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and ethical usage. Confidence in using GenAI is moderate, correlating with frequency of use, and students are acutely aware of AI’s limitations, including bias and the risk of over-reliance. The research highlights the need for universities to develop clear policies and curricular interventions that foster responsible, critical, and equitable engagement with GenAI. By foregrounding students’ voices, this study contributes to ongoing debates about the pedagogical, ethical, and institutional challenges posed by AI in management education, and underscores the imperative for collaborative policy-making and the integration of AI literacy as a core component of contemporary higher education.
