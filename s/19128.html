Anthropomorphic AI in Sustainability Communication and Stakeholder Silence

Authors: Parul Nalwa IILM UNIVERSITY, GURUGRAM; Dipanwita Chakraborty Indian Academy of Management (INDAM)

Session overview

As organizations increasingly deploy AI-enabled systems to communicate their sustainability commitments, there is growing concern that these technologies, despite their promise of transparency, may inadvertently suppress stakeholder voice and delay accountability. This study examines how anthropomorphic AI communication systems that present claims using human-like language, relational signals (e.g., “we”), and moral appeals can unintentionally contribute to stakeholder silence during early stages of harm recognition. Drawing from research on anthropomorphism (Epley et al., 2007; Castelo et al., 2019), organizational wrongdoing (Detert & Edmondson, 2011), and algorithmic governance (Ananny & Crawford, 2018), the study proposes the novel concept of “response paralysis”: a process in which humanized AI messaging deters questioning, escalation, and collective action before controversy reaches public visibility. Using a qualitative, theory-building design, this research analyzes multi-source data from organizational cases involving AI-generated sustainability communications. These include public reports, disclosures, stakeholder interviews, and legal investigations. Through inductive coding, it identifies a process model wherein anthropomorphic communication leads to perceived authority and response paralysis causes delayed publicization. The study contributes to organizational theory by reconceptualizing anthropomorphism not merely as a persuasion tool but as a structural force shaping stakeholder sensemaking. It also deepens understanding of how communicative affects governance outcomes. Practically, the findings inform design strategies for organizations and regulators seeking to ensure that AI-enabled sustainability communication promotes accountability, not deference.
