Walking the Fine Line: Agentic Cues and Crowdfunding Performance in Social Investment

Author: Jinnan Hou The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

This study investigates how entrepreneurs’ agentic cues influence crowdfunding performance in social investment contexts, and how gender shapes these effects. While agency is often seen as inherently positive, emerging evidence suggests that different forms of agency can produce divergent outcomes in prosocial settings. We distinguish between agentic assertiveness, which signals dominance and self-promotion, and agentic competence, which reflects skill and task-focused engagement. Using institutional theory, we argue that social investors evaluate entrepreneurs not only on their ability to execute projects but also on alignment with communal norms and ethical expectations. Agentic assertiveness may violate these norms, reducing legitimacy and funding success, whereas agentic competence aligns with institutional expectations, enhancing perceived reliability and performance. Furthermore, we propose that female entrepreneurs experience a relative advantage: gender moderates these relationships by buffering the negative effects of assertiveness and amplifying the positive effects of competence.
