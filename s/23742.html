A Multilevel Analysis: Digital Infrastructure Adoption, Digital Natives, and New Venture Creation

ENT
Kai Cher Tay (University of Louisville); Daniel L. Bennett (University of Louisville)
The prevailing conceptualization of digitalization as a linear external enabler of entrepreneurship offers an incomplete account of its influence. Challenging this view, we investigate the conditions under which macro-level digital infrastructure adoption (DIA) influences an individual's propensity for new venture creation. We develop and test a multilevel framework that integrates Network Externality Theory with the External Enabler/Disabler (EED) framework. We hypothesize that the relationship between country-level DIA and individual’s propensity of new venture creation is curvilinear. At low levels, negative network externalities trigger disabling mechanisms (exposing and demand erosion), while surpassing a critical mass activates enabling mechanisms (demand creation, resource access, and conservation). Furthermore, drawing on Generational Cohort Theory, we find that digital natives are less hindered by the initial disabling phase and better positioned to capitalize on the subsequent enabling phase. Using a multilevel dataset of 646,529 individuals across 90 countries, our results support our hypotheses.
