Session overview

In this symposium, we offer two broad approaches to moving the organizational justice literature forward without being redundant. Given established organizational justice relationships, the authors in this symposium strive to clarify how and why these relationships play out. While many studies have already begun to explore potential mechanisms, the scope of available data combined with modern methods allow for direct analytic comparisons of competing conceptual explanations, and there are likely many meaningful mechanisms for organizational justice relationships which have yet to be discovered. Additionally, the authors propose continuing to expand the organizational justice literature by examining justice in modern contexts. Given the highly influential role that organizational justice plays in the lives of employees, the authors of this symposium strive to maintain an up-to-date understanding of how organizational justice unfolds in new and evolving contexts. The specific subjects of presentations include comparing mechanisms of justice-performance and justice-withdrawal relationships, exploring loneliness as a potential mediator of justice-well-being and justice-knowledge-sharing relationships, investigating how justice perceptions are driven by features of virtual employment platforms, and determining the role of technology-based compensation systems and pay communication on perceptions of pay equity.
