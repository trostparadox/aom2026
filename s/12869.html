Session overview

This PDW convenes scholars from SIM and related AOM divisions to build a research community on the societal implications of the circular economy (CE). CE is often framed as an alternative to linear production and consumption through reducing waste and pollution, enabling reuse, and supporting regeneration. Although CE research has grown rapidly, scholarship that directly examines social outcomes and tensions—including labor conditions, inclusion/exclusion, community wellbeing, and who benefits or loses from “circular” value creation—remains underdeveloped. Our goal is to (1) synthesize core themes and emerging debates on the social implications of organizing for circularity and the idea of a “circular society,” drawing on ongoing empirical and review work; and (2) catalyze collaboration by collectively identifying priority research gaps and shared challenges. The PDW is structured so that participants will join facilitated roundtables focused on the most frequently identified gaps, especially the challenge of engaging societal actors in ways that are reciprocal and sustainable followed by a plenary reflection and Q&A. The PDW will produce (as outcomes) (i) a shared agenda of high-priority research questions and collaboration opportunities and (ii) a post-PDW synthesis circulated to participants (and potentially the wider SIM/AOM community), with the aim of developing it into a management-journal publication.
