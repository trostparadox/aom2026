Assembling Rehabilitation: An Actor-Network Analysis

Authors: Babeecha Devi Keisham Indian Institute of Management Bodh Gaya; Tarun Kumar Vashisth Indian Institute of Management Bodh Gaya

Session overview

Persons with Disabilities face numerous challenges in gaining employment and leading a productive life. We examine the rehabilitation and social integration of visually impaired individuals in a closely knit community setting in an India NGO. Drawing on a qualitative case study of rehabilitation from an Actor-Network Theory lens, the analysis conceptualizes inclusion as an effect of heterogeneous networks composed of human and non-human actors. These include NGO leadership, visually impaired beneficiaries, employers, labor markets, housing infrastructure, transport systems, and funding arrangements. The study traces how processes of translation align these actors. Findings show that disability actively shapes enrolment, authority, and coordination rather than operating solely as a condition of exclusion. Wage differentials and informal labor relations are shown to be integral to network functioning rather than external deviations. By foregrounding the fragility and reversibility of these alignments, the paper advances an ANT-informed understanding of rehabilitation and inclusion as contingent, continuously assembled network effects.
