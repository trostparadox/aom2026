Session overview

Despite more than two millennium since Heraclitus coined the phrase “the only constant in life is change”, large-scale organisational change remains a challenge beset by low success rates, high rates of employee stress, and reduced wellbeing. A significant contributor to this challenge is the persistent gap between the richness of contemporary organizational change scholarship and the methodologies commonly practiced in organizations. While many organizations frequently rely on traditional, linear methodologies of change (e.g., Kotter’s eight-steps), research over the past two decades has advanced more dynamic, participatory, and complexity-attuned approaches that are better suited to enabling large-scale and complex change. Universities play a major role in shaping knowledge, yet many change curriculum continue to emphasise a narrow set of traditional change methodologies–reinforcing the very approaches that scholarship has moved beyond. This symposium follows the premise that bridging this scholar–practitioner divide requires first addressing the scholar–educator divide, meaning that how change is taught fundamentally shapes how change is practiced. This session brings together international experts in organizational change to share their insights on contemporary change methodologies and demonstrate how they can be effectively taught to practitioners. Participants will develop an expanded repertoire of evidence-based change methodologies for teaching and practice and leave better equipped to align pedagogy with the realities of leading change in modern-day organisations.
