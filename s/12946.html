Session overview

Startups are critical drivers of innovation and economic growth. Yet, their impact depends not only on founders but also on the employees who choose to join them. These “joiners” provide essential human capital that enables startups to develop new technologies, build organizations, and scale their operations. A growing body of research has begun to examine who joins startups and why, highlighting that startups differ systematically from established firms as workplaces and that startup employees differ from other employees in terms of skills, motives, and career trajectories. Despite recent advances in our understanding of startup employees, this emerging literature is developing across multiple disciplinary traditions and levels of analysis, risking fragmentation and missed opportunities for cumulative progress. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) takes stock of recent research on joiners and startup employment and aims to shape a forward-looking research agenda. It brings together established scholars and junior researchers from different disciplinary backgrounds to synthesize existing findings, clarify core conceptual tensions, and identify promising directions for future work. We will also pay attention to how employment in innovation-based startups is evolving in response to technological change, including AI and new forms of remote and flexible work, and we will discuss emerging opportunities for empirical work, such as matched employer–employee data, experiments, and digital trace data. The PDW combines expert talks, a cross-disciplinary panel, and interactive roundtables to foster critical discussion, idea generation, and community building. Participants will leave with a clearer understanding of the state of the field, concrete research ideas, and connections to a growing community focused on joiners and employment in innovation-based startups.
