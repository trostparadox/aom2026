An Activity Theory Explanation of Contradictions in Lean-Digital Integration

TIM
Isuru Gamage (University of Peradeniya); Mohsin Malik (Swinburne University of Technology); Amir Andargoli (Swinburne University of Technology); Roberto Chavez Clavijo (Swinburne University of Technology)
Digital transformation initiatives frequently encounter resistance when integrating advanced technologies into Lean-based operational environments, yet information systems (IS) research lacks robust explanations of why and how these socio-technical tensions systematically emerge. Drawing on Activity Theory through a critical realist lens, this study examines contradictions arising when digital technologies are embedded within Lean work systems across twelve organizational cases. Our findings reveal four interrelated generative mechanisms that produce multi-level contradictions: misaligned representations create primary contradictions within activity system elements; erosion of human agency generates secondary contradictions between two different elements i.e., rules vs. division of labour, and tool vs community; strategic logic mismatches produce tertiary contradictions between legacy Lean and transformative digital objectives; and institutional impositions spark quaternary contradictions at organizational boundaries. By theorizing contradictions as structural manifestations of deep-seated socio-technical mechanisms rather than temporary implementation challenges, this study advances IS scholarship on digital transformation as an inherently contested, dynamically evolving process. Our mechanism-based explanation provides theoretical foundations for understanding how algorithmic control reconfigures human-centred improvement practices, offering practitioners actionable insights for managing Lean-digital integration tensions. We contribute to IS literature by demonstrating how Activity Theory, combined with critical realism, illuminates the causal architecture underlying socio-technical change in digitally mediated operational contexts. Keywords: Digital transformation; Activity Theory; Critical realism; Lean production; Contradictions; Socio-technical system
