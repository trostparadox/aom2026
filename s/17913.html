Re-Engaging Inactive Ventures: How Institutional Messaging Shapes Formalization Re-Entry

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Chioma Okpalaeke Oklahoma State University; Matthew W. Rutherford Oklahoma State University; Jorge Arteaga-Fonseca Mississippi State University; Curt Moore Oklahoma State University

Session overview

Governments increasingly rely on communication-based formalization interventions such as reminders, incentives, and enforcement notices, to encourage entrepreneurs’ compliance with regulatory requirements. Yet these interventions often misfire, particularly among administratively inactive ventures?firms with prior experience in the formal system that disengaged from compliance requirements. Existing research largely treats entrepreneurs as first-time entrants to formalization, offering limited explanation of how previous institutional experience shapes how inactive firms interpret and respond to re-engagement efforts. We address this gap by examining how institutional messaging shapes formalization re-entry decisions among inactive ventures. Drawing on regulatory focus theory, we conceptualize institutional messages as motivational framing devices that function as interpretive signals rather than compliance prompts. Importantly, we argue that motivational responses alone do not translate into re-entry intentions, motivational responses translate into re-entry intentions only when compliance is perceived as feasible and institutional signals are credible. Using a mixed-method design combining exploratory interviews and a survey experiment with administratively inactive ventures in Nigeria, we develop and test a theory explaining why communication-based formalization interventions systematically misfire in re-entry contexts.
