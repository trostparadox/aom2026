Leading Neurovariant Professionals: Merging Empathetic and Transactional Leadership Styles

Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Congress B

Participant: Eleanor J. Thompson Marymount University

Session overview

The neurodivergent (or 'neurovariant') population, which may account for up to 20 percent of the world (Etra, 2023), suffers from unemployment rates as high as 90 percent by some estimates Ovaska-Few (2018); even when gainfully employed, many neurovariant professionals find their strengths and talents are not fully leveraged by their organizations. Much popular, and some scholarly, literature exists on subjects of workplace accommodations, hiring practices, and job roles to support neurovariant workers, but very little research is available on management approaches and leadership styles best suited to leading neurologically diverse teams. Management of neurovariant professionals is not a "one size fits all" proposition. A prior study concluded that transformational leadership methods exacerbated anxiety in autistic workers, but scant literature exists on leadership approaches that have been proven to be effective with professionals diagnosed with autism. This study investigates management and leadership styles that take a direct and individualized personal perspective, such as transactional and empathetic leadership, to determine if they are preferred by autistic professionals. Using grounded theory methodology and phenomenological exploration of lived experiences, this qualitative study employed open-ended questions in written questionnaires and/or interviews with working autistic adults to determine if a hybrid leadership approach that is both empathic and transactional meets their needs to elicit their best job performances, thus benefitting the autistic individuals, their employers, and their organizations.
