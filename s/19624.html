Session overview

In today’s uncertain business environment, strategists and entrepreneurs can achieve competitive advantage by acting like scientists, generating and testing hypotheses about new opportunities. The Theory-Based View (TBV) formalizes this approach, conceptualizing such actors as theorists who develop, test, and refine theories to guide strategic choices under uncertainty. This perspective has gained traction in strategic management (STR) and entrepreneurship (ENT) research, linking to topics such as strategic cognition, learning, imagination, experimentation, and value creation. By encouraging development of unique theories of value creation, TBV highlights the active role of strategists and entrepreneurs in shaping novel opportunities. This PDW will deepen participants’ understanding of TBV and its implications for strategy and innovation. It will examine how to evaluate the quality of strategic theories and how decision-makers gauge confidence in those theories. The workshop will also address theory performativity (how acting "as if" a theory is true renderis it true), the organizational and social dimensions of theory development, and TBV’s boundary conditions. The 90-minute session includes a brief introduction, expert panel presentations, interactive roundtable discussions with participants on key TBV topics (e.g., theory evaluation, performativity, organizational implications), a joint discussion and Q&A, and closing remarks. The panel features leading scholars such as Jay Barney, Todd Zenger, Russ McBride, Rebecca Karp, and David Reetz, with Anastasia Sergeeva as organizer. Overall, this PDW offers an interactive forum to advance TBV research and practice, bridging strategy and entrepreneurship perspectives. No pre-registration is required.
