Analyzing Individuals’ Perceptions and Interpretations of Corporate Social Responsibility

Session overview

The perceptions and interpretations on corporates social responsibility are not the same among individuals. The effects of various factors such as gender, educational background, cultural differences, and employment status can play a crucial role in shaping an individual’s mindset regarding their perceptions and interpretations of corporate social responsibility. In fact, by having a clear perspective on how individuals perceive and interpret corporate social responsibility, corporate entities can better identify, select, and execute their corporate social responsibility initiatives. The purpose of this research is to explore the relationship between individuals’ interpretations and perceptions on corporate social responsibility based on three perspectives: corporate ethical responsibility, corporate philanthropic responsibility, and corporate economic responsibility. The results of this study highlight that individuals’ cultural differences, employment status, gender, and areas of study affect their mindset toward corporate ethical, economic, and philanthropic responsibilities. The findings contribute to a diagnostic and training framework across stakeholder segments.
