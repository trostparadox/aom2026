Mental Health in Organizations: A Qualitative Study

HCM
Neha Tripathi (Not Associated); Nitin Sharma (Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad); Sukanya Sangar (Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad)
This study explores how mental health is conceptualized, interpreted, and perpetually defined within organizations. Analyzing sixty-two semi-structured interviews, we develop an inductive model of mental health constructed cognitively, relationally, and institutionally by the organizational incumbents, the HR personnel in organizations. Our Inductive thematic analysis, conducted with AI-assisted coding with human oversight and lexicon-based emotion analysis (NRCLex), revealed five interrelated subdimensions: Relational, Psychological, Cognitive–Emotional Burden, Mind–Body Duality, and Identity, that build and define the mental health construct. Our analysis suggests that mental health is not depicted as a fixed psychological state but as an emergent, multi-dimensional construct that interconnects emotion, cognition, relations, and identity. Utilizing lexicon-based emotion analysis (NRCLex), we further demonstrate that HR professionals’ discourse is characterized by both positive and negative discrete emotions, including trust, joy, surprise, anticipation, fear, sadness, anger, and disgust. We build a theory elaborating a framework to describe the interpretive conduits through which mental health is understood in organizations. This study also advances health care management scholarship by showing how organizational actors construct mental health as a relational, emotional, and identity-based process, highlighting HR professionals as critical intermediaries in workplace mental-health governance.
