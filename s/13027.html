Session overview

In chemistry, amalgamation is a technique used to extract precious metals, such as gold and silver, by dissolving them in mercury, followed by a chemical process to recover the remaining value embedded in the ore (Amankwah et al,2010). Drawing on this metaphor, this workshop explores how diverse insights can be articulated without losing rigor or specificity into a predictive analytical model for mastering digital transformation projects in large French firms and public administrations. In literature, amalgamation refers to the articulation of multiple entities, systems, disciplines, or models into a coherent, unified structure. (El Haddad, 2021, 2023; Dornbos & Walshe, 2013; Dani & Pareek, 2012; Sultan et al.,2016). Adopting a conceptual approach, partial and parsimonious models are amalgamated through a quality-focused and structured process to generate strategic, managerial, and operational value in a digital environment. The purpose of this PDW is to address a central research challenge: partial models are often difficult to operationalize, and standardized frameworks prove insufficient, as shown by the high rate of unsuccessful digital transformation projects. Using a dialogical process and a qualitative methodology tailored to the research context (Aguinis, 2025), researchers will engage with scholar-practitioners and professionals to collectively assess the plausibility of amalgamating partial models derived from theories and empirical insights into a unified, holistic model of mastery for digital transformation projects. Keywords: Holistic Model, Partial Models, Project Mastery, Digital Transformation, France
