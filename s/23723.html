“Need Matters”- Study of Work-Family Guilt and Coping in Case of Low-Wage Employed Mothers

DEI
Sasmita Dash; Ashna Sehgal (XLRI School of Management)
Recently, studies on work-family guilt (WFG) in case of employed mothers have gained momentum. Prior studies have traced many negative consequences of WFG like turnover intention, counter productive work behavior, reduced job satisfaction, continuous contemplation, reduced well-being and happiness and psychological distress. However, studies on coping mechanisms for managing this guilt are still low and dominantly covered well paid mothers in developed nations. Moreover, low wage mothers in developing nations have been rarely studied. Thus, present study explores how the low wage-earning employed mothers cope with their guilt experience. This study uses both conservation of resource (COR) and stress-appraisal theory to explain the coping mechanisms. Data was collected through in-depth interviews of 35 mothers employed in fulltime low-wage jobs in different sectors in the city of Ahmedabad, Gujarat. By using thematic analysis on the interview transcriptions, we found six primary themes i.e. acceptance of guilt, highlighting life priorities, religious coping, opting for social comparison, getting listener’s comfort and finding relevance-sense of universality. These primary themes got grouped as inward and outward coping mechanisms as final themes. Managers can help their employees to understand the life priorities and focus on what they have than what they are missing. Listening techniques can be taught to the employees and conducive environment could be developed for open discussion on emotional issues. Work-family guilt of low-wage women and coping has rarely been studied in this national context.
