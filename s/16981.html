Session overview

Contemporary artificial intelligence (AI) systems are deeply entangled with data: they depend on extensive data transformations, generate new forms of data, and reshape the foundations on which they operate. Yet, research often treats data as stable inputs to AI, overlooking how data quality, provenance, uncertainty, and learning are actively produced through sociotechnical arrangements. This symposium advances discussion of the entanglement between data and AI as a core organizational concern. Across four complementary papers, the symposium investigates how data are redefined when they become training data for AI, how epistemic uncertainty is introduced upstream through data provenance practices, why AI learning loops fail to close in real-world use despite ongoing human intervention, and how data increasingly function as epistemic infrastructure that conditions organizational knowing and action. Additionally, these dynamics will be discussed at length by three discussants who bring their own insights to offer new ways of thinking about data and AI. Taken together, the symposium advances an understanding of the data-AI relationship and highlights its implications for organizing, decision-making, and knowledge production in data-intensive environments.
