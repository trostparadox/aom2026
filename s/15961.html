Session overview

Firms pursue a range of nonmarket strategies—including corporate social responsibility (CSR), corporate political activity (CPA), and corporate sociopolitical activism (CSA)—to shape their external environment and advance strategic objectives. While prior research has made substantial progress in understanding how these activities affect the focal firm’s performance and behavior, we still know relatively little about their second-order effects—that is, how a firm’s nonmarket actions influence other actors in its environment. These actors may include rival firms operating in similar products or geographic markets, as well as non-firm stakeholders such as activist organizations, policymakers, regulators, and the general public. This symposium brings together scholars to examine these indirect, sometimes unintended consequences of firms’ nonmarket engagement. The discussion will highlight both the positive and negative externalities of such strategies and consider how evolving stakeholder expectations and societal trends are reshaping the landscape of nonmarket strategy and interdependence among actors.
