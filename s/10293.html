These Soft Things That You Can Push Around Gendered Ageism in Academia

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
DEI

People

Authors: Maryam Baloch; Daniela Pianezzi University of Verona; Edileide Novais Oliveira Montclair State University

Session overview

This study examines how age and gender intersect to shape academic professionals’ experiences in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), with particular attention to gendered ageism. Drawing on Judith Butler’s work, Bodies that matter, we conceptualize aging not as a biological or chronological attribute but as a socially regulated identity shaped by institutional norms. We argue that academia continues to privilege an “ideal academic” figure, typically imagined as middle-aged, male, white, and not restricted, rendering other age–gender configurations less legitimate. Empirically, the study draws on semi-structured interviews with 25 academics at a large public university in the US, representing diverse ages, ranks, genders, and racial identities. Using thematic and critical discourse analyses, we examine how participants narrate their experiences of recognition, authority, and belonging across the academic life course. Findings reveal a persistent “never the right age” dilemma for women: younger women are frequently studentized and sexualized, while older women are perceived as marginal or lacking intellectual relevance. Across career stages, participants engage in forms of bodywork to enact academic legitimacy, with these dynamics intensified at the intersections of gender and race. The study contributes to research on ageism, gender, and academic work by extending Butler’s framework to the study of aging in organizations. In practice, the findings highlight the need for HEIs to move beyond symbolic diversity initiatives and to address age and gender coded norms that shape academic legitimacy and inclusion.
