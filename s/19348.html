Learning Pathways in Online and In-Person Management Education

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Author: Arkhyp Pavliuk University of Trieste

Session overview

This study addresses a central tension in contemporary management education: whether online and in-person training environments cultivate different forms of managerial learning, and with what consequences for subsequent innovation-oriented work. Rather than focusing on aggregate program outcomes, the study examines learning as an individual-level process and asks how delivery modality shapes managers’ progression through distinct learning mechanisms and developmental trajectories. Drawing on Experiential Learning Theory, learning is conceptualized as a dynamic cycle of experience, reflection, conceptualization, and experimentation. This framework is extended through insights from social presence, motivation, deliberate practice, and transfer of training to theorize why online and in-person formats may differentially activate these mechanisms. Empirically, the study analyzes individual-level data collected during corporate training initiatives delivered in parallel online and in-person formats. Learning processes observed during training are linked to two independent evaluations of post-training innovation projects: an academic-oriented assessment of conceptual rigor and methodological coherence, and a practice-oriented assessment of relevance, feasibility, and value creation. This design makes it possible to trace how modality-specific features-such as interaction quality, feedback density, opportunities for structured reflection, and digitally mediated engagement—shape learning pathways and translate into distinct project outcomes. The study advances a theory-driven model that explicates how delivery mode privileges different dimensions of learning, revealing potential trade-o!s between conceptual depth and practical experimentation. By reconstructing learning processes through behavioral traces, participant perceptions, and narrative accounts, the study contributes empirical and conceptual insights that inform the design of hybrid managerial education and deepen understanding of how learning environments shape individual managerial development.
