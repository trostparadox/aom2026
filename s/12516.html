Algorithmic Performance Evaluation Fairness and Transformational Leadership

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8

People

Author: Pingxuan Chen University of Leeds

Session overview

Algorithmic performance evaluation (APE) is becoming a central practice within algorithmic management as organizations use data-driven scoring and continuous monitoring to inform pay, promotion, and retention decisions. While APE is often introduced to improve decision consistency and reduce human bias, employees may experience it as opaque, difficult to contest, and low in individualized consideration—potentially undermining well-being and longer-term attachment to the organization. Drawing on organizational justice and dignity perspectives and on job demands–resources and conservation of resources theory, we propose that employees’ perceptions of APE fairness (procedural and informational justice; voice and appeal) and interpersonal dignity shape whether APE is appraised as a resourceful challenge or a resource-draining threat. Threat appraisals are expected to increase burnout and reduce affective commitment and organizational identification, whereas challenge appraisals are expected to increase work engagement and strengthen these outcomes. We further theorize that transformational leadership provides a critical social resource—sensemaking, support, and advocacy—that shifts appraisals toward challenge and buffers threat-driven resource loss. We will test the model using a three-wave field survey of employees working under APE systems, complemented by a vignette experiment manipulating APE fairness and leader communication. The study integrates justice/dignity, work design, and leadership perspectives in algorithmic management research and extends APE effects to deeper relational outcomes with practical guidance for designing and leading APE in ways that protect well-being and sustain employee–organization relationships.
