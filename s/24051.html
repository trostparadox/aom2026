Corporate Sociopolitical Activism: A Measurement Proposal

SIM
Luiza Braga; Marcelo Gattermann Perin; Matheus Tardin (FGV EAESP)
Grounded in signaling theory, this study develops and validates a multidimensional scale that measures Corporate Sociopolitical Activism (CSA) as perceived by stakeholders. The study follows established steps for scale development, including item generation, expert review, pretest, and EFA-CFA validation. Data were collected from two surveys with 800 respondents in Brazil and 600 in the United States. Results support a reflective-reflective second-order construct with four first-order dimensions (Messaging, Acting, Controversy, and Consistency), measured through 17 items. The scale was invariant across signaler types and countries, and CSA was positively associated with perceived societal impact. The scale enables cumulative tests of mechanisms and boundary conditions across actors, issues, and contexts. The study offers a concise tool to plan, benchmark, and evaluate CSA, including audits of alignment between talk and action. It supports transparent assessment of corporate stances on divisive issues and potential societal effects, and introduces the first signaling-theory-based CSA scale spanning companies, brands, and CEOs, integrating rhetoric and action with controversy and consistency.
