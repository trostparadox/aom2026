Session overview

Ten years ago, Burton et al. (2016) encouraged scholars to view entrepreneurship through a career perspective, an insight that has since become foundational for research on entrepreneurial careers. Their call emphasized the importance of understanding entrepreneurship not only as an entry decision but as a sequence of career transitions and evolving roles across the life course. Although scholars have begun addressing this call, particularly through emerging work on former entrepreneurs in paid employment, many questions remain about the nature, drivers, and consequences of these transitions. The four studies in this symposium offer new theoretical and empirical insights into diverse entrepreneurial career transitions and outline an agenda for future research. The first paper examines early developmental transitions within high-growth ventures and highlights how founders grow into entrepreneurial roles as they acquire capabilities for rapid scaling. The second investigates how personal-life shocks shape transitions out of entrepreneurship and influence post-transition performance. The third identifies distinct profiles of former entrepreneurs based on subjective career success and reveals how exit reasons and time since entrepreneurship relate to heterogeneous career experiences. The final paper maps entrepreneurial career pathways across the lifespan and suggests a more nuanced perspective on career transitions, including moves into ecosystem roles. Together, these contributions deepen the understanding of entrepreneurial careers as dynamic, multi-stage trajectories. The symposium discussant, Prof. Stanislav D. Dobrev, a multidisciplinary expert in careers, organizational theory, strategy, and entrepreneurship and a co-author of the careers perspective on entrepreneurship, will integrate these perspectives and outline promising avenues for future research.
