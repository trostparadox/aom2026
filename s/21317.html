Session overview

Organizational fields do not exist in isolation but are mutually co-constituted through inter-field exchanges, cross-fertilization, and power spillovers. Yet, despite the centrality of such cross-field processes in shaping contemporary organizational life, we lack deep theoretical understanding of how fields are structured in relation to one another and how boundaries between them operate. This PDW addresses a critical gap: while we have outlined a theory of fields, we have not systematically explored the relationship between fields: that is, how different communities interact across field boundaries, how various types of boundaries (economic, social, symbolic, professional) interrelate, and how boundaries change over time. And there is a clear reason for this. Cross-field processes present significant methodological challenges, which may explain why they remain understudied despite their importance. This workshop brings together leading scholars to examine different types of field boundaries and the dynamics that unfold at these interfaces. By focusing on a range of boundary types and dynamics – from interstitial infrastructures connecting academic and industry fields, to professional boundaries in organizational change, to moral and symbolic boundaries in contested domains – we aim to investigate the methodological challenges of cross-field studies. Doing so will enable novel theoretical insights into broader cross-field processes. Understanding how one field transforms (into) another field is increasingly urgent: the world is changing rapidly through macro-level processes that transcend any single organizational field. This PDW offers tools and frameworks for understanding how contemporary change unfolds across field boundaries.
