Beyond The Platform: Algorithmic Control Spillover Into Gig Workers’ Family Deviance

OB
Zijing Wang (Wuhan University of Technology); Chufu Chen (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Xiaoyan Su (Jinan University); Sibo Huang (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
Purpose-This study examines how algorithmic control in gig work elicits adverse psychological experiences that transcend organizational boundaries. Integrating psychological reactance theory and displaced aggression theory, we propose a sequential mechanism through which perceived algorithmic control fosters workplace objectification and anger, ultimately manifesting in family deviant behavior. We further explore the buffering role of online community interaction. Design/methodology/approach-This study employs a multi-method design across two studies. Study 1 utilizes a 2 (high vs. low algorithmic control) × 2 (high vs. low online community interaction) scenario- based experiment with 377 Chinese gig workers. Study 2 implements a three-wave, time-lagged survey of 355 full-time food delivery riders to test the full moderated sequential mediation model in a naturalistic platform work context. Findings-Results demonstrate that algorithmic control heightens workplace objectification, which provokes anger and contributes to deviant behaviors within the family domain. Importantly, online community interaction mitigates this spillover pathway by providing emotional and informational support that counters workers’ sense of dehumanization. Originality/value-This study makes three contributions: (1) it expands the outcome domain of algorithmic control to include cross-domain family deviance; (2) it applies displaced aggression theory to platform-based, non-hierarchical labor contexts; and (3) it identifies online peer communities as a critical boundary resource that disrupts algorithm-induced emotional transference, offering fresh insights into resilience in digital labor ecosystems.
