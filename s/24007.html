Polycentric Governance and National Technological Adaptation: Leapfrogging Evidence from Solar PV

STR
Martin Monroe
The global transition to renewable energy creates several challenges for strategists. Existing frameworks provide partial insights but do not capture the full structural, temporal, and configurational dynamics of adaptation. Consequently, this research builds upon a recently developed, novel framework - a polycentric governance perspective (PGS) - in which governance structures and their interaction through governance mechanisms are emphasized. Using this lens, a qualitative, longitudinal case study was undertaken examining a striking example of strategic “leapfrogging” (Hobday, 1995; Kim, 1997; Jack & Suri, 2014) – a reversal of competitive position in which Germany overtook another early mover, Japan, in domestic installation of PV solar capacity. Emerging from this analysis as a unique central construct is the notion of a polycentric adaptive capacity (PAC). In the path-dependent initial phase, Germany - unlike Japan - exhibits governance configurations that enable decentralized coordination and broader and deeper ecosystem formation. In a subsequent phase, centralized government authority standardizes and consolidates market and ecosystem outcomes. Beyond these phases, a dynamic sequencing of governance configurations is also revealed. Thus, this research introduces the use of the PGS framework to assess strategy outcomes and the evolution of governance structures and mechanisms across different temporal and spatial scales.
