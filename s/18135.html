Session overview

Much AI research has focused on knowledge work or the automation and augmentation of routine tasks. However, organizations are also exploring how AI can support leadership work (e.g., setting direction, sensemaking) or even be positioned in executive-like roles. Early evidence spans simulations, where AI can outperform human participants in data-rich strategic tasks, to organizational experiments in which firms appoint AI to C-Level roles (e.g., Tech firm NetDragon). Research further shows acceptance of assistive AI management, improved decision quality, and attribution of leadership traits to machines. These developments require scholars to revisit leadership concepts, assess the consequences of placing AI in leadership roles, and clarify when AI complements, rather than replaces, leadership. However, existing research remains fragmented across adjacent streams, which hinders cumulative theory-building and leaves organizational theory without shared constructs, boundary conditions, and methodological approaches for studying AI-driven leadership. This two-hour PDW responds to these gaps by bringing together scholars and professionals to examine AI as a leader, co-develop a forward-looking research agenda, and foster an interdisciplinary community. Through Lightning Talks and a panel discussion followed by an interactive AI-led boardroom simulation with breakout reflection and plenary synthesis, this PDW links emerging evidence to theory-relevant research questions and methodological pathways. Accordingly, the PDW is organized around three key themes: I. Conceptualizing AI as a leader, II. Advancing research methods and III. Developing implications for theory and practice. By engaging with these themes, participants will sharpen their conceptual foundations, clarify methodological options, and co-develop a research agenda for studying AI-driven leadership.
