Claiming vs. Granting the Lead: How Inclusive Leadership Shapes Informal Leadership Willingness

OB
Ioannis Tagos (University of Konstanz)
Research on informal leadership has gained significant attention, yet little is known about how formal leaders relate to subordinates’ willingness to claim and grant informal leadership. Drawing on optimal distinctiveness theory (Leonardelli et al., 2010), we propose that inclusive vertical leadership relates to subordinates’ willingness to claim leadership for oneself and to grant leadership to work group colleagues (DeRue & Ashford, 2010) by supporting needs for uniqueness and belongingness. We test psychological empowerment and team identification as mediators and examine perceived leader need and perceived leader group prototypicality as boundary conditions in a moderated mediation model. In a three-wave time-lagged observational study (N = 1,380), inclusive vertical leadership was positively associated with both willingness to claim and willingness to grant informal leadership. Psychological empowerment mediated the association with claiming, whereas team identification did not mediate the association with granting. Leader need weakened the inclusive leadership–claiming link and the indirect association via empowerment. Perceived leader group prototypicality strengthened the direct association between inclusive leadership and willingness to grant, but not the indirect path via team identification.
