Session overview

Leadership matters (House, et al., 2014) and research highlights the critical role of adaptive leadership to the success of societal, organizational and project outcomes (Porath, 2023; Raei & Rasmussen, 2021, Yukl & Gardner, 2020). One major leadership challenge is how to reconcile seemingly contrary findings with regard to homogeneity and heterogeneity in terms of the contributors and inhibitors of outstanding leadership considering national, societal, organizational and a variety of individual-level demographic variables (e.g. gender). In this PDW, we propose a novel approach to explore how leadership success can reflect both similarities and differences in vastly different countries and societies. We will explore both sides of the issue regarding leadership heterogeneity/homogeneity using the metaphor of “Janus”, the Roman God of two faces, each looking in opposite directions. One direction assumes ideal leadership is very similar across the world, a “universality” perspective. The other is the “context-specificity” perspective, often called the culture-context or culture-specificity perspective. We will kick off the workshop with a case study scenario to enhance engagement, and after presenting this new research model and findings, we will have two rounds of highly interactive discussion on the topics described.
