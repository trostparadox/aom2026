Session overview

There has been a growing backlash against diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) programs in organizations, state governments, and society as a whole (Adams & Chiwaya, 2024). One reason for this is that some organizational leaders believe that DEI programs are ineffective, foster reverse discrimination, lower hiring standards, create resentment, and promote unfairness (Palumbo, 2025). They also maintain that DEI programs neglect meritocracy and reduce overall organizational productivity. As a result of these beliefs, President Donald Trump signed an Executive Order in January 2025 eliminating these programs in the U. S. government (Federal Register, 2025). The executive order sent a signal to organizations that DEI was no longer needed, and should be abolished. Thus, a number of work organizations, U.S. state governments, healthcare, and educational institutions, eradicated their DEI initiatives (e.g., Meta, Zoom, Target, Snap, Tesla, DoorDash, Lyft, Home Depot, and Wayfair) (Blaclkstone et al., 2024; Crumley, 2024, Ray & Melaku, 2023). In addition, at least 50 percent of employees in DEI roles in organizations were eliminated (Crumley, 2024). Further, 26 states proposed or passed anti-DEI legislation designed to restrict or eliminated DEI initiatives, and states introduced bills that would restrict or eradicate DEI programs in higher education (Chronicle of Higher Education, 2025). Although all of these bills have not become law, some states (e.g., Florida, Texas, Utah) passed bills that eliminated DEI programs on college campuses. Further, the backlash has been far reaching, and DEI has become a scapegoat for many of the problems facing organizations and our society. For instance, when a large cargo ship collided with and caused the collapse of Baltimore’s Francis Scott Key Bridge in March of 2024, Utah state Rep. Phil Lyman argued that DEI was responsible even though the ship lost control and collided with the bridge. Rep Lyman noted that the state had used DEI in their hiring initiatives, but that had nothing to do with the ship colliding with the bridge. Despite the backlash against DEI, research has shown that diversity offers a number of key benefits for individuals and organizations (see Hebl & King, 2024). Such research has shown that DEI improves organizational performance (e.g., productivity, return on equity and market performance; Richard, 2000), enhances decision making, increases creativity and innovation (Roberge et al., 2011), heightens customer satisfaction, and intensifies employees’ perceptions of belonging, inclusion, and engagement (Shore, et al., 2018). Further, we believe that the elimination of DEI initiatives may exacerbate the shortage of labor in our society, and analyses have argued that there are a number of unintended consequences associated with their eradication. For instance, when organizations abolished their DEI programs customers protested and boycotted some companies (e.g., Target). Some companies lost talented employees, gained a bad reputation, and ultimately decided to reinstate the programs because the elimination of the programs created several problems (e.g., loss of customers and revenues, turnover of talented employees, increased claims of unfair discrimination, and reduced ability to compete in a worldwide marketplace that includes diverse customers) (Li, 2025). Given the backlash against DEI programs, we believe that a better understanding is needed about the basis for the backlash against DEI programs, and the consequences for companies that eradicated these programs (Dobbin & Kalev, 2016). For example, a recent survey (Resume.org, 2025) found that companies that abolished DEI programs had had more negative outcomes than those who maintained the initiatives or just modified the titles. Some of them experienced the following consequences: 10% had reduced investments, 33% had fewer underrepresented minority employees, 50% had reduce morale, 20% had increased incidences of unfair discrimination claims, and many of them experience reduced innovation, and disengagement of workers (Resume.org, 2025). Given that there has been relatively little research reasons for the backlash against DEI, and the unintended consequences associated with purging these programs, the primary purposes of the proposed presentation are as follows: The proposed symposium will (a) consider several of the reasons for the backlash against DEI programs in organizations, (b) relay the results of recent research deals with the backlash against these programs, (c) review the expected consequences and unanticipated consequences associated with abolishing these programs, and (d) offer directions for future research and practice on the reduction of these programs in organizations.
