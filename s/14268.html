Breaking the Bonding Trap: The Corrective and Constraining Roles of Entrepreneurial Education

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 4

People

Author: Kyounghee Chu Chosun University

Session overview

This study challenges the prevailing “more is better” view of social capital by identifying a “Bonding Trap” in South Korea, where strong ties exert isomorphic pressures that suppress entrepreneurial self-efficacy (ESE). Integrating Social Capital and Social Cognitive Theories, we examine the dual, opposing roles of Theory-Oriented Entrepreneurial Education (TOEE). Analyzing survey data from 334 South Korean young adults using Structural Equation Modeling, we confirm that while bridging social capital enhances ESE, bonding social capital significantly undermines it. Crucially, findings reveal TOEE’s contingent efficacy: it functions as a “corrective mechanism” for individuals entrapped in bonding networks, buffering negative social pressures through academic legitimacy. Conversely, for those rich in bridging capital, rigid TOEE acts as a “constraint,” creating a crowding-out effect that dampens their inherent potential. By elucidating these asymmetrical dynamics, this research makes a distinct theoretical contribution: it rehabilitates theoretical education not as a static relic, but as a vital “cognitive shield” and equalizer for the socially constrained, providing them with the analytical logic necessary to withstand the isomorphic pressures of collectivist conformity. This finding shifts the pedagogical discourse from the exhaustive “theory versus practice” dichotomy to a learner-centric contingency framework, emphasizing that educational impact is determined by the interplay between pedagogical method and social embeddedness. Ultimately, the study challenges educators to abandon “one-size-fits-all” curricula, advocating instead for segmented interventions that deploy theoretical rigor to empower those facing normative pressures, while preserving flexibility for the resource-rich.
