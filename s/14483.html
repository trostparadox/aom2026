Effects of Message Framing and Achievement Motivation on Responses to Startup Support Programs

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Authors: Kikyoung Park Gyeongsang National University; Gangseog Ryu Korea University

Session overview

Entrepreneurship-support policies are widely implemented to encourage entrepreneurial behavior; however, their effectiveness depends on how individuals perceive and respond to them. This study examined how message framing (gain vs. non-loss) interacts with achievement motivation—defined as the joint operation of hope of success and fear of failure—to influence intentions to participate in startup support programs and entrepreneurial intention. We proposed that incongruence between message framing and individuals’ motivational orientations increases cognitive resource allocation and promotes more systematic information processing, thereby enhancing perceived program value and entrepreneurial responses. A 2 (message framing) × 2 (achievement motivation) between-subjects experiment with 166 adults revealed a significant two-way interaction. Individuals lower in achievement motivation reported greater intentions under gain-framed messages, whereas individuals higher in achievement motivation responded more favorably to non-loss-framed messages, although the latter effect was not statistically significant. The findings extend research on framing effects to the entrepreneurship domain and underscore the importance of the relationship between frames and motivational orientations. Practically, the results suggest that policy communication, beyond program design, plays a critical role in promoting entrepreneurship, highlighting the value of segment-based framing strategies.
