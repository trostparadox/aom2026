Intelligence Capital Resources: A Multilevel Theory of Human-Artificial Intelligence Work Systems

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Author: Shawn Zhou University of South Carolina

Session overview

Artificial intelligence (AI) is playing an increasingly prominent role in organizational work systems. While existing research often frames AI as a tool that augments human work, this view provides limited insight into how AI functions as a productive resource at the unit level. In this article, I develop a multilevel theory of Intelligence Capital Resources (ICR) to explain how human and artificial intelligence jointly generate unit-level productive capacity in human–AI work systems. I define ICR as unit-level capacities arising from human and artificial intelligence that are accessible for unit-level purposes. Building on the human capital resources framework, I conceptualize AI capacity as a foundational source of productive capacity, alongside human capital, and distinguish between individual-level intelligence capital and unit-level ICR. I further develop a multilevel formation model that explains how ICR emerge through two primary pathways: the emergence of individual-level intelligence capital arising from human activation of AI, and the embedding of AI capacity directly into unit-level work systems. This framework clarifies how AI contributes to performance through a distinct pathway. Overall, the ICR framework provides a foundation for understanding performance generation in contemporary work systems and offers new directions for research on human capital and firm performance in the age of AI.
