Session overview

Organizations rely on evaluation systems, HR policies, and formalized procedures to guide decisions about recruitment, promotion, compensation, and performance. Yet these very systems, while seemingly neutral, can unintentionally reproduce or amplify gendered and intersectional inequalities. This PDW brings together leading scholars whose work investigates how institutional design, evaluative practices, policy structures, and comparison systems shape inequalities in contemporary organizations. Through brief presentations, interactive discussion, and roundtable collaboration, this PDW aims to: (1) consolidate emerging insights on how evaluation and policy design influence gendered career outcomes; (2) identify common mechanisms through which organizations reproduce inequality; (3) examine where institutional interventions succeed or fail; and (4) develop an interdisciplinary future research agenda. This workshop is highly relevant for scholars interested in inequality, HR systems, organizational design, and policy evaluation. Participants will leave with new conceptual frameworks and actionable research ideas for understanding and addressing gendered disparities through organizational systems.
