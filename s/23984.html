Human-AI Ensembles in Mergers and Acquisitions

STR
Joonas Kalle Juhani Kauto (University of St.Gallen)
Organizations increasingly employ artificial intelligence (AI) in strategic decision domains once governed entirely by human judgment. Mergers and acquisitions (M&As)—rare, heterogeneous, and causally ambiguous events—represent a setting in which traditional, human-only learning systems repeatedly fail to convert experience into improved performance. We theorize how hu-man–AI (H–AI) ensembles, in which human and artificial agents jointly generate and aggregate judgments, reconfigure the mechanisms of organizational learning in M&As. Building on be-havioural perspectives of learning, we develop a process model of hybrid intelligence that ex-plains how ensembles create, transfer, and retain experiential knowledge through the combina-tion of codified algorithmic pattern recognition and human interpretive reasoning. We show how ensemble architectures reshape the applicability, accessibility, and adoption contingencies that govern the experience–performance relationship, thereby addressing long-standing puzzles about why acquisition experience so often fails to yield superior outcomes. By conceptualizing H–AI ensembles as learning architectures rather than decision aids, our theory enriches organi-zational learning with a technology-conscious account of how hybrid systems accumulate, pre-serve, and deploy experiential knowledge in complex strategic contexts. This perspective also broadens research on AI in management by illustrating how hybrid intelligence alters the condi-tions under which organizations learn from rare, high-stakes decisions.
