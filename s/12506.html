Session overview

Drawing upon multiple methods (survey, archival, and experimental) and theories (negative partisanship theory, cronyism, conviction narrative theory, labeling theory), the present symposium examines how language impacts perceptions, support, and behaviors of leaders, employees, and others both inside the traditional workplace and beyond. First, the symposium starts in the political sphere by demonstrating how political leaders use polarization and co-optation to their advantage (Shaw & Hall). Moving within more traditional firms, this symposium sheds light on the perceived morality of firms (Tang et al.) and the perceived support and donations that firms accrue according to how they are labeled (Roberson et al.). Lastly, work in this symposium highlights how narratives of cancel culture influence the decision of employees to engage in larger social issues at work (Rogers et al.). Overall, the papers in this symposium address timely and important research questions that help us to understand how language shapes employee and organizational outcomes.
