Custom GenAI Personas as Methodological Augments in Interview-Based Qualitative Research

RM
Mehmet Ali Yetim (University of Scranton); Furkan Amil Gur (Northern Illinois University); Anthony Baptiste (Northern Illinois University)
Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly incorporated into qualitative research, most often as a technical tool supporting tasks such as transcription, coding, or translation. In this study, we position GenAI in a specific role: as a simulated interview participant to explore its potential across all stages of qualitative inquiry, from protocol design through data collection to analysis. We compare 23 semi-structured interviews with top managers in employee-owned and cooperative organizations against 23 corresponding interviews with GenAI personas customized to reflect each manager's professional background and organizational context. Through comparative case analysis, we identify three distinct functions that GenAI-simulated interviews serve across the qualitative research: refining interview protocols during study design by surfacing question-design weaknesses, supporting researcher reflexivity throughout data collection by enabling examination of researcher assumptions and interpretive moves, and enabling validity checking during analysis by providing structured comparison points for assessing the depth and contextual specificity of human-generated data. Across these functions, GenAI-simulated interviews do not replace human participants; rather, they add methodological value when combined with actual interviews, and active human oversight is essential to maintain methodological rigor, preserve contextual accuracy, and protect participant confidentiality.
