A Systematic Literature Review on Culture and Entrepreneurship

ENT
Nikolaus Seitz (Bauhaus-Universität Weimar); Martina Buratti (Bauhaus-Universität Weimar)
This paper provides the first comprehensive and systematic literature review on the intersection between culture and entrepreneurship. Despite a growing body of research showing that cultural values, norms, and identities significantly shape entrepreneurial behavior, the literature remains highly fragmented. Studies differ widely in terms of analytical levels, cultural transmission mechanisms, and entrepreneurial types, making it difficult to grasp the field’s overall structure. To address this gap, we examine 277 peer-reviewed journal articles published between 1991 and 2022. We adopt a mixed-method approach that combines bibliographic coupling, unsupervised Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques for semantic clustering and qualitative content analyses. We identify three main macro clusters and six semantic subclusters. Our findings reveal how the literature has evolved over time, with growing attention to communities, identity, and context-specific mechanisms. We identify key emerging trends — including ecosystem approaches, psychological and educational factors, and gendered cultural dynamics — and outline future research directions based on existing gaps. This study contributes with a structured map of the literature and offers a foundation for advancing research on how culture shapes entrepreneurship.

Entrepreneurship and Emerging Technologies: A Theoretical Review and Future Agenda

ENT
Endrit Kromidha (University of Birmingham); Nathanael Ojong (York University); Jintong Tang (Saint Louis University)
Increasingly, entrepreneurship cannot be understood outside the context of emerging technologies (ETs) that have significant potential for socio-economic. New opportunities for value creation arise at this meeting point, but theoretical perspectives remain fragmented and insufficient to capture the co-evolutionary dynamics. Drawing on 72 studies and 53 theoretical approaches uses in them, this systematic review classifies them into five domains: technological change, entrepreneurial behavior, organizational advantage, contextual environment, and ethical identity. Reflecting on their intersections, four theoretical directions are proposed for a future research agenda: responsible socio-technical engagement, executive technological advantage, institutional innovation influence, and contextual change alignment.

Overcoming Entrenched Incumbents: Property Rights Innovations to Water in the American West

ENT
Devin Stein (University of Alabama); Thomas J. Dean (Colorado State University); Maria Minniti (Syracuse University)
Entrepreneurial actors seeking to innovate new forms of value often confront entrenched incumbents who resist changes threatening their established positions. While research on competitive dynamics and stakeholder strategy shows how actors navigate incumbent retaliation, we know less about how actors successfully introduce novel property rights innovations which generate entirely new claims to resources and challenge those who benefit from existing structures. Property rights innovations not only threaten incumbents’ competitive positions, but also challenge the legitimacy and recognition of the resource uses themselves. Drawing on property rights theory's emphasis on distributional conflict, we develop a theory of strategic entry under incumbent resistance. We argue that novel claims enter where dispersed incumbents cannot coordinate collective opposition, whereas traditional claims succeed regardless of the presence of concentrated incumbents. We further propose that actors introducing novel claims strategically position initial entries to minimize interference with existing users, and that the interference profile of initial entries enable or constrain further entry over time. We test our theory using an original panel dataset tracking the emergence of environmental water rights across all 750 hydrological basins in the American West from 1940 to 2020. This is a setting where actors fought for decades to establish non-consumptive uses as legitimate claims within systems dominated by agricultural use. Our findings demonstrate that strategic positioning enables entry in fragmented systems, and that the interference potential of these early rights shapes expansion trajectories. We contribute to research on stakeholder dynamics and market entry by demonstrating how incumbent concentration shapes the effectiveness of entry strategies, and by identifying how the interference potential of novel claims shapes incumbent resistance to property rights innovations.

Sustainable Migrant Entrepreneurship: A Systematic Review and Critical Discussion

ENT
Sascha Fuerst (Tecnologico de Monterrey); Lilia Raquel Rojas-Cruz (Tecnologico de Monterrey); Miguel Gil (Jönköping University); Maria Elo (The George Washington University)
This systematic literature review examines research at the intersection of migrant entrepreneurship and sustainability. Migrant sustainable entrepreneurship is defined as entrepreneurial activities initiated or led by migrants that intentionally address environmental and/or social challenges while pursuing economic viability. Following established protocols, we searched Scopus and Web of Science databases, screening 428 records through multi-stage procedures, identifying 27 empirical articles published between 2011-2025. Research remains nascent and fragmented, with publication activity accelerating only from 2019 onwards. Methodologically, qualitative approaches dominate, while large-scale quantitative studies remain scarce. Theoretical perspectives predominantly draw on social capital, mixed embeddedness, and bricolage frameworks, though environmental sustainability dimensions remain undertheorized. The literature shows migrant sustainable entrepreneurs operate in precarious, resource-constrained environments where ventures simultaneously foster empowerment while potentially reproducing dependencies. Critically, we argue that the mechanistic nature of the systematic review introduces terminological and conceptual problems, lacking semantic reflection on terms like "migrant" and "sustainability" across disciplines. Geographical and contextual gaps exist, with disproportionate attention to refugee contexts. We identify opportunities for methodological diversification, theoretical innovation beyond framework recombination, and deeper engagement with semantic, ontological, and epistemological dimensions of the phenomenon.

Social Entrepreneurial Teams: A Systematic Literature Review

ENT
Fatima Zahra Badgaoui (University of Bergamo)
Social entrepreneurial teams (SETs) create and manage social ventures. Our systematic literature review of 49 articles discussing the existing literature on SET and providing avenues for future research. Teams are a growing academic literature in entrepreneurship literature, however, focus on hybrid organizations such as social enterprises is still in its infancy. Unlike commercial entrepreneurship reviews focusing on NVTs (Klotz et al., 2014; Jin et al., 2017) or corporate entrepreneurship via TMTs (Saeed et al., 2025), no systematic review maps SETs across the full lifecycle using IMO framework in hybrid contexts. Analysing the articles on studies in SETs, some limits are highlighted, for example the focus on established ventures, the limit in effectiveness measures used, the need for more quantitative studies and more indeph studies to respond to the dynamics at team formation, but also studies analysing other SE’s phases. To provide an overview of common research areas and findings, we discussed the articles on SETs into three topics and discussing inconsistencies in the literature. We suggest prospective future research avenues that combine socio-economic research approaches (longitudinal qualitative studies) and quantitative studies with larger samples to enable a holistic system perspective on SET.
