Anger Editing as a Strategic Anger Expression in Organizations

OB
Anna Czaplewska-Jaffery (The London School of Economics & Political Science)
The existing anger research tends to focus on its expression or suppression, often failing to integrate Emotion Regulation and strategic expression literatures. We propose “anger editing” as a new form of workplace emotion expression strategy that draws from emotion regulation tactics. Adopting an inductive approach and utilizing interviews with 56 men and women in organizational settings, we discover that employees perceive anger as unprofessional and risky, while also recognizing the benefits associated with expressing its core message. Our analysis revealed that employees actively “edit” their anger to increase its strategic advantages while minimizing the penalties for expressing this unwelcome emotion. We conceptualize “anger editing” as a deliberate, two-step process through which employees attempt to “edit out” the aspects of anger that are related to negative perceptions while retaining the beneficial signaling properties of anger. It is an active emotion management process that draws on both emotion regulation and expression strategies. Our study contributes to the workplace emotion literature by introducing an alternative yet integrative approach to managing and regulating emotions, that can be especially used by employees attempting to avoid the negative consequences associated with expressing full-blown anger, without incuring the costs of suppressing it.
