Unveiling the Paradox of Performance Pressure: A Meta-Analytic Investigation

OB
Renpu Lang (Shandong University); Yifan Song (Texas A&M University)
Performance pressure is a key feature of modern workplaces. Despite extensive research, findings on the relationship between performance pressure and performance outcomes remain mixed. This underscores the need to systematically examine why and when performance pressure may have beneficial or detrimental effects. Drawing on Conservation of Resources Theory, we developed an integrative theoretical model that delineates two distinct pathways (i.e., resource investment and resource depletion) through which performance pressure influences task, citizenship, and counterproductive performance. We further investigated boundary conditions, including cultural differences and sociodemographic characteristics. A meta-analytic investigation of the performance pressure literature was conducted, which synthesized data from 124 studies with 146 independent samples (N = 45,595). Findings revealed that resource investment and resource depletion mediated the effects of performance pressure on the three performance outcomes in the hypothesized directions. Power distance, collectivism, and masculinity strengthened the positive effects of performance pressure on both resource investment and resource depletion, while uncertainty avoidance weakened the positive effect of performance pressure on resource investment. For more educated or younger employees, the positive effect of performance pressure on resource investment was stronger. Our study offers integrative and novel insights into how and when performance pressure affects work performance.
