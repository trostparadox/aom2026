Entrepreneurial Judgment and Microfoundations of Informal Economic Activities Among Registered Firms

IM
Daniel L. Bennett (University of Louisville); Sadek Showkat (Loyola University Chicago)
Within international business (IB) literature, informal economic activities are typically associated with unregistered firms. While informal activities are traditionally viewed dichotomously as involving either registration or nonregistration, we argue that even registered firms engage in informal economic activities to navigate perceived institutional weakness. Drawing on judgment-based approach, we develop a microfoundational perspective that emphasizes how individual entrepreneurs form beliefs of formal institutions and make strategic decisions about when to comply with or circumvent formal rules. Rather than relying on the dominant explanation of weak institutions or increased transaction costs, we theorize and test how perceptions influence the beliefs for informal activities. We also theorize that internationalization, specifically foreign ownership and export orientation, serves as an important external boundary condition on the belief that reduces entrepreneurial discretion by imposing external governance pressures, increasing reputational risks, and facilitating access to formal resources. Employing a dataset of 63,703 registered firms across 77 countries, we test and find empirical support for our conceptual model using hierarchical linear modeling.

International New Ventures (INVs) and Speed of Cross-Border M&A Deal Completion

IM
In Hyeock Ian Lee (Loyola University Chicago); Eunsuk Hong
Considering that international new ventures’ (INVs) success depends on how early and rapidly they can reach new customers in foreign markets, the ‘speed’ of INVs’ internationalization has been an important research agenda in the international entrepreneurship (IE) literature. However, previous studies on INVs’ rapid internationalization using an equity-based entry mode, such as cross-border mergers and acquisitions (M&As), have been scant in the IE literature. This study aims to fill this knowledge gap using a sample of 1,053 cross-border M&A deals conducted by INV acquirers from 52 countries in 1997- 2017, and it investigates which factors may expedite the completion speed of cross-border M&A deals when INVs are acquirers in the equity-based entry mode into foreign countries. The main findings from the study are three-fold: (1) when INVs acquire cross-border M&A targets that are co-located within the same geographic region, such intra-regional M&A deals expedite the completion speed of the cross-border M&As; (2) when INVs access knowledge endowed in foreign countries from the cross-border M&A deals, it further enhances the completion speed of the intra-regional M&As; and (3) when both INVs and their cross-border M&A targets co-participate in high technology sectors, it further enhances the completion speed of the intra-regional M&As.

From Absence To Presence: How Platform Ventures Leverage Informal Economies In Developing Countries

IM
Chanyong Yoo (Austin Peay State University); Andrew Spicer (University of South Carolina)
This paper examines how ventures in developing countries scale by engaging with informal institutional arrangements. Through a longitudinal case study of Gojek, an Indonesian venture that grew from a call center coordinating twenty informal motorbike taxi drivers to Indonesia’s largest on-demand multiservice provider within a decade, we challenge the conventional view that informal economies in developing countries represent challenges for venture growth. Instead, we find that Gojek systematically engaged with pre-existing informal arrangements as strategic resources for venture scaling. Our analysis reveals mechanisms through which ventures develop capabilities that combine formal coordination with preserved informal flexibility. These mechanisms enable standardization at the coordination layer that expands customer access while preservation of informality at the operational layer maximizes provider participation. The findings suggest that venture scaling in developing countries requires engaging with informal institutional elements rather than circumventing them, offering a presence-based alternative to absence-based perspectives on venture strategy in emerging market.

Cross-Border Crowdfunding: An Attention-Based View

IM
Christopher Courtney (Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University); Supradeep Dutta (Rutgers University-Camden); Yong Li
Reward-based crowdfunding, compared to conventional entrepreneurial finance, is unique in offering individuals (i.e., backers) near-instantaneous access to a vast array of global investment opportunities (i.e., crowdfunding campaigns). Yet given the limited cognitive and information processing capacities, backers attend to a limited set of crowdfunding campaigns. We draw on the attention-based view to analyze how backers’ attention is stimulated towards international crowdfunding campaigns and how attention stimuli have varying impact on campaigns of different types and stages. Since backers contemplating support for international campaigns have to navigate cultural differences between them, we further examine whether such differences heighten backers’ responsiveness to attention stimuli. We analyze a large sample of campaigns on the Kickstarter platform and find supportive evidence. Our work adds to research in international business at the intersection of attention, culture, and cross-border crowdfunding.
