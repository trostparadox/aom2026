Session overview

This caucus convenes key actors in Responsible Management Education to engage in a facilitated, discussion-driven dialogue on advancing SDG-aligned research. Through structured reflection on current work, shared gaps, and future collaboration, the session makes visible field-level sensemaking while inviting the wider community to observe and engage with emerging research directions. The Principles for Responsible Management Education (PRME) is a United Nations–supported initiative advancing Responsible Management Education (RME) as a way of aligning business and management education with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Through its global network of higher education institutions, scholars, and partners, PRME supports research that connects theory, pedagogy, and institutional practice in service of more responsible and sustainable approaches to management education. This caucus is designed as a discussion-driven session rather than an audience-facing or networking event. The core conversation will take place among invited representatives from journals, research networks, academies, and PRME who are actively shaping the RME research landscape. The goal is to surface how this work is currently being advanced, where it is getting stuck, and where greater alignment or coordination could strengthen the field’s contribution to SDG-related research and impact. The discussion will be structured around participants sharing critical RME research questions, identifying key constraints, and establishing commitments we can make to compound to meaningful change. The caucus is open to the wider conference community, who are invited to attend as observers and listen in on this conversation, with limited opportunities for input at designated moments. The goal of this session is to provoke meaningful discussion and inspire future collaborations for RME. The session is intended to offer transparency into how the RME research ecosystem functions and how priorities are being shaped, rather than to provide individual networking opportunities. In doing so, the caucus helps participants and observers alike better understand where their own work might connect in the future, while helping participants and observers better understand the RME research landscape and PRME’s role in shaping coordination and exchange across SDG-aligned research communities.
