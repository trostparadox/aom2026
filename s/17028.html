Session overview

A focus on profit maximization and short-term gains has dominated management practice over the past half-century. Yet, a growing number of stakeholders, including business leaders, governmental leaders, employees, consumers, investors, and community members are calling for an expanded understanding of corporate purpose that includes social and environmental goals alongside financial ones, or “beyond-profit” commitments. While many firms publicly claim to support such social and environmental initiatives, few are meaningfully sustaining these commitments over time. Prior literature leads us to expect “greenwashing,” “purpose-washing,” and cooptation, yet some organizations have stayed true to a beyond-profit purpose for decades or longer. This symposium advances new research at the cutting-edge of organizational purpose by examining how organizations and internal employee activists can sustain social and environmental commitments and overcome strong industry and institutional pressures to conform to dominant financial logics. The four papers in this session explore unique forms of resistance including “untempered,” more radical employee internal advocacy for corporate sustainability, hiring and retention tactics to sustain hybridity over time, organizational tactics to push back against instrumental logics, and internal social movement activism by employees despite backlash in the broader political environment. The methodological range of the four papers – two ethnographic, one quantitative, and one theory-based – allows for a rich, multi-method exploration of how resistance to dominant logics unfolds across different organizational contexts and levels of analysis. Together, these four presentations, the Presider and Discussant remarks, and the facilitated question and answer session are designed to develop new theoretical insights about the mechanisms through which beyond-profit purpose is defended and sustained over time.
