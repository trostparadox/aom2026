Unveiling Family MNEs’ Bifurcation Bias and Post-Entry Resource Re-Combination Amid Pandemic Shock

IM
Kaiyang Sun (Adelaide University); Henry Shi (Adelaide University)
How family multinational enterprises (FMNEs) overcome bifurcation bias after foreign market entry remains theoretically underdeveloped, particularly under conditions of extreme uncertainty. Drawing on new internalisation theory, this study conceptualises bifurcation bias as a post-entry resource allocation problem and theorises when FMNEs re-combine family and non-family resources. We argue that severe exogenous shocks alter the efficiency-control trade-off underlying bifurcation bias, making deep international commitments a critical boundary condition for adaptation. Using longitudinal data on 765 Chinese listed MNEs from 2016 to 2022, we show that internationalisation per se does not mitigate bifurcation bias. However, when capital-intensive internationalisation coincides with the COVID-19 pandemic, FMNEs significantly reduce family ownership and management involvement and weaken family identification. By disentangling internationalisation depth from breadth, this study specifies when bifurcation bias is mitigated rather than reinforced, extending new internalisation theory and advancing understanding of post-entry adaptation in family multinationals under extreme uncertainty.
