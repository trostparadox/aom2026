Gendered Coordination Under Algorithmic Control: Commission Circumvention in Ride-Hailing

CTO
Tenace Kwaku Setor (University of Richmond); Faheem Ahmed Shaikh (California State Polytechnic University-Pomona); Damien Joseph (Nanyang Technological University)
Ride-hailing platforms operate as closed loops of algorithmic control, yet in cash-dominated markets drivers and passengers cancel in-app bookings and complete trips off-platform to avoid the platform’s fees and commissions. We theorize commission circumvention as an encounter-level, dyadic deviation between the driver and rider that requires tacit agreement under risk. In the absence of relational history, partners infer willingness to cooperate from visible social cues. We argue that gender serves as a central and readily available heuristic in these encounters, and that nighttime conditions heighten situational uncertainty and reliance on platform safety infrastructure, especially for women. We test these hypotheses using proprietary transaction-level data from a Ghanaian on-demand mobility platform covering 228,769 matched rides involving 3,782 drivers and 74,918 rider accounts. We construct a conservative proxy for circumvention that requires match acceptance, GPS-verified rendezvous within 25 meters, in-app cancellation, and a subsequent dark period consistent with offline completion. Results show that male driver–male passenger dyads are more likely to successfully circumvent than male driver–female passenger dyads, and that this gap widens at night. These findings extend algorithmic management research by locating compliance in horizontal coordination within service encounters and by identifying gender and temporal context as boundary conditions for collaborative rule deviation.

Sustenance of Platforms Work

CTO
Mahesh Kumar (S. P. Jain Institute of Management and Research, INDIA); Bikramjit Ray Chaudhuri (S. P. Jain Institute of Management and Research, INDIA)
Despite gig workers’ discontent, manifested through widespread strikes and collective action, participation in platform-based work continues to surge. While existing scholarship has extensively documented the comprehensive and often oppressive nature of algorithmic management, it has not adequately explained why workers remain engaged amid such dissatisfaction. This study aims to resolve this contradiction by examining how and why ride-hailing drivers in an emerging market maintain their relationship with digital platforms despite significant grievances. Drawing on Hirschman's exit-voice framework, our qualitative study reveals the phenomenon of ‘entrapment’ in platform work. We find that workers, initially drawn into platform work in the hope of emancipation from prior precarious employment, get entangled in platform work despite their growing dissatisfaction. Notably, instead of resorting to passive acceptance or neglect when exit and voice mechanisms prove ineffective, these workers proactively reorganise their practices to survive and sustain on the platform work. This study extends the literature on organisational conflict by identifying trapping, and subsequent active agency and reorganisation, as a critical yet overlooked response that explains the sustained participation of entrapped workers in the platform economy

Communicative Constitution of Free Speech: The Work of Moderators in Online Conspiracy Spaces

CTO
Elisabeth Marga Busch (EBS University of Business and Law); Tim Risse (EBS University of Business and Law)
This study examines how free speech is organized in an online conspiracy community through moderators’ communicative practices. Drawing on the communicative constitution of organizing perspective, we analyze the subreddit r/conspiracy as an informal digital space that is continuously constituted through communication rather than governed by formal organizational structures. Focusing on moderators’ speech, we identify three commentary-based practices, containing, shaping, and deflecting, through which moderators enact boundaries of legitimate participation and define what counts as acceptable content. We show how free speech functions not as a fixed normative principle but as an organizing tool that moderators mobilize to legitimate content limitation and to navigate, and at times circumvent, platform governance. In doing so, moderators both guide the meaning of conspiracy and stabilize the community by directing conflict outward. The findings highlight the ambivalent role of human guidance in conspiracy-oriented spaces and raise ethical questions concerning authority, control, and legitimacy in digital public discourse.

Managing Identities Across Online Communities

CTO
Desislava Bocheva (University of Bath); Olivia Brown (University of Bath); Joanne Hinds (University of Bath)
Online communities are fluid digital spaces enabling multigroup membership. We extend extant literature examining singular identification by highlighting the role of individuals with multiple identities in maintaining the fluidity of online spaces. Drawing on a qualitative analysis of 2,673 discussion posts from 30 individuals transversing two online communities within the “manosphere”, we identify mechanisms members use to manage their dual identity across a mainstream and fringe community. Specifically, we show how maintaining permeable boundaries, employing agentic group affirmation, manufacturing authority, and utilizing experience as a sensemaking anchor enables individuals to manage multiple identities online. We argue that these processes sustain a liminal organizational structure allowing communication and practices to flow across groups. This demonstrates how multigroup membership functions as a condition for its maintaining fluid digital spaces. Using the ‘manosphere’ as an extreme case, we explore how identification across multiple communities facilitates the mainstreaming of fringe ideology. In doing so, we advance organizational research by introducing the concept of boundary confluence as a mechanism utilized by dual identity individuals to facilitate and maintain fluid collectives. We further contribute to organization scholarship by showing how managing multiple, overlapping identities is central to the durability of digitally mediated forms of organizing.

Coordination Costs in Human-AI Hybrid Organizations: Theory Clarity & Enabling Codification

CTO
Sebastian Niederberger (Bocconi University); Abhinav Pandey (Bocconi University)
When organizations integrate AI agents alongside human workers, comparable technical implementations yield divergent coordination outcomes. We theorize that success in human-AI hybrid organizations depends on reducing coordination costs, the resources expended to ensure that both human and AI actions align with organizational objectives. We conceptualize coordination costs as comprising specification, integration, and oversight costs. Drawing on organizational design theory and the view of organizations as artificial intelligences, we argue that two capabilities, theory clarity (convergent understanding of value creation across organizational levels) and enabling codification (flexible knowledge structures that empower rather than constrain), jointly determine the degree to which human-AI hybrid systems can reduce these costs. When theory clarity and enabling codification operate as complements, they activate four mechanisms, causal translation, enabling codification, prediction-execution coupling, and strategic context propagation, that disproportionately reduce specification and integration costs while bounding oversight at a small, non-zero floor. Unlike coercive codification that creates algorithmic rigidity, enabling codification preserves human judgment while facilitating AI integration. We derive propositions, delineate rival explanations and boundary conditions, and outline a research agenda detailing operational measures and methodological approaches. We explain why technically sophisticated AI deployments fail when theory clarity is low or enabling codification is low, while simpler systems succeed when both capabilities are present. Our theory sheds light on the organizational structures that enable harnessing the synergies of human-AI agent coordination.
