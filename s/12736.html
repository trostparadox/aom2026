Walk the Talk: The Impact of Human Rights Rhetoric and LGBTQ+ Inclusivity on Operational Efficiency

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 7
SIM

People

Authors: Anna Land Boise State University; James Kroes Boise State University; Andrew Steven Manikas University of Louisville; Shaunn Mattingly Boise State University

Session overview

Firms increasingly emphasize human rights and diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) in corporate disclosures, yet the operational implications of such emphasis remain insufficiently understood. This study examines whether human rights rhetoric in corporate disclosures is associated with operational resource efficiency and whether this relationship is strengthened by demonstrated LGBTQ+ inclusivity. Drawing on rhetorical institutionalism, legitimacy theory, and sociotechnical systems perspectives, we conceptualize human rights rhetoric as a symbolic signal that shapes organizational legitimacy and coordination, with performance effects contingent on alignment with inclusive practices. Using 1,012 firm-year observations of publicly traded U.S. firms from 2015–2025, we integrate computer-aided text analysis of 10-K disclosures, Corporate Equality Index ratings of LGBTQ+ inclusivity, and firm-level estimates of operational resource efficiency derived from stochastic frontier analysis. Mixed-effects regression models accounting for industry and temporal variation yield three principal findings. First, greater emphasis on human rights in corporate disclosures is positively associated with operational resource efficiency. Second, this association is significantly stronger for firms with higher levels of LGBTQ+ inclusivity, indicating that alignment between rhetoric and practice amplifies efficiency gains. Third, LGBTQ+ inclusivity exhibits a direct positive relationship with operational resource efficiency, independent of disclosure rhetoric. These findings demonstrate that symbolic commitments to human rights are associated with tangible operational outcomes when reinforced by credible inclusive practices for firms that walk the talk of human rights. The study extends sociotechnical systems theory by showing how the integration of social and technical elements through aligned rhetoric and practice contributes to resource efficiency, and it highlights inclusivity as a strategic lever for improving operational performance in complex organizational systems.
