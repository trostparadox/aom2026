Physiological and Cognitive Aspects of Work Engagement

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
NEUB

People

Authors: Jing Xi Ng Nanyang Technological University; Syaheed Jabar; Jit Wei Aaron Ang, -; Elliot Chan

Session overview

Work engagement is commonly measured through self-reported state measures, leaving its underlying physiological and dispositional mechanisms less well understood. Integrating state-level physiological activation and trait-level attentional focus, the present study investigates how these processes jointly shape task performance. We operationalize vigor as a state-based physiological construct using heart rate variability (HRV) to index momentary physiological mobilization, and absorption as a trait-based cognitive construct indexed inversely through mind wandering, reflecting individuals’ chronic tendency toward attentional disengagement from task demands. Using a controlled experimental design with repeated task blocks and a recovery period, we examine (a) the relationship between task-engaged HRV and performance efficiency, (b) the moderating role of trait mind wandering in this relationship, and (c) the effect of resting-state HRV during breaks on subsequent performance improvement. Results showed that lower task-engaged HRV is associated with higher performance efficiency among individuals low in mind wandering. In contrast, this relationship is reversed for individuals with high mind wandering tendency. Additionally, higher resting-state HRV during breaks predicts greater improvement in post-break performance. This is particularly evident for low mind wanderers, who are both more sensitive to insufficient recovery and more responsive to effective recovery. These findings highlight the importance of attentional focus in converting physiological resources into productive work outcomes
