Targeting the Trust Gap: Segmenting E-Commerce Consumers for E-Payment Adoption in Montenegro

Authors: Suncica Vukovic University of Montenegro; Chahna Gonsalves King's College London; Ljiljana Kascelan University of Montenegro; Ivan Boskovic University of Montenegro

Session overview

The rapid growth of e-commerce in emerging markets such as Montenegro is constrained by the persistent reliance on Cash on Delivery (COD), used by nearly 70% of online shoppers. While COD facilitates early market adoption by reducing initial consumer risk, its continued dominance creates operational and financial inefficiencies that limit scalability. This study examines the behavioural drivers of persistent COD use, conceptualising it as a defensive response to elevated perceived risk and low institutional trust. Using theoretical triangulation, it integrates the Technology Acceptance Model, Perceived Risk Theory, and Institutional Trust to develop a comprehensive framework for explaining payment choice in an emerging market context. Applying K-means clustering to preliminary survey data from Montenegrin consumers, the analysis identifies three distinct segments: Secure E-Payment Adopters, Cash-Centric Skeptics, and Conditional Converters. The findings provide segment-specific insights for e-commerce managers and policymakers and offer an empirically grounded pathway to narrowing the consumer trust gap and accelerating sustainable e-payment adoption in emerging European markets.
