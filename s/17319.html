Best to Be a Slave? The Paradox of Autonomy Expectations in Doctoral Education

People

Authors: Jure Andolšek School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana; Tyler (Ty) Nicole Abayon Fezzey University of Alabama; Bojan Kitanovikj Ss Cyril and Methodius University in Skopje, Faculty of Economics

Session overview

PhD students are often promised scholarly autonomy, yet complete their training within institutional systems sometimes characterized by extreme hierarchy, evaluation, and compliance. Existing research largely interprets doctoral strain and attrition as consequences of temporary misalignment, assuming that improved fit restores persistence. We challenge this assumption by theorizing doctoral training as a context of persistent structural tension between autonomy expectations and institutional constraints. Integrating person–environment fit theory with a paradox perspective, we propose a dual-pathway model in which autonomy expectations activate divergent psychological responses: academic identity conflict, increasing exit intentions, or institutional trust, sustaining persistence despite ongoing tension. Planned large-scale survey data from Academy of Management doctoral students will provide an empirical test of this framework.
