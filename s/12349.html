Session overview

Recent organization and management research has started to pay greater attention to nature, not only as an external environment in which the organizations are embedded, but also as a system of more-than-human actors constituting part of the organizational processes as they interact with organizational actors. Despite an emerging interest in studying rhythms in nature and addressing the dissonance and asynchrony between organizational and nature’s rhythms, we are habituated to background nature’s rhythms as the environment over which organizations have control. Organizations thus tend to manipulate, dictate, or entrain to nature’s rhythms for organizational outcomes that only account for and benefit some organizational actors in the present, while discounting many other actors and our futures. The symposium invites a joint conversation between the panelists and the audience to collectively reflect on our asymmetric paradigm, which considers nature either under or beyond our command, and to explore how we may transition to a symmetric understanding of Organizing with Nature. With such an attempt, we will critically inquire into the rhythms of organizing with nature from a more-than-human perspective that does not privilege humans over other animate and inanimate beings and things a priori.
