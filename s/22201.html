When Talent Management Works Better: The Role of Mindful Organizing in Employee Reactions

Authors: Daniel Gajda University of Economics in Katowice; Przemyslaw Zbierowski King's College London

Session overview

Drawing on social exchange theory and positive organizational scholarship, this study examines how talent management (TM) practices influence employee outcomes in mindful organizations. We develop a multilevel model distinguishing between intended and perceived TM practices and propose perceived organizational support (POS) as a key mediating mechanism linking TM to employee motivation, organizational commitment (OC), and organizational citizenship behavior. We further argue that mindful organizing constitutes an important contextual condition that strengthens the effectiveness of TM practices. Using survey data from 830 respondents (730 talent pool members and 100 managers) nested within 33 organizations, we test the model with multilevel regression analyses and structural equation modeling. The findings show that intended TM practices are positively related to perceived TM practices. Perceived TM practices positively affect all examined outcomes via POS, with full mediation for motivation and citizenship behavior and partial mediation for commitment. Moreover, mindful organizing strengthens the relationship between perceived TM practices and POS. Overall, the study advances HRM theory by highlighting employee reactions and organizational context as central to understanding the effectiveness of TM.
