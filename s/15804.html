Session overview

This panel symposium highlights agrifood systems as a valuable empirical context for organization and management scholars as well as a practical field that is in urgent need of our scholarship. Agrifood systems are a complex web of social, environmental, and economic processes that are foundational to civilizations and that face great transformation pressures to maintain food security and mitigate the planetary crisis. Agrifood systems are important arenas for management scholarship, both from the perspective of their societal significance and their (underutilized) potential in advancing theorizing in the field. They are characterized by socio-material practices, sustainability challenges, complex power structures, processes of institutionalization, and social and technological change, while they have not received the attention they merit in organizational theorizing. The presentations and discussions in this symposium aim to foster insights across different domains of organizational scholarship to identify pathways for theorizing and publishing agrifood phenomena in central management outlets and to build an engaged community of organizational scholars interested in agrifood systems. The panelists represent different research programs and empirical fields while all have made significant contributions to management studies drawing on their empirical work in the agrifood systems. Each panelist will discuss the potential and challenges of theorizing agrifood phenomena in their disciplines, after which discussion will be opened for the audience to engage with the panelists. Together, we will explore concrete ways of addressing these challenges and further reflect on how our scholarly community has in the past brought once-peripheral phenomena as central to management theorizing.
