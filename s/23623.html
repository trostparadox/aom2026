Session overview

This session examines how identity, status, and social expectations shape leadership and performance across organizational settings. The papers explore age and participative leadership, role congruity and firm performance, and the ways representation and status influence outcomes in leadership contexts. Together, they offer fresh insight into the social mechanisms linking identity, leadership, and organizational effectiveness.

Empowered Age: How Status and Participative Leadership Shape the Link Between Age and Performance

DEI
Linshan Fu (Zhejiang Wanli University); Xiaoling YANG (Ritsumeikan Asia Pacific University); Li Lin
As workforces worldwide continue to age, understanding whether and how older employees can contribute proactively and perform effectively has become a pressing concern for organizations and scholars alike. Moving beyond the traditional “deficit” model that frames aging primarily as a process of decline, this study adopts a more positive perspective that highlights the enduring strengths and value-creation potential of older workers. Drawing on expectation states theory, we propose that workplace status functions as a central mechanism linking age with key performance outcomes as proactivity and job performance. Furthermore, we identify participative leadership as a critical contextual condition that activates the positive potential of age, thereby facilitating the translation of age into workplace status and subsequently proactivity and job performance. By highlighting the potential of aging workforce, this study contributes to a more balanced and comprehensive understanding of the current literature on older workers, reframing aging not as decline but as a source of sustained capability and organizational value.

U-shaped Relationship Between Female Executives and Corporate Environmental Investment

DEI
Haijian Liu; Yating Gao (Nanjing University Business School)
Prior studies on gender diversity and corporate social responsibility suggest either a competing or a complementary relationship between female executives and environmental investment. In this study, we unravel this puzzle by theorizing a U-shaped relationship between female executives and environmental investment.Through a detailed mechanism analysis, we unveil that the influence of female executives on corporate environmental investment is significantly shaped by environmental pressure within top management teams. Moreover, we find that the impact of female executives on environmental investment is amplified under conditions of heightened myopia within top management teams, increased public concern for the environment, and stricter environmental regulations.

Performing on the Glass Stage: Examining Role Congruity’s Effect on Firm Performance

DEI
Steven Vogel (Appalachian State University); Tiberiu Sergiu Ungureanu (Appalachian State University); H Kristl Davison (Appalachian State University); Jessica L. Doll (Appalachian State University)
Gendered expectations may shape performance when leaders are evaluated primarily by external audiences. Role congruity theory predicts penalties for women in agentic roles, yet prior evidence rarely traces these evaluations into objective performance outcomes. We test whether gender role congruity influences venture performance in a stakeholder-facing setting: Broadway productions. Using a multi-source archival dataset linking Internet Broadway Database records (1998–2023) with character descriptions, critics’ reviews, awards, and weekly financial and attendance data, we code each lead role’s agentic-communal profile and its congruity with the character’s gender, and we capture lead actors’ role consistency from prior Broadway roles. Results show no uniform performance penalty for gender-incongruent leads. Instead, effects depend on the information environment and historical period. Incongruent leads generate higher attendance and revenue when the lead has a role-consistent history, and congruity-performance relationships shift over time, indicating changing audience expectations. Moreover, market outcomes diverge from insider evaluations such as awards and critical reception. We extend role congruity theory from internal evaluations to market-mediated performance and identify temporal and informational boundary conditions under which incongruent leaders face headwinds or outperform.

Unpacking the Gendered Gap in Academic Leadership: A Bibliometric and Systematic Literature Review

DEI
Nairitee Sil (University of Petroleum and Energy Studies)
This study synthesizes scholarship on women’s leadership in higher education to map the field’s intellectual structure, identify persistent barriers to women’s advancement, and examine the consequences of gendered leadership gaps. Using PRISMA 2020 guidelines, a systematic review of 115 peer-reviewed articles from the Web of Science database was conducted. The review integrates bibliometric analysis using the Bibliometrix package in R with qualitative thematic analysis to reveal dominant research streams, theoretical orientations, and evolving trends. Findings indicate that women’s under-representation in academic leadership is sustained by interlocking social, organizational, and individual-level barriers, with implications extending beyond individual careers to institutional performance and societal equity. By consolidating a fragmented literature, this study advances a more integrated understanding of women’s academic leadership and identifies critical gaps for future theorizing and empirical inquiry. The review further highlights the need for structural and cultural interventions within higher education institutions, alongside the development of individual agency to navigate gendered constraints. Overall, this study contributes a systematic and theoretically informed synthesis that clarifies what is known, exposes what remains under-explored, and sets a focused agenda for advancing research on gender and leadership in higher education.
