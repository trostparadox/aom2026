Adaptation Through Attention: Formulating Nonroutine Problems in Interorganizational Relationships

STR
Nuno Oliveira (Tilburg University)
This study examines how managers formulate nonroutine problems in interorganizational relationships. Drawing on a longitudinal, embedded case of two consecutive building projects involving the same client and contractor followed by study of GitHub, we learnt how attention and language shape adaptive responses between organization under disruption. The analysis identifies hasty coupling – a process through which managers prematurely link problem vocabularies to familiar cooperation safeguards, such as contracts or professional roles, instead of diagnosing underlying causes. Hasty coupling channels attention into distinct information engagement modes: a negotiated mode that supports joint framing and an off-the-shelf mode that constrains it. Each mode has distinct implication for the quality of problem formulation. By showing how linguistic cues activate cooperation safeguards as cognitive anchors, the study advances an attention-based explanation of adaptation to nonroutine problems in interorganizational relationships.

Knowledge Network Structure and Firm Antifragility Under Technological Decoupling Shocks

STR
Fei (Faye) Li (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Qing Gao (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications)
Technological decoupling has fundamentally disrupted global knowledge flows, posing severe challenges to firm innovation and productivity. While some firms falter under these pressures, others demonstrate antifragility — the capacity to thrive through disruptions. Prior research predominantly attributes such adaptive responses to resource slack and dynamic capabilities, yet overlooks how the structural configuration of a firm’s knowledge base shapes its ability to benefit from adversity. Addressing this gap, we examine how knowledge network structures enable firms to achieve antifragility, manifested as total factor productivity (TFP) growth, during technological decoupling. Integrating antifragility principles with knowledge network research, we theorize that two structural dimensions — cross-cluster dispersion and whole-network decentralization — preserve latent recombination pathways activatable when external knowledge flows collapse. Using the 2018 US-China technological decoupling as an exogenous shock, we analyze Chinese digital economy enterprises from 2015 to 2021 through a triple-difference approach combined with propensity score matching. Results demonstrate that both network structures significantly enhance TFP following decoupling, with effects amplified in regions featuring strong intellectual property protection and advanced digital infrastructure. TFP decomposition reveals that these structures drive technological progress rather than efficiency improvements. We also uncover an “antifragility paradox” — structures advantageous during disruption impose efficiency costs during stability. Our findings establish a structural foundation for organizational antifragility research and advances knowledge network theory into disruption contexts, offering structural strategies amid rising techno-nationalism.

From Networks to Serendipity—and Back: Ego-Network Dynamics in Entrepreneurial Journeys

STR
Emin Altun (Venice School of Management - Ca' Foscari University); Anna Moretti (Venice School of Management - Ca' Foscari University)
Serendipity—the process of making valuable and surprising discoveries through unexpected connections—plays a central role in entrepreneurial journeys. Prior research shows that network structure and embeddedness enable serendipitous outcomes, but largely conceptualizes the relationship between networking and serendipity as linear and one-directional. We address this gap through a longitudinal qualitative study of 59 entrepreneurs participating in a technology-focused accelerator. Drawing on repeated interviews, ego-network data, and field observations collected over two years, we explain how serendipity and networking co-evolve within entrepreneurs’ ego-networks. We identify three micro-mechanisms of networking—tuning the networking, contingent adapting, and boundary-spanning networking—that shape how entrepreneurs engage with alters as problems and needs emerge. These mechanisms are not deterministically associated with serendipity; rather, serendipitous outcomes arise through contingent and nonlinear matching processes. In contrast, ego-network change occurs only when serendipity occurs, revealing an unexpected asymmetry. By theorizing serendipity as both enabled by networking and as a trigger of network reconfiguration, this study advances a bidirectional, processual account of network dynamics in entrepreneurial journeys.

How Interorganizational Dissimilarities Interact to Shape Knowledge Integration Modes

STR
Yingying He; Claudia Vecciolini (King's College London); Marcela Miozzo (King's College London); Damian Grimshaw
Interorganizational collaborations are widely viewed as a vehicle for integrating distributed knowledge, yet such collaborations are typically characterized by persistent dissimilarities in partners’ knowledge bases, routines, and strategic orientations. Prior research has largely treated these dissimilarities as barriers to be bridged, offering limited insight into how they shape the unfolding of knowledge integration. Drawing on an inductive, qualitative multiple-case study of seven collaborations between manufacturing SMEs and digital technology suppliers, we examine how different types of interorganizational dissimilarities interact to structure knowledge integration processes over time. We develop a routine -orientation coevolution framework that explicates the core shaping mechanism through which persistent knowledge dissimilarity is enacted in collaboration. Specifically, we show how the evolving interplay between collaborative routines and collaboration orientations activates knowledge dissimilarity as a generative resource, stabilizes it as an operational boundary, encloses it within project-specific fusion spaces, or redirects it toward internalization. These dynamics give rise to four distinct modes of interorganizational knowledge integration. By theorizing how dissimilarities are socially shaped through patterned interaction and interpretive alignment, this study advances a process model of knowledge integration and reconceptualizes interorganizational dissimilarity as a dynamic, constitutive condition of collaboration.
