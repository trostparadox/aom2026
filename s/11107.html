Scaling Agile Beyond IT

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12
ODC

People

Author: Yanli Chen American Public University System

Session overview

Companies today want to use agile methods everywhere, not just in IT. However, we do not fully understand how this expansion happens. We also do not know how it affects employees. We used the "organizational ambidexterity" theory and "adaptive performance" theory to study this. We used a mixed-methods design. First, we conducted deep case studies on three organizations. Second, we surveyed 450 employees across 15 organizations over one year. Our findings are significant. First, successful scaling requires balance. Companies must try new agile ways (exploration) while keeping old processes stable (exploitation). Second, this balance depends on leadership. Leaders must clarify roles during the change. Third, we found a surprise. Scaling agile actually increases confusion at first. This confusion can be good or bad. If the company provides support, employees will become more flexible. If the company does not provide support, employees feel helpless. This research shows how agile moves from teams to the whole organization. We propose a "three-phase process model." This model shows how the change happens over time. It tells managers what to do at each stage. These insights help managers navigate the change. They also help protect employee well-being.
