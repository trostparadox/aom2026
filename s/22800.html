Carrying the Past into the Future-Metamorphosis of Founding Imprints During Leadership Transition

MH
Hongqin Li; Will S Harvey (University of Melbourne)
How does the influence of founders simultaneously endure and change over time? We address this question by theorizing the formation and evolution of secondhand imprints—imprints transmitted not by founders directly, but through indirect carriers—during periods of leadership transition. Drawing on longitudinal, multi-source data, we develop a process model that reveals how secondhand imprints evolve unevenly across cognitive, structural, and cultural domains. We theorize that iterative micro–macro coupling—the recursive interplay between leader actions, organizational responses, and institutional contexts—drives imprint formation and evolution. Our analysis identifies three distinct yet interdependent pathways of imprint metamorphosis: cognitive imprint sedimentation, where core values crystallize while functional applications adapt; structural imprint polymorphism, where legacy structures persist alongside repurposed functions; and cultural imprint transformation, where core elements evolve with select rituals symbolically preserved. Our findings reveal that imprint evolution is both adaptive and path-dependent: successive leaders’ reimprinting efforts are shaped by their career imprints but bounded by inherited logics and temporal embeddedness.

Legitimacy in a Faith-Based Institutional Field: The Emergence of Islamic Finance, 1963 to 2008

MH
Andrew D A Smith (University of Birmingham); Akram Beniamin (University of Oxford); Neveen Abdelrehim (Newcastle University Business School)
This article explores the emergence and evolution of Islamic banking and finance as a distinct faith-based field from 1963 to 2008. Drawing on extensive oral histories with senior executives and policymakers, archival sources, and legitimacy theory, we examine how entrepreneurs, Islamic scholars, and state institutions collaborated to reconcile deeply held religious values with the realities of operating within a capitalist economy. We identify the mechanisms through which pragmatic, moral, and cognitive legitimacy were built across diverse national and cultural contexts, while highlighting the tension between commercial viability and adherence to religious principles. Our findings reveal that successful institutional development was most sustainable in states adopting a facilitative rather than coercive approach to Islamization. This research contributes to the literature on legitimacy, institutional entrepreneurship, and faith-based economic systems, offering broader insights into how value-driven market categories emerge and evolve.

Individuals Behind Faceless Ownership: Board Appointments in Finnish State-Owned Companies

MH
Pasi Nevalainen (University of Jyväskylä)
State-owned companies face the difficult task of balancing business objectives with political considerations. The trade-off between these potentially conflicting goals is not static, as historical research has shown; however, existing evidence remains fragmented, and the nature of change over time is difficult to specify. This study systematically investigates the long-term evolution of ownership control in Finnish state-owned companies from the 1930s to 2020. The analysis focuses on individuals appointed to supervisory boards, based on the assumption that board composition reflects the underlying logic of state ownership. The study reveals a significant transition from politically appointed board members to business professionals beginning in the late 1980s. This shift indicates not only a transformation in the objectives of state ownership but also a broader process of professionalization in corporate governance. Although the empirical evidence is drawn from the Finnish context, it is plausible that similar developments have occurred in other Western countries where the state participates in the economy through ownership. Finally, the findings invite a reconsideration of traditional theoretical perspectives on the impact of state ownership on corporate strategy.
