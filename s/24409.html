From Organizational to National Belonging: A Longitudinal Study of Migrant Trainees in Germany

OB
Anna Apostolidou (University of Konstanz)
The workplace, as a microcosm of society, can influence migrants’ sense of belongingness in the host country through the mechanism of organizational identification. This study examines whether positive and negative experiences during the first weeks of a traineeship influence organizational identification later on, and whether the latter influences changes in national identification over time. We followed 208 migrant trainees in Germany across 21 months using a longitudinal survey design. Results show that early work events significantly predict organizational identification three months later, whereas weekly fluctuations did not. Organizational identification, in turn, had a weak but positive effect on the trajectory of national identification, suggesting that workplace and societal belonging are interconnected. These findings highlight the importance of early work experiences for the inclusion of migrant trainees at work and in society. The study contributes to theories of social identity and inclusion by linking micro-level work events to macro-level integration and offers practical guidance for organizations aiming to foster belonging among young migrants.
