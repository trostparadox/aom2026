Consequences of AI Embeddedness Crafting: A Self-Determination Perspective

OB
Yuyan Meng (Wuhan University of Technology); Guanglei Zhang (Wuhan University of Technology); Yu-Heng He (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Sarah Xu (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has become deeply integrated into organizational and employees’ work processes. However, AI embeddedness is not only a top-down, organization-driven process; it also involves employees’ bottom-up proactive adaptation. Based on the literature on job crafting, we propose and define AI embeddedness crafting as employees’ proactive behaviors aimed at adjusting the depth, frequency, and domain of AI-use in order to meet work demands. This study develops a scale to measure AI embeddedness crafting and, drawing on self-determination theory, examines its underlying mechanisms influencing job performance and work meaningfulness. In particular, we investigate the mediating role of perceived competence and the moderating role of task characteristics. Based on an ESM, the results show that AI embeddedness crafting positively enhances employees’ perceived competence, which in turn improves three work-related consequences. Moreover, task uncertainty and task outcome impact both positively moderate these relationships. This study elucidates the micro-level mechanisms of AI embeddedness from an individual perspective and provides theoretical insights for organizations seeking to implement AI-enabled empowerment.
