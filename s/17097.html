Virtual Reality and Consumer Service in Housing Transactions

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 1
CTO

Authors: Yidi WANG Communication University of China; Zan Yang KTH Royal Institute of Technology

Session overview

Virtual reality (VR) technologies are increasingly adopted in consumer service settings to mitigate information gaps and enhance decision-making. However, empirical evidence on how VR reshapes service efficiency and service quality in complex, high-stakes transactions remains limited. This study examines the role of VR in the housing market, a setting characterized by extreme information asymmetry, high transaction costs, and tightly coupled online–offline service processes. We collaborate with the largest second-hand housing brokerage platform in China and exploit the platform-wide introduction of soft VR property tours. Using a multi-stage dataset that links online search behavior, offline property viewings, agent interactions, and final transactions, we investigate how VR affects both the efficiency and quality of consumer services. Our findings show that VR substantially improves service efficiency by reducing time on market and increasing the conversion rate of offline viewings. VR enables consumers to pre-screen properties more effectively, lowering unnecessary offline visits and accelerating transactions. We further show that VR partially substitutes for agents’ informational roles, reducing reliance on agent experience while enhancing overall service productivity. Beyond efficiency, VR reshapes service quality by narrowing mismatches between consumers’ preferences and purchased housing attributes, while simultaneously increasing consumers’ willingness to pay. This price effect reflects both preference updating during search and strengthened seller bargaining power during negotiation. By integrating digital technology into a traditionally human-intensive service process, this study advances research on technology-enabled service transformation and offers practical insights into how firms can strategically deploy VR to improve consumer service outcomes in complex markets.
