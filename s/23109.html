What Makes Talent Management Effective? The Role of Strategic HR Integration and Transparency

HR
Sarah Kost (University of Lucerne); Anna Sender (Lucerne University of Applied Sciences and Arts)
Organizations increasingly recognize talent management (TM) as a strategic response to complex human resource management (HRM) challenges posed by today’s competitive and dynamic labor market. Yet, making TM effective remains a challenge and a recognized gap in research. This study advances understanding of the conditions under which TM is effective at the organizational level. Using the HRM strength perspective, we argue that the strategic integration of the HR function in organizations fosters both transparency in TM processes and outcomes, which leads to greater TM effectiveness. In addition, we hypothesize that the inclusivity of TM practices strengthens this effect. Specifically, we postulate that organizations with greater strategically integrated HR function, transparency, and inclusivity can create a strong TM system that is characterized by high levels of distinctiveness, consistency, and consensus. Using survey data from HR professionals in 183 organizations in Switzerland with operational TM practices, we found that transparency in TM mediated the positive relationship between the strategic integration of the HR function and TM effectiveness. However, the inclusivity of TM practices did not significantly moderate these indirect effects. These results position transparency as a signal-transmitting mechanism through which strategically integrated HR functions create stronger TM systems, thereby enhancing TM effectiveness.

The Role of Managerial Human Capital in Resource Acquisition and Deployment

HR
Tsutomu Doiguchi (Augusta University); Dorothea Roumpi (The Pennsylvania State University)
In the strategic human capital literature, a substantial body of work has demonstrated that managerial human capital enhances the deployment of unit-level human capital resources (HCR), thereby generating synergistic value beyond what these resources can produce in isolation. Prior studies, however, have largely overlooked the role of managerial human capital in the acquisition of unit-level human capital resources, constraining a holistic view of human capital combinations. This research, therefore, addresses the following question: How does managerial human capital influence both the acquisition and deployment of unit-level human capital resources, ultimately contributing to organizational performance? Drawing from the theoretical framework of human capital combinations, we propose two distinct mechanisms through which managers contribute to competitive advantage: HCR acquisition and HCR deployment. Furthermore, we propose the concept of HCR orchestration, suggesting that these mechanisms are not independent but instead represent sequential processes whereby managers make acquisition decisions with anticipated deployment in mind. To test these hypotheses, we employ longitudinal data from more than 500 Japanese anime films and detailed work histories of over 1,800 creators. The results support the HCR orchestration mechanism and illustrate how managerial human capital becomes a strategic resource through its influence on both acquisition and deployment.

Automated HR System and Firm Innovation Performance: Competing Paths and Pseudo Proliferation

HR
Hossein Heidarian Ghaleh (University of East London)
The adoption of automated HR systems and HR metrics has become prominent in the agenda of human resource scholars and practitioners, yet their quests are open to criticism and the focus of much debate. Hastily, utilizing automated HR systems often ends up forcing employees rather than predicting their behavior. Fascinated by the efficiency and effectiveness of automation and metrics, scholars may have unintentionally overlooked the importance of incorporating employees' discretionary behavior and social interactions into designing their automated HR systems. Accordingly, this study is a departure from the past research. We propose an automated intellectual capital-enhancing HR system (AIC-EHR) which equally prioritizing target achievement and employees’ social interaction and discretionary behaviors. To do so, we propose that AIC-HER enhances firm innovation performance through the mediating role of employees’ prioritization of metricized behavior and taking charge. Furthermore, we attempt to show that a highly intensive automated HR system triggers employees’ behavioral responses through pseudo proliferation, where employees attempt to circumvent work processes and inflate their performance outcome to safeguard themselves without producing authentic performance. Conducting study 1 and 2 and collecting data from large fintech banks in two waves of time, we found empirical support for our prediction. We close our study with implications for theory and serve practitioners with several insights. We also open new frontiers for future research.

Shared Responsibility in Talent Management: the Devolution from HR Professionals to Line Managers

HR
Sarah Kost (University of Lucerne); Anna Sender (Lucerne University of Applied Sciences and Arts)
Talent management (TM) is a top priority for senior executives, HR professionals, and line managers. As a joint venture among multiple actors, its effectiveness depends on coordinated, multi-actor involvement across the TM process, with interdependent roles that must be aligned and coordinated rather than executed in isolation. In practice, however, patterns of TM devolution—defined as the allocation of TM responsibilities formally undertaken by HR professionals and now increasingly shifted to line managers—vary significantly, raising the question of how collaboration between HR professionals and line managers can lead to greater TM effectiveness. Employing a sequential mixed-methods, two-actor research design and drawing on the human resource management (HRM) strength perspective, we explored how TM devolution can create a strong TM system characterized by high levels of distinctiveness, consistency, and consensus. First, we hypothesized that the relationship between TM devolution and TM effectiveness follows an inverted U shape. Using survey data from HR professionals in 183 organizations in Switzerland, we confirmed this hypothesis and found that TM was least effective at very low and high levels of TM devolution and was most effective at medium levels. Second, interviews with 47 HR professionals and 16 line managers provided insights into how collaboration between HR professionals and line managers can create a strong TM system and, in turn, enhance TM effectiveness.

To Be Unique and to Belong: Cross-Level Effects of Talent Management Practices on Talent Job Perform

HR
Rentao Miao (Capital University of Economics and Business); Zhengrui Li (Capital University of Economics and Business); Fang Lee Cooke (Monash University)
Drawing on optimal distinctiveness theory, this study integrates both talent task performance and relationship performance into the research framework to systematically investigate the multi-level impacts and mechanisms of TM on talent job performance. Using a four-wave time-lagged design with 367 employees, 55 human resource managers and 55 immediate supervisors from 55 companies in China, we found that TM at the organizational level is positively related to both individual task performance and relationship performance; uniqueness need satisfaction and belongingness need satisfaction respectively show partial mediating roles in the above relationships, and the organizational justice climate positively moderates the relationship between TM and uniqueness need satisfaction. Furthermore, individuals’ talent status awareness and the organizational justice climate jointly moderate the effects of TM on the satisfaction of both uniqueness and belongingness needs. The positive relationships between TM and uniqueness need satisfaction and belongingness need satisfaction are weaker in a high organizational justice climate where the individual is aware of being recognized by the organization as a talent.
