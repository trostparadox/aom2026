A Theory of Micro Resilience in AI-Enabled Supply Chain Decision Making

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Author: Steven Formaneck Minnesota State University Mankato

Session overview

Organizations are integrating artificial intelligence (AI) into supply chain planning, forecasting, anddecision making. While AI promises improvements in accuracy and efficiency, human decision makers often struggle to adapt cognitively and behaviorally to AI-enabled workflows. This extended abstract develops a conceptual theory of micro resilience—the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral adjustments individuals make when interacting with AI-based decision tools. Drawing from managerial cognition, behavioral operations, resilience theory, and human–AI collaboration research, the framework explains how micro resilience emerges through three mechanisms: adaptive sensemaking, calibrated trust, and corrective action cycles. These mechanisms shape how individuals interpret AI recommendations, regulate uncertainty, and adjust decisions. The theory proposes that micro resilience enhances decision quality by improving override accuracy, reducing cognitive overload, and learning from AI feedback. It also identifies boundary conditions—such as algorithmic transparency, workload intensity, and organizational norms—that influence micro resilience processes unfold. This work contributes to Management and Organizational Cognition by introducing micro resilience as a construct explaining variation in human–AI decision performance. It extends behavioral operations research by integrating resilience processes into decision making and offers actionable insights for designing AI-enabled workflows that support human adaptability, and learning. The framework highlights future empirical research on trust calibration, and resilience-based training interventions in AI-supported organizational contexts.
