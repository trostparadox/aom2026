Can Social Signals Speak the Same to Human and Machines?

HR
Oscar Aliaga (University of Massachusetts Lowell)
The use of artificial intelligence in human resource management processes is becoming increasingly common, particularly for time-intensive tasks such as résumé screening and candidate evaluation. This development calls for greater scholarly attention to AI-augmented selection and to how quantitative social signals influence candidate assessments by both human and AI evaluators. The present study examines the signaling function of contacts and endorsements embedded in LinkedIn profiles. Drawing on signaling theory and social evaluation theory, we propose that these quantitative indicators operate as social signals that convey candidates’ otherwise unobservable traits of warmth and competence. In turn, these evaluations shape perceptions of employability in contemporary recruitment contexts. Using an experimental design, the study tests how human and AI evaluators interpret quantitative social signals and assesses the extent to which their evaluations converge or diverge. Overall, the findings advance understanding of nontraditional digital signals in personnel selection and extend signaling theory to AI-augmented hiring environments in which human and algorithmic evaluators increasingly coexist.

Buying an Insurance for Those Being Brokered:Multiplex Brokerage and Work Proactivity

HR
Man Cao (Nanjing University of Science and Technology); Hongzhi Chen (Fudan University); Shuming Zhao (Nanjing University); Yixuan Zhao (Nanjing University); Jiahe Zhang; Boya Xu (Nanjing University of Science and Technology)
While the benefits of brokerage for brokers are well established, far less is known about the experiences of those who are being brokered (i.e., alters). In this paper, we introduce multiplex brokerage — open triads consisting of both friendship and work advice ties — as a relational context that enhances alters’ psychological safety and their proactive behavior. Further, we theorize that brokers’ collectivism would strengthen this relationship. Using survey data from 170 network brokers and their 409 alters in Chinese work teams, we find that multiplex brokerage increases alters’ psychological safety, which subsequently promotes work proactivity. Contrary to our hypothesis, brokers’ collectivism weakens, rather than strengthens, the positive effect of multiplex brokerage on alters’ psychological safety. Our study shifts the focus of brokerage research from brokers to alters, introduces the construct of multiplex brokerage, and applies a multilevel design to explore how structural and relational features jointly shape workplace interactions in a Chinese cultural context.

The Elusive Returns to AI Skills: Evidence from a Field Experiment

HR
Anastasia Danilov (Humboldt University Berlin); Teo Firpo (Humboldt University Berlin); Lukas Niemann
As firms increasingly adopt Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies, how they adjust hiring practices for skilled workers remains unclear. This paper investigates whether AI-related skills are rewarded in talent recruitment by conducting a large-scale correspondence study in the United Kingdom. We submit 1,185 résumés to vacancies across a range of occupations, randomly assigning the presence or absence of advanced AI-related qualifications. These AI qualifications are added to résumés as voluntary signals and not explicitly requested in the job postings. We find no statistically significant effect of listing AI qualifications in résumés on interview callback rates. However, a heterogeneity analysis reveals some positive and significant effects for positions in Engineering and Marketing. These results are robust to controlling for the total number of skills listed in job ads, the degree of match between résumés and job descriptions, and the level of expertise required. In an exploratory analysis, we find stronger employer responses to AI-related skills in industries with lower exposure to AI technologies. These findings suggest that the labor market valuation of AI-related qualifications is context-dependent and shaped by sectoral innovation dynamics.

Fostering and Utilizing Team HR Flexibility: Role of Paradoxical Leadership and Task Interdependence

HR
Hanyu Gao (University of Macau); Yan Zhang (Peking University); Ying Zhou (Wuhan University of Technology)
Drawing on unit HCR emergency theory, we propose that paradoxical leadership cultivates human resource flexibility within teams, which in turn, promotes team adaptive behavior and social integration simultaneously. We also examine task interdependence as a boundary condition shaping these effects. To test our model, we conducted a multi-wave, multi-source field study with 1210 employees and 187 supervisors nested within 187 teams from ten firms in China. Results show that human resource flexibility mediates the effect of paradoxical leadership on team adaptive behavior and social integration. Although task interdependence does not moderate the relationship between paradoxical leadership and human resource flexibility, it weakens the positive effects of human resource flexibility on both outcomes. We discuss the theoretical and practical implications of the resource-based mechanism promoting team adaptive behavior and social integration, and how paradoxical leadership cultivate it.

Employee Mobility Barriers and Firms’ Breakthrough Innovation: Evidence from Judicial Litigation

HR
Zheng zhiqi (Nanjing University); Songyang Qi (Nanjing University Business School); jiaqian chen (Nanjing University Business School)
Breakthrough innovation serves as the core underpinning for enterprises to break technological path dependence and build long-term competitive advantages. As the carrier of knowledge, the dynamic allocation of human capital is crucial to the achievement of breakthrough innovation. While employee mobility may give rise to risks of core knowledge spillover and talent drain, strategies that excessively restrict employee mobility are likely to conflict with the demands for heterogeneous knowledge inflow and risk-taking experimentation required for breakthrough innovation. In practice, non-compete agreements are the mainstream tool for enterprises to establish employee mobility barriers. Existing studies have mostly focused on the restrictive effects of non-compete agreements on employee mobility, yet failed to systematically reveal their in-depth impacts on breakthrough innovation, especially overlooking the inherent tension between "human capital protection" and "innovation vitality stimulation". Taking Chinese A-share listed companies on the Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges from 2014 to 2023 as the research sample, this study innovatively adopts enterprises’ active initiation of non-compete litigation as the measurement indicator of employee mobility barriers, addressing the limitations of existing macro-level indicators for gauging employee mobility barriers. By employing the multi-period difference-in-differences (DID) method, this paper thoroughly analyzes the impact of employee mobility barriers on corporate breakthrough innovation and its mechanisms. The empirical results show that employee mobility barriers exert a significant negative effect on corporate breakthrough innovation, which functions through two channels: weakening the attractiveness to inventors and reducing the risk-taking level of top management teams.
