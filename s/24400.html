Phase-Specific Entrepreneurial Self-Efficacy and Resilience in Immersive Entrepreneurship Training

ENT
Matteo Borghi (Henley Business School); Phatcharasiri Ratcharak (University of Greenwich)
Debates about short, immersive entrepreneurship programs increasingly focus on what changes and for whom. Building on social cognitive theory and a process view of venture creation, we examine phase-specific entrepreneurial self-efficacy (ESE)—searching, planning, marshaling, implementing-people, implementing-financial—as the proximal mechanism linking pedagogy to motivation, and resilience as a boundary condition. Using matched pre–post surveys from four in-person cohorts (2024-2025) in the UK and Malaysia, each following a consistent, mentor-led one-week experiential sequence, we test (a) pre–post change in phase-specific ESE, (b) whether individual differences in ESE gains are associated with post-training entrepreneurial intentions, and (c) resilience moderation. Results show significant gains across all five ESE phases. At the main-effects stage, implementing-people gains relate positively to intentions, whereas marshaling gains relate negatively; searching, planning, and financial gains are non-significant. In the moderation model, resilience has a positive main effect and selectively strengthens the translation of marshaling and financial capability gains into intentions, with a marginal attenuation for implementing-people. We advance entrepreneurship education theory by clarifying which phase-specific capability gains are proximally motivational in short formats and when resilience strengthens capability-to-intention translation. Practically, we recommend sequencing to build implementing-people capability early and pairing marshaling/finance content with resilience-informed appraisal.
