Session overview

Despite decades of organizational change, gender inequalities in pay, promotion, and access to career-advancing relationships continue to shape who advances and who exits the career pipeline. A growing body of research highlights the importance of professional networks in shaping career outcomes, yet less is known about how gender inequality emerges through the interaction of organizational systems, relational processes, and individual experiences. This symposium integrates four papers that examine these dynamics across multiple levels of analysis. The papers document where gender inequality persists in organizational outcomes, including firm-level variation in pay gaps, and examine relational, temporal, and psychological processes that shape access to and engagement with career-relevant ties. Together, the symposium shows how organizational reward systems, sponsorship and mobility dynamics, and emotional barriers to networking work in tandem to produce and sustain gender inequality over time, while pointing to actionable levers for change at both the organizational and individual levels.
