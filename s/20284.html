Session overview

Entrepreneurial ecosystems increasingly recognize students as vital actors in shaping innovation and social responsiveness, yet entrepreneurship education still struggles to identify the pedagogical key that consistently unlocks entrepreneurial action. This poster presents a dual-pillar conceptual model for educating future innovators and entrepreneurs that integrates empathy-driven engagement with technology-enhanced capacity building. The model argues that empathy-based learning strengthens students’ capacity to identify socially relevant problems, form a responsible entrepreneurial identity, and engage stakeholders in meaningful ways. In parallel, technology-enhanced learning develops students’ ability to prototype, validate, and iterate solutions, moving beyond abstract ideation toward full-cycle implementation. Building upon experiential and action-based perspectives, the study articulates four propositions linking the pillars to entrepreneurial self-efficacy, venture implementation capacity, social impact potential, and universities’ ecosystem contribution. It also proposes a qualitative, multilevel exploratory research design to refine and empirically examine the framework through interviews, focus groups, and course-document analysis using reflexive thematic analysis.
