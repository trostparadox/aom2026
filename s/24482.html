One Size Doesn’t Fit All: Evidence on Women’s Motivation to Sign Up for Entrepreneurship Training

OMT
Naja Pape (INSEAD)
Despite increasing evidence of the benefits of entrepreneurship training, women enroll in such programs at disproportionately lower rates than men. In this paper, I examine whether the way programs are currently designed and communicated contributes to these lower signup rates. I triangulate literature on female participation in male-typed fields, research on inclusion, and an inductive study with stakeholders and entrepreneurs in India’s entrepreneurial ecosystem. Building on these insights, I theorize that inclusion signaling – the intentional design and presentation of training features to convey inclusivity to potential participants – is a potential remedy, increasing aspiring female entrepreneurs’ perceptions of a program’s inclusivity and thereby shaping both their preferences for such training and their decision to sign up. I test this theory in a pre-registered choice experiment in the field with 586 aspiring female entrepreneurs in India. I first elicited participants’ ex ante preferences for inclusive training by asking them to choose between realistic but hypothetical workshop descriptions, before giving them a real opportunity to sign up for one of two actual training programs – one default and one inclusive. Results show that women chose the inclusive workshops 55.7% of the time – significantly more than expected by random chance – and this preference pattern is mirrored in actual program signups, with 54.1% enrolling in the inclusive version. Importantly, perceived inclusivity, my theorized mechanism, strongly predicts signup, with rates rising from 44% at the lowest perceived inclusivity level to 63% at the highest. These statistically robust effects identify inclusion signaling as a low-cost, evidence-based lever for increasing women’s participation at the pre-entry stage of entrepreneurship, where gender disparities first emerge and can most effectively be addressed.
