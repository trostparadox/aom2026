Session overview

Strategic human capital is fundamental to firm performance and innovation, yet firms increasingly face labor markets where workforce formation, evaluation, and allocation unfold through structures they only partially control—credentialed training pipelines, gatekeeping intermediaries, geographic dispersion, demographic constraints, and digital platforms. Traditional research focuses primarily on internal practices such as selection, training, and retention, treating labor market conditions as exogenous constraints. However, contemporary firms must actively engage external labor market structures that shape workforce availability, composition, and deployment well before and after hiring decisions. This symposium brings together five papers examining how firms build competitive advantage when human capital processes extend beyond organizational boundaries. Spanning diverse empirical contexts and methods, the papers examine critical stages of labor market engagement: entry into training pipelines, evaluation and screening by intermediaries, internal versus external deployment during expansion, long-run workforce renewal, and the observability of labor market dynamics. Together, the symposium advances our understanding of strategic human capital management under conditions of partial firm control, highlighting trade-offs between access and deployment across the talent lifecycle.
