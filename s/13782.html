The Instability of Informal Workplace Roles in LMX Relationships

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7
OB

People

Author: Elizabeth Jordan McBride University of Georgia

Session overview

Extending research on leader-member exchange, I challenge the inherent assumption of stability in the informal roles that develop from high LMX relationships. Leader-member exchange is observed as dyadic relationships that form between a supervisor and an employee. Despite the emerging research revealing some short-term fluctuations in perceptions of LMX, these dyadic relationships are widely assumed to remain stable once crystalized. First, I contest this assumption by exploring when a high leader-member exchange relationship can dissolve, resulting in a loss of the informal role that was once solidified. Additionally, I detail how the stronger the LMX relationship, the greater perceived risk the leader experiences by integrating interdependence theory. Finally, I explore potential reactions a leader may have when there is a risk of informal role dissolution. In particular, this threat may result in the leader taking two routes as a means for self-preservation, replacement and undermining. I conclude that LMX relationships are in fact malleable after they crystalize and challenge the idea that high LMX relationships always produce positive outcomes for both the leader and follower.
