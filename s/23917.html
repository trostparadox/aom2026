Mission-Oriented Innovation: National Strategic Planning for General-Purpose Technologies

TIM
Mengyu Zhuang (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Wenjun Li (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Yawen Li (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Si Zhang (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences); Jingwen Liang (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications)
As global technological competition grows, innovation in general-purpose technologies (GPTs) has become very important for national innovation systems and technological competitiveness. But existing research mainly looks at basic or applied research, and it does not say much about how GPTs innovation can be supported and what mechanisms drive it. In this study, we use mission-oriented innovation theory. We take the 14th Five-Year Plan as the main institutional context, and we use artificial intelligence as a representative GPT. We build firm-level GPTs innovation indicators with the K-means++ algorithm, and we run empirical analyses to study the effects of national strategic planning. The results show that alignment with national strategic planning clearly promotes GPTs innovation in two ways. It increases firms’ tolerance for innovation failure, and it strengthens the intensity of collaborative R&D. Through these two ways, national strategic planning reduces behavioral market failures and organizational coordination failures. Additional analyses show that these effects are stronger in technology-intensive industries, when competitive pressure is higher, and when the institutional environment is relatively flexible. This study deepens our understanding of the micro-level mechanisms by which national strategic planning shapes GPTs innovation, and it offers policy suggestions for how to improve mission-oriented science and technology strategies.
