More Ideas, Less Innovation? AI-Enabled Ideation and the Decoupling of Ownership and Identification

Authors: Philipp Bubenzer HES-SO / ETH Zurich; Anika Stephan

Session overview

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming a routine partner in organizational ideation, enabling teams to broaden search, accelerate recombination, and reduce experimentation costs (Haefner, Wincent, Parida, & Gassmann, 2021). Yet accounts of whether AI-assisted ideation improves innovation performance remain mixed and often paradoxical: gains in idea volume, speed, or novelty do not reliably translate into selection quality, development momentum, implementation, or long-term value creation (Haefner et al., 2021; Raisch & Krakowski, 2021). We argue that this ideation–implementation gap is partly driven by a neglected attachment mechanism: AI reshapes how innovators psychologically attach to ideas from human–AI interaction. Building on psychological ownership and social identification (Weisman, Raghuram, & Garud, 2022; Ashforth & Mael, 1989; Pierce, Kostova, & Dirks, 2001), we develop a process model that distinguishes idea-level psychological ownership (“this is my/our idea”) from practice-level identification with AI-enabled innovation (“this is how people like me innovate”). We propose that AI can strengthen identification with an innovation practice while simultaneously weakening ownership of ideas by externalizing generative agency (Vanneste & Puranam, 2024). The model specifies how agency attributions shape appropriation behaviors that (re)create ownership, how identity work shapes identification (Pratt, Schultz, Ashforth, & Ravasi, 2016), and how their alignment (or decoupling) predicts implementation effort and performance. We introduce a typology of attachment profiles (AI co-creators, compliant adopters, resistant owners, disengaged distancers) and theorize boundary conditions that determine when AI-enabled ideation yields sustained innovation rather than failed implementation.
