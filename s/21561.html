On "Good Teaching": Organizational Factors that Enable and Constrain Good College Teaching

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 12
ODC

People

Authors: Catherine Zhang University of Pennsylvania; Damani White-Lewis University of Pennsylvania; Dennis Espejo University of Pennsylvania

Session overview

Debates over the quality and purpose of college teaching have intensified amid growing public scrutiny of higher education, shifting institutional missions, and evolving sociopolitical pressures. At research-intensive universities in particular, instructors are often expected to deliver high-quality, student-centered instruction within organizational environments that prioritize research productivity and provide uneven support for teaching. Drawing on 110 semi-structured interviews with instructors across three R1 universities, this study examines how postsecondary instructors conceptualize “good” teaching and identifies the organizational factors that facilitate and constrain their ability to enact effective pedagogical practices. Findings indicate that instructors widely associate good teaching with student engagement, clarity, responsiveness, and adaptability, yet emphasize that their capacity to teach well is shaped primarily by organizational context rather than individual motivation or skill. These findings highlight teaching quality as an organizational outcome produced through institutional policies, cultural norms, and resource allocation. We conclude by discussing implications for administrators seeking to create productive, non-intrusive, and cooperative teaching environments that support instructional excellence while respecting faculty autonomy.
