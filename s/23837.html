The Attributional Perspective on Coworkers’ Reactions to Unethical Pro-Family Behavior

OB
Jie Xiao (Renmin University of China); Yixuan Jing (Shandong University); Shijie Zhao (Renmin University of China)
Despite clear prohibitions from organizations and leaders, unethical pro-family behavior (UPFB) persists in the workplace and substantially undermines organizational interests. To unravel this puzzle, we shift scholarly attention from how UPFB occurs and its effects on actors, to the reactions of third-party coworkers. Drawing on attribution theory, we identify family-financial-pressure perception as a key boundary condition that shapes coworkers’ attributional pathways, which in turn guides their decisions to internally report an actor’s UPFB. Specifically, when coworkers perceive higher family financial pressure of the UPFB actor, they tend to attribute UPFB to external factors and experience perspective taking, thereby reducing the likelihood of internal reporting. Conversely, when family-financial-pressure perception is lower, coworkers are more likely to attribute UPFB to internal factors, which fosters moral obligation and further increases the tendency to report internally. Our hypotheses are supported through two pre-registered studies: a two-wave critical incident survey and a scenario-based experiment. We contribute to the literature on UPFB, broader unethical prosocial behavior, and workplace internal reporting dynamics.
