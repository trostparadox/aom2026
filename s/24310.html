Sycophancy as a Double-Edged Sword: Dual Pathways to Satisfaction in AI Companionship

CTO
Jiayi Weng (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Qianxu Tan (The University of Hong Kong); Yifan Fan (Southeast University)
AI companions often produce emotionally expressive responses. Among them, sycophancy which refers to excessive alignment with user opinions arising from training-induced adaptation in LLMs, can influence user experience in complex ways. Drawing on the Computers are Social Actors paradigm and the Emotions as Social Information framework, we propose a dual-pathway framework. Sycophantic responses from AI companions increase user satisfaction by enhancing perceived empathy while reduce satisfaction by lowering perceived sincerity. Two experiments support this competitive mediation. We also find that relationship closeness between user and AI weakens both effects. Our findings offer insights into human–AI interaction and guide the design of adaptive AI companions.
