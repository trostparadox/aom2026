Institutional Density and Sustainability Ambidexterity:Towards a Process Model for Born Global Firms

ENT
Madhurima Basu (Symbiosis Institute of International Business)
This study examines how social born global ventures navigate divergent sustainability expectations while entering diverse institutional density contexts of developed and developing markets. Drawing on institutional theory, we introduce the concept of "sustainability ambidexterity," highlighting the ability of these start-ups to reconcile compliance-driven sustainability prominent in developed markets with embedded, socially inclusive sustainability dominant in developing contexts. This study introduces a conceptual framework mapping institutional density against sustainability orientation of social born global firms, explaining the way organizations navigate varying sustainability expectations across international markets by positioning themselves within the embedded, ambidextrous, or compliance-dominant zones. The framework is substantiated through theoretical pattern matching using illustrations of Indian and African agri-tech born globals, and further extended into theoretically grounded and testable propositions that culminate in a process model of sustainability ambidexterity. This study contributes to the international entrepreneurship, institutional complexity and sustainability literature by illustrating how institutional diversity shapes sustainability strategies of early internationalizing firms and offers practical implications for entrepreneurs and policymakers.
