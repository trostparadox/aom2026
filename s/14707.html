Quiet Influence: Exploring Mediated Social Capital Among Introverted Entrepreneurial CEOs

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 3
ENT

People

Authors: Noor ul Amin University of Science and Technology of China; Qingxiong Weng

Session overview

Research on entrepreneurial leadership has long emphasized extroversion as a driver of networking and external engagement, while introversion remains an understudied and often misunderstood dimension of leadership behavior. This conceptual paper introduces the idea of mediated social capital, proposing that introverted CEOs can extend their external reach by leveraging the social networks of extroverted top management team members rather than personally engaging in boundary-spanning activities. Drawing on insights from leadership composition and team complementarity, this framework explains how introverted CEOs organize and balance their teams to maintain control, trust, and centrality while accessing critical business and political ties. It further highlights potential vulnerabilities, such as dependency, information asymmetry, and the risk of CEOs becoming “visitors” in their own networks. Five propositions are developed to clarify how introverted CEOs construct, monitor, and benefit from mediated ties in entrepreneurial ventures. Although this paper is conceptual, interviews with approximately 20 CEOs are currently in progress to empirically explore and refine these mechanisms. The ongoing qualitative study will illustrate how introverted CEOs in entrepreneurial and small-firm contexts build mediated social capital, access external networks, and maintain leadership centrality. This research contributes to entrepreneurship and leadership scholarship by reframing introverted leadership as strategically adaptive and introducing mediated social capital as a distinct structural mechanism for external resource acquisition.
