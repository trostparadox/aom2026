Managing Intergenerational Conflict Through Culture: Leadership-Based Transmission Mechanisms

Session overview

Stereotypical assumptions about generational differences often frame workplace interactions and can intensify intergenerational tensions, yet research linking generations to organizational culture has produced inconsistent findings. Thus, the study shifts attention from cohort differences in cultural outcomes to how culture is transmitted across cohorts, and how these transmission processes can prevent or resolve intergenerational conflict by shaping expectations, emotional climates, and day-to-day interaction norms. Drawing on the Competing Values Framework, emotional leadership theory, and a behavioral view of culture, we examine whether generational cohort moderates the effects of leadership styles and relational-emotional mechanisms on culture. Using survey data (n = 816), MANOVA results show no significant generational differences in culture profiles, suggesting cultural stability across cohorts. However, multigroup analyses reveal generation-contingent leadership-culture pathways, indicating that leaders foster shared norms through different relational and emotional routes for different cohorts. In turn, these routes may reduce friction around authority, communication style, and mutual respect. The findings position leadership-driven culture transmission as a conflict management resource that supports cohesion in multigenerational workplaces while cautioning against deficit-based generational stereotypes.
