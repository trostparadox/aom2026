Deprivation or Learning? The Impact of Successful Aging at Work on Young Workers' Job Crafting

CM
Zhuojing Li (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Qiaozhuan Liang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Jie Li; Yifan Li
As the number of older workers in the workplace continues to rise worldwide, organizations are implementing more initiatives to support older workers in achieving successful aging at work. Yet these efforts have largely ignored the perceptions of younger workers. Based on the contrast and assimilation model of social comparison theory, this study examines the dual-edged effects of successful aging at work on young workers. We construct a dual-path model to confirm that successful aging at work can both promote and reduce young workers' job crafting through perceived learning and relative deprivation. In addition, positive intergenerational affect serves as a moderator that enhances the positive relationship between successful aging at work and perceived learning while diminishing the positive relationship between successful aging at work and relative deprivation. Two time-lagged surveys (Study 1: N = 317; Study 2: N = 311) supported our hypotheses. We enrich the literature on successful aging at work by exploring the double-edged sword effect of successful aging at work on young workers, providing novel insights for both academic research and practical applications in managing a multigenerational workforce.
