Session overview

Creativity is crucial for both employees and organizations, as it enables innovation, enhances performance, and fosters a competitive advantage. Although a growing body of work recognizes that creativity unfolds as a dynamic journey rather than a discrete outcome, research remains fragmented in explaining how employees sustain creative engagement over time—across idea generation, implementation, and the setbacks that accompany creative work. This symposium addresses this fragmentation by presenting four papers that integrate self-regulation, daily resources, interpretive processes, and social context in the study of creativity. Specifically, this symposium asks one central question: How do individuals sustain creative engagement over time—across idea generation, implementation, and setbacks—given shifting self-regulatory demands, fluctuating resources, and social-contextual cues? The four papers examine this question using complementary approaches and methods, including studies of regulatory modes associated with creativity versus improvisation (as mindsets vs. enacted behaviors), daily sleep quality as a resource shaping problem-oriented daydreaming and depletion, managerial portfolio framing and failure normalization as predictors of resilience after idea rejection, and a multi-level model linking undergraduate institution ranking to creativity through cognitive orientations, supportive contexts, and ideation networks. The symposium will conclude with a discussion led by Dr. Christina Shalley, who will integrate the papers, highlight boundary conditions and mechanisms, and identify future directions for research on sustaining creative engagement.
