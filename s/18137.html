Session overview

What does it mean to conduct organizational research responsibly in worlds marked by violence, dispossession, and inequality? Despite growing recognition of the limitations of WEIRD samples in Management and Psychology, organizational knowledge remains shaped by geopolitical and epistemic biases centered in the Global North, constraining its relevance and justice-oriented potential. This symposium brings together scholars researching precarious environments and marginalized communities to critically examine how knowledge is produced, represented, and legitimized in organizational and social research. Moving beyond the mere relocation of empirical settings, the symposium foregrounds epistemic justice, voice, representation, and researcher responsibility in contexts marked by structural inequality, environmental conflict, and political violence. Rather than rejecting rigor, it advances alternative standards grounded in relational accountability, reflexivity, transparency, and co-production. The first presentation examines ethical tensions surrounding voice, representation, and co-authorship in precarious fieldwork. The second explores social cartography and collaborative autoethnography as decolonial interventions in Indigenous and extractive-conflict territories in Latin America. The third analyzes engaged, non-extractive university–community partnerships in contexts of environmental racism. The final presentation addresses meta-ignorance and meta-insensitivity in research with marginalized groups, proposing epistemic humility and sustained accountability as political commitments. Together, the symposium positions precarious contexts not as sites of deficiency, but as critical spaces for methodological innovation and alternative organizational imaginaries.
