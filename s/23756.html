How Social Ventures Combine Effectuation, Bricolage, and Bootstrapping.

ENT
Morteza Sardari (University of Niagara Falls, Canada); Manzar Mousavizadeh
Social ventures are expected to create social value while remaining financially viable, often under enduring conditions of resource scarcity and uncertainty. Although prior research highlights effectuation, bricolage, and bootstrapping as key responses to these challenges, these logics are typically examined in isolation. This study adopts a more integrated perspective by exploring how social entrepreneurs combine and prioritize these resourcefulness logics and how such patterns relate to venture performance. Drawing on survey data from 202 Canadian social ventures, we show that ventures that rely strongly and simultaneously on effectuation, bricolage, and bootstrapping report higher levels of performance than other social ventures. We further find that founders’ characteristics shape how these logics are prioritized: growth-oriented entrepreneurs tend to favor effectuation over bricolage and bootstrapping, whereas higher levels of education are associated with a greater emphasis on bricolage and bootstrapping relative to effectuation. By highlighting resourcefulness as a matter of orchestration and trade-offs rather than isolated tactics, this study offers a more nuanced and practice-relevant understanding of how social ventures mobilize resources and sustain their missions under constraint.
