Outplacement Training Needs Among Replaced and Soon-to-Be Replaced Workers in South Korea

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1

People

Authors: Shinhee Jeong The University of Texas at Tyler; Jinwoo Hwang Korea University; Sumi Lee Texas A&M University

Session overview

This study conducts a systematic training needs assessment of outplacement programs for displaced and soon-to-be displaced workers in South Korea. Utilizing Borich’s needs assessment model and the Locus-For-Focus (LFF) analysis, this descriptive study examines perceived discrepancies between current and future importance across 24 outplacement training topics categorized into five domain areas and identifies priorities among them. In addition, the study explores gender-based differences in identified training priorities. Analyzing data consisting of 250 participants, the findings reveal that outplacement needs are multidimensional and are most strongly concentrated in psychological adjustment and future-oriented career planning domains. Gender-specific patterns further indicate that male workers prioritized needs related to emotion and health management while female workers emphasized access to information, asset and financial planning, and institutional support resources. These differences suggest that workers experience and prioritize outplacement needs differently depending on gender, highlighting the importance of individualized support strategies. The results suggest the importance of tailored, needs-based outplacement programs that address both immediate adjustment and longer-term career transitions, contributing to the advancement of data-driven outplacement practice.
