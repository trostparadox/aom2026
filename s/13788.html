Non-Recognition and the Erosion of Meaningfulness: When Pay-for-Performance Matters

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Qianyuan Xiao Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Qinxuan Gu Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Session overview

While employee recognition is a vital motivational tool, organizations often fail to acknowledge all worthy performers—a phenomenon termed “non-recognition”. Drawing on two-market theory and justice literature, we investigate the detrimental impact of non-recognition on task performance through work meaningfulness, and how this process is jointly moderated by Pay for Individual Performance (PFIP) and Team Distributive Justice Climate (DJC). We propose that Team DJC functions as a “meta-context” determining the legitimacy of social and monetary markets. We developed and validated a new scale for non-recognition. Analysis of multi-source data from 349 employees across 92 teams reveals a significant three-way interaction. In teams with low distributive justice, non-recognition severely damages meaningfulness, but this effect is neutralized by high PFIP (monetary substitution). Conversely, in teams with high distributive justice, non-recognition does not harm meaningfulness, rendering PFIP unnecessary. We identify non-recognition as a distinct construct and further enrich Two Market Theory by identifying justice climate as a critical boundary condition for the interplay between social and monetary incentives.
