Session overview

Stakeholder theorists have long emphasized the important role of collaboration and cooperation in business; however, they have left competition largely unexamined, raising a central question: What is the role of competition in stakeholder theory? This symposium invites stakeholder theorists and strategy scholars to explore how competition shapes stakeholder relationships, managerial decision making, and the creation and distribution of value. Because stakeholders are often shared across rival firms, competition can shape how relationships form, evolve, and constrain or enhance value creation, yet stakeholder theory offers limited treatment of these dynamics. By opening space for a broad conversation about how competition fits into stakeholder thinking—whether through its conceptual foundations, ethical implications, or strategic consequences—this session aims to enrich stakeholder theory and provide more realistic accounts of how firms operate in competitive environments.
