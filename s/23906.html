Unpacking AI Awareness and Employee Incivility in Hospitality: A Moderated and Mediation Model

TIM
Zulqurnain Ali (Zhejiang University of Science and Technology); Aqsa Mehreen (Zhejiang University)
Despite the rapid adoption of digital technologies, workplaces remain vulnerable to new forms of strain. This research addresses a critical yet understudied question: how AI awareness shapes employee uncivil behavior at work, particularly toward customers. Limited knowledge exists on why and under what conditions AI awareness triggers employee incivility. Drawing on Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, this study identifies key mechanisms and boundary conditions explaining how AI awareness influences employee incivility in hospitality settings. We propose that emotional labor strategies— surface acting and deep acting—mediate the relationship between AI awareness and employee incivility toward customers. Additionally, we establish a moderated-mediation model to explain how various levels of harmonious work passion shape these linkages. Across two hospitality studies (Study 1: N = 343; Study 2: N = 321), findings consistently demonstrate that emotional labor mediates the AI awareness–incivility linkage. Notably, the indirect effect of AI awareness through deep acting is reduced when employees possess higher harmonious passion. Likewise, the indirect effect through surface acting weakens only under high harmonious passion. These results highlight harmonious passion as a critical resource that buffers the detrimental effects of AI awareness on employee behavior. Overall, this research offers fresh insights into how AI awareness permeates service organizations and how employees’ motivational resources—particularly harmonious passion—reshape internal emotional processes to mitigate incivility toward customers.
