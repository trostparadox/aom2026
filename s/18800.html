Relational Generalization in Guilt-by-Association Processes

Author: Thi Ha Trang (Lucy) Dang National Chung Cheng University

Session overview

This study advances a relational perspective on the guilt-by-association phenomenon by showing how negative evaluations of innocent firms arise through relational generalization. I argue that, beyond categorical similarity and causal attribution, evaluators extend negative judgments to firms based on their embeddedness in interorganizational networks. Relational generalization renders innocent firms evaluatively salient because of their relational proximity and structural position relative to an offending organization, even when those firms bear no direct responsibility for the negative event. Moreover, I theorize that guilt-by-association processes unfold dynamically rather than as one-time reactions, such that the magnitude and persistence of evaluative spillovers vary as negative events progress and informational conditions change. I examine these arguments in the context of corporate failure, a consequential negative event that allows us to observe evaluative spillovers across stages of financial distress, bankruptcy, and the post-bankruptcy period. Using data on surviving firms embedded in 88 industry alliance networks comprising 54,893 alliances from 1997 to 2021, we analyze how relational proximity and network position shape the timing and durability of negative evaluations. The results show that innocent firms more closely connected to a failed firm experience stronger and more persistent negative evaluations, with relational effects varying systematically across stages of the failure process and intensifying when the failed firm occupies a central network position. By introducing relational generalization, this study extends research on guilt by association by demonstrating that evaluative spillovers reflect relational embeddedness and evolve as processual outcomes rather than static, category-based responses.
