Emotionally Intelligent Robots at Work: Balancing Support, Trust, and Boundaries

TIM
Armaan Garg (Dr B R Ambedkar National Institute of Technology Jalandhar)
Emotionally intelligent robots are increasingly entering academic workplaces, where emotional labor, stress, and interpersonal interaction are integral to everyday work. Although such systems promise affective support and adaptive assistance, their adoption raises important socio-technical concerns related to trust, privacy, emotional boundaries, and role appropriateness. Drawing on a participatory study with students and faculty, this research identifies a core tension in user expectations: while emotionally responsive behaviors such as stress awareness and supportive communication are valued, users strongly resist continuous emotional monitoring, over-familiar interactions, and opaque decision making. In response, we develop a socio-technical design framework that conceptualizes emotional intelligence in robots as an organizationally embedded practice rather than a purely technical capability, emphasizing consent-based emotion sensing, role-sensitive interaction, selective emotional memory, and transparent behavioral justification. By integrating empirical user insights with design principles, this study contributes to research on human–robot interaction and technology adoption by demonstrating how emotionally intelligent robots can achieve acceptance and legitimacy in human-centric work environments when emotional support is balanced with trust, autonomy, and clearly defined social boundaries.
