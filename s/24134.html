Pragmatist Inquiry In Routine (Re-)Design: Performativity and the Struggle for Creative Action

OMT
Frithjof E. Wegener (University of Twente)
Organizational routines are not only enacted—they are also (re-)designed. This paper explores how actors engage in situated inquiry to reconfigure routines in response to misalignments, breakdowns, and systemic reform. While Routine Dynamics has highlighted the performative flexibility of routines, it still struggles to theorize the creativity of action—how intentions form, how alternatives are projected, and how new paths are forged under uncertainty. Drawing on pragmatist theory, I conceptualize routine redesign as a form of co-inquiry: a process through which actors collaboratively diagnose problems, experiment with interventions, and reconstruct shared practices. While routines are understood as situated, less attention has been paid to the creativity of action and the performative capacity of actors to transform their circumstances. Even recent work on reflective agency often treats intention as given or externally triggered, rather than as emerging through struggle, projection, and inquiry. This paper develops a pragmatist critique of Routine Dynamics by re-centering inquiry and the creativity of action in routine redesign. Building on Joas and Emirbayer & Mische, I theorize agency as practical-evaluative and projective, unfolding through situated experimentation and judgment. Empirically, I study emergency care reform in Germany through eighteen months of engaged fieldwork, combining ethnographic observation with co-design workshops. These workshops served as interventions that surfaced, disrupted, and reorganized habitual action patterns through collective reflection.
