Beyond Instrumental Legitimacy: A Relational and Moral Account of AI Aversion in Expert Work

SIM
Mario Schultz (Franklin University Switzerland); Vera Grigorievna Dianova (Franklin University Switzerland); Giulia Miniero (University of Milan)
This paper investigates AI aversion as a legitimacy judgment evolving in morally laden, relational contexts. Existing research on algorithm aversion predominantly addresses an individual’s reluctance to engage with AI, from an instrumental perspective, while not fully accounting for how AI may disrupt relational processes through which relational expertise is socially recognized and legitimized. Drawing on legitimacy theory and relational expertise literature, we reconceptualize AI aversion as a legitimacy judgment that extends beyond instrumental effectiveness to encompass relational and moral evaluations salient in morally laden settings, like medicine. Adapting Tost’s (2011) and Pakarinen and Huising's (2023) frameworks to micro-level patient-physician encounters, we identified three relational processes (contextual generation, empathetic application, and affective recognition) through which individuals evaluate AI across instrumental, relational, and moral legitimacy dimensions. Consequently, we define AI aversion as a legitimacy judgment that arises when AI is perceived as incapable of engaging in the relational processes within an expert setting, resulting in an evaluation of illegitimacy on one or more of the three dimensions: instrumental, relational, or moral. We discuss implications for AI deployment in professional contexts characterized by vulnerability and fiduciary obligations, emphasizing the need for context-specific, relationally grounded approaches to AI ethics.
