Beyond Likes: Credibility, Trust, and Persuasion in Lebanon’s Food and Beverage Influencer Economy

MC
Jocelyne Boulos Eid; Rasha Khaled Abdulkhalek (Sagesse University); Elie Abou Zeid
Influencer marketing has reshaped how brands communicate, especially within visually rich sectors such as food and beverage (F&B). Yet, most empirical work addressing influencers’ persuasive power has centered on Western and Asian contexts, leaving the Middle East underexamined. This study bridges that gap by investigating how influencer credibility and visual engagement jointly shape consumer responses in Lebanon’s dynamic yet largely unregulated digital advertising landscape. Drawing on the Source Credibility theory (Ohanian, 1990) and the Persuasion Knowledge Model (Friestad & Wright, 1994), this research examines how four key influencer attributes trustworthiness, expertise, attractiveness, and entertainment value interact with visual complexity and persuasion knowledge activation to influence consumer attitudes, brand evaluations, purchase intention, and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM). A quantitative survey of 165 Lebanese social media users reveals that trustworthiness and expertise strongly shape positive brand-related attitudes, while attractiveness and entertainment value cultivate engagement and downstream behaviors such as eWOM. Notably, consumers’ awareness of sponsorship does not diminish persuasion when influencer credibility and content authenticity are high. The study advances theory by contextualizing influencer persuasion within an emerging market characterized by cultural intimacy, media-savvy consumers, and minimal regulatory oversight. Practically, it offers guidance for marketers and consultants seeking to design credible, transparent, and emotionally compelling influencer campaigns. Findings highlight the importance of visual storytelling, congruence between influencer identity and brand values, and authentic communication in reinforcing trust and sustaining consumer engagement.
