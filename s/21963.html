From Exposure to Coping: Climate Change Competencies Among Entrepreneurs Affected by Extreme Events

Authors: Mikaela Daiane Prestes Floriano; Bruno Anicet Bittencourt

Session overview

Extreme climate events have increasingly disrupted entrepreneurial activity, particularly in regions marked by structural vulnerability. This study examines how climate change competencies explain entrepreneurs’ responses to extreme climate events, drawing on empirical evidence from floods that affected Rio Grande do Sul, southern Brazil, in 2024. Adopting an exploratory qualitative approach, the research was conducted as a case study based on in-depth interviews with entrepreneurs directly impacted by the disaster. Data were analyzed using the climate change competency framework proposed by Siponen et al. (2024), which guided the categorization and interpretation of empirical evidence. The findings reveal that extreme events generated profound and multidimensional impacts on businesses, including material losses, prolonged operational disruptions, infrastructure damage, and effects on workers and their families. In responding to these challenges, entrepreneurs predominantly mobilized competencies related to active engagement in networks, courage, determination, and adaptability, which were critical for coping with uncertainty and mobilizing collective support. In contrast, competencies associated with systemic understanding, climate values, and justice emerged less explicitly, while collaborative climate leadership varied according to firm size and structure. The study also identifies important limitations of existing theoretical frameworks, as climate change competencies did not manifest in a linear or planned manner but rather emerged in improvised and situational ways, shaped by emotional strain, institutional constraints, and resource scarcity. By adopting a Global South perspective, this research advances climate change competency theory by highlighting the need to incorporate contextual, affective, and resource-related dimensions to better capture entrepreneurial responses under conditions of extreme vulnerability.
