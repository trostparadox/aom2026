Synchronizing Structured AI Investment and Leveraging Strategies in Entrepreneurial Firms

ENT
SHIJIE LI (Central South University); Shengyuan Lin (Central South University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly adopted by entrepreneurial ventures, but how to structure AI investment to realize its performance potential remains unclear. This study investigates the lagged performance effects of structured AI investment and examines how these effects vary based on investment intensity relative to rivals and alignment with leveraging strategy focused on innovation. Using longitudinal recruitment and financial data from entrepreneurial ventures in China, we find that AI investments generate delayed but positive performance outcomes. Ventures that invest below rival levels tend to realize earlier gains, while those investing above average experience short-term drawbacks before later improvements. In addition, aligning AI investments with leveraging strategy focused on innovation helps reduce the delay in performance realization. These findings extend the structuring dimension of resource orchestration theory and offer a temporal lens to understand how AI contributes to capability development and value creation in uncertain entrepreneurial contexts.
