Between Profit and Purpose: Navigating the Paradoxical Sustainability Tensions

ONE
Regina Menezes (Fundação Getulio Vargas); Thomaz Wood (FGV EAESP)
This article investigates the sustainability paradoxical tensions and the strategies used by an organization to manage them, analyzing the company simultaneously balance economic, social, and environmental objectives in complex contexts. The study adopts a qualitative approach based on a single case study in an Angolan telecommunications company. Data collection included institutional and public documents, semi-structured interviews with managers, specialists, and former executives, as well as direct observations made by the author. The analysis associated Burke's (1945) dramaturgical analysis with Miles and Huberman's (1994) coding and interpretation framework, allowing for the articulation of organizational narratives, emerging patterns, and tension management mechanisms. The results show that the organization predominantly developed a strategy of acceptance paradoxes, combining technical solutions, social initiatives, and stakeholder engagement to strengthen legitimacy and balance conflicting demands. Sustainability practices were gradually incorporated throughout the projects, showing capacity of adaptation when structural restrictions occur. The article proposes an analytical framework that relates identified tensions to adopted strategies, allowing the association of organizational responses to persistent paradoxes. Contributions to the body of knowledge include broadening the understanding of paradox management, especially in emerging countries, and include the presentation of six propositions derived from analytical generalization.

Managing Meaning at Scale: Frame Plurality in Grand Challenge Entrepreneurship

ONE
Bart Clarysse (ETH Zürich); Maria Gradillas Garcia (ETH Zurich); Jana Thiel (EDHEC Business School)
We examine how entrepreneurial organizations mobilize and sustain support for grand challenges under persistent uncertainty. Drawing on a longitudinal inductive case study of The Ocean Cleanup (2013–2024), we theorize frame plurality: the mission-anchored governance of multiple, audience-specific frames that coexist and evolve over time. Our multi-source dataset combines extensive archival materials, critical documents, digital trace data from social media, and two waves of executive interviews, analyzed using temporal bracketing, computational text analysis, and inductive coding. We uncover three mechanisms that govern frame plurality: creative dissent, mission anchoring, and mission-gated resourcing, and a cross-cutting dynamic of adaptive updating through which frames are selectively recalibrated as evidence and activities evolve. Together, these mechanisms constitute an intraorganizational narrative governance capability that enables an entrepreneurial venture to sustain legitimacy and support while avoiding fragmentation or mission drift. Our study (1) extends framing theory from substitution to governed multiplicity, (2) elaborates framing as a strategic renewal capability in entrepreneurial, not only incumbent, settings, and (3) advances understanding of how grand-challenge ventures remain viable when uncertainty does not resolve.

The Influence Mechanism of Chief Sustainability Officers on Firm Performance

ONE
Taiwo Abraham (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Sibin Wu (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Xuan Wang (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Jorge A. Gonzalez (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Deniz Atik (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley)
This research examines how Chief Sustainability Officer (CSO) human capital influence organizational performance. Drawing on Upper Echelons and Human Capital theories, the study conceptualizes CSO human capital as encompassing both general (GHC) and sustainability-specific (SHC) dimensions that interact with job functions to shape outcomes. The theoretical framework proposes that SHC primarily influences non-financial sustainability performance, while GHC drives financial sustainability performance. Further, champion and ambassador functions moderate the proposed human capital and performance relationships. Using a mixed-methods approach analyzing 239 CSOs, the study employs hierarchical regression and logistic regression to identify multiple pathways to sustainability excellence. The research advances theoretical understanding of specialized executive roles while providing practical guidance for optimizing CSO effectiveness through aligned human capital and job functions.

Founders’ Inner Calling and the Formation of Regenerative Organizations in the Brazilian Amazon

ONE
Silvia Ferraz Nogueira De Tommaso (FIA Business School); Roberto Carlos Bernardes
This study examines how entrepreneurs in modern societies acquire ecological knowledge to build regenerative organizations. Using longitudinal, qualitative, multiple-case study and participatory action research techniques, the research explores regeneration as a response to destabilized entrepreneurial ecosystems. Our findings indicate that regenerative organizations originate in founders who respond to a deep inner calling that propels them into entrepreneurial action. This calling materializes in the creation of ventures whose core mission is to generate positive impacts for multiple human and non-human stakeholders while respecting planetary boundaries. An entrepreneurial journey is a vehicle for aligning personal purpose with an organizational design oriented towards long-term ecological and social flourishing. Our processual model identifies five interrelated mechanisms that guide the formation and consolidation of regenerative practices: Reconnection Triggers, Ecological Learning, Ancestral Knowledge Sharing, Spillover Governance, and Stewardship Commitments. They describe regenerative entrepreneurship as an ecological system of interdependence.
