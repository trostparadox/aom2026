Do Self-Transcendence Values Influence Admiration and Emulation for Exemplary Coworkers?

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 8
OB

People

Authors: Gerardo Alejandro Miranda Reina University of Alaska Fairbanks; Atoshi Borna Podder; Jennifer L. Welbourne UTRGV

Session overview

We examine the factors that lead employees to admire and emulate coworkers who are moral exemplars. Integrating appraisal theory of emotion (component process model; Scherer, 2009) and values theory (Schwartz, 1992), we explored admiration as an emotional response to coworkers who display self-transcendence (ST) values. ST values emphasize benevolence and justice towards others (Schwartz, 1992). We examined the role of both personal ST values and organizational ST values in eliciting admiration and intentions to emulate a fictional coworker who expresses ST. Using an experimental vignette method, we collected data from 261 United States working adults. Our findings indicate that coworker ST expression elicited admiration, and that this relationship was stronger for individuals who personally prioritized ST values, but was not strengthened by organizational ST prioritization. Admiration was associated with intentions to emulate the coworker. Moreover, the indirect effect of coworker ST expression on emulation through admiration was moderated by personal ST values. Keywords: Admiration, emulation, self-transcendence values
