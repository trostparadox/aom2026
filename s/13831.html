Session overview

Despite decades of research and organizational efforts to promote equity in work and careers, inequalities in labor market outcomes persist. Emerging research suggests that these disparities are not driven solely by overt discrimination, but by how status signals such as educational credentials, career trajectories, gender, and family status are evaluated and acted upon within organizations and labor markets. This symposium brings together five empirical papers that examine how seemingly meritocratic processes of evaluation, mobility, and advancement systematically reproduce inequality over time. Across diverse empirical settings, the papers show that inequality often emerges endogenously through routine evaluative and organizational processes rather than explicit exclusion. Drawing on large-scale panel, platform, and longitudinal data, the studies examine how human judgment, organizational routines, algorithmic tools, and public policies shape access to opportunity. Together, the papers highlight the socially constructed nature of merit and advancement, offering new insights into how inequality persists across contemporary labor markets and organizations.
