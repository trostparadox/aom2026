Making the Office Worth the Trip: Aesthetics as a Key Change Factor in the Post-Pandemic Workspace

ODC
Benjamin Bétrisey (University of Fribourg); Eric F. G. Davoine (University of Fribourg); Bertrand Audrin (EHL Hospitality Business School)
The shift to post-pandemic hybrid work has prompted organizations to redesign workspaces while managing tension between increasingly optimized, standardized and regulated spaces and an employee presence that remains (partly) voluntary. Research on organizational space is rich but fragmented and tends to overlook aesthetics. This study addresses this gap by examining how organizations narrate and operationalize workspace redesign and by identifying the logics through which they govern space in hybrid regimes. Semi-structured interviews with 19 HR leaders and project owners across 17 Swiss organizations that recently redesigned their workspaces were analyzed inductively. Our findings show that workspace decisions are shaped by four established logics (economic, regulatory, identity and control) and by a fifth, emergent aesthetic logic that supports well-being and gently encourages return-to-office. We provide a structured framework for understanding how narratives and spatial decisions influence behavior in hybrid contexts. Positioning aesthetics as a distinct logic highlights how intentionally designed atmospheres and experiences can support voluntary presence, organizational identity and sustainable workspace adoption. This study offers a framework positioning aesthetics as a distinct logic, integrating dispersed literature on organizational space and extends it by demonstrating how sensory/experiential qualities of space shape processes in post-pandemic office redesign.

When Success Tests Career Capital : Career Capital Architectures in Creative Organizations

ODC
Dongju Kim (Setsunan University)
This study examines how career capital development systems are tested when creative organizations enter maturity after success. We conduct a comparative case analysis of two globally successful creative organizations: Studio Dragon, a leading Korean drama production company, and Noma, the Danish fine-dining restaurant. Our findings show that career capital development systems that function effectively during organizational growth exhibit diminishing returns as organizations mature, accompanied by rising costs and increasing selectivity. We explain these patterns through two ideal-typical career capital architectures: the Career Capital Factory (CCF), exemplified by Studio Dragon, and the Career Capital Lab (CCL), illustrated by Noma. By reframing maturity not as the end of creativity but as a critical inflection point at which career capital development systems shift from enabling growth to sustaining organizational capability.

The Ebb-Flood Cycle: Tenure and Perceptions of Organizational Dimensions

ODC
Betania Tanure; Luisa Tanure Tanure; Carolina Marra; Adriana Piedade (UNIVERSIDADE FEDERAL DE MINAS GERAIS - UFMG)
The “Valley of Death” has traditionally described critical phases of organizational change, when cultural transformation temporarily undermines performance and meaning. This study extends the concept to the individual level by examining organizational tenure (length of stay) as a key lens for understanding how employees’ organizational perceptions evolve. Grounded in cultural change, organizational socialization, and sensemaking, the model shifts attention from discrete “change events” to recurring dynamics of meaning reconstruction that sustain employees’ permanence. Survey data from 8,646 professionals in large Brazilian organizations reveal a systematic nonlinear relationship between tenure and perceptions of Organizational Dimensions. Results show a U-shaped pattern: more favorable perceptions in early years, a pronounced decline in intermediate tenure, and partial recovery at later stages, especially beyond 15 years. Contrary to expectations, the lowest point does not occur between 5 and 10 years, but peaks in intensity between 10 and 15 years. The decline and recovery are not uniform: Organizational Dimensions contribute unevenly across tenure stages. The intermediate-stage downturn can be anticipated and addressed through targeted people management and leadership practices
