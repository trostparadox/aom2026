Epistemic Adaptation and the Effectiveness of Climate Risk Governance

ONE
Celina McEwen (University of Technology Sydney); Martina K. Linnenluecke (University of Technology, Sydney)
Many firms have adjusted governance structures in response to climate risk regulation; however, these changes often fail to produce effective practices or coherent assessments of climate exposures, responsibilities and strategic implications. This persistent disconnect raises a theoretical puzzle: why do similar structural and systemic governance adaptations lead to markedly different organizational outcomes? Drawing on social constructivist and practice theory perspectives, we examine governance adaptation across large Australian for-profit and not-for-profit organizations and identify a missing mechanism in existing explanations. We theorize epistemic adaptation as the process through which organizations surface, align and revise shared understandings and discursive frames surrounding climate risk. Our analysis shows that variation in epistemic adaptation explains why some organizations translate governance reforms into cohesive and forward-looking strategies, while others generate conceptual ambiguity that frames climate risk governance as burdensome compliance. We contribute to research on organizational adaptation and climate governance by introducing epistemic adaptation as a generative mechanism linking climate risk conceptualization to structural and systemic change and by showing how its absence constrains the effectiveness of governance adaptation.
