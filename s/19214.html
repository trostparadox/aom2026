From Leader Humility to Employee Bricolage: The Power of Organizational Provocation

Session overview

Drawing on social information processing theory, this study examines how leader humility translates into employees’ ability to creatively recombine existing resources at work (bricolage). Rather than focusing only on direct effects, we propose that humble leaders shape employees’ interpretations of their work environment by encouraging organizational provocations, defined as intentional challenges to routines and established ways of thinking, which in turn stimulate bricolage. We further argue that this process depends on employees’ perceptual moral attentiveness, or the extent to which they notice and interpret moral cues in their work context. Using survey data from employees across organizations in Montenegro and testing a moderated mediation model with Hayes’ PROCESS approach, the findings show that leader humility is more strongly associated with organizational provocations when employees are high in perceptual moral attentiveness, and that these provocations are associated with higher levels of bricolage. As a result, the indirect link between leader humility and bricolage through organizational provocations emerges primarily when perceptual moral attentiveness is moderate to high. By clarifying when and how leader humility is converted into creative, adaptive employee behavior, this study contributes to leadership and innovation research by highlighting the joint role of leadership signals, organizational processes, and individual moral cognition in enabling bricolage. Keywords: organizational provocations, leader humility, bricolage, perceptual moral attentiveness, Montenegro
