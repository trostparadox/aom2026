Session overview

Calls to “follow your passion” remain deeply embedded in contemporary understandings of work and careers. Work passion is often treated as a signal of commitment, fit, and potential. However, a growing body of research suggests that passion is neither uniformly beneficial nor evenly distributed in its consequences. This symposium brings together four papers that examine the promise and perils of work passion for careers and organizations. Collectively, the papers show that work passion operates as a powerful but context-dependent social signal. By shaping career choices, employee evaluations, and behavior, passion can motivate performance, yet it can also reinforce inequality and produce other unintended costs. Together, the papers further our understanding of when passion improves work outcomes and when it generates risks for employees and organizations.
