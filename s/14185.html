Session overview

This symposium aims to open a conversation on marginalized groups as central—yet often overlooked—participants in positive social change. As social crises and challenges intensify, different forms of marginalization have become more salient, prompting growing scholarly attention to addressing these challenges. However, despite increasing work on disrupting the structures that sustain marginalization, the agency and emotional experiences of marginalized groups that influence the effectiveness of these efforts remain underexamined. Bringing together a group of impactful scholars in organization theory, marginalization, and emotions, this symposium aims to explore and synthesize the roles of emotions, organizations, and institutions in shaping marginalized groups’ engagement with change initiatives. Through open discussion, the panel seeks to surface key theoretical and empirical gaps in existing research and to introduce different perspectives that inform a future research agenda on marginalized groups—particularly the organizational and institutional dynamics that shape their engagement with positive social change.
