Social capital, Empowerment and Entrepreneurial Orientation of Small Medium-Sized Entrepreneurs

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 6

People

Authors: Juana Du Royal Roads University; Junyu Zhou Bangor University; Yulei Dou

Session overview

This study addresses a significant gap in the literature by examining the entrepreneurial orientation of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in China’s emerging markets. Specifically, it explores the dynamic role of social capital (SC) and its interaction with empowerment in shaping entrepreneurial orientation (EO). Drawing on dynamic capabilities perspectives, the study investigates how social capital influences SMEs’ propensity toward entrepreneurial behaviors, including innovativeness, proactiveness, and risk-taking. The findings indicate that higher levels of social capital are positively associated with stronger entrepreneurial orientation among SMEs. Moreover, both structural and psychological empowerment are found to moderate the relationship between social capital and entrepreneurial orientation, suggesting that empowerment mechanisms enhance the translation of social resources into entrepreneurial tendencies and behaviors. The study offers several practical implications for SMEs operating in China’s emerging market context.
