How Temporal Expectations Shape the Paradox of Control and Freedom

MC
Heta Seikkula (Aalto University)
The modern work environment is undergoing transformative changes, leading to a complex tension between organisational control and employee autonomy. As employee expectations shift towards flexibility, control requires a shift from traditional surveillance to cultural norms emphasising employee ownership. By conducting 28 interviews in an international technology consultancy, this study unravels the paradox by showing that organisational control operates not only through structures or culture but through shared assumptions of temporal applications. I surprisingly found that instead of continuously balancing between flexibility and agility - who can decide on what, how and where to work, the tension builds up in the mutual but contradictory expectations of time use, creating temporal expectations. For employees, these expectations are 1) to own one’s time, 2) to have clarity and structure, and 3) to be a good worker, and for managers 1) to trust to manage oneself, 2) to prioritise and perform, and 3) to optimise. By identifying that temporal expectations blur the boundary between freedom and control, the study reveals how employees are free to manage their time, but bound by internalised norms of efficiency, availability, and structure. Keywords: control, freedom, paradox, temporal expectations, expectations
