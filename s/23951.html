AI Capability and Innovation Novelty: Evidence from Clinical Trials

TIM
Chandrika Rathee (Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University)
Artificial intelligence is increasingly adopted as an algorithmic search technology that can expand exploration and raise expectations of more novel innovation. Yet novelty is risky, and it remains unclear when AI capability translates into novel projects that organizations are willing to advance rather than merely producing more candidate ideas upstream. We address this question in a setting where proof requirements are explicit and staged: clinical trials. Using 55,527 industry-sponsored interventional trials from ClinicalTrials.gov (1999 to 2025) conducted by 6,611 lead sponsors, we measure novelty as the average rarity of drug–disease pairs relative to the historical trial record and proxy organizational AI capability with cumulative prior AI-related trial experience. Sponsor fixed-effects models show that greater AI experience is associated with lower novelty on average. However, this relationship is contingent: the negative association is substantially weaker in early-stage trials than in late-stage trials, and novelty under greater AI experience is more likely when organizations commit to broader validation designs, proxied by a larger number of primary outcomes. Diagnostic checks show no detectable association between AI experience and trial duration or completion or termination outcomes. Together, the findings suggest that AI expands upstream search but does not automatically increase downstream novelty; novelty is filtered by stage-specific proof burdens, and when AI-linked novelty is pursued it is accompanied by heavier measurement commitments.
