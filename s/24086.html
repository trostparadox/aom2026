Access-Structured Sensegiving in Planned Change

ODC
Ignacio Pavez (Universidad de Chile)
Planned change depends not only on what leaders decide but also on whether employees can access the sensegiving occasions through which change meanings are clarified and evaluated. Treating sensegiving as a distribution problem, this study examines change management interventions (CMIs) as formal venues whose reach may be stratified by hierarchy. I analyze a strategic planning initiative in a Chilean public-sector organization supported by three CMIs. Cross-sectional survey data were collected in an early implementation phase (complete responses N=147; 12 failed an attention check, yielding N=135). OLS models predict a formative index of perceived change design quality (ChD-Index; N=134; N=131 with empowerment). Exposure is modeled as participation in each CMI and as cumulative exposure (0–3); hierarchical position is drawn from organizational records. Participation in each CMI, especially full cumulative exposure, is positively associated with ChD-Index, and psychological empowerment is also positively associated. The hierarchy–perception association is reduced once exposure is taken into account, and additional analyses indicate that hierarchy predicts intervention exposure. Conceptualizing CMIs as employee-level sensegiving exposure shifts attention from intervention availability to intervention reach and advances an access-structured account of planned change in a hierarchical organizations.
