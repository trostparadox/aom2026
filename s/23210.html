AI Use Among Marginalized Entrepreneurs: A Social Cognitive Theory Perspective

ENT
Brandon Ofem (University of Missouri-Saint Louis); Ikenna Uzuegbunam (Howard University); Joseph Fox (The University of Akron)
Generative artificial intelligence (AI) offers entrepreneurs powerful tools to enhance productivity, streamline operations, and drive innovation. Yet, little is known about how demographic identity shapes AI adoption among historically marginalized groups. Drawing on Social Cognitive Theory, this study examines how gender, race/ethnicity, and disability status influence generative AI adoption, and whether technological self-efficacy (TSE) moderates these relationships. Using a nationally representative sample of 1,349 U.S. entrepreneurs, we find that Black and Hispanic entrepreneurs report significantly higher adoption rates than White entrepreneurs, while gender differences diminish when accounting for other factors. Entrepreneurs with disabilities also report higher adoption, though their use appears less influenced by TSE. Moderation analyses reveal that TSE amplifies adoption particularly among women and Black entrepreneurs. These findings highlight the need for inclusive AI policies and interventions that address both structural inequities and psychological enablers to ensure equitable access to AI’s benefits in an increasingly digital entrepreneurial landscape.

Artificial Intelligence as an Information Engine in Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Innovation

ENT
Muhammad Salman Shabbir (York St. John University); Rabia Salman (York St. John University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming how entrepreneurial ecosystems generate, distribute and use information, shaping innovation processes and coordination among actors, particularly SMEs. However, existing scholarship remains scattered and lacks a unified perspective on how AI-driven information systems operate across individual, organisational and ecosystem levels. This study proposes an integrated information management framework that positions AI not only as a tool, but as a cognitive, organisational and institutional actor embedded within entrepreneurial systems. Based on a structured review of 100 peer-reviewed publications from 2017–2025, four main research strands emerge: AI-supported decision-making, digital knowledge and learning infrastructures, adaptive ecosystem coordination, and governance of intelligent technologies. The synthesis illustrates how AI restructures opportunity recognition, resource flows and innovation patterns under uncertainty. The framework advances information management thinking by conceptualising AI as an active system that shapes ecosystem design, governance and performance. For practitioners and policymakers, the study emphasises the importance of investing in data infrastructures, AI capabilities and governance mechanisms to build more inclusive and adaptable entrepreneurial environments. It also outlines future research directions to empirically validate the framework and examine fairness, transparency and power dynamics in AI-mediated innovation systems.

Augmented Intelligence and Entrepreneurial Decision-Making: A Study on Entrepreneur–AI Collaboration

ENT
Luise Kaufmann (Saïd Business School, University of Oxford); Miriam Bird (Technical University of Munich)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has a transformative impact on the decision-making processes, focusing on the transition from task automation to augmented intelligence that enhances cognitive capabilities. However, it remains unknown how entrepreneurs integrate AI-generated information into their decision-making processes in emotionally charged settings. This research addresses that gap by employing a mixed-methods approach, which includes topic modeling, computer-aided emotional text analysis, and thematic content analysis. In particular, this study examines entrepreneurs' experiences with AI adoption as both an emotional and cognitive augmentation. Findings reveal that entrepreneurs engage in a complex decision-making process characterized by the critical evaluation of AI outputs, trial-and-error problem-solving, and iterative adjustments to AI algorithms based on their insights. This tension-filled process poses challenges to their cognitive capacity in managing the volume and velocity of information produced by AI. The research contributes to the fields of entrepreneurial cognition and decision-making, augmented intelligence theory, and offers new insights into the dynamics of AI adoption.

How Entrepreneurs’ Responsible AI Practices Influence Consumer Responses

ENT
Sara Luisa Kappelhoff (Technical University of Munich); Nadja Mirjam Born
Applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI) provide unlimited business opportunities for entrepreneurs and their ventures. However, they also introduce novel ethical challenges, urging entrepreneurs to navigate tensions between AI-enabled innovation and responsibility. While consumer judgements are pivotal for entrepreneurial success, so far, it remains unknown how consumers respond to entrepreneurs’ use of responsible AI. We investigate in a preregistered experiment (N = 376) how consumers react affectively, cognitively, and behaviorally to entrepreneurs’ responsible AI practices. Drawing upon signaling and consumer inference theory, we examine, using AI-based videos (pretested with N = 283), whether consumers reward entrepreneurs’ responsible AI practices with a higher willingness to pay and reduced boycott intentions. Results of our experiment show that consumers react with more positive emotions (affective response) and increased fairness perceptions (cognitive response) to entrepreneurs who use AI more versus less responsibly. We find that entrepreneurs’ responsible AI practices elicit reduced boycott intentions, mostly driven by perceived fairness. Furthermore, they increase consumers’ willingness to pay a premium, mainly through positive emotions. However, we observe that the effect on consumers’ willingness to pay a premium is comparatively small, suggesting that consumers appraise responsible AI practices, but not at all costs. We advance the literature on AI in entrepreneurship and responsible AI by offering a demand-side approach to investigate positive outcomes of entrepreneurs’ use of responsible AI empirically.
