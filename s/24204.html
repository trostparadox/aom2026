Tracing Dynamic Capabilities: A Longitudinal Process of Sensing, Seizing, and Reconfiguring

SAP
Hsin-Peng Shiahhou (Fu Jen Catholic University); Chao-Ching Chang (National Taipei University of Education); Meng-Hsien Yen (Fu Jen Catholic University); Chin-Lun Tsai (Fu Jen Catholic University)
This study examines how firms develop the dynamic capabilities necessary for achieving a sustainable competitive advantage in volatile, high-velocity environments. Leveraging a unique longitudinal dataset from a controlled, multi-year business simulation, the research meticulously tracked 30 competing firms across eight decision periods and four distinct markets. It moves beyond theory by operationalizing and measuring the development of sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities through objective, behavior-based indicators derived from the firms' actual strategic decisions over time. Consistent with prior research, initial single-capability models indicate that sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring each contribute significantly and positively to firm performance when analyzed independently. However, a key theoretical insight emerges from the integrated model: when all three capabilities are included simultaneously, their individual statistical significance is nullified. This surprising result suggests the capabilities operate as distinct, independent mechanisms, each contributing to performance through its own unique pathway, rather than as a unified, synergistic system. This provides nuanced evidence for the independence of dynamic capability effects. The results indicate that firms can achieve competitive advantage through heterogeneous capability pathways, suggesting a one-size-fits-all approach to capability building is ineffective and fundamentally challenges conventional wisdom on the topic.
