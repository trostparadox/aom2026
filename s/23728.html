Ethnic Fractionalization and SMEs’ External Resource Acquisition in Sub-Saharan Africa

ENT
Ru Zhang (Renmin University of China); Hai Guo (Renmin University of China); Meitong Dong (University of Science and Technology of China); yining wang (Hong Kong Baptist University)
Access to external credit—a core component of external resource acquisition—is widely acknowledged as a pivotal driver of small and medium-sized enterprises’ (SMEs) survival and expansion, yet it remains severely constrained for firms in developing economies. While prior research has emphasized firm-level determinants of credit access, less attention has been paid to how broader social structures shape firms’ financing opportunities. This study advances this literature by examining ethnic fractionalization as a critical social structural determinant of firms’ credit access. Drawing on firm-level data from the World Bank Enterprise Survey (WBES) covering 36 Sub-Saharan African countries between 2011 and 2024, we investigate how ethnic fractionalization affects firms’ likelihood of obtaining credit and whether this effect differs between female-led and male-led firms. We argue that ethnically fragmented environments undermine information and normative foundations that help mitigate concerns arising from information asymmetry, thereby elevating perceived lending risk and constraining access to credit. Moreover, female-led firms are more vulnerable to increases in ethnic fractionalization because gender acts as observable cues shaped by gender role expectations. The results support our predictions. Our study illuminates how social structure perpetuates disparities in SMEs’ resource acquisition and underscores the unique challenges faced by women in developing economies.
