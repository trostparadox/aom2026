Session overview

The rapid diffusion of generative and agentic artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping foundational assumptions about opportunity, judgment, knowledge, and uncertainty, and these very concepts anchor classical and contemporary theories of entrepreneurship and strategic decision-making. As AI systems increasingly participate in information discovery, prediction, creativity, and even stakeholder interaction, long-standing concepts articulated by Frank Knight, F.A. Hayek, and Israel Kirzner require critical re-examination. This PDW revisits the foundational concepts by placing them in dialogue with three influential contemporary frameworks, i.e., Opportunity creation, the Judgment-based approach, and the Theory-based view of entrepreneurship. This PDW brings together scholars working at the intersection of entrepreneurship, strategy, and technology to explore whether AI alters the nature of entrepreneurial judgment, redistributes loci of knowledge, or transforms processes of opportunity discovery and creation. By revisiting the intellectual roots of uncertainty, dispersed knowledge, and alertness, the workshop aims to stimulate integrative theorizing that clarifies what aspects of entrepreneurship remain uniquely human and which may become augmented or displaced by AI. This PDW is especially timely as journals increasingly seek conceptual, methodological, and empirical work that addresses AI’s implications for entrepreneurial action, strategic decision-making, and market processes. Participants will receive developmental feedback from senior scholars, identify publication pathways, and help shape emerging conversations in leading management outlets.
