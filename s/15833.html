A Quantile Regression Perspective on the Satisfaction of Employees.

Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 7

Authors: Stephanie Christine Mercedes Benz Philipps University Marburg; Thomas Armbruester

Session overview

This study examines the influence of five organizational factors and satisfaction, inspired by the historic Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs. The foundational concept in the field of human relations and inspiration for a broad range of motivational theories, has always been criticized for its limited empirical validation. Traditional research, investigating the motivational predic-tors, often only follow a mean-centered regression approach. This study enriches the academic discourse on satisfaction by employing quantile regression to examine how the impact of organizational factors varies across the distribution of the outcome variable. The data was collected in Germany between 2016 and 2019. The sample includes 576 firms and a total of 144,578 employees. The model was analyzed via OLS regression before conducting the quantile regression (QR). By comparing the significance and effect sizes of key predictors across multiple percen-tiles, the paper shows, that all five factors effect the employee’s satisfaction significantly. In alignment with the current academic discourse self-actualization has the strongest impact. However, QR highlights for example, that the need for safety is stronger in lower percentiles of the conditional distribution of satisfaction compared to social needs.
