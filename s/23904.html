How Does Human–AI Ambidextrous Learning Drive Employee Innovation?

TIM
guojian zhang (Liaoning Technical University); Lixia Niu (Liaoning Technical University)
Amid firms’ accelerated digital–intelligent transformation, generative artificial intelligence (GAI) is becoming deeply embedded in organizational learning processes, reshaping the fundamental logic of employees’ knowledge acquisition and innovative behavior. Drawing on organizational learning theory, this study develops a Human–AI dual-learning model —“human input–AI generation–human learning and optimization–AI relearning and optimization–human optimized output”—to examine how employees achieve the dynamic orchestration of exploratory and exploitative learning through synergistic interaction with AI, thereby enhancing innovation performance. Using three-wave survey data from 443 respondents employed by manufacturing enterprises located in six high-tech parks, the findings indicate that Human–AI dual-learning significantly improves employees’ innovation performance, with Knowledge co-creation serving as a mediating mechanism. Moreover, Task complexity positively moderates the pathway through which Human–AI dual-learning affects innovation performance via Knowledge co-creation; this mechanism is more pronounced under high Task complexity. This research moves beyond the traditional paradigm that treats AI merely as a tool, enriches the organizational learning mechanisms from the perspective of Human–AI collaboration, and provides theoretical grounding and practical insights for building an effective AI-enabled innovation system within firms.
