Control Over Creativity: Micromanagement as a Boundary Condition of GenAI-Enabled Intrapreneurship

Session overview

Organizations are rapidly deploying generative AI (GenAI), yet it remains unclear whether and under what conditions employee GenAI adoption translates into intrapreneurship. We propose a focused model in which GenAI adoption enables proactive work redesign by encouraging employees to increase challenging job demands, a challenge-seeking job crafting behavior that fosters intrapreneurship. Because micromanagement restricts employee discretion and limits proactive work redesign, it attenuates the relationship between GenAI adoption and job crafting and, consequently, weakens the indirect effect of GenAI adoption on intrapreneurship. We tested this moderated mediation model using multi-source data from 340 matched dyads consisting of employees and their supervisors in Montenegro. Employees reported GenAI adoption, micromanagement, and challenging job demands, while supervisors rated intrapreneurship. Results supported a conditional indirect effect. GenAI adoption predicted increases in challenging job demands, but this relationship tended to weaken under higher micromanagement. Increases in challenging job demands predicted intrapreneurship, whereas the direct effect of GenAI adoption was non-significant. The indirect effect was present at low and moderate levels of micromanagement but not at high levels, supporting moderated mediation and underscoring micromanagement as a boundary condition for GenAI-enabled intrapreneurship.
