Performance by Design: Unlocking the Strategic Recipes of Supply Chain Executive Impact

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 6
OSCM

People

Authors: Angel Simon The Ohio State University; Terry Esper The Ohio State University

Session overview

Firms increasingly elevate supply chain leaders to the C-suite with mixed performance results. Drawing on Upper Echelons Theory, we examined when supply chain executives (SCEs) enhance firm performance as a combination of executive attributes, organizational design artifacts, and global market conditions. Using fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) (Ragin, 2008), we analyzed 38 S&P 1500 firms with SCEs on their top management teams in 2021 and assessed performance outcomes in 2023, identifying six distinct configurations that are sufficient for high performance. Configurations combined SCE work experience, formal logistics and supply chain management (LSCM) education, and compensation, with firm size and internationalization, together with country-level logistics infrastructure and cultural dimensions. Performance was assessed across revenue, ROA, COGS, CCC, and cost per sale. Two configurations showed broad excellence across all five outcomes, while others delivered selective strength in growth and profitability or cost discipline. Overall, findings highlight substitutions across domains and trade-offs across performance dimensions, demonstrating that there is no single ‘best’ SCE profile.
