All Alone: A Belongingness Perspective of Skilled Migrants’ Social Isolation and Loneliness

IM
Vesa Peltokorpi (Hiroshima University); Jinju Xie (School of Economics and Management, Tongji University)
While loneliness affects all individuals, migrants are particularly vulnerable due to the loss of established social relationships in their home countries and the challenges of forming new ones in host countries. This paper examines the causes and consequences of loneliness among skilled migrants in Japan, a country with a low proportion of foreign residents and a growing demand for skilled foreign labor. The findings of narrative interviews with 160 skilled migrants suggest that social rigidity, characterized by cultural and linguistic uniformity, and social categorization contributed to host country national-initiated and self-initiated social isolation, which intensified skilled migrants’ loneliness. These feelings of loneliness eroded their sense of belongingness in the host country. This paper contributes to the literature by (1) examining work-related loneliness among skilled migrants and showing that host country national-initiated and self-initiated social isolation contribute to loneliness in work- and non-work domains; (2) advancing belongingness theory by showing how structural and societal factors shape loneliness through skilled migrants’ ability to develop and sustain social relationships with host country nationals; and (3) that local language proficiency alone does not ensure acceptance or social integration in countries with low migrant populations.
