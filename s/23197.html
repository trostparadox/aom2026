Entrepreneurial Behavior in Organizations: A Mulitlevel Process Perspective

ENT
Aroma Kousar (Lahore University of Management Sciences); Sidra Raza (University of Management and Technology)
As organizations operate in increasingly volatile and uncertain environments, entrepreneurial behavior within established firms has become essential for adaptation and sustained performance. This study develops a dynamic multilevel process model that examines entrepreneurial behavior across three interconnected levels: organizational structure, organizational culture, and individual action, while accounting for environmental uncertainty as a cross-level contextual condition. The framework theorizes how (1) structural design features including decentralization, autonomy, and resource flexibility enable (2) the development of entrepreneurial orientation and learning-and-failure cultures, which in turn shape (3) individual opportunity recognition, risk-taking, and innovative behavior. Environmental uncertainty is proposed to strengthen these cross-level relationships under conditions of heightened ambiguity. By integrating structural, cultural, and individual-level perspectives, the model advances multilevel entrepreneurship theory and provides a foundation for future empirical research on entrepreneurial behavior within organizations.

Entrepreneurial Intention to Action: Education, Culture, and Gender in Latin America

ENT
Lucia Rodriguez-Aceves (Universidad Panamericana); Ilaria Cascavilla (University of Bergamo); Linda Elizabeth Ruiz (Tecnologico de Monterrey); Geraldina Silveyra (Tecnologico de Monterrey)
This study examines the influence of entrepreneurship education (EE) on entrepreneurial intentions (EI) and subsequent behavior (EB) within the Latin American context, focusing on the moderating roles of cultural dimensions and gender parity. Grounded in the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), we employ a longitudinal design using GUESSS data (2021 and 2023) from 356 students across four Latin American countries. Results from structural equation modeling indicate that EE does not directly predict EI, challenging the assumed universal efficacy of EE programs. However, EI strongly predicts EB, confirming TPB's core premise. Notably, the EI–EB link is significantly moderated by cultural factors: individualism weakens it, while masculinity and power distance strengthen it. Contrary to theoretical expectations, we observed a non-significant trend suggesting that higher national gender parity might be associated with a weaker, not stronger, conversion of intentions into behavior. These findings underscore the critical role of contextual factors in the entrepreneurial process and call for EE programs and policies that are culturally sensitive and gender-aware, moving beyond standardized approaches to consider local institutional and socio-cultural realities and the complex interplay between formal institutions and entrepreneurial action.

Entrepreneurs’ Budgeting Intentions and Behaviors: A Longitudinal Exploration

ENT
Krittaya Sangboon (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Leon Schjoedt (The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley); Kelly G. Shaver (MindCette, LLC)
In this study, we investigate whether entrepreneurs intend to engage in budgeting and whether they follow through with these intentions. We utilize the Theory of Planned Behavior as the conceptual framework for our longitudinal study. Analysis of data from 317 entrepreneurs and their budgeting practices reveals that, while they intend to budget using computer software, an app, or professional accounting services, in practice, there are important differences between these intentions and the entrepreneurs’ budgeting behaviors.

The Impact of Generative AI on Individuals’ Career Choices and Entrepreneurship Intentions

ENT
Chang Liu (University of Wisconsin-Madison); Florence E M Honore (University of Wisconsin-Madison)
The widespread adoption of generative AI is reshaping individuals’ career choices, yet existing research has paid limited attention to how generative AI influences career intentions and through which channels these effects unfold. Two well-discussed channels capture these mechanisms. Augmentation refers to humans collaborating with AI to enhance capabilities and improve work performance, whereas automation refers to AI substituting for human labor to increase efficiency in work routines. To disentangle these channels and investigate underlying mechanisms, we conducted a field study in a U.S. biotechnology firm. We implemented a two-wave survey experiment with full-time employees, in which the key treatment educates respondents about either an augmentation view or an automation view of AI. Our results show that augmentation plays a larger role in shaping employees’ internal mobility intentions, and that this effect strengthens over time. In contrast, automation plays a larger role in shaping employees’ entrepreneurial intentions, and this effect also increases over time. We further examine mechanisms underlying these snowball patterns. We find that use of the firm-specific LinkedIn Learning system, an AI-enabled career development tool, mediates the relationship between the augmentation treatment and internal mobility intentions. We also find that the use of other generative AI tools mediates the relationship between the automation treatment and entrepreneurial intentions. Overall, the study offers implications for how firms and employees adapt to generative AI in the workplace and how AI shapes career intentions over time.

The Intention-Action Gap Revisited: A Construal Level View of Entrepreneurial Behavior

ENT
Ishara Maharaj (United Arab Emirates University); Maurizio Sobrero (United Arab Emirates University); Paul D. Savage (United Arab Emirates University)
Entrepreneurial intention is widely regarded as a key antecedent of entrepreneurial behavior, yet intentions do not consistently translate into action. This study examines how and when entrepreneurial intentions become action by distinguishing between different types of entrepreneurial behavior and integrating the Theory of Planned Behavior with Construal Level Theory. Using longitudinal survey data from university students in the United Arab Emirates, we differentiate between engaging in recent entrepreneurial activities and being an operating founder, capturing variation in psychological distance and commitment. The findings show that entrepreneurial intentions are positively associated with both forms of action, but more strongly with recent activities than with founding. Perceived environmental conditions do not exert uniform effects: environmental support is associated with founding, while environmental dynamism selectively strengthens the intention–action relationship among highly motivated individuals. Perceived social support, once intentions are taken into account, is negatively associated with action. Together, the results demonstrate that the intention–action relationship is contingent on both the type of action and its contextual interpretation, highlighting the importance of integrating psychological distance and environmental perceptions into models of entrepreneurial behavior.
