The Recovery Cost of Activation-Based Emotional Dissonance in Positive Emotions

OB
Yubei Xiao (Peking University); Xiang Yao (Peking University)
Employees often encounter emotional dissonance in workplace settings, suppressing negative emotions to display positive emotions. This study challenges emotional labor perspectives traditionally arguing that employees who are already experiencing positive emotions can easily meet organizational requirements to show positive displays. Instead, we highlight positive activation dissonance, where employees adjust arousal levels despite feeling positive, incurring costs through arousal regulation load. Emotional journey theory is integrated into the effort-recovery model to examine the effects of positive activation dissonance. Positive activation dissonance is conceptualized as an emotional journey, with recovery costs determined by emotional distance (the magnitude of activation difference) and emotional direction (whether activation is upregulated or downregulated). Across an experimental design (Study 1) and an experience sampling study (Study 2), we find that greater emotional distance and upward emotional direction that requires displaying positive emotions with higher activation resemble an "uphill" journey. The positive activation dissonance evokes particularly intense affective rumination during work breaks, with negative effects on goal progress, organizational citizenship behavior (OCB), and eudaimonic workplace well-being. Emotional crafting, the proactive emotion regulation strategy to identify and cultivate positive experiences, acts as a personal resource to buffer recovery costs. Findings indicate that employees who have positive emotional states suffer negative recovery consequences when they must alter the activation level of their emotional displays in the workplace.
