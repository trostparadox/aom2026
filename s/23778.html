Making Sense of Control-Oriented HR: Shaping Qualitative Job Insecurity Through Ethical Leadership

HR
Alexandra Jussli (Not Associated); Hendrik Huettermann (University of the Bundeswehr Munich)
In the face of ongoing crisis and economic disruptions, many organizations rely on cost- and performance-oriented HR strategies. This raises important questions about how employees interpret such strategies and how these interpretations shape their sense of qualitative job insecurity, defined as the perceived threat of losing valued aspects of one’s work. Drawing on Uncertainty Management Theory, we examine how and when control-oriented HR attributions (i.e., employees’ perceptions that HR strategies primarily serve cost-efficiency goals) may reduce rather than heighten qualitative job insecurity. We argue that such attributions can foster goal clarity, a cognitive mechanism that helps employees orient themselves under uncertainty and assess the future quality of their work. Yet whether this clarity alleviates insecurity depends on the interpretive context. We theorize that ethical leadership strengthens the negative association between goal clarity and qualitative job insecurity by legitimating and balancing organizational goals. Two-wave survey data from 513 employees support our model: Control-oriented HR attributions indirectly reduce qualitative job insecurity through goal clarity, but only when ethical leadership is high. These insights hold valuable practical implications for the responsible management of cost- and control-oriented HR strategies.
