Why Do You Not Lead? Reconceptualized Reluctant-To-Lead

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 11

People

Author: Xinlang Zhou Drexel University

Session overview

Leadership roles are increasingly important in the contemporary workplace, yet media reports that people are less interested in being leaders these days. Existing research has largely addressed this challenge through the Motivation to Lead (MTL) framework, which conceptualizes leadership motivation as an approach-oriented desire to assume, persist in, and exert effort in leadership roles. However, MTL offers limited leverage for explaining why capable individuals hesitate, resist, or selectively disengage from leadership despite possessing the skills, legitimacy, and opportunity to lead. This paper introduces Reluctance to Lead (RTL) as a theoretically distinct, avoidance-oriented motivational construct that complements rather than opposes MTL. Drawing on the approach–avoidance framework (Elliot, 2006), we conceptualize leadership motivation as governed by two partially independent systems: MTL reflects an approach-oriented toward rewards and growth, and RTL captures an avoidance-oriented toward threats, costs, and identity risks. More importantly, individuals may experience high MTL and high RTL simultaneously, producing hesitation, ambivalence, or conditional leadership engagement. Building on the structure of the MTL framework (Chan & Drasgow, 2001), we theorize three dimensions of RTL—affective–aversion, normative, and calculative reluctance—each reflecting distinct sources of leadership avoidance. We further argue that the consequences of RTL depend on motivational context: in supportive and developmental environments, avoidance motivation may be regulated constructively as reflective restraint and shared leadership, whereas threat-laden contexts amplify withdrawal and disengagement. By reconceptualizing leadership motivation as a dual-process system, this framework provides a more comprehensive account of leadership hesitation, ambivalence, and engagement.
