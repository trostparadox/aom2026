Raising the Bar: Societal Aspirations and Organizational Alignment

OMT
Brenda Jaewon Myung (University of Michigan)
I propose a novel theory of how external stakeholders influence organizations to pursue new goals. External stakeholders hold expectations for both the goals that organizations should pursue, as well as levels of performance on these goals. I refer to such expectations as societal aspirations, and argue that when societal aspirations rise beyond current performance expectations, organizations adopt goals valued by external stakeholders and change their behaviors accordingly. I test this theory using data on corporate board appointments before and after the 2010 SEC Board Diversity Disclosure Rule, which required public firms to disclose how they consider diversity in selecting board candidates amidst calls from investors to “do more” for diversity; the rule did not implement a quota or regulated outcome. Analyzing S&P 1500 firms 2008 to 2023, I find that after the rule, low-performing firms—firms performing below their industry peers—were more likely to appoint a woman or person of color. By contrast, high-performing firms—those already above industry peers in board diversity—were no less likely to appoint a woman or director of color. I conclude by discussing implications for the behavioral theory of the firm, social movement theory, and new stakeholder theory.
