How Does the U.S.- China Trade War Destroy Jobs in the United States?

TIM
Wenjing Lyu (Zhejiang University); Yuanhao Huang (Zhejiang University); Jin Liu (Renmin University of China)
We examine the impact of the U.S.-China trade war since 2018 on domestic employment and firm performance. Our findings reveal that, despite the trade war’s objective of revitalizing domestic manufacturing and boosting employment, it paradoxically led to a substantial decline in the hiring of domestic talent. This effect is primarily driven by a reduced demand for low-skilled positions, lower leverage, and decreased production efficiency. The impact is particularly pronounced in manufacturing firms, firms employing Chinese workers, small-scale firms, and IT-scarce firms, suggesting that the trade war disproportionately affected certain U.S. industries. The tariffs prompted U.S. firms to adjust their workforce structures—most notably by reducing the share of low-seniority workers—and to cut back on leverage, all of which contributed to further employment declines. And firms impacted by tariffs not only reduced current employment but also scaled back future labor demand, highlighting the disruptive consequences of trade war on U.S. labor market.
