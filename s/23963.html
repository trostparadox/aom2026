Public Stances, Private Stakes: CEO Activism on Polarizing Issues and Reputational Dynamics (WITHDRAWN)

STR
Louisa La Porta (Freie Universität Berlin); Miriam Flickinger (Free University of Berlin)
CEO activism, the practice of corporate leaders speaking publicly on sociopolitical issues, has become increasingly prevalent in recent years. While such stance-taking has been looked at as risky in polarized societies, it may also generate reputational gains. This study examines how and under which conditions CEO activism affects CEO reputation, an individual-level outcome that has so far been neglected in previous research on the phenomenon. Building on the literature of social evaluation, morality, and signaling, we conceptualize activism as a moral signal that makes information on moral character available to stakeholders. We theorize that the morality signaling mechanism of activism enhances reputation. Furthermore, we investigate the impact of different characteristics of activism, namely framing and consistency of activist communication. Using a panel data set on S&P 500 CEOs across six years, we analyze CEO individual reputation as an outcome linked to a unique dataset of CEO activism on LinkedIn. We find that reputation increases after CEO activism and that these reputational gains can be realized with weak framing and inconsistent engagement. The study contributes to debates on business leaders’ involvement in the social and political domain by clarifying how CEOs’ public stances shape individual-level outcomes.
