Innovation's Last Mile : How BG Characteristics & Governance Shapes Patent Commercialization

STR
Syed Azhar Husain Jafri (Indian Institute of Technology Bombay); Snehal Awate
Patent commercialization (PC) is a critical yet underexamined capability for firms in emerging markets where challenges like institutional voids, weak appropriability regimes and capability gaps make it far more challenging than invention alone. Building on the research on innovation execution and business group (BG) advantages, this study examines how BG affiliation and group characteristics influences commercialization outcomes in India. Using a novel patent level commercialization dataset for over 382 Indian manufacturing firms, we show that BG affiliated firms commercialize a significantly larger share of their patented inventions than standalone firms. We also find inverted U relationships between BG size and PC, and between BG diversity and PC, indicating that group affiliation benefits diminish when BGs become excessively large or diversified. Further, foreign promoter ownership strengthens the positive effects of BG size and diversity and mitigate the negative effects. We find strong support for these hypotheses after subjecting our analysis to a battery of endogeneity and self-selection tests, ensuring the robustness of our results. By integrating organizational structure and ownership heterogeneity to downstream innovation outcomes, our study contributes to the literature on emerging market innovation capabilities, business groups and technology commercialization.
