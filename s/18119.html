From Innovation Process to Public Value: A Framework Proposal for the Public Sector

Authors: MARLON JORGE LIEBEL Pontifical Catholic University of Parana; Danielle Denes Dos Santos Pontifical Catholic University of Parana; Carlos Olavo Quandt; Anthony Arundel Maastricht University

Session overview

Innovation and the public sector are often seen as incompatible, because governments must provide the same services (health, security, schooling) over time. This poster analyzes the concepts of innovation, strategic goals, and public value to present a framework for public innovation processes. Although it may be implemented in any public area, the suggested application is in tax administration, as it is responsible for providing most of the financial resources a government needs to deliver public goods and services. We make five propositions in the framework that intend to identify what support practices and constraints influence the innovation process in a tax administration, if the innovations developed by tax administrations help them achieve their strategic goals and deliver public value, even if as an unknown by-product of the process, and if the tax administrations will learn from their innovation process and outcomes, constantly improving the innovation process. Data will be collected through interviews, and qualitative data analysis based on Grounded Theory will be used to code the interview transcripts. This poster contributes to the public-sector literature by proposing an innovation process framework designed for that sector, and by relating the innovation process to public value delivery and strategic goal achievement. It also contributes to public value theory by proposing that the innovation outcomes enhance public value delivery. Finally, public managers may benefit from implementing the framework, particularly through the continuous learning process it provides.
