Session overview

“Open” has emerged as a dominant paradigm in management and organization studies, as organizations increasingly confront novel social, ecological, and technological phenomena that call for more open organizing processes (Splitter et al., 2023). Acute social-ecological crises call for diverse actors to engage in green transitions in more open and participatory ways (Etzion et al., 2017). Such partition is enabled by new digital infrastructures that enable real-time collaboration across geographies, sectors, and actors (Bertello et al., 2021; Hilbolling et al., 2021). Despite the growing interest and such technological affordances, the term ‘open’ has often been used without clear conceptualization. It is conventionally celebrated as the opposite of closed. However, with the recent consolidation of approaches grounded in relational ontologies in management and organization studies such as practice theory, process theory, and paradox theory, openness has begun to be understood as a situated phenomenon (Diriker et al., 2023), difficult to disentangle from closeness (Dobusch et al., 2019) and increasingly recognized as holding the potential for undesirable outcomes (Stoiber et al., 2025). Studying open and participatory forms of organizing also introduces methodological challenges regarding the scope and scale (Bednarek, 2024). This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to advance our understanding of the “open” conceptually and methodologically, identifying contemporary phenomena that help reveal the multiple facets of this evolving concept.
