Scaling Without Centralisation Imaginaries as Coordinating Infrastructures

CMS
Gemma Lord (The Open University); Thomas Burø
Scaling without centralisation is a persistent puzzle for prefigurative and alternative organising, which are frequently criticised for their limited capacity to expand without reproducing hierarchical or managerial logics. Within Organisation Studies, scaling is often implicitly equated with growth, replication or centralisation, leaving little conceptual space for understanding how alternatives proliferate while remaining value-consistent and often rendering ‘scale’ synonymous with managerial control. This paper addresses that gap by examining how prefigurative organising achieves coherence across sites without formal hierarchy. Drawing on activist ethnography across urban and rural permaculture initiatives, the analysis reframes scaling as an organisational achievement of distributed coherence rather than expansion. The paper advances a mechanism-focused contribution by conceptualising social imaginaries as cultural coordinating infrastructures. Empirically, it shows how imaginaries operate infrastructurally through three interrelated mechanisms: shared orientation that stabilises understandings of purpose and value; translation work that enables practices to travel through adaptation rather than replication; and socio-material carriers - such as pedagogies, routines, artefacts and named practices - that render imaginaries durable and portable across contexts. In doing so, the paper specifies how concerted action can be sustained across dispersed sites without central governance, while remaining open to contestation and plurality. The contribution advances Critical Management Studies by reframing scaling as a cultural and organisational accomplishment grounded in everyday practice, offering a robust alternative to growth-centred and managerial accounts of scale.
