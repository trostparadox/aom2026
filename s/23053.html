Portfolio Reconfiguration Under Media-Amplified Uncertainty: The Bounded Flexibility of Real Options

IM
Yazhou Song (Shandong University); Yimin Wang (Shandong University); Xiaocong Tian (Nankai University)
This study reexamines multinational expansion under uncertainty by shifting attention from country-level attractiveness to the strategic value of growth opportunities embedded in firms’ international portfolios. Drawing on real options reasoning, we argue that foreign entry generates hedging benefits by reallocating exposure to volatile information environments, but that these benefits are bounded by coordination and commitment constraints. Using data on 15,700 foreign subsidiary establishments by Chinese MNEs across 131 countries (2013–2023), we find robust evidence of an inverted U-shaped pattern: as portfolio-level exposure to negative sentiment increases, firms initially exhibit stronger entry propensity toward favorable host countries, but this tendency reverses beyond a threshold. We further show that this curvature reflects two independent mechanisms. Host-country operating experience strengthens firms’ ability to convert uncertainty into option value, steepening the ascending slope, while accumulated exercised options accelerate declining marginal returns by constraining strategic flexibility. These findings advance research on global strategy by identifying systematic limits to real options logic and by demonstrating how information environments shape the dynamic value of international growth opportunities.

Identifying the Roles and Importance of Foreign Subsidiaries: The Global Network of Japanese MNEs

IM
Kozo Kiyota; Kiyohiko Ito (University of Hawaii at Mänoa)
We analyze and evaluate the roles, importance, and structural location of foreign subsidiaries quantitatively by integrating the Global Integration–Local Responsiveness (GI-LR) framework and network analysis. Multinational enterprises (MNEs) manage their foreign subsidiaries directly from their headquarters as well as indirectly through other foreign subsidiaries. However, the roles, nature, and strategic importance of intermediary and terminal international subsidiaries in MNEs’ global network have been understudied quantitatively. We explore whether the GI-LR framework can be used to characterize foreign subsidiaries’ roles and their importance in the global network of MNEs. Foreign subsidiaries may serve as special purpose entities or regional headquarters or fulfill R&D, production, and marketing functions, and such exploration is essential to understanding the organizational structure of MNEs. We use PageRank centrality to analyze the MNEs’ global network. Based on data on the top 100 nonfinancial Japanese MNEs, we empirically find that special purpose entities, regional headquarters, and R&D function subsidiaries are located upstream and have more ties with other subsidiaries, while production and marketing function subsidiaries tend to be located downstream and have fewer ties with other subsidiaries. Thus, PageRank centrality provides valuable insight into the importance of foreign subsidiaries quantitatively and aids in identifying different functional roles.

Temporal Fit as a Strategic Resource: How Temporal Distances Shape MNE Subsidiary Performance (WITHDRAWN)

IM
Liang Wen (Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University); Stefan Volk (The University of Sydney)
Temporal distance is emerging as a critical factor influencing multinational enterprise (MNE) performance. While progress has been made in research on time zone differences, we know little about the role of chronotype differences—variations in biological predispositions toward morning or evening activity. Bridging the resource-based view (RBV) with configuration theory, this study posits that temporal distance is best understood as a configuration of two distinct enablers: the structural enabler of time zone differences and the physiological enabler of chronotype differences. We propose that the fit or misfit between these two dimensions creates distinct temporal configurations. These configurations either enable or constrain the temporal resources available to MNEs, which in turn influence subsidiary performance. Furthermore, we theorize that cultural distance serves as a significant boundary condition, amplifying both the positive effects of high fit and the negative effects of low fit. This highlights the importance of MNEs strategically integrating both time zone and chronotype dimensions into their global operational management. Using a sample of 16,003 subsidiaries established by 54,618 Japanese MNEs across 39 countries during 1991 and 2018, we test and find robust support for our hypotheses.

Performance Feedback and Foreign Subsidiary Portfolio Adjustment Under Tight Coupling in MNEs

IM
Youngbin Ji (Yonsei University)
This study examines how performance feedback influences foreign subsidiary portfolio adjustment in MNEs. We conceptualize foreign subsidiary portfolio adjustment along two dimensions—intensity and direction. We analyze how deviations between firm performance and aspiration levels affect these dimensions using panel data on MNEs affiliated with Korean business groups from 2018 to 2023. The results show that performance below aspiration levels is associated with more active portfolio adjustment through increased foreign subsidiary entries and exits. In addition, performance above aspiration levels is associated with portfolio adjustments that tend to portfolio expansion. Also, these relationships are moderated by business group diversification. These findings contribute to performance feedback research by extending search behavior outcomes to the boundaries of the MNE and to IB research by integrating foreign entry and exit decisions from a portfolio perspective. The study highlights how higher-level organizational structures condition the strategic interpretation of performance signals in MNEs.
