Session overview

Our understanding of leadership and followership is still uneven. Leadership continues to receive most of the attention, and we know far less about how leadership and followership are co-created, how they shift over time, and how they operate in different contexts and cultures. This imbalance persists partly because research has emphasized individual traits and behaviors more than the underlying cognitive or relational and co-constructed processes through which influence actually emerges. Addressing these gaps calls for renewed attention to how leadership and followership are studied together as part of the same system of influence. This panel symposium, therefore, tries to discuss three important questions: How can followership be more clearly defined and integrated into leadership research, and what is its current state of development in the field? What do we know, and not yet know, about how these roles work together? What new directions should we explore to better capture the dynamic, shared nature of leading and following?
