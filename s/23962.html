Who Are You? A Field Analysis of Exit Strategies of Facial Recognition Business in Leading Companies

STR
Jingxu Zhao (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Chengli Shu (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Yang Qi
Facial recognition technology has boomed in recent years, with application scenarios evolving rapidly. Yet following the U.S. Black Lives Matter (BLM) movement, many leading firms refused to provide this technology to law enforcement—and some abandoned the business entirely. Why would leading firms exit a thriving facial recognition business? What exit paths did they collectively adopt? Given the nascent nature of the facial recognition business and the exploratory scope of theoretical development, we conduct a cross-case comparison of leading global facial recognition firms (IBM, Amazon, Microsoft). Our findings include three key insights: Drawing on Bourdieu’s field theory, firms’ facial recognition activities form a distinct business field (composed of actors, logics, capital, and habitus) encompassing two subfields—technological research and technological application. The field’s stability depends on three factors: intra-element competition, inter-element misalignment, and inter-subfield incompatibility. Exit paths are shaped by field conflicts: intra-element competition drives full exit, inter-element misalignment leads to partial exit, and inter-subfield incompatibility results in suspended exit—reflecting diverse paths and differentiated drivers. We develop a field-based perspective on facial recognition business, extend the boundaries of field theory, offer a template for field-based analyses of other businesses, and provide theoretical and practical guidance for facial recognition firms’ field-aligned operations.
