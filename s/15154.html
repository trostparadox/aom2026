Supporting Onboarding with Generative AI: Evidence from Global Virtual Teams

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: Wendy Farrell MCI - The Entrepreneurial School; Ernesto Tavoletti University of Macerata; Marketa Rickley University of North Carolina at Greensboro; Vas Taras University of North Carolina at Greensboro

Session overview

Onboarding is increasingly understood as a learning and unlearning process that shapes how newcomers make sense of their roles, tasks, and teams. In global virtual teams (GVTs), onboarding is particularly challenging due to limited informal interaction, delayed access to colleagues, and high coordination demands. This study examines whether access to a trained generative AI–based support tool designed to assist onboarding is associated with perceived team efficacy over time and overall team satisfaction in GVTs. Using data from a nine-week global virtual consulting project involving 2,291 students across 436 teams from more than 50 countries, we analyze naturally occurring use of a domain-trained GPT that provided guidance on project expectations, collaboration, and team processes. Perceived team efficacy was measured weekly, and team satisfaction was measured at project completion. Although human-provided support was rated slightly higher on average, results from mixed-effects multilevel models indicate that using the trained GPT is positively associated with perceived team efficacy over time and with higher team satisfaction. These findings suggest that generative AI may function as a scalable onboarding support resource in global virtual contexts by offering continuous access to onboarding-related information, which is associated with more positive perceptions of team efficacy and satisfaction.
