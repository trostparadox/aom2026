Public Sector Innovation Management in the Shadow of Co-Creation and Co-Production

TIM
Marina Carelli Reis (University of São Paulo); Ana Paula Paes Leme Barbosa (University of Sao Paulo); Jacob Brix; Mario Sergio Salerno
Research on public sector innovation has increasingly emphasized co-creation and co-production, often treating innovation management as a secondary or implicit concern. This paper takes a different stance by examining innovation management as a deliberate organizational arrangement through which public organizations seek to cope with uncertainty, technological change, and bureaucratic constraints. Focusing on how innovation is organized rather than on innovation outcomes alone, we analyze how public organizations design and operate innovation management systems. The study addresses the question: What works well and less well when public organizations rely on innovation management arrangements inspired by the private sector? Empirically, the paper draws on a multiple case study of five national police agencies located across five continents, based on semi-structured interviews, documentary sources, and observations from an international innovation network. The analysis shows that, while all agencies established innovation units with similar formal responsibilities, they differed substantially in how innovation management systems were configured and sustained. We identify three distinct configurations—Strong Heavyweight, Light Heavyweight, and Lightweight—and show how differences in the activation of enabling and constraining mechanisms help explain variation in innovation capacity across highly institutionalized public-sector contexts.
