Session overview

Recent developments, including the rapid digitalization of work, are transforming the virtual work landscape in ways that may create new challenges and opportunities for women’s careers. However, research into the new promises and perils of evolving virtual work for women’s career equality remains nascent. In this panel symposium, we aim to discuss how current advances in virtual work may support or hinder women’s career outcomes. These advances include emerging forms, such as the widespread adoption of hybrid work, virtual work extending beyond traditional organizational boundaries (e.g., gig work and social-media-based entrepreneurship), and human–AI collaboration; as well as the use of contemporary technologies in virtual interactions (e.g., virtual reality, electronic performance monitoring systems, digital platforms, and online meetings). We convene an interdisciplinary panel of scholars whose research lies at the intersection of virtual work and gender, who examine varied outcomes across different forms of virtual work, and who use diverse methodological approaches. The panelists will share their research insights and engage the audience in an interactive discussion to identify important future research directions. This discussion is both timely and urgent to ensure a more inclusive future workplace for women.
