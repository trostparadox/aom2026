Artificial Intelligence, Real Decisions: Evaluating an AI-Based Career Coaching Intervention

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 1
CAR

People

Authors: Rebecca N Mintz George Mason University; Sarah Wittman George Mason University

Session overview

Making career-related decisions is challenging. Career coaches can make the process easier. However, there are significant obstacles, such as lack of time and perceived stigma, that prevent people from utilizing career coaching services. The advent of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) provides the possibility for broader access to career coaching services without the downsides of human-based coaching. There is limited literature on the effectiveness of GenAI chatbots in career coaching contexts. For this study, we developed a custom career coach chatbot, which was tested by 249 undergraduate students. After interacting with the AI career coach for a maximum of 30 minutes, participants evaluated the bot on a variety of characteristics. We found that the AI career coach functioned as an effective intervention as most participants either made their decision or were closer to deciding than still being undecided after their interaction. The participants who increased their decision certainty valued the AI career coach more highly, and participants valuing the AI coach more were more open to reengaging with it. Additionally, participants evaluated the chatbot on understanding of emotion, human-like personality, and human-like empathy. We found that higher perceptions of these characteristics were associated with higher perceived value of the chatbot. These findings indicate that there is a future where AI career coaches can democratize access to career coaching resources.
