Building Trust in AI: A Meta-Analysis of Employee-AI Relationship Mechanisms in Organizations

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 4
TIM

People

Authors: Hengrui Chen Renmin University of China; Songbo Liu Renmin University of China; Zixin Huang Renmin University of China; Kairui Zhang Renmin University of China

Session overview

As AI deepens into organizational business, employees’ trust in AI becomes key to human-machine collaboration effectiveness. By using Tripartite Theory of Attitude (ABC Model) as a theoretical basis, this study performs a meta-analysis (MASEM) based on 87 studies of employees’ trust in AI through literature retrieval, filtering, and encoding, to explore the influencing mechanisms and boundary conditions of individual factors on employees' trust in AI. The results show that affective mechanisms (positive affect, negative affect) and cognitive mechanisms (perceived functional value, perceived social attributes) play a significant mediating role between dispositional attributes, acquired competencies, perceived contextual cues and trust in AI. Path comparisons show that dispositional attributes mainly activate positive affect, while acquired competencies are the strongest precursors to building cognitive mechanisms and mitigating negative affect. Furthermore, the effects of perceived functional value on trust predominate, followed by positive affect, indicating that rational cognition is the core driver of trust establishment, and emotional experience acts synergistically. Furthermore, AI type significantly moderates the downstream paths: robotic AI amplifies the negative impact of negative affect on trust, while virtual AI reinforces the positive effect; AI with image characteristics (robotic AI and virtual AI) amplifies the positive effects of perceived social attributes. This study constructs an integrated human-machine trust model, providing theoretical contributions and practical insights for human-machine trust management in organizations.
