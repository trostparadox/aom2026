Constrained Moral Agency: Organizational (Non)Compliance Under Coercive Stakeholder Demands

SIM
Abdul-Kadir Ameyaw (Indiana University Kokomo); Darryl B. Rice (Miami University); Maxim Storchevoy; Sofia Glebova (Higher School of Economics)
Organizations are often assumed to engage stakeholders through dialogue, negotiation, and discretion. Yet in some institutional environments, stakeholder claims are enforced coercively, narrowing organizations’ capacity to deliberate ethically. Drawing on an inductive study of organizations responding to the Russian government’s military conscription mandate during the Special Military Operation in Ukraine, we theorize how organizations act when confronted with powerful, morally contested stakeholder demands. We show that organizations interpret such demands through a process of moral sensemaking that shapes both the meaning of the claim and the range of actions seen as viable. Although all firms faced the same coercive mandate, they varied in how they understood their ethical agency under constraint, resulting in five distinct (non)compliance strategies: substantive, expressive, instrumental, creative, and principled. Our study reveals how organizations can occupy a position of moral marginalization, in which coercive authority limits their ability to align their actions with their convictions.
