The Cost of Innovation: Evidence from Capital Market Reactions to Technological Setbacks in AD

TIM
Daniel Runkel (Philipps-University of Marburg)
This study examines the stock market reaction of Tesla’s stock price to fatal accidents involving Tesla vehicles in the United States between 2013 and 2025. Using event study methodology, we analyze 361 event days associated with 398 fatal accidents, of which 44 were attributed to Tesla’s autopilot system and 354 to human error. We test three hypotheses related to the existence, cause, and temporal evolution of abnormal returns. Our findings indicate no statistically significant abnormal returns in response to fatal accidents, regardless of their cause or timing. Nevertheless, descriptive patterns reveal that autopilot-related incidents are associated with consistently more negative reactions than accidents caused by human error, while later accidents do not provoke stronger responses than earlier ones. These results suggest that capital markets appear resilient to safety-related incidents in the context of autonomous driving, with broader market factors and macroeconomic news exerting stronger influence than fatal accidents. Therefore the study contributes to research on first-movers and disruptive innovation, associated with technological uncertainty by demonstrating that safety incidents in autonomous driving do not materially affect investor perceptions.
