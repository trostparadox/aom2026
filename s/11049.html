Session overview

Qualitative research has expanded markedly within management scholarship, intensifying questions about purposeful sampling (case and informant sampling) and saturation (the extent to which data are analyzed). Although both criteria are widely referenced, their underlying logic is often left implicit, with limited methodological justification and few transparent rationales for how decisions were reached. This proposed workshop introduces and clarifies these core concepts, drawing on management methodology as well as insights from adjacent disciplines. A central focus is the level-of-analysis challenge that is especially salient in management research: cases seldom map onto single individuals and more often involve aggregated actors or collective entities—teams, units, firms, or organizational forms—whose boundaries, internal heterogeneity, and comparability require explicit analytical defense. Building on this foundation, the workshop provides a concise synthesis of how prior studies have operationalized purposeful sampling and saturation, with attention to influential methodological traditions including the Gioia method, the Eisenhardt approach, and flexible pattern-matching strategies. The session then shifts to an interactive format featuring facilitated roundtable discussions in which participants share practical challenges and exchange solutions from their own projects. Conversations are organized around recurring themes—such as business model levels, interorganizational dynamics, and international research settings—and conclude with brief group syntheses to consolidate insights, support cross-table learning, and catalyze continued dialogue.
