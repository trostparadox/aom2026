Enabling Serendipity in Unbounded Entrepreneurial Ecosystems

ENT
Hina Ahmed (University of Oslo); Nele Terveen (The University of Auckland); Truls Erikson (University of Oslo)
This conceptual paper explores how serendipity can be enabled and theorized within unbounded entrepreneurial ecosystems (UEEs). While much of the entrepreneurial ecosystem literature focuses on structure, intentional coordination, and linear models of value creation, we argue that these assumptions underplay the emergent, uncertain, and often surprising nature of entrepreneurial discovery. Building on recent work on unbounded ecosystems (Audretsch, 2024) and the evolving theory of serendipity (Busch, 2024), we develop a processual understanding of how openness, relational plurality, and distributed agency contribute to serendipitous outcomes. Rather than treating serendipity as a rare exception, we suggest it is a central, yet undertheorized, feature of entrepreneurial ecosystems, particularly in dynamic contexts. By integrating insights from ecosystem literature, strategy-as-practice, and serendipity theory, we offer a novel lens for understanding how entrepreneurial opportunities unfold beyond intention or design. This framing calls for new conversations around emergence, uncertainty, and value creation in entrepreneurship.

Just My Luck: An Embedded Ethnographic Study on (Seren)dipity in Entrepreneurial Strategy

ENT
Rayan Chelli (McGill University)
Research on serendipity in entrepreneurship and management literature shows that serendipity plays an important part in the process of opportunity discovery and in a venture’s strategic decision making. The first author conducts an eight-month embedded ethnography in a late-stage technology startup – Saber – to look at how enabling (serendipity) and disruptive (zemblanity) surprising events shape the strategic direction and adaption of the startup as it attempts to enter its first commercial deployment. The authors subsequently analyze and trace three vignettes of serendipity and zemblanity and find that both event types impact the short-term and long-term goal setting and strategic focus of the startup. Additionally, we explore the interrelationship between serendipity and zemblanity by looking at how Saber was able to navigate an initial disruptive surprise and, by means of serendipity, turn it around into a positive outcome.

Serendipity Capability in Entrepreneurial Pitches: Implications for Funding Outcomes

ENT
Marilyn A. Uy (Nanyang Technological University); Jin Tan (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences); Yi Huang (Tsinghua University)
We theorize and test serendipity capability in high-stakes entrepreneurial pitches. Serendipity requires agency, surprise, and value. Building on this foundation, we conceptualize serendipity capability as the capacity to notice, recombine, and enact value from surprise triggers in dynamic interactions. Using 1,441 Shark Tank pitches across 16 seasons and applying large-language model (LLM) based techniques to capture founders’ serendipity capability during investor interactions, we examine its effect on funding outcomes. We found that serendipity capability positively predicts both the likelihood and quality of funding support, with effects amplified by unpleasant surprise triggers and attenuated by pleasant ones. By showing how entrepreneurs convert unexpected events into investor support, our study advances theory on serendipity as a strategic capability and clarifies the conditions under which it delivers value.

Surprising Discoveries From Agentic Actions: The Role of Leisure-Work Synergizing

ENT
Brindha S (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode); Nandakumar Mankavil Kovil Veettil (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode); Alexander Hamrick (University of Richmond)
Much research focuses on how entrepreneurs intentionally identify and frame opportunities. But entrepreneurial success often depends not only on deliberate effort but also on serendipity, which is defined as unexpected yet valuable discoveries arising from the interplay of chance and agency. Drawing on serendipity theory and agentic actions, we study how serendipity can be actively cultivated. This study examines how leisure–work synergizing, the intentional integration of work-relevant elements into leisure activities, enables serendipity among entrepreneurs. We position leisure-work synergizing as the agentic behavior that enhances serendipity, and the role of team size in facilitating this. Drawing on a three-wave, time-lagged study of 210 Indian entrepreneurs, we find that leisure–work synergy positively predicts serendipity through enhanced social skills. Additionally, the impact of leisure–work synergy on serendipity through social skills is more pronounced in smaller entrepreneurial teams. By identifying a nonwork, agentic pathway to serendipitous outcomes, this study contributes to the emerging discourse on entrepreneurial serendipity and broadens the understanding of leisure as a proactive domain that fosters entrepreneurial discovery.

When the Innovation Runway Ends: Exaptation, Serendipity, and Entrepreneurial Survival in AI Ventur

ENT
Amir Taherizadeh (McMaster University); Hamid Etemad (McGill University)
This longitudinal qualitative case study examines the evolutionary journey of a maturing Canadian artificial intelligence (AI) startup (Pseudo named as Aspire-AI) as it searched for a sustainable business model between 2018 and 2024. Due to the adverse impact of rapidly expanding AI industry, the firm aspired to transition from an information technology service-provider toward a dedicated AI venture; while facing persistent resource constraints, ambiguous, fragile demand, and partner-dependent commercialization pathways. Across this period, Aspire-AI undertook three major strategic pivots spanning aerospace design optimization, aircraft inspection, renewable-energy infrastructure, and ultimately to timely building roof inspections and repairs. Consistent with prior work , the findings showed that entrepreneurial action oscillated between goal-driven causation and means-driven effectuation under uncertainty. However, these logics alone would not fully explain how the Aspire AI survived repeated setbacks when the previous opportunity pathways collapsed. We theorize entrepreneurial survival as hinging on unevolutionary exaptation - the repurposing of an existing core technological capability for novel functions - activated by serendipity as an intervening condition. Specifically, unexpected external events and abrupt constraints (e.g., client withdrawal, access bottlenecks, public safety constraints, regulatory shifts, etc.) redirected attention to reveal potentially promising new application domains, enabling the firm to transform its technological functions and rebuild validating conditions. By conceptualizing entrepreneurial exaptation as the pivoting process by which an entrepreneurial start-up’s existing capabilities are repurposed to new contexts to perform functions for which they were not originally adapted, we explain how the firm repeatedly reconfigured its innovation trajectory. We further show how patterns of serendipity-enabled exaptation triggered successive pivots, enabling the venture to move from one developmental stage to the next following the collapse of prior opportunity pathways. This study contributes to research on business model innovation in technological entrepreneurship and advances evolutionary perspectives on entrepreneurial action in fast-evolving AI ecosystems.
