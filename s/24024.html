Out of Scope? The Spillover Effect of Environmental Penalties on Firms' Carbon Performance

ONE
HaoJing SONG (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Panikos G. Georgallis (University of Amsterdam); Ming Jia (Northwestern Polytechnical University); Yi Xiang (Xi'an Jiaotong University); Du Yongzhi (Harbin Institute of Technology)
The spillover effect of environmental penalties constitutes a key mechanism by which regulation extends beyond penalized firms to shape corporate environmental strategies. Prior research has documented positive spillovers, as regulatory sanctions on peers trigger firms to improve their environmental performance in order to preempt future regulation or secure legitimacy in the eyes of the state. However, given the tendency of prior research to focus on specific issues, the spillover effect of environmental penalties on other issues that lie within the same domain but outside the scope of penalties has been largely overlooked. In this paper, we propose that environmental penalties imposed on peers motivate unpenalized firms to focus on penalty-covered issues, leading them to neglect penalty-uncovered issues. Using a sample of 3,994 Chinese publicly listed firms, we find a negative spillover effect of environmental penalties on carbon performance – a penalty-uncovered issue. This effect is strengthened by penalty severity, media attention to penalized peers, similarity between firms, and prior penalty experience. By offering new insights into the direction, scope, and mechanisms underlying environmental penalties’ spillover effects, this study contributes to research on environmental regulation and carbon performance, and to the broader literature on corporate sustainability.
