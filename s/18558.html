Session overview

This Would Never Get Published is a practical, story-driven PDW for scholars working on projects that feel “too weird” or “too messy” for mainstream publication. Many CTO-type studies—platform and algorithm audits, fast-moving AI contexts, proprietary or sensitive datasets, hybrid computational– qualitative designs, and idiosyncratic digital settings—stall after feedback such as “editors won’t go for this” or “CTO and IS reviewers won’t see the contribution.” This PDW opens that black box. We begin with brief testimonials from scholars who have successfully published atypical digital work in IS and Management journals, as well as CTO outlets. They walk participants through what they originally pitched, where editors pushed back, how they reframed the contributions and methods, and which concrete artifacts (figures, paragraphs, cover letter) helped them move forward. We then run two “hot-seats”: participants present current projects that feel “unpublishable,” such as null platform effects, complex hybrid designs, or highly contextual digital settings. An editor/AE panel offers targeted suggestions on repositioning, framing data constraints, and choosing venues, followed by short reactions from the room. In the final part of the PDW, all participants work in small groups to build a one-page “Convince-the-Editor Plan” for their own projects or ideas. They can choose to focus on an ongoing paper, a neglected project they had to drop, or a “fantasy” study they have never dared to frame for a CTO, IS, or Management outlet. The PDW normalizes uncertainty about digital work while foregrounding rigor and integrity. It is suitable for scholars at all career stages who want concrete language and strategies to turn unconventional research on communication, technology, and organizations into contributions that IS, Management, and CTO communities can recognize and publish.
