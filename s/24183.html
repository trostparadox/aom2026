Creativity as a Social Process: Self-Verification and Self-Expansion Pathways to Relational Outcomes

MOC
Yixin Yang (University of Macau); Xiaowan Lin (University of Macau)
Employee creativity has primarily been conceptualized as an individual behavior that drives performance outcomes, with comparatively little attention paid to its interpersonal consequences. Adopting a social process perspective, we conceptualize employee creativity as a relationally embedded phenomenon that shapes coworkers’ responses to creative employees. Integrating self-verification theory and self-expansion theory, we develop a dual-pathway framework that explains how employee creativity influences coworker relationship outcomes through coworkers’ self-related perceptions. Specifically, employee creativity promotes coworker trust by signaling self-verification striving and increases coworkers’ willingness to cooperate by fostering coworker self-expansion. We further identify coworker trait cynicism and learning goal orientation as dispositional boundary conditions, such that trait cynicism weakens the self-verification pathway, whereas learning goal orientation amplifies the self-expansion pathway. In addition, we propose team cognitive diversity as a contextual moderator that intensifies both pathways by promoting deeper processing of creative ideas and increasing exposure for novel insights, making creativity more likely to be interpreted as self-verification striving and as a source of self-expansion. By theorizing employee creativity as a social process yielding positive relational outcomes through self-related mechanisms, this paper advances creativity research, extends self-based theories to workplace interactions, and offers an inspiring framework for future theorizing on creativity and workplace relationships.
