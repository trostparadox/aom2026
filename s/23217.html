Calibrated Conformance: How Social Ventures Mobilize Resources Over Time in Misaligned Contexts.

ENT
Anjali Chawla; Bandita Deka Kalita
Entrepreneurs depend on sustained resource flows to survive and grow, yet we have a limited understanding of how ventures mobilize resources over time when their core cultural elements are not sufficiently aligned with resource holders' priorities. Drawing on an 11-year longitudinal case study of a university-based social venture in Canada, we examine how poorly aligned ventures create and renew resonance to repeatedly mobilize resources while maintaining their core mission. Our findings reveal calibrated conformance—a path-dependent process through which ventures iteratively attune their meaning-making by strategically accessing and leveraging cultural elements valued by resource holders at different points in time. Our findings contribute to the existing literature on cultural entrepreneurship and resource mobilization, as well as to the literature on social entrepreneurship.

Economic Liberalization, Career Entry, and Individuals’ Subsequent Entrepreneurial Decisions

ENT
Han Jiang (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen); Jiapeng Li (Central University of Finance and Economics); Rui Wu (University of Science and Technology Beijing); Delin Yang (Tsinghua University)
In this study, we examine an intriguing yet understudied question in prior entrepreneurship literature, i.e., the implications of economic liberalization for individuals’ entrepreneurial decisions. We develop an institutional imprinting framework to address this question, proposing that after experiencing economic liberalization during their career entry, individuals with more available resources will be less likely to become entrepreneurs throughout their subsequent career development. We test this theoretical proposition in an intriguing historical setting, i.e., the college labor market liberalization in China between 1984 and 2000, an economic liberalization process to establish marketized institutions for college graduates to search for jobs and lift the traditional job assignment system controlled by public authorities. Evidence from a large-scale life-course alumni survey of an elite university in China indicates that after experiencing the college labor market liberalization process during their career entry, college graduates from families with higher economic status or institutional linkages will generally be less likely to engage in entrepreneurial activities throughout their subsequent career development.

Legitimacy of the Social Entrepreneur: Perspectives from the Market and the Vulnerable Community

ENT
Allegra Levandoski
The research investigates how social entrepreneurs gain legitimacy and resources from different audiences, such as the market and peripheral communities. It is justified by the paradox between the logic of commercial entrepreneurship and local social demands. The study is relevant for analyzing discursive strategies in the case of Gerando Falcões and their applicability to other social contexts. The methodology used is a single case study with a content analysis of videos featuring entrepreneur Eduardo Lyra (2014–2024) available on YouTube.

Timing the Leap: A Real Options Analysis of First-Time Lobbying by Entrepreneurial Ventures

ENT
Miranda J. Welbourne Eleazar (University of Iowa); Jinsil Kim (The College of New Jersey); Seung-Hyun (Sean) Lee (The University of Texas at Dallas)
Entrepreneurial new ventures must make the most of their inherently uncertain regulatory environment with limited resources. Although venture capital funded entrepreneurial ventures with little experience with non-market strategies may be tempted to devote resources toward market-based proven “safer” bets, allocating resources toward more uncertain non-market strategies may offer substantial payoffs for the ventures. We take a real options perspective to address the question: what makes ventures decide to devote valuable resources toward non-market strategies in the form of lobbying earlier, shortening the time to first-time lobbying? We develop an Entrepreneurial Lobbying Option framework, in which we theorize that more market options directly related to economic exchange (such as patents), or threats to market options (such as lawsuits), can spur new ventures to more quickly recognize the need to obtain a non-market option through engagement with political actors in the form of lobbying. We further theorize that this effect is facilitated through knowledge transfer from venture capitalists with lobbying experience. We find support for our hypotheses, contributing to the literature on entrepreneurship, real options, and lobbying.

Urban Entrepreneurship Meets Institutional Contradictions: A Case Study

ENT
Yung-Ching Tseng (Yuan Ze University); Hua-Wei Hung (National Tsing Hua University)
Urban entrepreneurship is increasingly central to city development strategies, yet research has largely emphasized successful public–private collaboration while underexamining the institutional tensions that destabilize such initiatives. Drawing on an institutional contradiction perspective, this study examines why urban entrepreneurial ventures are especially vulnerable in politicized governance environments. We analyze a longitudinal qualitative case of iFiberCo (a pseudonym), an entrepreneurial firm established under a 25-year Build–Operate–Transfer arrangement to develop a citywide fiber-optic network in Taipei. Although initially promoted as a flagship smart-city partnership, the project gradually deteriorated into political contestation, administrative conflict, and legal dispute. Our analysis identifies three interrelated contradictions—political intervention, misalignment between public and private interests, and erosion of social legitimacy—that interacted recursively over time, progressively destabilizing governance routines and entrepreneurial engagement. We contribute to urban entrepreneurship research by foregrounding institutional conflict as a dynamic condition shaping entrepreneurial participation in city governance.
