Bricolage as a Mediator between Transformational Leadership and Intrapreneurship

Session overview

Intrapreneurship is a critical source of innovation and renewal within established organizations. Although leadership has been widely recognized as an important contextual driver of intrapreneurial behavior, empirical evidence regarding the role of transformational leadership remains mixed, suggesting that leadership effects may depend on underlying behavioral mechanisms. Addressing this gap, this study examines entrepreneurial bricolage as a mechanism linking transformational leadership to intrapreneurship. Drawing on proactive motivation theory (Parker et al., 2010), we propose that transformational leadership enables intrapreneurial behavior by fostering employees’ capacity to “make do” through creatively recombining and mobilizing available resources under constraints (Baker & Nelson, 2005). The mediation model was tested using dyadic, multi-source data from employees and their immediate supervisors, with job tenure included as a control variable. Results indicate that transformational leadership is positively related to entrepreneurial bricolage, which in turn is positively associated with intrapreneurship. Entrepreneurial bricolage fully mediates this relationship, highlighting bricolage as a central action-enabling mechanism for intrapreneurial behavior.
