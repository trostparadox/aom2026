Feel Their Pain or Take Pleasure? Third Party Responses to Witnessing Territorial Infringement

CM
Xingwen Chen (Fudan University); Zheng Zhu; Hanyi Wang (Fudan University)
Territorial infringement occurs when a person, without entitlement, claims, takes, or uses a territory that another individual perceives as belonging to them. Although prior research has primarily focused on victims’ reactions to territorial infringement, far less is known about how third-party observers respond emotionally and behaviorally to such events. Drawing on appraisal theory of emotion, this study examines how competitive climate shapes observers’ reactions to territorial infringement. We propose that in low competitive climates, observers are more likely to appraise the infringement as a threat to collective objectives; such appraisals elicit sympathy, a prosocial emotion that motivates supportive behaviors. In contrast, in high competitive climates, observers are more likely to take pleasure in others’ misfortune, thereby eliciting schadenfreude and reducing coworker support. In study 1, we follow a multistep procedure to develop and validate a scale of territorial infringement. In Study 2, we test the proposed model using time-lagged data from 223 employees, and the results largely supports the hypotheses. These findings offer important implications for theory and practice.
