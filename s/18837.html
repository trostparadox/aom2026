Talent Management as a Driver of Psychological Capital

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Daniel Gajda University of Economics in Katowice; Adrian Pyszka University of Economics in Katowice

Session overview

Although talent management (TM) has become a central component of contemporary human resource management (HRM) systems, little is known about its effects on employees’ psychological resources. Addressing this gap, this study examines how TM practices, as perceived by talent pool members, influence psychological capital (PsyCap), and investigates the roles of talent-perception incongruence and perceived organizational support (POS) as underlying mechanisms. We conceptualize TM as a system of HRM practices that signals employees’ value and future potential. Drawing on signaling theory and organizational support theory, we argue that the effectiveness of talent-related signals depends not only on the presence of TM practices but also on their ability to foster employees’ perceptions of organizational support. Talent-perception incongruence — a misalignment between organizational designation of employees as talent and employees’ self-perceptions — is expected to weaken these signals by undermining POS. To empirically test the proposed relationships, we used survey data from 730 employees formally designated as talent pool members in 33 large organizations. Structural equation modeling shows that perceived TM practices are positively related to PsyCap and POS, while also reducing talent-perception incongruence. Although incongruence does not directly affect PsyCap, it negatively influences POS, thereby limiting its role in fostering employees’ psychological capital. Mediation analyses indicate that POS fully mediates the relationship between incongruence and PsyCap. The findings demonstrate that TM practices enhance employees’ PsyCap through perceived organizational support, and that incongruence in talent perceptions undermines PsyCap indirectly by weakening this support.
