Democratizing Organizations Through Employee Emancipation

OMT
Nazik Beishenaly (KU Leuven); Emily S. Block (University of Alberta); Johanna Mair (European University Institute)
Organizational democracy is often conceptualized as a rights-based approach that emphasizes workers’ participation in safeguarding individual freedoms. This perspective assumes employees possess civic knowledge and capacities for self-governance. However, in authoritarian contexts marked by hierarchical traditions and surveillance, such autonomy is constrained, limiting meaningful participation. Drawing on emancipatory entrepreneurship literature, this study explores how organizational leaders can enact democratic organizing by fostering employees’ “democratic character.” Through an in-depth case study of Ololo – a network of creative hubs in Kyrgyzstan, a post-communist society with persistent democratization challenges, we examine how founders embed democratizing principles into organizational practices. Based on an extensive qualitative study, we develop a process model of democratization through four mechanisms: de-normalizing, dignifying, reaffiliating, and responsibilizing. We conceptualize this as an emancipation-enacting approach, contrasting with the dominant rights-protecting view. Our findings contribute to understanding democratization as a staged process, where emancipation precedes effective rights protection, and highlight implications for research and practice in organizational democracy.
