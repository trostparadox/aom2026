Does Digital Transformation Undermine or Uplift Leaders?

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MOC

People

Authors: Jiyu Li; Zhenyuan Wang East China Normal University

Session overview

As organizations increasingly implement digital transformation, leaders’ experiences with automation versus augmentation may differentially affect their perceived status through distinct psychological pathways. Drawing on threat rigidity theory, we propose that leaders’ experiences with automation-driven transformation trigger perceptions of identity threat, which in turn undermines their perceived status. In contrast, experiences with augmentation-driven transformation enhance perceptions of goal congruence, thereby elevating perceived leader status. Furthermore, we posit that task structure moderates these indirect effects: it amplifies the negative indirect effect of automation via identity threat, while attenuating the positive indirect effect of augmentation via goal congruence. We test this model using a multi-study design incorporating field and experimental methods across diverse cultural contexts. Our findings offer novel theoretical insights into how digital transformation shapes leader status and provide practical guidance for managing leader responses to technological change.
