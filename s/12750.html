Session overview

Recent years have seen a marked increase in shareholder activism, defined as actions taken by shareholders to influence firms’ strategic policies and governance practices. Because boards of directors are central to setting and overseeing firms’ strategic direction, activist shareholders increasingly rely on securing board representation as the primary mechanism for advancing their preferred strategic changes in targeted firms. Despite the growing prevalence of activist-appointed directors, management and strategy research has lagged in developing theory that explains how activist influence operates inside the boardroom and how it translates into strategic choice, strategic change, and value creation. Through a set of complementary perspectives examining activist board interventions, institutional investor heterogeneity, interorganizational spillovers, board engagement dynamics, and activist–organization fit, this symposium aims to stimulate theoretical dialogue and advance understanding of how shareholder activism reshapes firm strategy from within boards.
