International Business on the Grand Chessboard: The Effect of Sanctions on Divestment

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 3
IM

People

Author: Anna Andronovich Vienna University of Economics and Business

Session overview

International sanctions have become a central instrument of geopolitical competition and increasingly shape the strategic environment of international business. Despite growing scholarly attention to sanctions, we still lack systematic firm-level evidence on why some MNEs divest from sanctioned markets while others remain. This study examines how power-dependence relationships at both country and firm levels influence divestment decisions following the unprecedented sanctions imposed on Russia after 2022. Drawing on power-dependence theory and institutional perspectives, we argue that firms’ exposure to US-led geopolitical power structures and alternative power poles, particularly the BRICS countries, shapes their strategic responses to sanctions-induced political risk and uncertainty. Using hierarchical logistic regression on a panel of 1,261 international firms operating in Russia between 2022 and 2024, we test the effects of political alignment with the United States, BRICS affiliation, and firms’ market dependencies on divestment decisions. The study contributes to international business research by integrating geopolitics, sanctions, and power-dependence theory, and by offering novel insights into firm heterogeneity in response to major geopolitical shocks.
