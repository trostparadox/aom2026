Session overview

Social class research in entrepreneurship is gaining momentum, and a growing community is exploring how social class and social mobility shape entrepreneurs and entrepreneurial outcomes. This PDW welcomes both scholars who already have projects underway and those new to the conversation who want to learn, connect, and get inspired. Social class and mobility offer a particularly generative lens for entrepreneurship research. They bring economic, sociological, and psychological perspectives into dialogue, and they connect micro experiences to meso and macro contexts. They are also multidimensional, spanning economic resources, social ties, and cultural repertoires; class origins and current position; and objective indicators, as well as subjective lived experience. This richness opens many research avenues, from who enters entrepreneurship and why, to how opportunities are pursued, resourced, and experienced over time. Yet this same richness can make publishing harder: audiences differ, and authors must make a small set of clear, defensible choices about theory, constructs, measures, and claims. The PDW tackles these challenges through four themes: theory and contribution; measurement and analysis; navigating the review process; and having impact with my work. For each theme, an emerging scholar shares an author perspective and a distinguished scholar who is or has been an editor at a leading journal responds with feedback and transferable advice, followed by a brief audience Q&A. The session concludes with theme-based roundtables. Participants will leave with practical strategies, new ideas for research avenues, and connections to a supportive community.
