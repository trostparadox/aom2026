Session overview

International migration has become a defining feature of contemporary organizations and labor markets around the globe. Immigrants now constitute a rapidly growing share of the workforce across North America and Europe, particularly in knowledge-intensive sectors such as science, technology, healthcare, and professional services (Kerr, 2013). Despite their importance to organizational functioning and economic growth (Hernandez et al., 2025), immigrant workers and their experiences at work remain only partially integrated into mainstream management and organizational scholarship. A central limitation of existing research in management is that immigration is often treated as a demographic status—capturing who immigrants are—rather than as a set of structural, institutional, and relational processes that shape immigrants’ lived experiences at work and in labor markets. As a result, dominant organizational theories of work, careers, identity, and inequality continue to rest on assumptions of stable citizenship, unrestricted mobility, nationally bounded careers, and culturally homogeneous work environments—assumptions that do not hold for a substantial and growing segment of today’s workforce. This panel symposium seeks to advance organizational scholarship by centering immigrant workers and their lived experiences as a core phenomenon worthy of sustained theoretical and empirical attention. Bringing together complementary scholarly perspectives, the panel aims to highlight how migration shapes organizational and labor market dynamics, particularly in knowledge-intensive and globally connected sectors.
