How Dual Authoritarian Leadership Influences Workplace Procrastination: The Role of Perfectionism

OB
Tingko Lee (National Defense University); Wan-Ju Chou (Chung Yuan Christian University); Kuoching Yen (National Defense University); Tsung hsien Yu; Chih-Hsing (Sam) Liu (National Kaohsiung University of Science and Technology); Heng-Guang Song
Drawing upon Conservation of Resources (COR) theory and the organizational frustration model, this study extends the literature on authoritarian leadership (AL) by examining how its dual dimensions—discipline-focused AL and dominance-focused AL—affect subordinate work procrastination through the mechanism of organizational frustration. Specifically, we investigated the moderating roles of two distinct dimensions of perfectionism: excellence-seeking perfectionism (ESP) and failure-avoiding perfectionism (FAP). Using a three-wave time-lagged design with 14-day intervals, we collected valid responses from 402 participants. The results indicate that: (1) discipline-focused AL reduces organizational frustration, thereby mitigating work procrastination; (2) dominance-focused AL increases organizational frustration, subsequently exacerbating work procrastination; and (3) ESP strengthens the indirect effect of discipline-focused AL on procrastination via frustration, whereas it weakens the corresponding effect of dominance-focused AL. However, the moderating effect of FAP is not significant. Theoretical implications, managerial applications, and directions for future research are discussed based on these findings.

Measuring Narcissistic Leadership: A New Scale And Its Validation Across Organizational Contexts

OB
Gerry Suryosukmono (National Taiwan University of Science and Technology); Tuong Vy Nguyen (National Taiwan University of Science and Technology)
This study aimed to develop and validate a multidimensional Narcissistic Leadership Scale (NLS) questionnaire, focusing on employees’ evaluations of narcissistic leaders. Grounded in contemporary narcissism research that emphasizes the three-factor model, we conceptualized three dimensions of narcissistic leadership: Attraction Narcisstic , Hostile Narcisstic, and Detachment Narcisstic. We then tested the factor structure to conduct item reduction and assess convergent validity through Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) in Study 1 with a student sample, and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) in Study 2 with a professional worker sample. In Study 3, we examined predictive validity by linking narcissistic leadership to employee outcomes, specifically employee silence and quiet quitting. The findings showed that narcissistic leadership positively influenced both employee silence and quiet quitting. Taken together, the results suggest that the NLS is a useful new multidimensional measurement tool that can advance our understanding of the characteristics of narcissistic leadership.

Two Faces of Narcissistic Leadership: How Grandiose and Vulnerable Leaders Shape Follower Outcomes

OB
Nishtha Arora (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Nishant Uppal (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Nivedita Joshi (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow); Prabhat Kumar (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow)
Narcissistic leadership undermines follower work outcomes, yet the distinct impacts of grandiose versus vulnerable subtypes remain underexplored. The present research examines how these dimensions independently and interactively influences follower work outcomes. Hypotheses are tested using multi-source data from leader-follower dyads (n = 334) across 13 multinational pharmaceutical firms operating in India. Results reveal that both grandiose and vulnerable narcissism harm outcomes, though their interaction weakens these negative effects, suggesting a compensatory dynamic where high levels of one subtype buffer the other's toxicity. Findings advance dark leadership theory by illuminating subtype interplay and contextual boundary conditions in high-stakes organizational settings.

Narcissistic Leadership and Workplace Flourishing: A Serial Mediation Model

OB
Theophilus Tagoe (University of Wollongong); Shamika Almeida (University of Wollongong); Alfredo Paloyo (University of Wollongong)
Grounded in the Conservation of Resources (COR) theory and the Narcissistic Admiration and Rivalry Concept (NARC), this study investigates how agentic and antagonistic narcissistic leadership influence workplace flourishing through sequential resource-loss processes in the AI age. Serial mediation models were tested using survey data from 502 employees in Australia. The findings indicate that agentic narcissistic leadership was indirectly associated with lower workplace flourishing via a fully mediated pathway, whereby leader behaviour heightened emotional exhaustion, which subsequently depleted psychological capital and reduced flourishing. In contrast, antagonistic narcissistic leadership exerted both direct and indirect negative effects on flourishing, with emotional exhaustion and psychological capital partially mediating this relationship. Across both leadership styles, emotional exhaustion emerged as a pivotal initial mechanism triggering further resource erosion. Overall, the results demonstrate that narcissistic leadership undermines employee flourishing through a sequential, rather than parallel, process of resource loss, extending COR-based explanations of leadership and wellbeing dynamics.

The Hallo’s Shadow: A Meta-Analysis of (Im)Moral Leadership Behavior and Employee Knowledge Hiding

OB
fan xu (Zhejiang Normal University); Changfeng Wang (Zhejiang Normal University); Yanglei Cao (Zhejiang Normal University)
Knowledge hiding (KH) is an intentional behavior with a clear moral dimension. It directly affects organizational innovation and performance. Scholars have examined some core antecedents of KH across levels, particularly leadership behavior. However, existing studies do not agree on how leadership behaviors influence KH. Based on social cognitive theory, we develop moral and immoral leadership behaviors and conducts a meta-analysis on 86 empirical studies with 89 independent samples (N=30,782). The results show that: (1) immoral leadership behavior increases employees’ KH, while the negative effect of moral leadership is not supported, and the difference between them is significant; (2) psychological safety plays an asymmetric mediating role in this process, as moral leadership behavior mainly reduces KH indirectly through psychological safety, while immoral leadership is more likely to directly trigger this behavior; (3) employee gender moderates only the immoral leadership path, with female employees more likely to engage in KH under high threat. Long-term orientation culture strengthens the negative effect of moral leadership behavior on KH, and the weakening effect of individualism is nonsignificant but is of potential meaning. These results clarify inconsistencies in the literature and reveal asymmetric employee responses to moral and immoral leadership.
