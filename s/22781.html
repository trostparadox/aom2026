Tackling Grand Challenges at the Community Level: Orchestration for Collective Action (WITHDRAWN)

SIM
Rossella Rocchino; Christina Julia Hertel; Sophie Bacq (IMD - International Institute for Management Development)
Social isolation has emerged as a defining societal grand challenge, with consequences that extend beyond individual well-being to shape health, educational, and economic outcomes at the societal level. While global in scope, grand challenges such as social isolation are deeply embedded in local contexts, where they are shaped by place-based relationships, social structures, and cultural dynamics. Consequently, neither top-down interventions nor purely bottom-up, community-led approaches are sufficient on their own. Top-down solutions often lack contextual sensitivity and risk reinforcing dependency, whereas many bottom-up solutions lack the resources, legitimacy, or organizational capacity required for effective self-organization. Leveraging the lens of orchestration, this paper examines how orchestration enables the combination of top-down and bottom-up approaches to collective action at the local community level in addressing a societal grand challenge, social isolation. Drawing on an in-depth single case study of a private foundation acting as an orchestrating organization, we analyze how collective action is coordinated to tackle social isolation. We show how the foundation establishes community hubs through a dual approach: mobilizing supporters such as governments, businesses, and NGOs to secure resources and institutional support, while simultaneously activating community agency and building social capital and local legitimacy. Crucially, the orchestrating organization creates organizational structures that bridge bottom-up and top-down approaches by providing shared direction and accountability among diverse actors. Our findings contribute to research on collective action for grand challenges and orchestration by identifying the mechanisms through which an orchestrating organization leverages key resources while fostering local capacity, enabling communities with limited self-organizing potential to become meaningful agents of their own change.

Organising Traditional Knowledge Commons for Rural Revitalisation: The Role of Platform Cooperatives

SIM
Neeraj Singh (Nalanda University); Kushankur Dey (Indian Institute of Management Lucknow)
Traditional knowledge commons (TKC) are embedded in rural communities, with collective ownership of socio-cultural resources that shape their livelihood pathways and well-being. However, these knowledge forms are vulnerable to erosion and extinction given the dominance of explicit knowledge forms underlying the hegemony of the neoliberal paradigm, which results in poor market integration, precarious livelihoods, and rural decline. The TKC struggle to integrate with contemporary digital markets dominated by platform enterprises due to their ‘tacitness’ and are often pushed to the margin, thereby serving as an accelerant for their marginalisation. The existing literature highlights the importance of TKC for rural revitalisation and the role of patron-governed platforms in this process. However, how platform cooperatives organise TKC remains ambiguous and underexplored in the literature. We bridge this gap, drawing on nexogenous development theory and the quintuple helix model of the knowledge economy. We develop a processual theoretical framework, which is empirically tested and refined through within-case and cross-case analysis of three platform cooperatives, namely Looms of Ladakh, SEWA, and Kudumbashree. The findings show that platform cooperatives serve as institutional intermediaries for scouting TKC from living repositories and endowment of rural communes, codifying and activating them, and responsibly translating them into market offerings. We also present the manifestation of platform cooperatives scaffolding complementors across the quintuple helix to unlock the potential of TKC for rural revitalisation. Keywords: Nexogenous development theory; Platform cooperatives; Quintuple helix; Rural revitalisation; Traditional knowledge commons

Digital Technology for Good: Unpacking Digital Pathways to Poverty Reduction in Rural China

SIM
Wan Liu (China Jiliang University)
Advancements in digital technologies have opened new possibilities for achieving digital poverty reduction, significantly reshaping the development landscape of rural areas. However, persistent challenges such as infrastructure limitations and the digital divide highlight the critical importance of integrating context-sensitive and inclusive approaches to ensure sustainable and equitable outcomes. This study aims to identify effective strategies for addressing the digital divide and achieving dual outcomes in the economic and social dimensions of digital poverty reduction. Adopting a mixed-methods approach that combines qualitative grounded theory with the quantitative Entropy Weight Method, this research conducts case analyses of five representative villages in Zhejiang Province, a leading demonstration zone for digital rural development in China. The results indicate that utilizing "Technology for Good" orientations through the pathways of "digital coupling" and "digital inclusion" can effectively reduce poverty and promote sustainable rural development. The findings emphasize that the integration of technology with local contexts and the implementation of participatory and inclusive strategies are essential for attaining economic and social poverty alleviation outcomes in rural areas.

Cultural Preservation as Grand Challenge: Organizing Community Archives as a Cultural Commons

SIM
Pacey Foster (University of Massachusetts Boston); Tony Passino
In this paper we contribute to theorizing new frontiers of commons scholarship by using the community archives movement as an example of cultural preservation as a grand scholarly challenge. Using the case of the founding of the Massachusetts Hip-Hop Archive, a community engaged archive of regional hip-hop arts and culture, we identify how an ethics of the commons served as a nascent unifying principle connecting the creation, curation, collection and digitization of community artifacts as they were transformed from privately held collections into a digital cultural commons and community program at the University of Massachusetts, Boston. Using granular data on the action arena of the establishment of the Massachusetts Hip-Hop Archive, we explore the complex dynamics and tensions in power, governance, ownership and community engagement with academic institutions as the collection evolved over forty years.

Institutional Entrepreneurs as Co-Facilitators for Rural Transformation

SIM
Yiwen Pan (Arizona State University)
The Chinese Rural CEO initiative is arguably one of the largest and most deliberate experiments in applying a proven urban-industrial development "playbook" to rural settings. We introduces the "Rural CEO" not merely as a manager importing urban tools, but as a catalyst operating in a "developmental incubator-space" where divergent sustainability objectives collide. Can the urban "efficiency logic" be successfully woven into the rural "resilience logic" ? Drawing on an inductive multiple case study of the "Rural CEOs" in Hangzhou city of Zhejiang Province in China, we explore how these actors conduct active re-coding through "institutional translation" to introduce, test, and adapt urban protocols to fit rural-agrarian contexts. These protocals include 1) structural bricolage to bridge the gap between administrative bureaucracy and market autonomy; 2) asset revaluation to transform ambiguous rural resources into exchangeable market assets; and 3) identity reconstruction to build legitimacy to fit local realities. By unpacking the black box of micro-institutional work in peripheral fields, this research enriches the micro-foundations of institutional logics and offers a nuanced framework for understanding the adaptive modernization of rural China.
