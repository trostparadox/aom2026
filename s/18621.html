The Missing Link: A Meta-Analysis of the Relationship between Impulsivity and Job Performance

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 10:00AM–11:00AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4

Authors: Peng Zhao The University of Texas at San Antonio; Carlos Chavarria The University of Texas at San Antonio

Session overview

Trait impulsivity is a fundamental driver of human behavior, yet it remains largely overlooked in the management literature despite its well-established predictive power across psychology, neuroscience, and related behavioral sciences. This study addresses that disconnect through a comprehensive qualitative and quantitative review—including a psychometric meta-analysis—of the relationships between impulsivity and key job performance criteria. Using the UPPS-P framework, we examine how specific facets of impulsivity—Urgency, (Lack of) Premeditation, (Lack of) Perseverance, and Sensation Seeking (UPSS)—differentially predict task performance, organizational citizenship behavior (OCB), counterproductive work behavior (CWB), and safety outcomes. Drawing on a mature evidence base (47 prior meta-analyses), more than 100 mappable impulsivity measures, and a substantial pool of primary studies from adjacent disciplines, combined using machine learning, we derive rigorous estimates of these associations and test the incremental validity of impulsivity relative to the Big Five. We make four primary contributions: (1) advancing theory through a granular, facet-level account of how impulsivity shapes employee task performance, OCB, CWB, and safety behaviors; (2) identifying boundary conditions that alter the strength and direction of impulsivity–performance relationships; (3) demonstrating that impulsivity explains unique variance in job-relevant outcomes beyond broad personality traits; and (4) outlining actionable implications for selection, training, and risk mitigation in high-stakes organizational contexts. Thus, this work bridges a critical gap between personality science and management and lays the groundwork for a sustained research agenda on impulsivity in organizational settings.
