Session overview

From team meetings to interviews to making decisions and requesting help, conversations are an essential part of organizational life, allowing individuals and organizations to make effective decisions. Yet, communications often fall short, as they depend on people’s willingness to share and disclose (rather than hide) information, and on the inferences people make about one another’s communications. In this symposium, we present novel research on organizational and interpersonal contexts where communications and resulting attributions critically influence both informational and relational outcomes. In particular, the papers presented will show how people’s (mis)perceptions affect choices around (1) initiating conversations and (2) self-disclosure. Next, we discuss how people can effectively (3) signal their priorities, and (4) disclose their identity (e.g., non-binary), by identifying how people interpret and respond to communications. Together, this symposium highlights the importance of communication, and points to avenues for improving the effective flow of information within organizations.
