Session overview

Donor advised funds (DAFs) are the fastest-growing form of philanthropy in the United States, but the least understood by nonprofit professionals. Using the latest research on DAFs, this workshop will explain how DAFs are changing the way people give to charity and how professional fundraisers and the nonprofits they work for can best solicit DAF grants and build relationships with DAF donors. The research presented in this workshop explains nationwide trends in DAFs and how they are changing the landscape of philanthropy. It uses account-level data from 111 DAF sponsors to explain individual and household giving patterns through DAFs. Survey data from over 2,000 DAF donors will shed light on donor motivations and strategies. And a rich body of qualitative research will explain how donors think about giving through DAFs and how fundraisers work effectively with DAF donors. The main takeaways from the workshop will be a clear, data-informed understanding of how DAFs work and how they change philanthropy. Participants will create actionable donor relationship strategies to effectively receive DAF grants, properly acknowledge and steward their gifts, and then customize their cultivation and solicitation strategies informed by in-depth research on donor behavior. This workshop is an abbreviated version of a workshop designed by the presenter and developed in collaboration with the Association of Fundraising Professionals. It is also currently being used to develop courses at universities that teach fundraising classes.
