Session overview

Public investments in science increasingly operate through scientific infrastructures, such as data repositories, analytical tools, and institutional arrangements that shape how research is conducted. While prior work emphasizes their effects on research productivity, less is known about how these infrastructures influence the trajectory of scientific research—specifically, which areas of inquiry receive attention and how research investment and effort are reallocated across research trajectories. This presenter symposium brings together empirical studies examining how public scientific infrastructures reshape research trajectories across scientific and technological settings. The papers show how public data releases, investments in data infrastructure, and widely available scientific tools affect problem choice and organizational responses. Across contexts, the studies demonstrate that public scientific infrastructures do more than expand research output: they actively shape what gets studied and by whom, including the visibility of historically neglected domains. The symposium contributes to strategy, innovation, and science policy research by clarifying the mechanisms through which public investments influence research investment decisions and the trajectory of research.
