Session overview

This study examines ambivalence as a psychological mechanism linking organizational commitment to employee voice during organizational change. Using two-wave survey data from 223 employees, we find that normative commitment is positively associated with ambivalence, whereas affective and continuance commitment show only marginal effects. Ambivalence mediates the relationship between normative commitment and voice behavior and shows marginal mediation for continuance commitment. Information sharing attenuates the positive relationship between normative commitment and ambivalence. Overall, the findings identify ambivalence as an underexplored driver of constructive voice during organizational change.
