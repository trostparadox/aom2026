Putting General Obligation to the Test: Meta-Analysis of Leader-Member Exchange and Job Performance

OB
Kabiru Maitama Kura (Bahrain Polytechnic)
Leader-member exchange (LMX) predicts behavioral outcomes from an organizational behavior perspective, but unclear is why LMX shapes employees’ behaviors. The mechanisms through which LMX influences job performance are especially fragmented, and little attention has been given to employee-side mechanisms that are grounded in social exchange theory and the principle of norm reciprocity. During a meta-analysis, I used perceived general obligation (PGO) to explain the link between LMX and employee performance. A comprehensive search of electronic databases yielded 131 published and unpublished studies, which collectively assessed 48,880 individuals. Results of the meta-analysis’s structural equation model suggest that LMX affects both task performance and contextual performance positively, and counterproductive work behaviors negatively. An indirect relationship between LMX and task performance through PGO was also found, and PGO mediated the relationship between LMX and contextual performance. An indirect relationship between LMX and counterproductive work behaviors through perceived general was also evident.
