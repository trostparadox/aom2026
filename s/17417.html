Session overview

Entrepreneurship research in Latin America often involves engaging with complex institutional environments, informal practices, and hybrid organizing arrangements that challenge fundamental assumptions in entrepreneurship theory. Scholars working in these contexts frequently encounter conceptual, methodological, and theoretical tensions when existing frameworks fail to fully capture entrepreneurial processes as they unfold. How can scholars move beyond treating Latin American settings as sites for applying theory and instead leverage them as sources of theoretical insight? This interactive Professional Development Workshop (PDW) addresses these challenges by creating a structured research incubator focused on early-stage idea development and collective sensemaking. The workshop provides a developmental space for scholars to refine research questions, clarify theoretical positioning, and explore methodological approaches suited to contextually embedded entrepreneurship. Through facilitated small-group discussions and plenary synthesis, participants and senior-scholars engage in identifying promising directions for theory building. The PDW supports scholars at all career stages who seek to advance entrepreneurship research that is context-sensitive, analytically generative, and theoretically impactful.
