Session overview

Mindsets, also referred to as implicit beliefs or lay theories, are individuals' naïve assumptions about the nature of human characteristics and social constructs. Research has extensively demonstrated the influence of mindsets on various outcomes, including employee coaching and performance appraisals. In recent years, three key trends have emerged in the study of mindsets in management: (1) expanding the scope to include more diverse, domain-specific mindsets beyond general intelligence and personality, (2) moving beyond the traditional framework of fixed mindsets (entity theory) versus growth mindsets (incremental theory), and (3) extending the study of mindsets from the individual level to meta-perception and organizational levels. Building on these exciting trends, this symposium presents cutting-edge research exploring how mindsets operate across levels and domains to shape behavior and outcomes. The first two papers investigate how beliefs about intelligence operate in novel contexts: cultural competence and artificial intelligence, demonstrating how mindsets shape participants’ cultural learning speed and their willingness to use AI following failure. The third and fourth papers examine the implications of mindsets at the organizational and societal levels, exploring how executives’ mindsets about organizational resources influence ambidexterity and how frontline poverty-alleviation workers’ mindsets about poverty shape their attitudes, motivation, and behaviors. Finally, the fifth paper investigates how meta-lay theories about entrepreneurial potential affect identification with role models and entrepreneurial aspirations. Together these presentations underscore the critical role of beliefs in driving progress, equity, and innovation.
