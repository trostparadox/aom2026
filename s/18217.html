When AI Writes Like You: How Generative AI Crowds Out Human Expression

CTO

Author: Xiaotian Dai Warwick Business Schoool

Session overview

As generative AI systems produce increasingly human-like text, a paradox emerges: rather than AI adapting to human norms, humans are adapting to avoid resembling AI. This study investigates stylistic crowding-out, wherein knowledge workers abandon authentic expressive practices that have become associated with machine-generated output. Drawing on affordance theory, we introduce the concept of affordance contamination—a legitimacy-based constraint that renders certain pre-existing human practices non-actualizable, not through functional limitation, but through social cost. Unlike replacement or augmentation, this pathway operates independently of whether workers use AI tools; it reshapes expressive possibilities for all. Through qualitative inquiry with knowledge workers across diverse organizational contexts, we aim to document which stylistic features become marked as contaminated, how workers engage in avoidance and substitution strategies, and who bears disproportionate adaptation costs. We anticipate systematic variation in crowding-out intensity, shaped by contextual conditions including the source of AI-suspicion cues, their situational salience, potential sanctions, and the availability of alternative expressions. This research seeks to extend affordance theory by showing how technology existence, rather than use, can constrain affordance actualization through legitimacy rather than function. In doing so, it reveals a dark side of AI’s workplace impact: the progressive narrowing of legitimate stylistic variation as human expression is crowded out from expressive territory that AI has come to occupy.
