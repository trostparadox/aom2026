Session overview

Extreme events such as wars, terrorism, forced displacement, natural disasters, and pandemics are transforming the ways entrepreneurship develops and functions. This PDW examines crises from a full lifecycle perspective, encompassing early warning signs, unfolding disruptions, and long-term recovery. We emphasize three key areas for theory development, such as viewing crises as ongoing processes, understanding the conflicting experiences of stakeholders, and moving beyond theories designed for stable environments. Methodological challenges include identifying early signals, analyzing both short-term and prolonged crises, and tracking recovery patterns over time. Through expert panels and interactive roundtables, the session bridges theory and practice, offering practical research directions for studying entrepreneurship in extreme situations.
