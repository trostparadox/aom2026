Toxic Environment, Silent Members: How Aggressive Culture Undermines Belonging Online

CTO
Mengru Zhao (University of Tsukuba); Sen Zhang (Kyoto Tachibana University)
Online communities have emerged as vital platforms for communications across temporal and geographical boundaries, yet internet aggression has become a significant barrier to community development. While previous research has predominantly focused on internet aggressive behaviors and their impact on individual victims, the community-level mechanisms through which aggressive culture influences member psychological connection and engagement remain underexplored. Drawing on ecosystem theory and social cognitive theory, this study examines how perceived aggressive culture influences members’ belongingness-related behaviors through sense of virtual community, while investigating the moderating roles of Big Five personality traits. Through a time-lagged field study of Reddit members, we reveal that aggressive culture negatively affects sense of virtual community, which in turn reduces belongingness-related behaviors such as information-seeking behavior and communication within the community. Extraversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, and openness weaken the negative relationship between aggressive culture and sense of virtual community, while neuroticism amplifies it. These findings advance our understanding of environmental factors in online communities and provide practical implications for community management by identifying protective mechanisms at technological and community-building levels.
