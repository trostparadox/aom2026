Session overview

We describe the past, present, and future of Artificial Intelligence (AI) capabilities and risks for organizations. AI has evolved from narrow, task-specific applications to pattern-matching systems operating with greater autonomy. We are at an inflection point where AI's productivity benefits are overshadowed by amplified risks. Organizations have reported that ‘shadow AI,’ the unauthorized and ungoverned use of AI by employees, has made it more difficult to address data breaches resulting from cyberattacks, increasing remediation costs by more than $600,000 per incident. Agentic AI enables systemic automation that improves efficiency but also amplifies risks—enabling zero-click attacks, prompt injection, and proliferating non-human identities like deepfakes. In the future, Artificial General Intelligence and Artificial Superintelligence will enable AI systems to match or surpass human capabilities, thereby creating additional benefits and risks for organizations. Quantum computing is on the horizon, and there may come “Q-Day” when the speed and capabilities of quantum systems make current cybersecurity and cryptography obsolete.
