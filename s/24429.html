Female Angel Investors Challenge Gender-Role Bias in Early-Stage Funding

ENT
Yunus Emre Isik (King's College London); Ylva Baeckström (King's College London)
Gender role expectations shape how investors evaluate entrepreneurs, advantaging men in commercial ventures and women in social ventures. Entrepreneurs who defy these roles face early-stage funding challenges. We examine whether female angel investors mitigate these biases. Using a novel dataset of 7,336 investment decisions made by 111 angel investors across 14 countries, we find that male investors systematically rely on gender role expectations: they are 1.47 times more likely to invest in male-led commercial ventures and 1.43 times more likely to invest in female-led social ventures. Female investors, by contrast, show little evidence of using such heuristics. They are more likely than male investors to support gender role-defying founders. We further find that role-defying ventures - female-led commercial and male-led social ventures - when funded by female angel investors, achieve subsequent funding and exit outcomes comparable to role-conforming ventures backed by male angel investors. Our findings highlight the role of female investors in paving the way for more gender equal access to early-stage funding without compromising performance.
