Strategic Management and Limits of AI Deployment

STR
Navaneeth KP (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode)
Can algorithms replicate or even replace human strategic reasoning? This paper theorizes that increased adoption of AI results in the displacement of strategic autonomy from firms. The four-stage attention displacement describes how AI adoption shifts cognitive focus and agency across organizational levels, leading to erosion of strategic agency at the firm level. This paper theorizes the strategic limits of AI by introducing the concepts of non-computability and ecological bounded rationality to define the limits of AI deployment. Using computation theory, the attention-based view, and ecosystem framing, the paper argues that strategy-making is a non-computable process because it requires moral-ethical judgment and abductive reasoning, which cannot be replicated using algorithms. I conceptualize AI compatibility as a boundary condition for the responsible deployment of AI in strategic contexts and propose an AI–ESG Compatibility Framework that links computational feasibility with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) goals. In combination, these two frameworks offer a multi-level theory of how firms can responsibly implement AI in alignment with cognitive and ESG constraints. The paper positions AI not as a universal optimizer but as an instrument whose effectiveness is contingent on human oversight and ESG compliance. Implications for responsible AI transformation, governance, and strategy professionals are discussed.
