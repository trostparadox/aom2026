Session overview

This PDW explores the entrepreneurial process from both the innovation management and organizational design perspectives, focusing on how entrepreneurs design, use, and scale Artificial Intelligence (AI) technological toolkits across stages of venture creation. Moving beyond views of AI, and more recently generative AI (GenAI), as a standalone tool or black box, the workshop examines how purposeful AI experimentation, ranging from early ideation to firm-wide adoption, reshapes entrepreneurial judgment, entry, and market outcomes. The panel convenes leading scholars studying scientific innovation, evaluation, strategic decision-making, and agentic systems to examine how the technological landscape evolves as entrepreneurs act as contrarian innovators who identify and pursue opportunities differently from others in the market. Extending beyond the entrepreneurial contexts, the workshop also features a more generalized examination of complementarity, co-invention, and architectural complexity within organizations from a process perspective. Following the panel presentations, the PDW concludes with interactive sessions with Q&As and roundtable feedback for pre-submitted abstracts. Participants will leave the workshop with a mechanism-level understanding of what makes GenAI distinct from earlier general-purpose technologies such as personal computing and the internet, and how these differences reshape entrepreneurial action, organizational design, and innovation outcomes.
