Session overview

Algorithmic decision-making and artificial intelligence (AI) have proliferated in organizations. Unlike prior technologies, AI can perform complex tasks that once required human labor. This novel capability, along with the substantial economic gains that AI promises, has driven the widespread adoption of AI across industries. Yet despite AI’s growing prominence in organizations, many open questions remain about AI systems and one of the most revolutionary technologies: large language models (LLMs). Insufficient understanding of AI and LLMs has already produced serious consequences, including documented cases of unintentional discrimination in high-stakes situations. This symposium explores the implications of the rapid adoption of AI and focuses on three interconnected components: how human judgments and biases shape algorithms, how algorithms make decisions, and how individuals respond to algorithmic decision-making. Drawing from experimental and computational methods, the presentations explore both the risks of algorithmic decision-making and the potential for AI to enhance research and organizational decision processes. Our discussant, Juliana Schroeder, the Harold Furst Chair in Management Philosophy and Values at the UC Berkeley Haas School of Business, will lead a discussion about the presentations and future research directions in the field.
