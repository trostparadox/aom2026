Less Help, Better Ratings? Impact of Perceived AI Availability on Leader Helping and Effectiveness

OB
Haixin Zeng (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Chiyu Chen (Huazhong University of Science and Technology); Yun Bai (Huazhong University of Science and Technology)
With the rapid proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) in the workplace, the dynamics of workplace interpersonal interactions are undergoing significant transformation. While predominant research has focused on the effects of AI usage on employees outcomes, the leader-centric implications in the contemporary workplace remain obscured. Drawing on conservation of resources theory, this study investigates how leader perceived employee AI availability alters resource allocation, specifically, dependence helping, which in turn affecting their leader effectiveness. Using data collected from 140 leader-subordinate dyads through a three-wave time-lagged research design, we received support for this mediation model. Furthermore, we identified a critical boundary condition: the negative relationship between leader dependence helping and leader effectiveness was buffered when leader sacrifice is higher. These findings contribute to literatures on leadership, helping and AI by highlighting a nuanced predictor of leadership effectiveness in the digital era.
