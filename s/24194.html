From Theory to Model: A Holistic Perspective on the Antecedents of Value Co-Destruction

RM
Hao Zheng (Eastern Institute for Advanced Study, Eastern Institute of Technology, Ningbo); Menghan Li (University of Nottingham Ningbo China)
The dark side of co-created services, known as value co-destruction, is receiving increasing attention as these services gain popularity. While existing literature primarily focuses on the value co-destruction process and its outcomes, there is limited exploration of its antecedents. This research adopts a holistic approach to investigate value co-destruction antecedents, focusing on the roles of companies, employees, and customers in the interactive co-creation process within a Business-to-Consumer context. The study reveals that value co-destruction can arise from various interactions among actors. Specifically, any combination of interactions between companies, employees, and customers can lead to service failures in a dyadic approach. Moreover, value co-destruction can also emerge from the collective influences of all three parties. Through qualitative analysis, we explore different scenarios of interacting effects that cause value co-destruction among these stakeholders. The empirical data gathered supports the development of analytical models that comprehensively encapsulate the dynamics of value co-destruction. Notably, we use the Taylor series approximation for modeling, which enables the formulation of four distinct types of value co-destruction models: the Direct Cascade value co-destruction model, the Dual Cascade value co-destruction model, the Sequential Influence value co-destruction model, and the Convergent Influence value co-destruction model.
