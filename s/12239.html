Session overview

Economic and organizational theories have long provided powerful frameworks for understanding inter firm relationships, particularly in areas such as governance, coordination, and value creation. However, these frameworks often rely on assumptions of human judgment, information processing, and relational governance that are increasingly challenged by the growing use of artificial intelligence and other algorithmic systems in interorganizational contexts. As firms rely on AI to evaluate partners, monitor performance, and allocate decision rights, traditional models may fail to fully capture how algorithmic mediation reshapes trust, power, and relational dynamics between collaborating firms. Expanding these foundational theories to incorporate algorithmic mediation allows for a deeper exploration of how digital technologies interact with managerial cognition, bounded rationality, and relational norms in shaping inter firm governance and collaboration. Algorithmic systems influence how partners are assessed, how uncertainty is perceived, and how control and coordination mechanisms are designed and enacted. These dynamics are particularly salient in complex interorganizational settings, such as alliances, platforms, and ecosystems, where relational considerations and information asymmetries play a central role. By addressing these dimensions, researchers can develop a more nuanced understanding of how firms manage inter firm relationships, allocate control, and adapt governance structures in digitally mediated environments. This Professional Development Workshop aims to explore these intersections by bringing together research on artificial intelligence and algorithmic technologies with theories of interorganizational relationships and governance. Building on prior PDWs that have examined digital technologies or inter firm governance in isolation, this workshop shifts toward deeper engagement with how algorithmic systems mediate relationships between firms. The PDW is designed to foster theory building and empirical development by encouraging scholars to integrate insights from strategy, organizations, and innovation. The 2.5 hour session will follow a three part format. In the first part, invited senior scholars will offer integrative perspectives on algorithmic mediation and inter firm relationships. This will be followed by presentations and discussions of focal research projects that examine AI and algorithmic governance in interorganizational settings. The session will conclude with a synthesis of key insights and future research directions. The second part will feature a semi structured panel discussion to facilitate interactive engagement. Finally, participants will engage in roundtable discussions to refine their research ideas with feedback from panelists and peers. Prospective participants will submit a brief description of their research ideas for discussion during the workshop.
