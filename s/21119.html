Do We Punish AI for Breaking Social Norms? The Role of SVO in the Pricing Negotiation with AI.

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 2
CTO

People

Authors: Bixiang Han; Bei Yan Stevens Institute of Technology

Session overview

Recent advances in generative AI enable systems to engage in human-like negotiation, allowing firms to deliver highly personalized customer interactions. Yet individuals may respond differently to offers presented by AI systems than to those presented by human counterparts. This study examines how Social Value Orientation (SVO; prosocial vs. proself) shapes behavioral responses to unfair offers from AI and human negotiators in an online buyback negotiation experiment. We find systematic differences across both offer unfairness and proposer type. Under slightly unfair offers, prosocial individuals are more likely to accept offers from human negotiators than from AI. In contrast, under highly unfair offers, they are more likely to accept offers from AI. In comparison, proself individuals are more likely to accept slightly unfair offers from AI and consistently reject highly unfair offers regardless of the proposer type. Beyond acceptance decisions, we analyze negotiation process behavior using text-based measures of negotiation engagement, constructed from two established dimensions: single-issue activity and substantiation. When offers are slightly unfair, prosocials exhibit greater negotiation engagement with human partners, while under highly unfair conditions, proselfs engage more actively with AI partners. The interaction between SVO and negotiation engagement helps explain observed acceptance patterns, revealing fundamentally different ways individuals respond to AI versus human negotiators. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for social preferences and partner type when designing adaptive AI negotiation systems to enhance effectiveness in human–AI interactions.
