When Leaders Exploit: Retaliation Fear Linking Exploitative Leadership to Evasive Knowledge Hiding

Authors: Kristina Vlaovic University of Montenegro; Aleksandra Jovanovic University of Montenegro; Stefan Jovetic University of Montenegro, Faculty of Economics; Nadja Laban; Ivan Radevic University of Montenegro

Session overview

Organizations depend on knowledge sharing, yet employees sometimes respond to interpersonal threat by withholding requested information in subtle and deceptive ways. Drawing on a self-protection perspective, this study examines how exploitative leadership, characterized by leaders’ self-interested and pressuring behavior, is associated with employees’ evasive knowledge hiding. We propose that exploitative leadership heightens employees’ fear of retaliation, but that such fear does not uniformly translate into evasive knowledge hiding because deceptive withholding can impose costs on the requester. We therefore identify spitefulness as a critical boundary condition that channels retaliation-based fear into evasive knowledge hiding. Using survey data from 230 employees in Montenegro, we tested a moderated mediation model. Results show that exploitative leadership is positively associated with fear of retaliation and that fear predicts evasive knowledge hiding primarily among employees higher in spitefulness. This study contributes by bridging retaliation-fear research with knowledge hiding, clarifying when fear produces deceptive (rather than purely avoidant) withholding, and highlighting spitefulness as a dispositional trigger in threat contexts.
