AI Decision Making in Organizational Layoffs: A Simulation Study of Decision Efficacy and Outcomes

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 3
HR

People

Authors: T.J. Raineri Rutgers University; Yixiao Shen Rutgers University

Session overview

Organizational decision makers are increasingly relying on generative artificial intelligence (AI) models to assist with critical business tasks, such as layoffs, which has generated substantial attention amongst business leaders and the media. Leaders have begun using mass-market models such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT and Google’s Gemini to support these choices. Despite this growing adoption, empirical investigations of the consistency and organizational consequences of AI-guided layoffs remain limited, creating an important gap between research and practice. Accordingly, we investigate key organizational outcomes of AI-guided layoffs using simulated human resource information system (HRIS) datasets. We compare workforce reduction decisions generated by (1) human decision makers without AI support, (2) mass-market AI models using minimal, non-engineered prompts, and (3) mass-market AI models using engineered prompts. This study makes three primary contributions. First, it helps bridge the research–practice gap in contemporary human resource management by providing systematic evidence on the use of generative AI in high-stakes HR decisions, particularly layoffs. Second, it integrates firm-specific human capital (FSHC) theory with the emerging literature on AI adoption, illustrating how AI-supported decisions may differentially affect the deployment and preservation of valuable human capital. Third, we theorize whether AI-driven decision making can constitute a meaningful component of a firm’s asset bundle, with implications for the development of sustained competitive advantage.
