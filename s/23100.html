Gig Crafting: Conceptualization and Cross-Country Scale Development and Validation

HR
Sari Mansour (TÉLUQ university)
Gig Crafting refers to proactive, self-directed strategies through which gig workers shape and optimize their work, such as diversifying across platforms, adjusting working hours, and pursuing income-enhancing opportunities under algorithmic management. Building on a multi-stage, cross-country research process, the multi-study process establishes the scale’s psychometric robustness, including its construct validity, measurement invariance, and predictive relevance. Results demonstrate that Gig Crafting is influenced by algorithmic management and is positively associated with both technostress and playful work design. Moreover, the findings highlight the role of Gig Crafting in supporting gig workers’ intention to continue working in gig economy settings, through mechanisms involving technostress and playful work design. This research is the first to conceptualize and systematically measure Gig Crafting, providing scholars and practitioners with a reliable tool to examine how gig workers proactively adapt to algorithmically mediated work. The validated scale offers insights for understanding gig workers’ strategies and outcomes, and for designing platform practices that support sustainable work across diverse cultural contexts.

‘I Am Your Coach and Your Colleague’: The Interplay of Job Demands and Resources of Internal Coaches

HR
Yi-Ling Lai (University of Southampton Business School); Yizhen Lu (University of Southampton Business School); Genping Ye (University of Southampton Business School)
In recent years, there has been a rise in internal coaching roles as organizations begin to seek existing resources to support their talent development strategy through internal coaching schemes. However, research is scant on how individuals who become internal coaches on top of their main role cope with this transition. Through two field studies, we explore work challenges that arise for individuals in this unique job arrangement, internal coaches, as they grapple with their job as both an employee and an internal coach in the organization. In the qualitative study, we found that internal coaches deal with a high level of job ambiguity, as their dual roles have unaddressed incompatible design and tensions that require clarity as they become internal coaches for the organization, and how they manage these challenges individually. In our field survey, our findings corroborated with findings from the qualitative study, where internal coaches respond to role ambiguities by regaining clarity over their roles (i.e., job crafting). However, they met with drawbacks, including lower creative behavior and organizational citizenship behavior. Our results reflect the need for internal coaches to be sufficiently supported in organizational settings, and we discuss the implications of managing internal coaches in organizational settings.

Agile HR: An Integrative Review Based on HR Flexibility Theory

HR
Swati Suravi (Indian Institute of Management Calcutta); Amit Dhiman (Indian Institute of Management Calcutta)
The modern business environment is characterized by rapid changes, frequent disruptions, and intense competition. In this context, organizations are increasingly adopting agile HR practices to more effectively leverage people resources to cope with environmental dynamism and uncertainty. Rooted in agile principles and methods that emerged in the software development context, agile HR involves the application of agile and lean principles to transform organizations’ HR units and functions in order to develop capabilities of quick adaptation, responsiveness, and resilience in human resources. These capabilities are key for organizations to swiftly adjust to changes and effectively cope with sudden unexpected disruptions. Both agile and flexible HR practices share the common overarching goal of achieving organizational adaptability through human resources capabilities; nonetheless, agile HR is distinctive in applying agile and lean principles such as short iterative cycles, waste reduction, continuous feedback, and customer centricity to enhance the operational efficiency of HR and maintain customer satisfaction amidst constant changes and disruptions. The relationship of agile HR with the HR flexibility theory is underexplored yet is pertinent to understanding how agile HR adds to the extant strategic HRM literature. In this integrative review paper, we use the theoretical framework of HR flexibility to systematically map the agile HR literature and integrate the findings. Based on our review, we highlight key agile HR practices, elucidate the distinctive characteristics of agile HR, and discuss how agile HR extends the HR flexibility theory, thereby enhancing conceptual clarity on agile HR.

Mapping the Theoretical Terrain of Job Crafting: Taking Stock of 25-Year Research

HR
Muzhar Javed (Rabat Business School, International University of Rabat); Mayowa T. Babalola (The University of Western Australia); Moazzam Ali (International University of Rabat)
Despite numerous valuable reviews and meta-analyses, the theoretical foundations of job crafting remain underexplored. Drawing on 325 studies over 25 years, we map the theoretical terrain of job crafting. Our review reveals the application of over one hundred distinct theories, highlighting the field’s conceptual diversity while simultaneously exposing the need for greater theoretical coherence. The job demands-resources model emerges as the most commonly employed framework, followed by the conservation of resources theory, highlighting their centrality in shaping scholarly discourse on job crafting. The other leading frameworks include job crafting theory, self-determination theory, and social exchange theory. Building on approach-avoidance model of job crafting, we categorize dominant theories into proactive orientation/motivation and reactive orientation/motivation theories. Using this perspective, we mapped the What, Why, How, When, and Where dimensions of job crafting. Additionally, we propose a dual-path framework that outlines how these two contrasting theoretical perspectives explain the positive and negative outcomes of job crafting, based on the dynamic interplay between contextual conditions and individual factors. We conclude the study by offering a forward-looking research agenda

Deploying Human Capital Across Team Boundaries:The Double-Edged Moderating Role of MTM in HCRE

HR
Jie Huang (Hunan University of Technology); Dejun Cheng (Nanjing University Business School); Hongzhi Chen (Fudan University); Jianquan Lyu (Nanjing University Business School); Binqing Zheng (Hunan University of Technology)
Project-based organizations increasingly rely on multiple team membership (MTM) to deploy human capital across team boundaries, yet prior MTM research has largely treated MTM as an antecedent and emphasized its “double-edged sword” association with performance. Reframing MTM through human capital resource emergence (HCRE) theory, we conceptualize MTM as a dynamic human capital allocation mechanism that shapes emergence-enabling processes and, in turn, moderates how different forms of human capital translate into performance across levels. We distinguish task-cognitive human capital (more technically general and cognition-based) from affect-interactive human capital (more context-specific, relationship- and affect-laden, and interaction-based) and develop multilevel arguments for when MTM enables versus constrains their value creation and emergence. Using archival personnel and project participation records from an IT project-based organization in East China (2,053 employees in 135 project teams; March 2018–March 2019), we estimate multilevel contextual-effects models and test within-level product terms at Levels 1 and 2 (individual MTM; project-level MTM scale). Results reveal a clear double-edged pattern: project-level MTM scale strengthens the positive relationship between team task-cognitive human capital and team performance, consistent with MTM facilitating cross-level amplification indicative of HCRE; in contrast, individual MTM weakens the positive association between affect-interactive human capital and individual project performance, suggesting that concurrent memberships can hinder the effective mobilization of relationship-based, affect-laden resources. Overall, in project-based contexts, MTM is more efficient in optimizing dynamic cross-boundary deployment and promoting cross-level emergence for human capital that is more technically general (rather than context-specific) and more cognitive (rather than affective)— namely, task-cognitive human capital—than for affect-interactive human capital. Importantly, the team-level MTM effect is not a simple aggregation of individuals’ memberships; rather, team-level MTM functions as an independent collective construct that shapes emergence processes. This study advances MTM research by introducing an HCRE lens and clarifying the multilevel meaning of boundary spanning, and offers actionable guidance for designing cross-boundary staffing to improve human capital deployment and project team performance.
