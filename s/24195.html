Can We Cease to be Distant Researchers? A Hermeneutic Critique of Grounded Theory & Gioia Methodology

RM
Mohammad Zainuddin (The Australian National University); Israr Qureshi (Queen's University Belfast)
In this paper, we critique the epistemic assumptions of Grounded Theory and Gioia Methodology, highlighting how their fundamental components create a distancing spiral—subjectivity, reflexivity, and interpretive distancing—which is not spatial but epistemological, distancing researchers from the nuanced realities of their subjects. This distancing can obscure the rich, interpretive understanding essential for exploring complex organizational phenomena. We advocate for hermeneutics—with its techniques of hermeneutic fusion, circle, and suspicion—as a means to counteract this spiral, proposing a more engaged, interpretive approach that deepens the researcher's involvement in the co-construction of knowledge.
