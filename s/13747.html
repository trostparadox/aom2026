Session overview

Recent entrepreneurship research increasingly recognizes the whole-entrepreneur, a shift from viewing founders as purely economic actors to acknowledging their values, identity, emotions, and ethics. Yet, while ethical issues and founder values have gained interest in entrepreneurship research, these discussions remain fragmented and often disconnected from a broader developmental view. This PDW explores entrepreneurship as a moral practice with spiritual dimensions including purpose and inner development. Drawing on Carroll’s (2001) typology of moral, amoral, and immoral management archetypes, we map entrepreneurial orientations along an ethical spectrum and revisit them through the lens of ancient Greek philosophy. Specifically, Aristotle’s virtue ethics for the moral end; Gorgias’ sophistic claims (via Plato’s Gorgias) for intentional amorality; Plato’s account of ignorance (amathia) in the Republic for unintentional amorality; and the Thrasymachean view of justice as the advantage of the stronger (Plato, Republic I; Nawar, 2018) for the immoral end. We extend this framing through entrepreneurial identity research, showing how moral orientations find expression in founders’ roles, values, and purpose. In doing so, we provide a developmental framework linking ethical archetypes, philosophical grounding, and identity expression. Participants will use this tool to reflect on their own (or others’) moral journey as entrepreneurs.
