Session overview

The study of organizations intrinsically involves the study of hierarchy-related processes such as power, status, influence, and leadership. And, like many other organizational phenomena, these processes often reflect two distinct factors. The proposed symposium introduces a new range of two-factor perspectives on concepts that reflect the recent hierarchy framework proposed by Korner and colleagues (2025): The presentation by Hays, Lee, Blader, and Galinsky focuses on power and status (bases of hierarchy); they offer a new Social Relations-Approach Theory of Social Hierarchy, arguing that status is a relatively ignored basis of hierarchy relative to power, and that the study of both bases can be advanced by recognizing approach and social relations as the two fundamental dimensions underlying hierarchy. The next two presentations focus on influence (the outcome stage). Overbeck, Lewis, and Kirley present data connecting specific influence tactics (behavioral expressions of hierarchy) with the two dimensions proposed by the Social Alignment Theory of Power (Fast & Overbeck, 2022), showing how influencing behaviors reflect resource control and capacity for volitional influence. Bohns, Sommers, and Schlund also examine influencing; rather than focusing on its drivers, they examine outcomes of influence, demonstrating how more associative influencing behaviors can produce a sense of informed consent, elevating the influence beyond simple compliance. Finally, Mitchell, Hahn, Boora, and Hollenbeck present on leadership (a related state that emerges from the outcome phase). Together, these presentations shed light on how the underlying drivers of power and status differ from one another and produce different consequences; how benevolent, functional, and consent-based influencing differs from coercive, dominating, compliance-based influencing, leading to different manifestations of power and status in organizations; and how claims and grants of leadership may vary depending on the formal authority (i.e., the structural power and status) held by a potential leader.
