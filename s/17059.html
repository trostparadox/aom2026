The Coffee Machine Effect: Configural Paths and Bottlenecks in Intradepartmental Knowledge Seeking

Saturday, Aug 1 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 1
MC

People

Authors: Anna Slawik Uniwersytet Jagiellonski; Aleksandra Rudawska University of Szczecin

Session overview

Knowledge-intensive firms benefit strongly from intra-departmental knowledge flows. Employees’ knowledge-seeking from co-workers is associated with higher work quality, stronger individual performance, and faster problem-solving. Prior research identifies individual, relational, and work-context antecedents such as learning goal orientation, social network, and psychological safety. Yet it typically offers a probabilistic, additive account of net effects, leaving causal complexity underspecified. We propose a configurational and necessity-based explanation of why some employees seek peer knowledge intensively while others do not. Combining sufficiency logic (tested by Qualitative Comparative Analysis) with necessity logic (tested by Necessary Condition Analysis), we examine how multiple conditions co-occur to produce intensive knowledge seeking and which conditions are indispensable for the outcome to occur at all. Our results show that intensive knowledge seeking does not materialize without a social network foundation: minimum thresholds of in-person encounters and close relationships with co-workers, which we term the “coffee machine effect”. Beyond these must-haves, we identify three distinct sufficient pathways specifying various profiles of intensive knowledge seekers, which we label “Networkers,” “Learners,” and “Opportunity Takers”. These pathways demonstrate that the same outcome can arise from different combinations of individual motives, relational resources, and work-context features, including configurations where knowledge seeking persists despite low psychological safety. Our contribution is to determine “must-have” enablers and “recipe-like” paths to success, offering managers and consultants diagnostic guidance to prioritize interventions and tailor knowledge-flow practices to different employee profiles.
