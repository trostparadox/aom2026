Reconfiguring Moral Agency in Algorithmically Mediated Organizations

Authors: Mansura Nusrat The University of Texas at El Paso; Richard Posthuma The University of Texas at El Paso; Prajya Rakshit Vidyarthi The University of Texas at El Paso

Session overview

As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes embedded in high-performance work systems (HPWS), organizations increasingly delegate morally consequential employment decisions to algorithmic processes. Although prior research has examined ethical risks associated with AI, existing behavioral ethics theories remain grounded in human-centered assumptions about moral agency, responsibility, and self-regulation. These assumptions become theoretically inadequate when decisions are mediated by algorithmic systems that lack moral intentionality yet exercise authoritative influence. In this conceptual article, we re-specify social cognitive theory to explain how AI-enabled HPWS reconfigure moral agency in organizations. We theorize AI not as a contextual influence on ethical behavior, but as a constitutive element of an organization’s algorithmic moral infrastructure, the system of algorithmic authority, opacity, and governance arrangements that shapes how organizational actors perceive legitimacy, accountability, and ethical responsibility.
