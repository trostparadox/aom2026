Two Wings of Growth: Altruism and Self-Compassion as Dual Drivers of Career Success

CAR
Yunita Sofyan; Ivan Radevic (University of Montenegro); Matej Cerne (School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana)
In a world where workplaces oscillate between burnout and resilience, this study uncovers the transformative power of altruism—not as a sacrifice, but as a secret weapon for career success. Leveraging three-wave data from 306 Indonesian employees across private and government sectors and 280 employee-supervisor dyads in Montenegro, we employed PROCESS macro to test a moderated mediation model. The results reveal a compelling truth: employees who help others do not just lift their colleagues—they elevate themselves. Self-compassion emerges as the golden bridge between altruism and career success, while workplace spirituality amplifies this connection, transforming ordinary workplaces into ecosystems of mutual growth. Our moderated mediation analysis confirms that in spiritually nurturing environments, altruistic employees experience stronger self-compassion development, leading to greater career satisfaction and promotability. Together, the finding offer a framework for positive psychology and career scholarships. These findings not only challenge transactional views of career advancement, offering instead a blueprint for organizational cultures where compassion and purpose drive professional success. But also offer implications for practice that organizations should intentionally design policies that recognize and reward altruistic behaviours, as these create virtuous cycles of compassion and success. When organizations honour the human spirit at work, and employee align daily tasks with transcendent purpose, they unlock unprecedented potential in their workforce.
