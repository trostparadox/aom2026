Authentic-Ambiguity Leadership Theory: A Theory of Leading Amid Irreducible Value Conflict

OB
Mohammed Yekini (Newcastle University); Sana Zahabiyoun (King’s College London)
Authentic leadership theory has become one of the most influential yet contested perspectives in leadership scholarship. While existing research has richly elaborated the ingredients of authenticity, it largely assumes contexts in which values can be coherently enacted and moral clarity is attainable. However, many contemporary leadership situations are characterised by irreducible value conflicts: persistent, morally legitimate tensions that cannot be resolved through ethical ranking, yet nevertheless require action. In this paper, we develop a theory of leadership for such conditions. Drawing on an abductive analysis of reflective narratives, we introduce Authentic–Ambiguity Leadership (AAL)— a leadership approach aimed at enabling groups to navigate conflicting values or priorities by surfacing the tensions that matter, holding space for their open exploration, guiding collective sensemaking, and supporting value aligned action without imposing a single viewpoint. We theorise moral–relational ambiguity tolerance as the developmental capability that allows individuals to act with integrity, relational openness, and agency when coherence is structurally unavailable. Our findings reveal a developmental trajectory in which repeated engagement with irreducible value conflict cultivates this capability, enabling the enactment of authentic self-leadership behaviours amid irreducible value conflicts. Crucially, we demonstrate that the four dimensions traditionally associated with authentic leadership: self-awareness, balanced processing, relational transparency, and an internalised moral perspective, are best understood as forms of authentic self-leadership behaviours, rather than as a leadership style. Building on this foundation, we theorise AAL as a distinct interpersonal leadership approach characterised by tension surfacing, tension holding, collective sensemaking guidance, and integrity-enabling facilitation.
