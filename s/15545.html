Session overview

The CTO division at AOM is deeply interdisciplinary, attracting researchers and practitioners from Information Systems (IS), Communications, Management and related fields. Across these disciplines, experiments (field, online, or lab) serve as a central methodological tool. Like the domains represented in CTO, experiments are inherently sociotechnical; the methodology relies on scientific methods to systematically investigate social phenomena and their interaction with technological systems. As a result, they play a critical role in advancing our understanding of human behavior. In recent years, scholars in these fields have shown increasing interest in how advanced technologies shape individual attitudes, decisions, and actions. Yet despite this growing focus, experimental research on the societal and behavioral implications of emerging technologies is still in its early stages, underscoring the need for renewed attention to experimental methods. To address this need, we propose a 90-minute PDW dedicated to experimentation. The workshop will feature a panel of experienced experimentalists who will share their insights, perspectives, and practical advice. Attendees will have opportunities to engage directly with the panelists, ask questions, and receive guidance related to their own experimental designs and challenges. The workshop will conclude with an open discussion about the future of experimental research within our fields. By including this PDW at AOM 2026, we aim to highlight the value of experimental approaches and to encourage their broader adoption across the disciplines represented in CTO, ultimately deepening our understanding of the core intersection between individuals and technology that defines the division.
