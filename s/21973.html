Managing Complexity in Long-Lived Platforms: Patterns of Technical Debt Management

Author: Amarin Mülthaler Aalto University

Session overview

Technical debt (TD) has evolved from a developer-centric metaphor into a strategic liability that shapes a firm’s capacity for digital innovation and long-term system health. It is increasingly viewed as an often unavoidable consequence of system evolution that requires explicit governance and alignment between technical and business priorities. This study examines how the lifecycle of TD evolves in operationally critical open-source infrastructure and what governance patterns help balance increasing structural complexity with operational reliability over time. Focusing on Apache Kafka, an open-source distributed event-streaming platform that has been under continuous development since 2011 and functions as a critical data backbone for many large enterprises, the study uses longitudinal data from the project’s source code repository and Jira issue tracker. By linking trends in structural code complexity with the lifecycle of reported issues, the analysis traces how TD is surfaced, prioritized, repaid, and mitigated in a decentralized governance setting. Preliminary results show that, despite rising structural complexity and an overall increase in reported issues, the backlog of high-priority functional issues has declined since around 2019, and the total stock of open issues has dropped noticeably since mid-2024, indicating evolving TD management practices that stabilize reliability as the system grows more complex. The study contributes a dynamic, system-level perspective on TD management in data- and event-pipeline infrastructures and offers governance-oriented insights for those seeking to balance rapid architectural evolution with long-term stability.
