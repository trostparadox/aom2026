Willingness to Pay for Sustainable Rice Attributes: Evidence From a Discrete Choice Experiment

Authors: Luigi Orsi University of Milan; Chiara Mazzocchi University of Milan; Jacopo Bacenetti University of Milan; Federico Zilia University of Milan

Session overview

Rice production is central to food security and rural livelihoods, yet it is associated with greenhouse gas emissions, high water use, and agrochemical pressures. This study examines how much consumers are willing to pay for specific sustainability attributes in rice and whether preferences differ across consumer segments. We implement a discrete choice experiment (DCE) embedded in a mixed-mode survey of adult consumers in Italy (n = 413). Respondents evaluated rice packages described by five attributes: organic certification (yes/no), reduced synthetic fertilizer use (yes/no), water-saving practices (yes/no), greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reduction (0%, 10%, 20%), and price (€3.50-€4.40/kg). Each respondent completed 13 choice tasks (two blocks), choosing between two product alternatives and a no-buy option. We estimate a panel mixed logit model with random coefficients to capture preference heterogeneity and compute willingness-to-pay (WTP) in €/kg. Results show that all sustainability attributes increase utility, with the highest WTP for organic certification (€0.83/kg) and fertilizer reduction (€0.63/kg), followed by water saving (€0.33/kg) and GHG reduction (€0.19/kg). Interaction analyses suggest meaningful heterogeneity by gender, age, education, consumption frequency, and environmental-impact knowledge. The findings imply that consumers value both established labels (organic) and process-based practices (water and fertilizer management), but that less visible attributes require clear, credible communication. These results inform labeling strategies and market-based instruments that can support the diffusion of more sustainable rice production practices.
