Cultivating New Product Development Team Agility

TIM
Yossi Yitshaki (Technische Universität Berlin); Abraham Carmeli (Tel Aviv University)
In knowledge-intensive, high-velocity environments, new product development (NPD) teams must respond both rapidly and flexibly—a capability often labeled team agility. Yet research has provided limited, integrated theorizing about how formal leaders cultivate team agility as a capability (beyond implementing agile methods) and when agility translates into performance benefits. Drawing on multisource, semi-structured interviews with 39 R&D professionals across 32 organizations, we inductively develop a leadership-based process framework that explains the emergence of NPD team agility through three mutually reinforcing pathways: psychological empowerment (leaders enable disciplined autonomy and reduce bottlenecks), cognitive (leaders build continuous learning and know-how), and relational (leaders cultivate strong relationships in the team). We further identify boundary conditions shaping the agility–performance relationship: agility is most performance-enhancing under high dynamism, high uncertainty, and critical response-time demands, but less so in more stable contexts. Overall, our work contributes to research on leadership and agile organizing by clarifying what leaders do to build team agility and when this capability is most likely to improve performance.
