Session overview

Scholars across disciplines are increasingly debating the methodological and ethical challenges of researching in extreme contexts. These contexts are often characterized by high risk environments that lie outside of organizational control and include events such as public emergencies, war, and social disruption. In an era marked by global polycrisis, cascading disruptions, and geopolitical tensions that erode established orders, researching in extreme contexts has become indispensable. Such contexts are not only challenging to study but offer opportunities to make visible the hidden dynamics and fragile relationships that remain obscured in stable settings, providing rich insights into how organizing and sensemaking unfold under pressure. Less discussed, but equally important, is the issue of identifying an appropriate methodology when researching in extreme contexts. While scholars emphasize the value of such studies for generating transferable knowledge, they caution that traditional and classical research approaches are inadequate under conditions of risk, uncertainty and urgency. As such, we suggest that a professional development workshop (PDW) would be beneficial for potential and interested authors to collaboratively discuss special issues in advancing research related to sociology and organization studies.
