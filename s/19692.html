When Impostor Leaders Take Risks and Aspire to Lead: The Role of Self-Disclosure

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM

Authors: Jia Seo Seoul National University; Sunghyuck (Ryan) (Ryan) Mah Seoul National University; MIN JU OH Seoul National University; Seokhwa Yun Seoul National University

Session overview

It is paradoxical that impostorism is prevalent among high-achieving individuals. This study examines how leader impostorism—leaders’ internalized feelings of self-doubt—is linked to leadership aspiration. Drawing on self-discrepancy theory, we found that self-disclosure serves as a boundary condition under which impostor leaders engage in risk-taking. Specifically, when leaders disclose their perceived actual self, leader impostorism becomes positively associated with risk-taking, which reflects alignment with the ideal leader self and, in turn, fosters leadership aspiration. Regression analyses were conducted using data from 244 full-time managers collected across three waves over a two-week interval. Results show that self-disclosure moderates the otherwise non-significant relationship between leader impostorism and risk-taking. Moreover, risk-taking mediates the conditional effect of impostorism on leadership aspiration, weakening its negative indirect effect under conditions of self-disclosure. This study advances the literature by identifying the conditional motivational mechanism among impostor leaders through which self-disclosure activates risk-taking, thereby shaping leadership aspiration.
