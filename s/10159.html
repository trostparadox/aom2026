Session overview

Decision-making lies at the heart of management and leadership. However, most decisions are not purely rational or emotional. While rationalists believe that human decisions result from a logical and calculative process, free from the influence of emotions, instincts or biases; researchers argue that individuals are limited by cognitive, temporal, and informational constraints. This professional development workshop addresses the nuanced relationship between emotion, reason, and spirituality in decision-making for educators and academic managers. Through experiential activities and reflective exercises, participants explore cognitive–affective conflicts and discover how spirituality can bridge these dimensions, fostering more integrated, ethical, and resilient choices. The workshop equips attendees with practical frameworks to enhance decision-making wisdom and effectiveness in complex professional environments. No additional registration is required. Participation is open to all AOM attendees. Attendance is limited, and seats will be allocated on a first-come, first-served basis. Seating will be arranged in clusters to encourage interaction and small-group discussions.
