Session overview

Mathematical and computational models offer unique strengths for theory building in management research, enabling rigorous examination of complexity and dynamics beyond traditional empirical methods. Classic contributions include organizational learning, search processes, and competitive interactions. However, substantial technical barriers have limited accessibility, leaving theoretical opportunities unexploited. This situation is changing dramatically. Generative AI tools have lowered coding barriers, making computational modeling accessible to researchers with basic technical knowledge. Yet what matters now extends beyond coding techniques—the critical challenge is developing modeling judgment: extracting relevant aspects from complex phenomena, simplifying appropriately, and focusing on key mechanisms. This requires tacit knowledge from modeling experience. This symposium features four junior scholars who demonstrate fresh approaches to computational modeling. Two papers innovatively reinterpret classic models: one examines how interpretation differences trigger decision instability in goal-aligned organizations, while another decomposes knowledge transfer dynamics across task similarities. Two papers model underexplored phenomena: one endogenizes nonprofits in value-based strategy to identify when interventions backfire, while another reconceptualizes problemistic search as political coordination amid interdependence. The symposium serves two objectives. First, presenters share practical wisdom about abstracting phenomena and translating them into models, making these methods accessible. Second, two experienced discussants provide feedback on what makes modeling theoretically compelling and what remains needed. This creates a learning opportunity for the audience to understand both the craft and evaluation of computational theory building in an era of methodological democratization. By demystifying modeling practices, we promote next-generation theory development across strategy, organization theory, and methods domains.
