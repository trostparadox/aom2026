When Regulation Meets Reality: A Comparative Diagnostic Intervention in Two Lebanese Exchange Houses

MC
Jocelyne Boulos Eid
Lebanon’s financial collapse has profoundly reshaped the country’s monetary landscape, accelerating a shift toward a predominantly cash-based economy and positioning exchange houses as central financial intermediaries. As traditional banking functions deteriorated, exchange dealers assumed expanded operational and systemic roles, exposing them to heightened risks related to compliance, fraud, and organizational breakdown. In response, regulatory authorities introduced reforms aimed at strengthening oversight, mandating compliance functions, and reinforcing internal control mechanisms within exchange houses. Building on prior intervention research conducted in the Lebanese exchange dealer sector (Author(s), 2025), this paper examines a consulting-led diagnostic intervention carried out in two exchange houses operating under the same regulatory constraints but characterized by distinct organizational cultures and governance structures. Adopting a Management Consulting and intervention research perspective, the study focuses on the diagnostic phase preceding the implementation of compliance programs. Using the Socio-Economic Approach to Management, the research compares organizational dysfunctions across both exchange houses in order to assess their convergence and to identify common intervention principles. The findings reveal that, despite contextual and cultural differences, both exchange houses exhibit remarkably similar dysfunctions related to work organization, decision-making, coordination, and control. The paper proposes a set of shared diagnostic insights while emphasizing the need to contextualize solutions according to each organization’s cultural and operational specificities. This study contributes to literature on organizational change under regulatory pressure, compliance and governance as organizational processes, and intervention-based research in fragile economic contexts (Savall & Zardet, 2011; Cummings & Worley, 2014; Van de Ven, 2007).
