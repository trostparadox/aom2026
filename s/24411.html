Waste Sorting and Emission Reductions: Spillover Effects in Waste-to-Energy Sector

ONE
Yuqi Wang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Jing Liang (Harbin Institute of Technology); Pengfei Liu (Rhode Island College); Xu Peng; Shulei Cheng (Southwestern University of Finance and Economics)
Rapid urbanization accelerates municipal solid waste generation and strains environmental sustainability. Many countries have shifted from landfilling to waste-to-energy (WtE) incineration because it reduces waste volume and recovers energy. Nevertheless, the WtE facilities remain pollution-intensive and emit greenhouse gases. While prior work has emphasized end of pipe controls, evidence remains limited on whether waste sorting policies lead to cleaner emission performance at WtE facilities. This study examines the spillover impact of municipal waste sorting policies on environmental emissions from WtE incinerators in China. We estimate panel regression models using monthly emission data of 1,028 facilities from 2020 to 2023. The results show that upstream waste sorting policies associate with an 8.2% reduction in total suspended particulate (TSP) emissions and additional declines in hydrogen chloride (HCl) and nitrogen oxides (NOx) of 6.9% and 3.2%. The abatement effect strengthens for facilities near administrative boundaries, which suggests that sorting policies curb boundary-related pollution. Furthermore, mechanism analyses indicate that sorting policies reduce emissions mainly by raising public engagement, stimulating green innovation, and increasing investment. Effects also vary across cities with different socio-demographic profiles. The findings show that waste sorting can improve the environmental performance of WtE systems and support more integrated waste–energy governance.
