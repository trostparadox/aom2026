Challenge-Hindrance Appraisals in Nurses’ Artificial Intelligence Adoption

HCM
Huiling Jiang (Tongji University)
This study takes a third-party perspective to examine how coworkers’ health service quality associated with AI usage influences observers’ own AI usage in health service delivery, and to offer implications for improving service quality. We recruited 437 nurses from three hospitals in China and conducted a two-month time-lagged survey. Coworkers’ AI-related service quality was modeled as the predictor, challenge appraisal and hindrance appraisal as cognitive mediators, AI understanding as a moderator, and observers’ AI usage as the outcome. To reduce common-method concerns, observers’ AI usage was rated by others, whereas the remaining variables were assessed via self-report. Structural equation modeling showed that higher coworker AI-related service quality was associated with greater observer AI usage and elicited both challenge and hindrance appraisals. In turn, challenge appraisal encouraged AI usage, whereas hindrance appraisal discouraged it. Moreover, AI understanding shaped how strongly coworker AI-related service quality translated into hindrance appraisal. Overall, the findings highlight cognitive appraisal as a key mechanism through which peer influences affect AI usage, suggesting that health service organizations should actively manage peer dynamics and strengthen AI understanding promoting safe, cost-effective, and high-quality care.
