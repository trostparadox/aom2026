Management Consulting of Standards and Norms. Contributions of the Tetranormalization Theory

MC
Laurent Cappelletti (CNAM); Florence Noguera (Paul Valéry Montpellier 3 University); Jean-Michel Plane
Tetranormalization is a concept initially defined by Professors Henri Savall and Véronique Zardet (2005, 2013). It designates the problems (their descriptions, causes and solutions) posed to companies and organizations by standards and norms inflation in the four (''tetra'' in Greek) major domains to which they are subject. From this concept, a theory of tetranormalization was developed thanks to the research of the eponymous international and inter-university network created in 2007, which today consists of nearly a hundred European, South and North American associate researchers in management, geography, economics, law and sociology (Boje, 2015; Cappelletti, Dufour, 2023; Savall, Zardet, 2023). The paper explains how the tetranormalization theory could be a fruitful framework to guide management consultants within all types of missions to help companies to better navigate standards and norms, and to create value through this better navigation.

”If We Pitch It,They Will Need It”: Consultants as Non-Neutral Channels of Diffusion

MC
Stella von Schnakenburg (Leuphana University of Lüneburg); Rainer Lueg
This study investigates the role of consultants in the diffusion of management accounting practices (MAPs). While prior research has largely examined the organizational adoption of MAPs, the specific role of consultants has received relatively little attention. This paper challenges the prevailing assumption in diffusion theory that innovations spread through neutral communication channels among relatively equal organizational actors. This assumption has shaped accounting research, which predominantly explains diffusion through demand-side factors. This paper develops a supply-side perspective that conceptualizes consultants as interest-laden intermediaries who translate, frame and legitimize management innovations, thereby reshaping diffusion processes. The study provides a focused review of peer-reviewed research that examines the interplay between consultants and diffusion of MAPs. Guided by Roger’s diffusion theory and the consultant’s role framework of Canato and Giangreco, this paper synthesizes insights from the literature to develop a diagnostic framework. The analysis shows that consultants do not merely facilitate the spread of MAPs. Their commercial and interpretive activities as information source, standard setter, knowledge broker and knowledge integrator actively construct what is diffused, how it becomes meaningful and when adoption appears legitimate. Their interventions transform diffusion from a communicative exchange into a commercially mediated process of guided compliance. The paper reconceptualizes diffusion as mediated governance rather than neutral communication. It exposes the political and commercial dynamics underlying the spread of MAPs and challenges the longstanding demand-side bias in accounting diffusion research. The diagnostic framework provides a critical lens for examining how consultants shape organizational change through translation, framing and legitimization.

Adaptive, Cross-Boundary Crisis Management: A Systems Framework for Consulting

MC
Mzamo P. Mangaliso (University of Massachusetts Amherst); Bradford J Knipes; Stephen A. Gazillo; Maureen Mo McGuinness (University of Massachusetts Amherst)
This paper contributes to management consulting practice by advancing an adaptive crisis management framework explicitly oriented toward systems-level and cross-boundary interventions. The model enables consultants to diagnose crisis readiness by examining how decision-making authority, leadership behaviors, and learning processes operate across organizational levels, functional silos, and stakeholder boundaries. The paper draws on insights from firms affected by the 9/11 attacks to develop an adaptive framework for consultants that integrates internal decision-making with external stakeholder coordination. The framework enables an alignment between organizational response capabilities and broader community or regulatory demands. It simultaneously emphasizes the complementary use of formal hierarchical authority and informal networked collaboration across organizational levels, thereby supporting rapid, accountable, and knowledge-rich decision-making. By emphasizing collaborative, multi-level structures and feedback loops, the framework provides actionable guidance for designing coordinated interventions during periods of extreme uncertainty. Although empirically motivated by the specific crisis context of 9/11, the model’s systems-based logic enhances its transferability across industries and disruption types. In doing so, it equips consultants to move beyond localized fixes toward integrative interventions that convert crisis response into sustained, organization-wide capability development, directly aligning with the Consulting Division’s focus on systems and cross-boundary change.

Strategic Implementation in Nested Cycles: A Methodology for the Synergistic Use of BSC and OKR

MC
Henrique Michel (Dom Cabral Foundation); Reed Nelson (Dom Cabral Foundation)
This article advances a replicable consulting methodology for strategic implementation based on nested cycles that integrate the Balanced Scorecard (BSC) and Objectives and Key Results (OKRs). Building on classical and emerging strategy approaches, the study develops and tests, through action research, an intervention architecture designed to be deployed by consultants or internal strategy advisors across heterogeneous organizational contexts. The methodology balances stability and flexibility by combining long-term strategic coherence with short-cycle adaptation, directly addressing the paradoxes inherent in contemporary strategizing. Empirical application in a real-world setting reveals gains in engagement, strategic focus, and organizational ambidexterity. The article contributes to the Management Consulting literature by theorizing a portable, reusable implementation scaffold that enables consultants to support organizational resilience and adaptation without sacrificing long-term strategic intent.

Bridging Science and People Analytics: Practitioner Frameworks for Assessment Development

MC
Nicholas Rosemarino (Teachers College of Columbia University); Catarina Dolsten; Eli Plaut
In response to the persistent concerns about the research-practice gap, this paper presents a comprehensive practitioner framework for translating the theoretically rich literature of management science into effective, accessible people analytics assessments designed for non-academic audiences. The paper is structured around two complementary frameworks that address the challenge of making management and organizational psychology constructs measurable and meaningful to business leaders. First, we present a four-phasic approach to guide the assembly of a people analytics assessment: (1) Aligning on your purpose, (2) Leveraging the academic research (3) Translation of academic research into client-friendly language, and (4) Item writing and ongoing model refinement. Second, we outline a five-step process for converting people analytics data into effective organizational interventions: (1) SECURE—stakeholder alignment and executive buy-in, (2) COLLECT—high-quality data through strategic survey deployment, (3) DISCOVER—critical drivers through robust data analysis, (4) PRESENT—clear visualizations and compelling narratives that translate into organizational strategy, and (5) IMPLEMENT—evidence-based solutions that drive business impact. Through detailed case examples and practical illustrations, this paper serves as a comprehensive and accessible guide for scientist-practitioners seeking to create impactful organizational assessments that maintain scientific validity while delivering clear, actionable insights to business stakeholders.
