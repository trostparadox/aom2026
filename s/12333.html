Session overview

Research on human–AI interaction in management has grown rapidly, yet conducting rigorous empirical studies in this domain poses substantial methodological challenges. Scholars must grapple with technological barriers (e.g., deploying real AI systems, ensuring reproducibility), conceptual ambiguities (e.g., defining and operationalizing “AI”), and design trade-offs (e.g., realism versus experimental control). As a result, many studies rely on simplified or hypothetical designs that limit validity, comparability, and cumulative knowledge building. This PDW brings together novel approaches and guidelines that explicitly aim to overcome these challenges by developing new methodological tools, research designs, and best-practice recommendations for studying human–AI interaction. The workshop has three primary objectives. First, it synthesizes emerging evidence on recurring methodological pitfalls and best practices in human–AI interaction research. Second, it introduces novel research infrastructures and design approaches that enable more realistic, scalable, and replicable empirical work. Third, it provides participants with hands-on opportunities to discuss their own projects and receive feedback from scholars actively developing and using these approaches. Ultimately, this PDW demonstrates how careful methodological design can help craft high-quality human–AI interaction research and contribute to more credible and cumulative evidence in management.
