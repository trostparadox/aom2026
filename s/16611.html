How VR shapes Applicant Attraction: A Mixed-Methods Study of Early Recruitment Communication

Authors: Caroline Maria Ahlborn Georg August University Göttingen; Fabian Jintae Froese Georg August University Göttingen

Session overview

Virtual reality (VR) is an emerging technology that enables immersive organizational presentations in recruitment contexts. Despite growing interest in VR-based recruitment, little is known whether and how VR enhances applicant attraction compared with conventional methods, particularly during the initial recruitment process. Drawing on Task-Technology Fit (TTF) theory and mixed-methods research design, we examine how the use of VR in recruitment influences potential applicants’ perceptions and reactions compared with conventional media. Using a between-subjects mixed-methods with pre-and post-surveys, we compare changes in potential applicants’ perceptions in response to a VR-based versus a traditional recruitment stimulus. To deepen understanding of the underlying mechanisms and enrich interpretation of the experimental results, we complemented the experiment with semi-structured interviews. Experimental results indicate that organizational attractiveness increases significantly more when recruitment communication is delivered through VR than through traditional tools. In contrast, perceived familiarity with the organization, indicative of information acquisition, demonstrated an increase in both conditions, with stronger effects for traditional recruitment media. Preliminary findings from our ongoing qualitative study help explain these divergent effects. Overall, the findings of this study advance understanding of whether and when immersive technologies enhance recruitment outcomes beyond conventional tools. The findings contribute to recruitment and technology research by clarifying the role of TTF in early-stage recruitment and provide guidance to organizations seeking to strategically use VR into early-stage recruitment communication.
