From Penalty to Premium: How Remote Work Reshapes Gender Pay Gap

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 4
HR

People

Authors: Wenjun Bian Zhejiang University; Shuai Yuan University of Nottingham Ningbo China; Junting Li Tsinghua University; Mengwei Li Tilburg University

Session overview

The proliferation of remote work has sparked a critical debate regarding its impact on gender-based labor market inequities. While workplace flexibility is often theorized to facilitate female labor force participation, empirical evidence regarding pay outcomes remains equivocal. Drawing upon role congruity theory, this study proposes a parallel moderated mediation framework to reconcile these inconsistencies. We argue that managers interpret remote work through dual, gender-congruent cognitive filters: a penalty mechanism driven by assumed family-work conflict (FWC) and a premium mechanism driven by assumed proactive networking behaviors. We plan to test our framework and hypotheses using three complementary methods: analyzing language patterns in large-scale text data, examining panel data on wages, and experiments. The proposed research contributes to the literature on remote work and gender inequality by uncovering the competitive attributional processes that shape gender pay gap in the virtual era.
