The Double-Edged Effect of Customer Evaluation Pressure on Gig Workers’ Customer-Oriented Helping

HR
Yushen Shi (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); Yongjia Duan (Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
Digital platforms have employed customer evaluation as a central governance mechanism for organizing and managing gig workers, yet the pressure created by the dynamic customer evaluations on gig workers’ work experiences remains insufficiently understood. Drawing on the transactional theory of stress, we conceptualize customer evaluation pressure as a distinct work stressor and develop a stressor–appraisal framework specifying dual pathways. We propose that customer evaluation pressure elicits both challenge and hindrance appraisals, which in turn have opposing effects on gig workers’ customer-oriented helping behavior and subsequent negative customer evaluations. We tested these ideas using a mixed-method design: a multi-wave field survey of gig workers (Study 1) and a semi-structured interview (Study 2). Results from Study 1 showed that customer evaluation pressure can generate both positive and negative effects on customer-oriented helping behavior and subsequent negative customer evaluations, operating through challenge and hindrance appraisals, respectively. Study 2 further provided qualitative evidence corroborating the proposed mechanisms and clarifying contextual factors that shape appraisal processes. Our research contributes to the gig work literature and provides practical insights to manage platform-based service workers in the digital era.
