Patients’ Experience of Outpatient Front-Desk Micro-Interactions From a Medical Center in Taiwan

Authors: Nain-Feng CHU; Kuo-Chen Tseng; Bor-Hwang Kang; wen-chen liang National Sun Yat-Sen University; Ming-jiu Chiou

Session overview

Outpatient front desks are key contact points where micro-interactions – greeting, tone of voice, body language, responsiveness and clarity of explanations – shape how safe and respected patients feel. These behaviours are seldom monitored in routine quality reports, yet they strongly influence satisfaction and the patient–hospital relationship. The purpose of this study was to use a patient survey in a medical centre in southern Taiwan to assess front-desk micro-interactions across service units and identify counters needing focused improvement. From June to September 2025, adult outpatients at a public medical centre in southern Taiwan were invited, immediately after their visit, to complete a 25-item questionnaire on front-desk micro-interactions. Five domains were assessed on a 5-point Likert scale: basic etiquette, service response, non-verbal behaviour, process clarity and overall satisfaction/loyalty. Internal consistency was examined using Cronbach’s a. Mean scores and differences across service units (registration, cashier, pharmacy and nursing counters) were analysed at unit level using one-way ANOVA and post-hoc comparisons. A total of 301 patients participated. All domains showed good internal consistency (Cronbach’s a =0.83). Overall ratings were positive, with mean scores around 4.2/5. However, scores differed significantly across units (all p<0.001). Nursing counters received the highest ratings, while registration and pharmacy counters were consistently lower, especially for non-verbal behaviour and process clarity. For example, nursing counters scored above 4.4/5 on non-verbal behaviour, compared with below 4.0/5 at registration. These patterns highlight specific locations and behaviours where patients experienced more uncertainty and weaker interpersonal connection. The survey proved feasible in busy outpatient settings and added actionable information beyond waiting-time indicators. Making front-desk micro-interactions visible from the patient perspective helped pinpoint counters requiring clearer explanations, friendlier non-verbal behaviour and more responsive service. The findings provide a concrete basis for targeted communication training and workflow adjustments to strengthen patient satisfaction and everyday patient–hospital relationships.
