Unclaimed Entrepreneurs: Within-Work Role Negotiation in Independent Artists

ENT
Akane Vallery Uchida-Venkatraman (Bayes Business School)
What does it mean to “be” an entrepreneur when one denies the label yet does the work? And what happens when entrepreneurial activity is embedded within a broader identity that downplays or even rejects its economically driven logic? These questions sit at the intersection of two major entrepreneurial identity research frameworks: within-work role identity research and social identity theory. They are also particularly salient in the context of creative entrepreneurship, where occupational identity (such as being an artist) is inseparable from deeply held group affiliations and the values and beliefs associated with it. While entrepreneurship, particularly in the early stages, is commonly associated with the ability to assume multiple roles (e.g., innovator, manager, salesperson), there is growing recognition that these roles do not exist in a vacuum but are interpreted and performed in relation to individuals’ broader social identities (Ashforth et al., 2000; Fauchart & Gruber, 2011; Powell & Baker, 2014). Extant research has illuminated how entrepreneurs switch between various work-related roles, and how these “hats” guide behaviour, decision-making and identity work (Leavitt et al., 2012; Mathias & Williams, 2017b). At the same time, social identity theory has offered critical insight into how group belonging influences entrepreneurial motivation and action (Fauchart & Gruber, 2011; Pan et al., 2019). Yet while both streams of research have deepened our understanding of the entrepreneur as a multifaceted actor, they have largely proceeded in parallel. Calls to explore their intersection remain underexamined, particularly in empirical work (Hogg et al., 1995; Powell & Baker, 2017; Pan et al., 2019). This paper addresses that gap by exploring how artists engage with the various roles they inhabit in the course of venture-building and how their overarching social identity shapes this process.
