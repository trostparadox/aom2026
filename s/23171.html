Generative AI Performance and Spillover of Algorithm Appreciation

CTO
Benjamin Richardson (University of Georgia); Aaron Schecter (University of Georgia); Panda Kumar Sachin
Generative AI has become a highly visible and ubiquitous form of algorithmic collaboration, raising new questions about how algorithm performance shapes algorithm use more broadly. We investigate algorithm appreciation in the context of generative AI, focusing on spillover effects—whether observing performance in a generative algorithm fosters appreciation toward other, non-generative algorithms. We introduce human–AI collective efficacy as a novel cognitive mechanism through which algorithm performance influences users’ reliance on algorithms and examine divergent thinking as a moderating individual difference that conditions users’ sensitivity to initial algorithm performance. We employ a between-subjects factorial experiment in which participants first complete a task using a generative agent exhibiting varying levels of competence and subsequently perform tasks supported by non-generative algorithms. We find that observing exceptional performance in generative AI increases downstream reliance on other algorithm tools by strengthening human–AI collective efficacy, an effect amplified among individuals high in divergent thinking. Post hoc experiments show that performance dimensions of usefulness and agreeableness of the generative algorithm are key drivers of the underlying mechanism. The findings extend research on algorithm aversion by demonstrating how generative AI can foster spillovers beyond the focal system and offer practical guidance for designing human-centered algorithm ecosystems.

Comparing Human and AI Persuaders: An Empirical Study of Differential Persuasive Effects

CTO
Dequn Teng (University of Cambridge); Xiaoting Yang (University of Reading)
This study examines the persuasive effectiveness of AI and human persuaders using a mixed-methods design. Drawing on persuasion, psychology, human– computer interaction, and information systems, a conceptual model was developed. It hypothesised that persuader type influences outcomes, with credibility and attractiveness as mediators, and prior attitudes toward AI and contextual consequentiality as moderators. A 2×2 online experiment with 508 participants was analysed using SmartPLS, followed by 22 semi-structured interviews to probe underlying mechanisms. Results indicated that human persuaders were perceived as more credible and persuasive than AI, though no difference was found in attractiveness. Credibility mediated the relationship between persuader type and persuasion effectiveness, while attractiveness did not. Prior attitudes toward AI moderated the link between persuader type and credibility, but not attractiveness or effectiveness. Contextual consequentiality showed no moderating effect. Interviews provided deeper insight. Participants noted that AI was judged differently from humans: its lack of emotional warmth hindered interpersonal bonding but could enhance neutrality and trustworthiness. Some participants trusted AI more in sensitive or high-stakes contexts, perceiving it as less biased or manipulative. Others criticised its limited empathy, which reduced effectiveness in emotionally charged situations. These nuanced views revealed that AI’s persuasiveness depends not only on technical competence but also on how people interpret its nonhuman qualities. This study advances understanding of AI as a persuasive agent, highlighting its limitations and potential strengths. It underscores the mediating role of credibility and the moderating influence of individual attitudes, and points to the psychological and contextual dynamics shaping persuasion in human–AI interaction.

From Description to Prescription: A Design Theory of Agentic AI for Skilled Immigrant Integration

CTO
Sijie Ren (Université Laval)
Despite their growing importance to organizational innovation, highly skilled immigrants (HSIs) often experience uneven post-hire workplace integration. Existing research has generated rich explanatory accounts of integration determinants, yet offers limited guidance on how such insights can be translated into actionable system designs. Addressing this gap, we develop a Design Theory that reframes immigrant workplace integration as a design challenge and theorizes AI as a mediator of promoter–adopter integration dynamics. Grounded in Self-Determination Theory, Self-Efficacy Theory, organizational socialization scholarship, and Dynamic Capabilities Theory, and operationalized through Affordance–Actualization theory, we articulate a multilevel logic linking individual and organizational integration needs to low-level technological affordances. Through design science research, we derive a coherent set of high-level affordances, meta-requirements, and meta-design principles, and specify testable design-product hypotheses that bridge descriptive mechanisms and prescriptive system features. An expository instantiation in IntegrateHub, a governed multi-agent AI prototype, illustrates how these design principles can be enacted in socio-technical integration routines. The study advances management and information systems theory by transforming integration research from an explanatory domain into a design-oriented one, fostering dialogue between soft social theories and hard computational design, and generating transferable knowledge-for-design for inclusive AI-enabled workplace integration systems.

When Advice Disagrees: The Role of Informational Diversity in Crowdsourced Consultations

CTO
Lan Wang (Peking University); Jie Zhang; Sarah Zheng (University of Victoria)
Crowdsourced advice platforms expand access to expertise but frequently confront users with contradictory recommendations offered in the absence of relational cues that typically help interpret conflict. Existing research on informational diversity has focused primarily on who provides advice within rich, interactive contexts, yielding inconsistent conclusions about its consequences. We advance this literature by shifting attention to contradictory informational diversity (CID), a content-based form of diversity capturing the extent to which recommendations within a consultation contradict one another. Using a bathtub research design, we integrate large-scale behavioral evidence from 1,016,340 consultations on a healthcare platform in China with experimental data probing underlying psychological processes (n = 1,637). Across methods, results reveal an inverted U-shaped relationship between CID and both advice taking and advice giving. For advisees, heightened information load accounts for declining advice uptake at high levels of CID. For advisors, supplementary analyses indicate an other-oriented restraint mechanism: contributors reduce advice provision not only due to their own cognitive burden but also out of concern that additional input may overwhelm advisees. By centering disagreement in advice content rather than advisor characteristics, this study offers a content-based perspective on informational diversity and clarifies when conflicting advice facilitates versus impedes knowledge exchange in digital crowdsourcing environments.

Too Clear to Care? Why AI-Assisted Counseling Answers Fail to Gain Recognition

CTO
Hangtao Li (Peking University); Han Li (University of Nottingham Ningbo China); Zhixue Zhang (Peking University)
Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly used in emotionally sensitive domains such as mental health, yet little is known about how AI assistance affects the performance of professional psychological counselors. Drawing on Social Presence Theory, we propose that despite AI’s strength in improving information clarity and structure, its influence on counselors’ language may inadvertently diminish the social presence that clients expect in therapeutic communication. We test this prediction using data from a large online counseling platform in China, analyzing 18,872 counselor responses posted between December 2022 and December 2023. Counselors who used AI assistance were significantly less likely to produce high-quality answers, as determined by both the platform and community. Text analysis shows that this effect operates through greater linguistic segmentation: AI-assisted responses use more bullet-points but less conversational, potentially weakening perceived social presence and lowering evaluations of counseling quality. Exploratory heterogeneity analyses further show that these adverse effects are concentrated among high-experience counselors. These findings challenge predominantly positive assumptions about AI augmentation by identifying linguistic mechanisms through which AI can undermine human performance in socially demanding contexts.
