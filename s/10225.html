Session overview

Global Talent Management (GTM) is well-recognized to be an established priority of organizations today, with executive oversight of talent management as part of the business’ management of its human capital management as a strategic resource of the firm. Having originally emerged as a stream of the IHRM literature and as a people-management activity within the HR function, GTM defined the importance of managing organizational human capital through the differentiated management of talented individuals with skills and knowledge aligned to business strategy and their development and deployment for high performance in identified business-critical positions. However, since its original foundations as one of a strategic bundle of well-designed and effectively delivered SHRM practices, GTM has evolved beyond the boundaries of IHRM now demonstrating its relevance across multiple levels and multiple disciplines. In today’s fractured world of complex inter-dependencies of economic, political, technical, scientific, social, and competitive pressures, the importance of effective GTM is difficult to overstate and the risks and opportunities for strategic value are difficult to over-estimate. GTM emerged in organizations and in the literature in the late 1990’s and early 2000’s, founded in large global multi-national organizations where effective HRM served as a stable foundation enabling further refinements in management of the organization’s workforce. In that context, of stable, progressive, globalization, GTM emerged as a further more refined lens on how organizations and their workforce can exchange value over time, to achieve competitive advantage, beyond the foundation of very good HRM. As GTM evolved, multi-disciplinary relevance began to emerge which positions GTM as a topic evolved beyond its IHRM roots. In contrast to the earlier context of steady, advancement of globalization, today’s businesses and indeed entire industries and nations, are faced with unprecedented degrees of uncertainty and complexity which require a more nuanced consideration of the role of GTM in organizations today. Examples of uncertainty abound including major disruption to trusted international trade; slowed globalization; the pace of technological change; political uncertainty within and between nations; changes in talent systems including talent in-out flows, skills shortages; national economic disruption leading to employment uncertainty, cost of living crises, and workforce dissatisfaction; heightened competition, and increased complexity in social health risk. Presenters in this panel symposium argue that GTM is not the same as IHRM and why that is important for management today. GTM has evolved such that it requires reconsideration as more than its foundational IHRM origins. In the modern context of exacerbating complexity and disruption, GTM has emerged as a strategic imperative of management to influence business stability and competitive advantage through its multi-disciplinary reach and impact.
