Session overview

Research has long recognized that creativity is inherently tension-laden. Earlier work shows that, at its core, creativity requires the simultaneous pursuit of novelty and usefulness, originality and feasibility, exploration and constraint. Importantly, creativity research has progressed by engaging seriously with these tensions. Rather than treating contradictions as anomalies to be averaged out, creativity scholars view them as theoretically meaningful and generative elements of complex phenomena. This tension-centered mindset has become even more critical as organizational contexts become more complicated. Rapid technological change, varied modes of participation in creative activities, and a rising reliance on team-based task structures—conditions that are reflected across the studies featured in this symposium—now shape how creative work unfolds. Extending and deepening tension-based approaches is therefore essential for understanding how people navigate creativity in contemporary organizational life. The five papers in this symposium employ distinct research approaches—ranging from outcome contrasts and rival explanations to competing mechanisms, boundary conditions, and antecedents’ adjudication—yet converge on a shared commitment to treating tensions as central analytic tools. Integrating these approaches, the symposium highlights the value of tension-focused theorizing in creativity research, as it offers more precise and nuanced insights into when, how, and why creativity unfolds in organizational settings. Collectively, the five papers featured this symposium demonstrate how a tension-centered lens provides a generative way for advancing creativity theory that better reflect the complexity of contemporary organizational life.
