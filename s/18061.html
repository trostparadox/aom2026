Session overview

The creator economy consists of “multisided platforms that connect contributors (or creators or developers), viewers (or consumers), and advertisers” (Bhargava, 2022), facilitating the monetization of digital content as a form of work. Such platforms, and those who use them, are generating increasing interest among industry professionals and scholars. In 2023, Goldman Sachs projected that the creator economy would double in size by 2027 to nearly $480 billion globally, and that the number of creators would grow 10-20% annually over the next five years (Sheridan, 2023). An estimated 40 million "economically consequential” content creators participated in the creator economy in 2024, ranging from “nano” creators with up to 10,000 followers, to “mega” creators with over 500,000 followers (Karp, Fu & Friis, 2024). Though online platforms have seen an uptick as the subject of scholarly work over the past decade (see for example Bailey et al., 2019), the creator economy has seen less focus than other types of platform work such as the “gig economy” and online marketplaces for goods. Despite this, the creator economy possesses distinct characteristics that make it an especially fertile setting in which to study important questions in management. Compared to the “gig economy,” the creator economy offers a strikingly high degree of entrepreneurial agency, with wide-ranging choice dimensions available to content creators as they navigate venture initiation, value creation, and self-expression in their work. For example, a video game streamer can choose which game to play, how to play that game, and how to interact with audience members. In contrast, a rideshare driver is much more constrained by factors such as geography and platform rules, and can often enact agency only in limited forms - such as choosing which hours to work. Further, the creator economy exhibits extremely low barriers to entry, and as a result, is characterized by novel dynamics of competition and institutional characteristics, as well as unique algorithmic operations of content creation platforms distinct to creator-audience markets - factors which position it as a context for rich theoretical insights. In this symposium, we empirically investigate ongoing questions such as who succeeds (and why) in the creator economy, what mechanisms drive performance measures including viewership and growth, and how content creators experience and navigate algorithmic mediation and visibility decisions. Through large-scale quantitative analysis, multi-year digital ethnography, and in-depth qualitative interviews, we aim to build on past AOM discussions exploring the emergent creator economy (Song et al., 2024; Apker & Calder, 2025), continuing efforts to empirically and theoretically understand this new context of work, and look forward to a productive discussion with all interested colleagues.
