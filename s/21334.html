Session overview

As societies confront increasing constraints on environmental and social resources, managerial agency has become central to sustainability transitions. This study examines how managers and intermediary organizations, influence the direction, speed, and effectiveness of such transitions. Focusing on the energy sector, we analyze the interaction between managerial actions, intermediary functions, innovation processes, strategic decision-making, and Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) objectives. Drawing on 35 in-depth interviews complemented by archival and industry data, the study identifies key mechanisms through which intermediaries enable transition dynamics. Findings show that managers within intermediary organizations act as strategic connectors, linking actors across organizational, institutional, and technological boundaries. Through purposeful knowledge exchange, coordination, and stakeholder alignment, they reduce uncertainty, foster collaboration, and accelerate the diffusion of sustainable energy solutions. The results offer important implications for management theory and practice, highlighting how deliberate cooperation with intermediaries can strengthen sustainability strategies, inform policy design, and support long-term competitive advantage in transitioning industries.
