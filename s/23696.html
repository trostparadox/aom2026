Reimagining SCCT: Understanding Post-Retirement Work Through a Career Theory Lens

CAR
Aviv Kidron (The Max Stern Yezreel Valley College); Hedva Vinarski Peretz (The Max Stern Yezreel Valley College); Natalie Paz
OECD countries with aging populations face growing concerns about older workers' employability and wellbeing as they work longer and comprise more of the workforce. However, the effect of post-retirement work on the wellbeing of retirees has not been sufficiently examined, despite the increasing proportion of older workers in the labor market. Only a few studies have used a qualitative approach to investigate the motivation for post-retirement work and the experiences of working during this period of life. This study employed a qualitative methodology to address this knowledge gap and expand Social Cognitive Career Theory (SCCT) into the field of retirement. This study aims to answer the question: How do white-collar workers experience the effect of post-retirement employment on their wellbeing, and what factors shape these experiences? Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 24 white-collar workers aged 62-74. Thematic analysis was conducted using a theory-driven approach based on six core elements of SCCT. The findings indicate that post-retirement workers experienced both high work and life satisfaction when organizational environments were supportive, although agism remained a significant barrier; self-efficacy perceptions were influenced by physical and cognitive abilities, and accumulated experience; work served dual purposes, both as a goal and a means, participants valuing meaningful contribution over career advancement; work and life satisfaction were interrelated, and employment was perceived as a choice rather than a constraint. This study makes a valuable theoretical contribution by extending SCCT into the post-retirement period, suggesting novel theoretical propositions for analyzing workers' experiences and wellbeing after retirement. The theoretical expansion answers scholarly calls for extending SCCT beyond traditional career boundaries into post-retirement occupational choices. The findings support policy development for integrating the aging workforce.
