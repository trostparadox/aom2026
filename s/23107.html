Not All Stars Shine the Same: Personality- and Status-Contingent Effectiveness of Employee Ownership

HR
Andrea Kim (Sungkyunkwan University); Kyongji Han (The University of Texas at Tyler)
Given its prevalence among high-performing organizations, this study investigates how and for whom broad-based employee stock ownership (BESO) increases work outcomes. Integrating the psychological ownership perspective with the theory of purposeful work behavior (TPWB), we theorize that formal ownership increases job performance and organizational citizenship behaviors (OCB) through organization-based self-esteem (OBSE), and that this mediational process is contingent upon personality traits and hierarchical level. Using a multi-wave, multi-source dataset of 400 employees in an organization, we found that the number of equity stakes owned by employees is positively related to job performance and OCB through OBSE. Moreover, this positive linkage is stronger among employees high in agreeableness. Finally, such motivational effects of the objective state of ownership are more salient among extraverted or agreeable employees at lower hierarchical levels. This research contributes to the literatures on employee ownership, TPWB, and OBSE by identifying a psychological process through which BESO and multiple individual characteristics interact to motivate employees. From a practical standpoint, our findings offer useful insights into how to motivate lower-level employees with equity-based rewards.

Different Stars, Different Collaboration Patterns? Insights from the NBA

HR
Zijing Yuan (Bocconi University)
Stars are widely recognized as vital contributors to organizational success, yet the definition and impact of stardom remain inconsistently conceptualized in the literature. We distinguish among three dimensions of stardom (i.e., performance, visibility, and status) and focus on "mismatched stars", individuals who excel in one dimension but not the others. Using detailed dyadic collaboration data from the National Basketball Association (NBA), we explore how colleagues differently allocate collaboration efforts toward performance stars, visibility stars, and status stars. Our findings show that visibility stars and status stars receive more collaboration from colleagues compared to performance stars who are typically more productive. We term these phenomena “visibility traps” and “status traps”. Furthermore, these collaboration patterns evolve with increasing familiarity among colleagues, showing an important role that colleague-specific knowledge plays here.

Cooperation or Competition? The Motivational Pathways from Successful Aging to Knowledge Hiding

HR
Wenjun Zhou (Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics); Xiao Jun Zhan; Fubin Jiang; Zhen Wang (Central University of Finance and Economics)
As organisations increasingly expect older workers to leverage their successful aging at work to act as knowledge transmitters, there are mixed findings regarding the relationship between successful aging at work and older workers’ knowledge hiding. To address this issue, we drew on the dominance– prestige theory of social status and developed a model to explore how and why successful aging at work influences older workers’ knowledge hiding toward younger colleagues through countervailing pathways. We tested our hypotheses using a three-wave survey of 364 older employees. The results indicated that successful aging at work reduced older workers’ knowledge hiding toward younger colleagues through status cooperation orientation but increased their knowledge hiding toward younger colleagues through status competition orientation. Furthermore, older workers in organisations with high age-inclusive human resource practices were more likely to translate successful aging at work into a stronger status cooperation orientation and a weaker status competition orientation. We discuss the theoretical and practical implications of our findings and propose avenues for future research.

How Does PFIP Affect Employees’ Intrinsic Motivation?

HR
Zejun (Jenny) Zhao (Florida State University); Saehee Sam Kang (Florida State University)
Drawing on cognitive evaluation theory and contemporary compensation research, this study examines whether pay-for-individual-performance (PFIP) undermines or enhances employees’ intrinsic motivation and, in turn, their task and contextual performance. Across two field studies, PFIP did not exhibit a crowding-out effect on intrinsic motivation; instead, its association with intrinsic motivation was marginally positive in Study 1 and significantly positive in Study 2. Job autonomy strengthened this relationship, such that PFIP was more positively related to intrinsic motivation under conditions of high autonomy. Moreover, intrinsic motivation was more strongly associated with contextual performance than with task performance. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.

Strategic Citizenship: How Star Performers Allocate Discretionary Effort Across a Project Lifecycle

HR
Jonathan Hendricks (University of Nebraska-Lincoln); Jia Yu (University of Nebraska-Lincoln); Nadia Shafi (University of Nebraska-Lincoln)
The literature on star performers to date has focused primarily on their task-related behaviors, with the implicit assumption that their behaviors do not change over the course of a project. However, work to date has largely omitted a discussion about the extent to which stars engage in OCBs. We integrate theory from temporal motivation theory (TMT) and team development into literature on stars in order to consider stars as strategic decision makers regarding their decision to engage in OCBs across a project’s lifespan. We propose that stars are more willing to engage in OCBs at the beginning of a project’s lifecycle, but that they gradually reduce their engagement of OCBs during the rest of the project. We further propose a critical boundary condition of this relationship is a star’s internal social prominence. We test these proposed hypotheses using a sample of 9,685 weekly observations of NBA players across the 2018-2019 season.
