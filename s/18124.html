Navigating Political Animosity in Host Countries: Managing Trust, Control, and FDI Over Time

Authors: Yueling Sima The University of Texas at Dallas; Uriel Stettner Tel Aviv University; Melike Nur Findikoglu Ozyegin University

Session overview

Geopolitical volatility increasingly dictates international investment strategies, yet research often treats national animosity as a static condition affecting only market entry. This project investigates the overlooked dynamic of how ongoing Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) governance evolves as bilateral political relations fluctuate. Specifically, it explores how political animosity decouples an investor's internal trust in local partners from external trust in host-country institutions, prompting adjustments in ownership and control. Focusing on private firms in Türkiye with Israeli equity holders (2005–2025), the study analyzes how shifts in Israeli–Turkish relations impact equity adjustments and firm survival. Using Cox proportional hazards models and CAMEO-based measures of bilateral relations, we examine 750 firm-level events across 229 Israeli–Turkish firms and a matched control group. Preliminary findings indicate that major political incidents significantly increase liquidation hazards for joint ventures, an effect not observed in Turkish-only firms. By distinguishing between partner-specific and institutional trust, this research advances a dynamic framework for FDI governance, offering actionable insights for policymakers and managers navigating cross-border collaborations amidst rising geopolitical tensions.
