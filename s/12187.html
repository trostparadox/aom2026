Session overview

Research on marginalization in established organizations has enjoyed increased attention in scholarly outlets and academic communities. However, explicit discussion of how marginalization transforms when individuals transition to entrepreneurship remains notably scarce. This is particularly surprising given the popular narrative of entrepreneurship as a pathway for overcoming labor market constraints. Nevertheless, a foundation for examining marginalization in entrepreneurship has already been laid. It is grounded in extensive scholarly discussion on topics such as necessity entrepreneurship, underdog entrepreneurship, institutional entrepreneurship, and the impactful work on intersectionality and social inequality in entrepreneurial contexts. This Professional Development Workshop (PDW) aims to integrate these diverse perspectives to foster dialogue and formulate a comprehensive research agenda on marginalization and entrepreneurship. Central to this PDW is the recognition that marginalization operates differently across institutional contexts. While organizational employment constitutes a constrained evaluative regime—where authority concentrates in hierarchical gatekeepers and merit is assessed through standardized proxies—entrepreneurship can constitute an open evaluative regime where authority disperses across independent evaluators and success definitions pluralize. This structural difference creates both new opportunities and new constraints for marginalized individuals. To this end, the PDW will begin with a panel of distinguished experts from across disciplines, sharing insights on the foundational elements and questions pertaining to how marginalization is reconfigured through entrepreneurship. Following the panel, participants at early-career stages will receive constructive feedback on their pre-submitted abstracts. A second panel discussion will feature emerging scholars sharing their perspectives on future research at this intersection, informed by their current research as well as themes emanating from the roundtable discussions. Ultimately, the PDW aspires to unite and support scholars already conducting research at this intersection and provide resources for those interested in joining the emerging conversation on how entrepreneurship transforms —rather than simply reproduces or overcomes—the mechanisms through which marginalization operates.
