Protean Career Orientation and Innovative Work Behavior: A Configurational Perspective

OB
Aditya Iyengar; Roopak Kumar Gupta (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode)
Protean careers, which refer to employees self-directing their careers based on their personal values, are drawing increasing attention from researchers as well as practitioners. However, prior research has not provided clear answers to some important questions regarding the Protean Career Orientation (PCO). First, is PCO associated with a specific set of values or a wide range of values? Second, while PCO emphasizes individual agency, how do key contextual factors affect its outcomes? To address these questions, this study examines the relationship between PCO and an outcome for which personal values and contextual factors are particularly important – Innovative Work Behavior (IWB). Personal values are incorporated based on the Schartz values framework, and team autonomy support and role innovation requirements are included as contextual factors. Survey-based data (single- and multi-source) is gathered at an Indian manufacturing organization. This data is analyzed using correlational (Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling) and configurational (fuzzy set Qualitative Comparative Analysis) techniques. Findings show that high-PCO individuals could possess a variety of value profiles. For PCO to facilitate innovation, it has to be combined with the appropriate combinations of values and contextual factors. Thus, the findings provide nuanced perspectives regarding PCO and the antecedents of IWB.
