Entrepreneurial Drivers of Forestland Parcelization and Fragmentation: A Strategic Management View

ENT
Yuxi (Lucy) Zhao (Concordia University)
Forestland parcelization and fragmentation pose significant socio-economic and environmental challenges, yet management scholarship has largely neglected the entrepreneurial forces driving these phenomena. While environmental science and land policy literature offer comprehensive frameworks for understanding land-use transitions, they seldom engage with essential management concepts such as firm strategy, entrepreneurial behavior, and value creation. This paper addresses this gap by integrating environmental insights into management theory, focusing on the entrepreneurial processes that shape forestland outcomes during amenity transitions. Drawing on land rent theory and strategic entrepreneurship frameworks, parcelization and fragmentation are reconceptualized as dynamic results of capital expansion and spatial circulation. Entrepreneurial land development strategies in forested regions are examined to extend literature on sustainability, firm strategy, and land-use governance, illustrating that forestland transformation is not an incidental byproduct of changing landowner preferences but a deliberate strategy for generating economic rents. Comparative case analysis highlights the ways entrepreneurial agency and market forces commodify landscapes and influence land-use patterns. This paper advances a theoretical framework that situates forestland parcelization and fragmentation within broader entrepreneurial capital flows, providing insights for policymakers and practitioners striving to balance economic development with environmental sustainability. By contributing to management research on sustainable entrepreneurship and the socio-spatial dynamics of land commodification, this paper underscores the critical role of entrepreneurial decision-making in shaping rural and forested landscapes.
