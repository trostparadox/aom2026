Emotional Intelligence in Relation to Employee Engagement and Organization Commitment in Hospitality

Authors: Pavit Raina Indian School of Business; Raminder Sira Punjabi University

Session overview

The hospitality sector faces numerous challenges regarding trends and customer expectations, this sector is growing at an exponential rate and hospitality organizations encounter several issues. These issues comprise personalization of the guest experience, high burnout, long working hours, implementation of technology, brand and reputation management, environment-friendly practices, and attracting and retaining a talented workforce. Another pressing issue is related to the high turnover intention among employees with employees quitting jobs after a span of six months and employees continuing to work for an average of two years. This brings into the picture the need to have emotionally intelligent employees. Thus, the management in this sector must focus on hiring emotionally intelligent, engaged, committed, and adroit employees since such employees help in developing and maintaining congenial relationships with all stakeholders in the context of this sector. Highly engaged and committed employees tend to deliver tremendous job and service performance, have a lower turnover intention, and create a conducive work environment. Thus, the impetus lies in hiring candidates who are emotionally intelligent and exhibit high levels of engagement and commitment toward their roles, responsibilities, and the organization. The objective of this study is to examine the role of dimensions of emotional intelligence on organization commitment via the mediation of employee engagement. A questionnaire using standardized scales was drafted and circulated among the hotels in Chandigarh, Punjab, Himachal, and Panchkula. Out of 400 questionnaires distributed, (n=360) qualified for further analysis. The data was analyzed using PROCESS Macro. The results show that all the dimensions of emotional intelligence are positively related to Organization Commitment and indicated dominant variables. The test of mediation indicated that employee engagement partially mediated this association. This is the first study in EI, engagement, and commitment literature that draws on the social exchange theory (SET) framework, where dimensions of emotional intelligence and organization commitment via the mediation of employee engagement are tested in hospitality settings. This study provides empirical evidence to support the relationship between the dimensions of emotional intelligence and organization commitment via the mediation of employee engagement.
