Session overview

Geography is widely used in management research to explain variation in innovation, entrepreneurship, and firm behavior, yet it is often treated as a general-purpose explanatory construct. This symposium advances a more precise view by distinguishing how geography operates across different domains of economic activity. Drawing on four empirical studies, the session examines geography’s role in shaping access to technical knowledge, the spatial allocation of talent, and firm location and mobility decisions. Together, the papers show that geographic proximity works through distinct mechanisms rather than a single logic, clarifying when geographic explanations are most informative and when their effects are limited. The symposium contributes to strategy, and technology and innovation management research by sharpening how geographic context should be theorized and used.
