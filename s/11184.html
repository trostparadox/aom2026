Undereducated Leaders' Curiosity Displays Form Positive Impressions: The Role of Leader Gender

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Jonathan Evans The University of British Columbia; Jiuk Yu The University of British Columbia

Session overview

Despite the positive connotation of leader curiosity displays, certain groups such as undereducated leaders may avoid displaying curiosity due to risks of signaling a lack of knowledge. Drawing on expectancy violation theory, we posit that this potential avoidance represents a missed opportunity since undereducated leaders’ curiosity displays can form favorable impressions through positive expectancy violation. We further propose a three-way interaction between curiosity displays, education level, and leader gender, such that the positive indirect effect on leader effectiveness via leader competence is strongest for undereducated male leaders. Results of an online experiment with 192 employees supported the hypothesized model. This study extends the understanding of how leaders’ demographic stereotypes affect social impressions formed by leader curiosity displays.
