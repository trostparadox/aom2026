Moving Beyond Individual Solvers in Innovation Challenges

TIM
Ademir-Paolo Vrolijk (Privy Council Office (Government of Canada))
The literature identifies domain expertise, knowledge distance, and gender diversity as key predictors of success in simple innovation challenges featuring individual participants. Little is known, however, about what drives success in complex ones when firms participate. To address this question, I analysed firm-level data on 684 Canadian firms participating in Impact Canada challenges between 2018 and 2021, matched with an additional 3,455 non-participating firms to address potential selection bias. Here, the established predictors only meagrely explained success. Instead, R&D expenditures emerged as a consistent predictor of winning. These results shed new light on how complex innovation challenges operate, such as identifying participants with strong innovation commitments. These results also inform how organizations should design and deploy this open innovation approach to achieve their strategic objectives.
