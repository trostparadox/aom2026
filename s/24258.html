Unblackboxing AI Business Value: Application-Level AI Capability Building

CTO
Adrian Schadl (University of Siegen); Mahei Manhai Li (University of St. Gallen); Andreas Janson (University of St. Gallen); David Sonnabend (University of Kassel); Björn Schäfer
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a strategic imperative, yet firms struggle to translate AI-related investments into business value. This creates a puzzle: AI adoption is increasingly widespread, but market-perceived value remains selective and difficult to explain. Current research addresses this tension through two connected perspectives. Work on AI strategy explains how firms build an AI capability by organizing for AI and establishing enabling conditions for adoption. Work on AI business value traces market responses to AI initiatives. However, these perspectives still fall short of explaining selective value attribution, as they mostly treat AI as an aggregated label. We argue that the relevant variation lies at the application-level. AI unfolds through heterogeneous, outcome-focused applications that produce distinct organizational outputs. We examine this problem in digital platform organizations. Using a sequential mixed methods design, Study 1 synthesizes prior literature with 43 expert interviews to map 15 outcome-focused AI applications across four clusters spanning internal and external value creation. Study 2 links these applications to market-perceived business value using a calendar-time portfolio approach. Results show a heterogeneous valuation profile: a small subset of application categories is robustly associated with annual abnormal returns of 8.52% to 10.44%, while additional categories become value-relevant only when similar disclosures are concurrently visible across multiple firms. Overall, we shift the AI capability discussion from aggregated adoption to an application-level view of capability augmentation, explaining why valuation focuses on applications that embed AI into services and platform and ecosystem mechanisms rather than on model-centric product disclosures alone.
