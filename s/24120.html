Rethinking Craft from the Global South: Artisanal Work and Livelihoods in Kenya’s Jua Kali Sector

OMT
Viktoriia Pisotska
This research seeks to explore the cultural, social, and economic dimensions of artisanal work in Kenya’s Jua Kali sector—an expansive informal economy where millions of artisans sustain their livelihoods through craft-based production. While recent scholarship on (neo)craft has positioned craft as a humanistic and relational approach to less alienated, post-industrial form of work, a way towards ecologically sustainable economy and degrowth, studies of craft in the Global South remain scarce. The Jua Kali sector, which employs over 85% of Kenyan workforce, provides a compelling lens through which to rethink craft beyond Western notions of luxury, creativity and hipster culture, viewing it instead as a lived practice deeply embedded in community, heritage, and tradition. Drawing on African philosophies such as ubuntu, this paper argues that craft in Jua Kali is still a form of economic survival but also a moral and relational enterprise, embodying values of communality, reciprocity, and spirituality. The ongoing study reveals key gaps in understanding how artisans perceive their craft, shape their identities, and navigate the cultural and practical challenges of sustaining their livelihoods. It calls for a paradigm shift in management and organization studies to embrace non-Western epistemologies, recognizing that in contexts such as Jua Kali, craft is life—a vital expression of identity, creativity, and community belonging.
