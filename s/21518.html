Beyond the Theory Crisis: A Multilevel Framework for Cumulative Knowledge

Author: Jasmin Banks University of North Carolina at Charlotte

Session overview

Management research faces a theory crisis characterized by fragmented, disconnected theories that fail to accumulate into coherent programmatic knowledge. This conceptual paper proposes the multilevel research paradigm as a meta-paradigm for addressing theoretical fragmentation. Drawing on the meso tradition and contemporary multilevel frameworks, I argue that adopting multilevel principles can enhance conceptual clarity by specifying construct levels, strengthen empirical rigor through cross-level causal modeling, and enable cumulative theory building by integrating micro and macro domains. The paper develops a conceptual framework illustrating three mechanisms through which multilevel research mitigates the theory crisis: level specification improves boundary clarity, cross-level testing enhances empirical rigor, and integration across levels facilitates cumulative theory. By positioning the multilevel paradigm as a structural solution rather than merely a methodological tool, this framework provides a theoretical blueprint for rebuilding coherence in management theory and fostering more integrative, rigorous, and practically relevant scholarship.
