Proactive Decision-Making, Entrepreneurial Self-Efficacy, and Entrepreneurial Intentions

CAR
Florian Blösl (MCI - The Entrepreneurial School); Reinhard Kunz (Bauhaus-Universität Weimar); Johannes Ulrich Siebert (MCI - The Entrepreneurial School)
Proactive decision-making – a systematic decision-making concept integrating proactive cognitive skills with proactive personality traits – is proposed as relevant for entrepreneurial career development. Drawing on an adapted version of the career self-management model of Social Cognitive Career Theory, this study examines how proactive decision-making relates to entrepreneurial self-efficacy and intentions. Using survey data from a diverse UK sample (n = 353), structural equation modeling shows that proactive decision-making is positively associated with all five task-specific dimensions of entrepreneurial self-efficacy. However, only the planning dimension of entrepreneurial self-efficacy predicts entrepreneurial intentions. Beyond an indirect effect, proactive decision-making shows a significant direct association with entrepreneurial intentions, indicating that systematic decision-making contributes to the development of entrepreneurial intentions over and above task-specific entrepreneurial self-efficacy. Overall, the structural model explains 35.3% of the variance in entrepreneurial intentions. These findings extend the Social Cognitive Career Theory by identifying proactive decision-making as a decision-oriented concept that supports the development of domain-specific self-efficacy and intentions in self-directed career contexts.
