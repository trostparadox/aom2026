Charisma for better or worse?

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 1
ONE

People

Authors: Tiffany Kreutschy University of Lausanne; Matthieu Légeret University of Zurich; Christian Zehnder University of Lausanne; John Antonakis

Session overview

Charismatic leadership is typically associated with positive social and organizational outcomes, yet its potential to promote harmful objectives remains understudied. Across three studies, we expose participants to a charismatic or non-charismatic message and examine its effect on incentivized decisions in one-shot individual settings, using samples from Switzerland (Study 1) and the United States (Studies 2 and 3). We find no effect of charismatic messaging on climate skepticism or donations to a climate-change charity (Studies 1-2). These findings identify an important boundary condition of charismatic influence: in one-shot individual settings, charisma alone is insufficient to persuade individuals to adopt counter-normative or socially harmful beliefs. We discuss how charismatic influence may instead rely on social context and collective reinforcement, pointing to group-based exposure as a critical direction for future research.
