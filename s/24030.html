Product Longevity as an Institutional Pressure in the Circular Economy

ONE
Daniel Stabler; Hannes Cordes (University of Hamburg); Henri Hakala; Anne-Laure Mention (RMIT University); Ahmad Alaassar (RMIT University)
Corporate engagement in the Circular Economy (CE) has historically focused on the end-of-life. More recently tighter value-retention strategies, centered on product longevity, and extended use, have emerged as increasingly important expectations. This paper examines how longevity has developed into distinct institutional pressures, and how manufacturers strategically legitimate this pressure through sustainability reporting. Drawing on institutional theory the study adopts a longitudinal and comparative design, analyzing sustainability reports from six major European consumer electronics firms 2015-2024. The paper combines computational keyword-based text mining with a reflexive thematic analysis, enabling both the systematic mapping of discursive change and the in-depth interpretation of strategic narratives. The findings reveal that while all firms increasingly acknowledge circularity, longevity is engaged selectively. The analyzed firms diverge in how they temporalize circularity, as an end-goal, an ongoing process of optimization, or a timeless design philosophy, and in where responsibility is located. These differences are shaped by distinct configurations of institutional pressures, illustrating how institutional confluence determines where and how longevity is enacted. Across the cases, longevity is most often legitimated through organizationally safe pathways that preserve firm control, while user and independent firm agency remain marginal. The paper contributes to CE and institutional theory by foregrounding product longevity as a distinct institutional pressure, advancing the concept of institutional confluence, and conceptualizing sustainability reporting as a key site where circular legitimacy is constructed.
