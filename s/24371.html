Market Reactions to Sexual Minority Appointments in Corporate Leadership

DEI
Wouter Torsin; Timothee Waxin
This paper investigates the equity market reaction to sexual minority leadership appointments. Building on signaling theory, we hypothesize that lesbian, gay, and bisexual (LGB) leadership appointments are perceived positively by investors. Our empirical results demonstrate higher positive abnormal returns around top management team appointment announcements relative to their non-LGB counterpart, with no statistical difference for LGB directors. This reaction occurs only when the executives' sexual orientation is observable to the financial market through biographical information and is stronger when this information stems from commonly accessible resources such as media outlets rather than through advocacy activities. We further demonstrate that the LGB-equity market premium exists only for male-identifying executives. We formally investigate the channels behind this market premium and document that LGB executives are exceptionally skilled and that market reactions are particularly pronounced when employee and consumer attraction gains are higher.
