Difference Not Deficit: Crafting Creativity Differently

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 8:30AM–9:30AM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: APARNA DWIVEDI Indian Institute of Management Bangalore; Piyush Gopal Indian Institute of Management Indore; Neha Garg

Session overview

Research on neurodivergent entrepreneurship has advanced three dominant perspectives: trait-based accounts linking atypical cognition to entrepreneurial entry and outcomes, strategic views highlighting innovation under uncertainty, and lived-experience work documenting masking, stigma, and emotional strain. While these streams explain who becomes an entrepreneur and which traits correlate with performance, they offer limited insight into how neurodivergent founders organize creative work and regulate identity in the course of producing entrepreneurial value. This gap obscures the micro-processes through which cognitive difference becomes a source of sustained advantage or overload and withdrawal. We address this gap by examining how neurodivergent entrepreneurs structure creative work, navigate identity tensions around masking and authenticity, and translate cognitive difference into entrepreneurial outcomes. We adopt an inductive, grounded theory approach based on 38 in-depth interviews with entrepreneurs who identify as neurodivergent (e.g., ADHD, autism, dyslexia). Our findings reveal that identity threats rooted in tensions between authenticity and acceptance are mediated through creative work, giving rise to three distinct pathways. Entrepreneurs engage in inward, translational, or systemic forms of creativity, often alongside selective masking, producing autonomy-driven, inclusion-driven, or reform-driven outcomes. We theorize creativity as a form of mediated identity work, advancing entrepreneurship and creativity research by showing how cognitive difference is actively organized, regulated, and sustained through work practices and identity management.
