Craft-Inspired and Artisan-Made: Staging and Contesting Authenticity

CMS
N G Yamini (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode); Rajeshwari C (Indian Institute of Management Kozhikode)
Prior research positions authenticity as central to valuing handicraft products and sustaining communities, often equating it with slow, handmade production. While acknowledging these claims, this paper critically examines the importance placed on authenticity for the survival of small handicraft enterprises. We argue that slow-making, as a marker of authenticity, brings in economic precarity and struggles for the artisans. Although craft is frequently framed as an alternative to capitalist production, we show how the staging of authenticity generates markets for handicraft work while simultaneously (in)visibilising pain, vulnerability, and unsustainable labour conditions. Drawing on ethnographic research in the Mysuru wood-inlay handicraft sector in India, we examine how authenticity is socially constructed, claimed, and contested, and how staging mediates tensions between handcrafting and market logics. We contribute a process model that explains how imposed authenticity reproduces power relations, limits artisan’s agency, and undermines social sustainability.
