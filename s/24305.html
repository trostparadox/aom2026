What Affects Gig Worker’s Well-Being: Roles of Algorithmic Management and Protean Career Orientation

OB
Linna Zhu (Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications); Yong Gao (Nanjing University of Finance and Economics); Yi Li (Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications)
Targeting at the double-edged sword effect of algorithmic management, this study integrates social information processing theory and social identity theory to explore when and why algorithmic management affects gig worker’s well-being differently. It emphasizes the mediating role of occupational identification, and the moderating effects of protean career orientation and educational level. Based on a three-wave time-lagged study over seven months, findings show that the indirect relationship of algorithmic management to well-being via occupational identification was positive when gig workers were low in protean career orientation, whereas the relationship was negative when they were high in this orientation. There is also a three-way interaction of algorithmic management, protean career orientation and educational level on occupational identification, where educational level weakened the buffering effect of protean career orientation. The indirect negative relationship was most pronounced among gig workers who were high in protean career orientation and low in educational level. By highlighting the personal role in gig work, this study reveals the psychological mechanism under the impact of algorithmic management on gig worker’s well-being, which not only demonstrates the double-sided effect of the management system, but also creates the possibility to reverse its negative impact from the personal perspective. It also provides a nuanced perspective to understand the inconsistent findings on gig worker’s well-being, and enriches literature on protean career orientation by introducing it into the gig work context.
