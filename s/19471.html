How Successor CEOs Use Disclosure & Impression Management to Signal Strategic Renewal After Dismissal

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
STR

People

Author: Binbin LI The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

This study examines how successor CEOs strategically respond to the legitimacy threat triggered by the past failure of their predecessors. Departing from prior research that has focused primarily on why CEOs are dismissed and how markets react to dismissal events, this study shifts attention to how successor CEOs actively manage external perceptions during the critical post-dismissal window. Drawing on legitimacy restoration and impression management theories, we argue that successor CEOs deliberately escalate voluntary disclosure as a behavioral mechanism to both distance themselves from the predecessor’s failure and signal strategic renewal to external stakeholders. We further propose that this disclosure escalation is shaped by the dismissal reason of predecessor and the origin of successor CEO, which is intensified particularly when the predecessor was dismissed for poor performance and when the successor is an outsider with greater symbolic latitude to break from the past. By reconceptualizing voluntary disclosure not merely as an informational tool but as a dual-purpose mechanism of self-protection and self-promotion, this study advances a successor-centric behavioral perspective on post-dismissal governance.
