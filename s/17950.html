Examining the Disclosures of Menstruating Women at Work: A Mixed Methods Investigation

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 11:30AM–12:30PM
Loews Philadelphia Hotel — Poster Wall 2
DEI

People

Authors: Aqsa Dutli Purdue University; Allison S. Gabriel Purdue University; Nitya Chawla Not Associated; Katina Sawyer The University of Arizona

Session overview

Menstruation represents a natural, yet highly stigmatized, health experience that many women manage on a recurring basis at work. Despite its regular occurrence and intersection with work, little is known about what may, or may not, contribute to women deciding to disclose their menstrual status in organizational settings. Drawing on an initial qualitative investigation as well as theories tied to impression management, we conduct a multi-wave field study to examine why and how women disclose (or conceal) menstruation at work—namely, motives tied to their experience of pain, concerns regarding their performance, or goals of building camaraderie with other working women. We also consider an alternate motive of hiding, such that women may aim to minimize their menstrual status at work. We further examine the downstream consequences of these motives and subsequent disclosures, elucidating that they differentially relate to employees’ relational closeness with coworkers, their health-related productivity, and their feelings of regret following disclosure. Together, these findings reveal the complex nature of menstrual disclosure at work, highlighting the different reasons women choose to disclose menstruation at work as well as the performance- and relationship-based implications of these choices.
