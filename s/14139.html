Beyond Tokenism: University Communication and LGBTQ+ Students’ Sense of Belonging

Friday, Jul 31 2026 · 4:00PM–5:00PM
Pennsylvania Convention Center — Poster Wall 5
MED

People

Authors: MAHSA GHAFFARI University of Portsmouth; Haiming Hang University of Bath; Valerio Deriu LUISS Guido Carli University

Session overview

Higher education institutions increasingly portray themselves as inclusive and socially responsible. Yet, for LGBTQ+ students, universities are experienced as spaces of vulnerability, marginalization, or performative inclusion rather than genuine care. This study examines how university communication strategies (particularly those enacted through institutional websites) shape LGBTQ+ students’ perceptions of authenticity, belonging, and engagement. Drawing on Communication Accommodation Theory (CAT) (Elhami, 2020) and verbal authenticity (Markowitz et al., 2023), we conceptualize institutional communication as a relational practice through which universities signal recognition, respect, and inclusion. Using a sequential mixed-methods design, this research comprises a completed qualitative phase and an ongoing quantitative phase. In-depth interviews with LGBTQ+ students in the UK (n = 6) reveal that authenticity, understood as “walking the talk,” is central to how students evaluate institutional commitments to inclusion. Participants described symbolic gestures such as rainbow flags or Pride-themed messaging as meaningful yet insufficient when not accompanied by tangible support structures, visible LGBTQ+ leadership, and institutional accountability. The second phase, currently underway, adopts an experimental design using manipulated university website stimuli to examine how perceived authenticity influences students’ sense of belonging, enrolment intentions, and academic engagement, while testing the mediating roles of perceived warmth, competence, and trust and the moderating effects of gender identity and psychological gender affirmation. Theoretically, this study extends CAT by positioning authenticity as a key mechanism through which institutions accommodate marginalized identities. Practically, it offers guidance for universities moving beyond performative inclusion toward communication practices grounded in care and social justice, reframing inclusion as a relational institutional practice.
