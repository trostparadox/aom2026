Entrepreneurial Teams' Collective Humility and Psychological Safety in Processing Peer Feedback

Monday, Aug 3 2026 · 2:30PM–3:30PM
Philadelphia Marriott Downtown — Poster Wall 5
ENT

People

Authors: Maya Kumar IE University; Gabriela Caso de los Cobos Tapia IE University

Session overview

This paper investigates how collective humility within entrepreneurial teams influences their capacity to process and learn from peer feedback. While teams frequently seek out feedback, they often struggle to engage with, reflect on, and act on the information provided. We argue that collective humility— characterized by teachability, appreciation for others’ abilities, and openness to new perspectives—enhances engagement with feedback and experimentation. By focusing on peer feedback as a distinct source of learning, we aim to have three contributions. First, we contribute to research on entrepreneurial learning by identifying collective humility as a critical condition for team-based feedback-based learning. Second, we extend feedback-processing models typically observed at the individual level to peer-based social contexts. Third, we advance a team-level perspective on how learning unfolds in early-stage entrepreneurial settings.
