Contextual Activation of Personality: Job Satisfaction Trajectories in Family and Non-Family Firms (CANCELLED)

Authors: Sen Chen University of Nottingham Ningbo China; Ziqian Li University of Nottingham Ningbo China

Session overview

Non-family employees in family firms often report lower job satisfaction, yet we know little about whether the drivers of their job satisfaction differ from those in non-family firms. This study asks whether Big Five personality traits shape job satisfaction trajectories differently across these organizational contexts. Drawing on Trait Activation Theory, we conceptualize family and non-family firms as distinct cue environments and highlight informality and relationship-based governance as core contextual features of family firms. Using 2016–2022 data from the China Family Panel Studies and multigroup latent growth models, we compare how personality predicts initial levels and change in job satisfaction over time. In non-family firms, results largely mirror established findings: agreeableness, conscientiousness, and extraversion predict higher initial satisfaction, whereas neuroticism relates to declining satisfaction. In family firms, openness and agreeableness surprisingly predict both higher initial satisfaction and positive slopes. Therefore, this study links Trait Activation Theory to the family firm context by showing how informal, relationally governed workplaces provide distinctive cues for trait expression. It also extends employee experience in family business research by examining how non-family employees’ personalities shape their evolving job satisfaction.
