Session overview

Careers increasingly unfold through movement both within and across organizations, yet the processes that shape career mobility and advancement remain unevenly understood. This symposium brings together four papers that examine how early conditions, organizational practices, and contextual dynamics jointly structure career trajectories over time. Drawing on large-scale longitudinal data across multiple industries and organizational settings, the papers investigate how educational experiences shape the quality of work individuals access, whether organizational changes to hiring standards expand upward mobility, how different forms of work effort are evaluated and rewarded within organizations, and how timing and status influence career entry and exit in emerging fields. Collectively, the papers illuminate how career mobility and advancement are shaped not only by individual attributes, but also by organizational and broader institutional contexts that differentially enable or constrain opportunity. By integrating perspectives on careers within organizations and across organizational boundaries, this symposium advances understanding of how advantages and disadvantages accumulate over time, with important implications for research on careers, human resource practices, and organizational inequality.
