Session overview

Interpersonal mistreatment is a pervasive feature of organizational life, ranging from everyday incivility and exclusion to more severe forms of harassment, grievance-related conflict, and, in extreme cases, violence. Yet scholars continue to grapple with why some responses to workplace harm restore trust and stability while others leave harm unresolved or allow it to escalate. This symposium brings together four papers that reconceptualize mistreatment as a dynamic trajectory of harm, interpretation, and response enacted by organizational members within institutional contexts. Rather than treating organizational interventions as inherently corrective, the contributions show that outcomes hinge on how employees interpret managerial intent, how coworkers and third parties respond to mistreatment, how perpetrators’ attempts at repair are perceived by recipients, and how unresolved harm may intensify over time. Drawing on diverse methods and theoretical perspectives, the papers examine grievance management, bystander and third-party responses, interpersonal trust repair following incivility, and the escalation of unresolved grievances into targeted violence. Collectively, the symposium advances a process-based account of how organizational systems, interpersonal reactions, and individual sensemaking jointly shape whether workplace harm is contained, reinterpreted, or allowed to worsen. By illuminating when responses restore trust, when they fail, and when they unintentionally legitimize escalation, this session offers new insight into the dynamics of mistreatment, conflict, and repair in contemporary organizations.
