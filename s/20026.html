Session overview

This presenter symposium focuses on how multi-business firms allocate and redeploy resources internally – a fundamental HQ function that decides which activities to fund, which to exit, and how to shift capital, assets, and people toward their new best use as environments change (Folta, Helfat, and Karim, 2016; Levinthal & Wu, 2025). Hence, resource allocation determines how firms strategically renew themselves and innovate, and whether they remain competitive. However, despite its theoretical and managerial importance, after early foundational contributions (e.g., Bower, 1970) strategy research saw a decline in work that treated resource allocation as the central object of inquiry. Instead, resource allocation largely served a secondary role as a background mechanism in studies on diversification, international expansion, or M&As (Amit & Livnat, 1988; Grant, 1987; Lubatkin, 1987; Markides & Williamson, 1994). In recent years, however, strategy research has seen a renewed stream of work that places resource allocation and redeployment at the center of analysis (e.g., see the reviews of Ao, Mahoney, & Sakhartov, 2025; Busenbark, Wiseman, Arrfelt, & Woo, 2017; Sengul, Almeida Costa, & Gimeno, 2019). However, findings from these recent studies are mixed with respect to the extent to which multi-business firms allocate resources efficiently across their business units. To move the understanding of this issue forward, corporate strategy scholarship requires renewed theory building alongside targeted empirical research. To address this issue, the symposium brings together contributions that approach the topic from multiple theoretical and empirical angles. Specifically, the symposium features four distinct perspectives on resource allocation, focusing on: (i) how HQ centralization affects worker redeployment through internal labor markets, (ii) how downturn-driven resource reallocation relaxes input constraints and enables cost-saving process innovation adoption, (iii) how airlines’ fleet/network configurations and labor-allocation routines shape redeployment during sudden demand shocks, and (iv) how firms trade off internal redeployment versus external hiring under employee-skill and shifting geographic constraints.
