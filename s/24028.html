Corporate Carbon Performance: An Integrative Review from an Organizational Complexity Perspective

ONE
Jiabao Li (School of Management, Northwestern Polytechnical University); Yuxin Tian (School of Management, Northwestern Polytechnical University)
As corporations increasingly become central to global climate governance, corporate carbon performance (CCP) has shifted from a technical emissions measure to a core organizational challenge. Firms must coordinate technological innovation, governance arrangements, and strategic responses amid rising regulatory pressure and environmental uncertainty. While research on CCP is growing rapidly, it remains fragmented across disciplines, limiting theoretical integration from an organizational perspective. This study draws on organizational complexity theory and synthesizes 274 peer-reviewed articles to reconceptualize CCP as a problem of organizing under cognitive, structural, and behavioral complexity. Using bibliometric analysis and topic modeling, we map the intellectual structure, thematic evolution, and emerging trajectories of CCP research. The analysis reveals that CCP scholarship closely follows global climate policy trends, with growing emphasis on digital technologies, governance mechanisms, and carbon disclosure, especially in high-complexity institutional contexts. Building on this synthesis, we propose a three-dimensional framework—Technology-driven, Governance structure, and Strategic response (TGS)—that integrates previously disconnected research streams. The framework shows how firms manage cognitive complexity through digital tools, structural complexity through governance, and behavioral complexity through strategic responses to carbon constraints. Rather than viewing carbon performance as a regulatory outcome, we frame CCP as an active managerial process shaped by organizational design and strategy. This study advances management research by: (1) integrating fragmented CCP literature through organizational complexity theory; (2) clarifying the roles of digital technologies and governance in shaping carbon outcomes; and (3) outlining a forward-looking agenda emphasizing AI, carbon information infrastructures, and strategic orchestration. By reframing CCP as an organizational challenge, this work encourages management scholars to treat climate governance as a central issue of organizing, not merely a peripheral sustainability concern.
