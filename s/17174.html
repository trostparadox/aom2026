Author: Yuting Chen The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Session overview

Authoritarian and benevolent leadership are two common leadership behaviors, yet there remains a lack of consensus in the literature regarding the conditions under which each is effective. Integrating the Cognitive Appraisal Theory of Emotion, Expectancy Violation Theory, and research on awe, we propose a third-party perspective: when leaders adopt authoritarian behaviors toward high-status employees, it may trigger threat-based awe, thereby increasing observers’ compliance; conversely, when leaders demonstrate benevolence toward low-status employees, it may evoke positive awe, subsequently promoting organizational citizenship behaviors among observers. To test this model, we first developed a two-dimensional measure of interpersonal awe, followed by experimental and survey methods to validate our hypotheses. Finally, we discuss the theoretical implications and practical significance of our findings.
