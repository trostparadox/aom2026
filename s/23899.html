Withdrawing Patent Subsidies: Innovation Adjustment and Employment Effects

TIM
Zhaoxing Hao; Yang Ye (Southwestern University of Finance and Economics)
Employment stability has become a pressing challenge in today’s uncertain global economy. While innovation policies aim to enhance firms’ competitiveness and drive economic growth, their effects on employment are not always straightforward, as firms’ responses to policy incentives can generate unintended outcomes such as “innovation illusions”. This study examines whether optimizing innovation policies can strengthen the link between innovation and employment. We develop a framework of “policy optimization—innovation correction—employment response”. To empirically test this framework, we exploit China’s 2021 termination of patent application subsidies as a quasi-natural experiment. Using panel data from A-share listed firms from 2017 to 2024 and a difference-in-differences approach, we find that policy optimization significantly increases firm employment, primarily by improving innovation quality and reallocating innovation resources. Further analyses show that optimization reduces negative employment fluctuations and enhances positive ones, with stronger effects for non-state-owned and smaller firms. Our findings highlight the importance of integrating employment objectives into innovation policy design and provide guidance for crafting innovation policies that support both firm competitiveness and labor market stability.
