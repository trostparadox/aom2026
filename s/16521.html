Organizational Design Under Continuous Learning: Rethinking the Information-Processing View

Sunday, Aug 2 2026 · 1:00PM–2:00PM
Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown Hotel — Poster Wall 4
OMT

People

Authors: Ana Milan Aleksic Miric University of Belgrade; Katarina Božic University of East Sarajevo; Anja Stevanovic University of Belgrade, Faculty of Economics and Business

Session overview

Classical organization design assumes that informational requirements can be specified ex ante, that roles precede tasks, and that coordination is achieved through stable structural mechanisms. Yet in digitally mediated environments, information-processing needs emerge within work, as machine sensing, patient self-triage, and system-level analytics continually rearrange tasks and coordination. Under such conditions, information-processing capacity is no longer the core challenge; rather, organizations struggle with orientation—determining what information matters, where interpretation should occur, and how learning should reshape coordination. Through an illustrative clinical scenario, we show how formal architectures and informal networks jointly produce organizing in motion. We reconstruct the information-processing view by shifting from capacity optimization to orientation under continuous learning, and articulate implications for organization design by conceptualizing design as the capacity to remain coherent while in motion.
