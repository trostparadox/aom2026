Session overview

Research in organization studies is increasingly returning to the body as a critical site for understanding work, institutions, and organizing. However, scholarship on embodiment remains fragmented and theoretically dispersed. Existing work conceptualizes the body in diverse ways (as a tool or resource for task execution, as a symbolic marker of occupational fitness and meaning, or as an object of organizational shaping and control) often across distinct theoretical terrains. At the same time, contemporary work is marked by technological mediation, heightened emotional demands, and new forms of embodied and affective labor; making questions of how bodies are organized and how they organize, increasingly urgent. This symposium aims to advance research at the intersection of bodies, emotions, work, and institutions by creating a space for dialogue across these fragmented approaches. Bringing together scholars whose work examines embodiment through relational, somatic, emotional, and institutional lenses, the symposium invites discussion around key tensions in how embodiment is conceptualized, how embodied and emotional experiences relate to institutional stability and change, and how such processes can be studied. Rather than offering a unified framework, the symposium seeks to clarify differences, identify points of convergence, and emphasize promising directions for future research on embodiment in organization studies.
