Session overview

This symposium, titled “Loss and Grief at Work: Social Recognition, Disclosure, and Response,” examines how loss and grief are experienced and addressed in organizational contexts. Loss and grief, arising from personal life events, professional setbacks, or broader disruptions, are common at work, yet organizational research has only begun to examine how these experiences unfold through social interaction. This symposium advances understanding by focusing on how loss is recognized or neglected, when and to whom employees disclose loss-related experiences, and how leaders, colleagues, and organizations respond. Across five papers, the symposium highlights the interpersonal, normative, and organizational dynamics that shape support, communication, and connection following loss. Drawing on projects using a variety of methods, the symposium offers new insights into the social processes of loss and grief at work and identifies implications for research and organizational practice aimed at fostering compassion and well-being. The symposium seeks to engage scholars interested in loss and grief at work, while offering actionable recommendations for future research and practice.
